# CSS prep: start here

> **Website:** https://mahad002.github.io/css-pakistan-practice/ has everything in this folder, plus the interactive syllabus checklist, MCQ practice, an exam builder and the downloads. Progress is saved in your browser.

**Target exam:** CSS 2028, your first attempt. Screening test (MPT) late Sep – mid Oct 2027; written papers late Jan – mid Feb 2028. These dates are projected from the CSS 2027 schedule, so confirm them on fpsc.gov.pk each July.
**Score target:** 700+ out of 1,200 in the written exam: pass every paper, then score high enough for a top group. (This is my target, not an FPSC figure. The rule is 50% aggregate = 600, but allocation to PAS, FSP or PSP needs well above that.)
**Study budget:** about 15–18 hours a week from 1 Feb 2027 (2 hours of it is daily reading and flashcards). Before then, only 20 minutes of Dawn a day.

This folder is your complete self-study course for CSS. It holds:

- verified rules and dates;
- a dated week-by-week schedule;
- full study packs for the screening test and all six compulsory papers;
- practice banks and model answers;
- tracking sheets so you always know what to do next and where you are losing marks.

It cannot replace two things: **writing by hand under a timer every week**, and **keeping current affairs up to date until the exam**. The schedule builds both in.

| Paper | Pass rule | Failed it (FPSC, 2019–2023) | Your target |

| Current Affairs | (same 120/300 rule) | 49–97% | 55+ |
| Pakistan Affairs | (same 120/300 rule) | 37–99% | 55+ |
| Islamic Studies | 40/100 | 18–81% | 60+ |
| Optionals (600) | 33% each | varies by subject | 350+ |
| **Written total** | **600/1,200** | 2–3% of candidates pass the written exam | **700+** |

**Essay decides the result.** In five years only about 3% of candidates passed it. That's why the essay gets a fixed 3–4 hours every week for a year.

## The source-quality rule

Trust sources in this order:

1. **FPSC documents:** rules, syllabus, notices, past papers, annual reports. They decide every question about format and rules.
2. **Official FPSC past papers:** the best guide to what is asked.
3. **This folder:** teaching, notes, model answers, drills and tracking. Every pack marks unverified facts `[verify]`.
4. **Coaching sites and forums:** extra practice or a second explanation only. Their "high-scoring subject" and "guess paper" claims are often wrong (see `04-Research/`).

## Six non-negotiable habits

1. **Write by hand.** Every essay, précis and answer goes on paper, timed. Typing hides your real speed and errors.
2. **Mark everything.** Use the scorecards; an unmarked essay teaches you half as much.
3. **Log every lost mark** in the error log: wrong MCQs, weak answers, grammar slips, facts you didn't know.
4. **Redo before it's fixed.** An error is closed only after two correct redos on different days.
5. **Read Dawn daily.** Current Affairs, Pakistan Affairs and the Essay all draw on it.
6. **Get human feedback.** Have at least one essay a month read by someone who has passed CSS (an alumnus of your university who is an officer, or a paid essay checker).

## Honest expectation

No folder can guarantee a pass: about 2–3% of candidates pass the written exam each year. Passing depends on a year of steady work, timed hand-written practice and honest marking. This folder gives you the content and the system, and `01-Plan/CSS-Plan.md` sets decision dates so you can judge, with evidence, whether to sit CSS 2028 or wait.
