# CSS Optional Subjects: Research Report

Researched 23 Nov 2026 for a first attempt at CSS 2028 (written exam about Feb 2028).

## 1. Short answer

- **Drop Computer Science.** FPSC's own figures for CSS 2023: 221 candidates appeared, 121 failed the subject (55%), 1 scored 60% or more, and none of the 221 was allocated. A third-party dataset for CSS 2025 gives a mean of 31.5% and a median of 32.5%, which is below the 33% pass mark. Failing one optional fails the whole written exam.
- **Drop History of USA and Governance & Public Policies.** History of USA: 68% failed it in CSS 2023, and the 2025 mean was 34%. Governance & Public Policies: fine in 2023, but the 2025 mean was 29%.
- **You do not need a history subject.** The rules let you take Group I (200) plus any four of the five 100-mark groups (III to VII).
- **Recommended:** Political Science (200) + Public Administration (100) + Gender Studies (100) + Criminology (100) + Journalism & Mass Communication (100).
- **Maths alternate:** swap in Applied Mathematics + Statistics, but only after a timed self-test on past papers (section 8).
- **A combination with no Group I subject appears to be allowed** (section 3). Confirm it with FPSC in writing before relying on it.

## 3. The rules (FPSC, official)

Source: CSS Competitive Examination Rules 2019, Appendix-I, and the FPSC syllabus. The CSS 2027 public notice (8 Nov 2026) says the exam is "governed entirely by the CSS CE Rules, 2019" and that optional subjects are chosen "as per Appendix-I".

| Group | Rule in Appendix-I | Subjects (code, marks) |
|---|---|---|
| I | One subject carrying 200 marks can be opted | 11 Accountancy & Auditing 200; 12 Economics 200; 13 Computer Science 200; 14 Political Science 200; 15 International Relations 200 |
| II | One subject of 200 marks (codes 16–17) OR two subjects of 100 marks each (codes 18–21) | 16 Physics 200; 17 Chemistry 200; 18 Applied Mathematics 100; 19 Pure Mathematics 100; 20 Statistics 100; 21 Geology 100 |
| III | One subject of 100 marks | 22 Business Administration; 23 Public Administration; 24 Governance & Public Policies; 25 Town Planning & Urban Management |
| IV | One subject of 100 marks | 26 History of Pakistan & India; 27 Islamic History & Culture; 28 British History; 29 European History; 30 History of USA |
| V | One subject of 100 marks | 31 Gender Studies; 32 Environmental Sciences; 33 Agriculture & Forestry; 34 Botany; 35 Zoology; 36 English Literature; 37 Urdu Literature |
| VI | One subject of 100 marks | 38 Law; 39 Constitutional Law; 40 International Law; 41 Muslim Law & Jurisprudence; 42 Mercantile Law; 43 Criminology; 44 Philosophy |
| VII | One subject of 100 marks | 45 Journalism & Mass Communication; 46 Psychology; 47 Geography; 48 Sociology; 49 Anthropology; 50 Punjabi; 51 Sindhi; 52 Pashto; 53 Balochi; 54 Persian; 55 Arabic |

Other rules that matter:

- **The total must be exactly 600.** Groups I to VII add up to 900, so you always leave groups out. The usual shape is Group I (200) + four of Groups III–VII. Group II can supply 100 or 200 of the 600.
- **Forbidden pairs:** none are listed separately. The pairs people mention (Business Administration with Public Administration, two law subjects, two histories) are blocked because both sit in the same group. Group II is the only group where you can take two subjects.
- **200-mark subjects have two papers of 100 each; 100-mark subjects have one paper.** Each paper is 3 hours.
- **MCQs:** 20 MCQs (20 marks, first 30 minutes) in every optional paper except Applied Mathematics and Pure Mathematics.
- **Regional languages, Persian and Arabic must be answered in that language** (Rule 11(24), syllabus Note-1). Punjabi means writing the paper in Punjabi.
- **Pass marks:** 33% in every optional, 40% in compulsory subjects, and **50% in the aggregate** (Rule 12). Appendix-I adds that the three General Knowledge papers (General Science & Ability, Current Affairs, Pakistan Affairs) "are to be passed collectively by scoring 120 marks out of 300".
- **The combination is final at the closing date of the written-exam application** (Rule 11(14)). A wrong combination means rejection (Rule 11(13)).

### Is a combination without a Group I subject allowed?

Very probably yes. Get it confirmed by FPSC in writing before you rely on it.

- The rule text says a Group I subject "can be opted". It does not say "must". The syllabus heading reads "To select one subject of (200 marks) only".
- **CSS 2023 (FPSC table):** Group I subjects account for 207 allocated candidates, but 210 were allocated. The marks balance exactly, so 3 allocated candidates had no Group I subject.
- **CSS 2025 (third-party data):** Group I subjects account for 354 of 355 written qualifiers and 169 of 170 allocated candidates. One had no Group I subject.
- These are my inferences from the tables. FPSC does not state it anywhere I could read, and coaching sites assume Group I is compulsory.

## 8. Recommendation

### Recommended combination

| Group | Subject | Marks | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Political Science | 200 | Passable in every year with data (2025 median 51.5%). Taken by 61% of allocated candidates in 2023 and 86% in 2025. Overlaps with Pakistan Affairs, Current Affairs and the Essay. Books are easy to get anywhere |
| III | Public Administration | 100 | Best recent Group III record (2025 median 58%). Overlaps with Political Science and with governance questions in Pakistan Affairs |
| V | Gender Studies | 100 | Short syllabus, good in 2023 and 2025, feeds essay topics. Watch the 2026–27 data |
| VI | Criminology | 100 | Short syllabus, no background needed, 2025 median 61%. Had a strict year in 2023 |
| VII | Journalism & Mass Communication | 100 | Highest-scoring non-language subject in 2023 and 2025. Overlaps with Current Affairs. Small pool (about 350), so watch for a correction |

My estimate for a well-prepared candidate: **about 310–400 out of 600**, most likely near 350. By subject: Political Science 95–125, Public Administration 50–70, Gender Studies 55–72, Criminology 50–70, Journalism 60–80.

On the same basis the provisional combination (Computer Science + Governance & Public Policies + History of USA + Gender Studies + Criminology) comes to roughly 225–345, with a real chance of failing Computer Science or History of USA outright.

The cost: Political Science is the largest reading load here (two papers: Western and Muslim political thought, comparative systems, Pakistan's political system). At 15 hours a week you have about 750 hours in total. Plan roughly 150 hours for Political Science and 50 each for the four 100-mark subjects, which leaves about 400 for the compulsory papers.

### Alternate 1: safe and popular

Political Science (200) + Public Administration (100) + Environmental Sciences (100) + Criminology (100) + Psychology (100).

This swaps the two picks that carry the most trend risk. Environmental Sciences replaces Gender Studies (the most crowded subject, and reported as targeted in 2026). Psychology replaces Journalism (a small pool, so its high marks may not last). Both replacements are science-style subjects that suit an engineer and use standard international textbooks. Environmental Sciences also overlaps with General Science & Ability. My estimate: about 300–380.

### Alternate 2: plays to the maths strength

Political Science (200) + Applied Mathematics (100) + Statistics (100) + Gender Studies (100) + Criminology (100).

- Maths papers are marked on right and wrong working, so the ceiling is higher than in essay subjects. In 2023, 14% of Applied Mathematics candidates scored 60% or more, against 0.5% in Computer Science and 0.2% in Political Science.
- The risk is also higher. Most candidates fail these papers (Applied Mathematics: 52% failed in 2023, mean 29% in 2025. Statistics: 73% failed in 2023, mean 46% in 2025). The 2025 standard deviations were 21–24 points, against 12–17 in most essay subjects.
- The Applied Mathematics paper is "attempt five of eight questions", calculator allowed, no MCQs. The CSS 2023 paper had questions on statics, Runge-Kutta, Newton's interpolation, vector calculus, Bernoulli and higher-order differential equations, power series, the secant and Gauss-Seidel methods, Fourier series and Laplace's equation. The choice of five lets you avoid most of the mechanics.
- Statistics has 20 MCQs plus written questions. It matches a university probability and statistics course, plus sampling, design of experiments and vital statistics.
- Pure Mathematics needs group theory, ring theory, and real and complex analysis, which a software engineering degree does not cover. I would not take it.
- Reading load is the lowest of any option: it is problem practice.
- My estimate: about 290–420. The range is wide because each maths paper could land anywhere from 40 to 85.
- **Test before choosing:** after about four weeks of revision, sit the 2024, 2025 and 2026 Applied Mathematics and Statistics papers under exam timing. Take this alternate only if you score 70% or more. The papers are on the FPSC site.
- **If FPSC confirms Group I can be skipped:** Applied Mathematics + Statistics + Public Administration + Gender Studies + Criminology + Journalism. This removes Political Science, the largest reading load. It is the lightest combination of all, and also the least tested.

### What would make me switch

- **FPSC Annual Report 2024 or 2025** (subject-wise table) is published: re-check every pick against it.
- **Gender Studies** shows a poor mean in CSS 2026 and CSS 2027 data: move to Environmental Sciences.
- **Journalism** candidate numbers jump, or its marks fall: move to Psychology.
- **Political Science** has a strict year while Economics stays high: Economics becomes the Group I choice for a strong maths candidate, at the cost of a longer and more technical syllabus.
- **Maths self-test** of 70% or more: move to Alternate 2.
- **You can write Punjabi well and hold Punjab domicile:** Punjabi becomes a realistic Group VII choice (57% scored 60%+ in 2023; 2025 median 52%).
- **Computer Science:** only if two successive FPSC years show a normal pass rate. Nothing suggests that today.

### Timing

The combination becomes final only when the written-exam application closes. For CSS 2027 that was 25 Nov 2026, so expect late Nov 2027 for CSS 2028. Start Political Science, Criminology and Gender Studies in Feb 2027, and keep the Group III and Group VII picks open until the CSS 2026 statistics and CSS 2027 marks are out.

## 9. Computer Science in detail

**Structure (FPSC syllabus pages 56–59, and the CSS 2023 and 2026 papers).** 200 marks in two papers of 100. Each paper is 3 hours: Part I is 20 MCQs for 20 marks in the first 30 minutes, with no negative marking. Part II is 80 marks: attempt four questions of 20 marks each, two from each section.

| Paper | Section A (50 marks) | Section B (50 marks) |
|---|---|---|
| Paper I | Introduction to Computing; Programming Fundamentals; Object Oriented Paradigm (C++ style: classes, operator overloading, inheritance, polymorphism, STL, files, exceptions) | Algorithms & Data Structures (analysis, sorting, hashing, trees including AVL and 2-3, graphs, heuristic search, genetic algorithms, DES and RSA); Software Engineering; Compiler Construction (lexical analysis, automata, LL and LR parsing, FIRST and FOLLOW, syntax-directed translation, code generation and optimisation) |
| Paper II | Computer Organization & Architecture; Computer Communications & Networks; Operating Systems Concepts | Database Systems; Digital Image Processing; Web Engineering & Technologies |

**How candidates score.**

- CSS 2023 (FPSC): 221 appeared, 121 failed, 99 scored 33–59%, 1 scored 60% or more, 0 allocated.
- CSS 2019–2023 (FPSC): the number passing fell from 317 (2021) to 145 (2022) to 99 (2023).
- CSS 2025 (third party): 177 appeared, mean 63/200, median 65/200, 4 passed the written exam, 2 allocated.
- Coaching sites describe Computer Science as "moderate, 90–120". The FPSC figures do not support that.

**Can a software engineer expect high marks?** No. The people taking this paper are already mostly computing graduates, and more than half of them fail it. A realistic range for a well-prepared software engineer is 70–100 out of 200, with a real risk of falling under 66 and failing the whole exam.

**Main pitfalls.**

- Twelve subject areas across two papers. Compiler construction, digital image processing, computer architecture and network protocols go well past daily software work.
- The questions are textbook theory and hand-worked problems, not code that runs. The CSS 2023 Paper II asked for DRAM refresh overhead, router queueing time, I/O channel transfer rates and image transmission time. Paper I asked for a finite automaton, a syntax tree and order-of-growth comparisons.
- A small candidate pool marked by academic examiners: 1 candidate in 221 reached 60% in 2023.
- Almost no CSS-specific preparation material or evaluated answers exist for this subject.
- It puts 200 marks on the Group I subject with the worst record.

## 11. What I could not confirm

- **FPSC's own statement on skipping Group I.** The rule text allows it and both data years imply a few allocated candidates did it, but FPSC does not say so in plain words. Whether the online form accepts it is untested.
- **Official figures for CSS 2024, 2025 and 2026.** The 2025 numbers come from one third-party site. The 2026 claims come from a coaching page quoting a video I did not see.
- **Highest marks per subject.** FPSC does not publish them. The only figures are single DMCs quoted by a coaching site (Economics 154/200, Political Science 145/200, Public Administration 79/100 in CSS 2026).
- **Toppers' own combinations, DMCs and interviews.** Not retrieved (section 2).
- **FPSC annual reports for 2019–2022** in the same detail as 2023. The 2022 file is a scanned image; reading it needs OCR.
- **Self-study material ratings.** These are my judgement, not checked against booksellers.

## 12. Points that differ from the existing notes in this folder

- `01-Plan/Optional-Subjects.md` says "at most one subject per group". Group II allows two 100-mark subjects.
- `01-Plan/Exam-Rules.md` says you need 40% in each compulsory paper. Appendix-I says the three General Knowledge papers are passed collectively at 120 out of 300. Rule 12 also requires 50% in the aggregate, which the guide does not mention.
- The CSS 2027 written exam starts on 27 Jan 2027, with applications closing 25 Nov 2026. If CSS 2028 follows the same calendar, the written exam may start in late January 2028, not February.
