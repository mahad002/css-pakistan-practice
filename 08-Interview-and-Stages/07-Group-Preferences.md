# The 12 groups and how to order your preferences

Compiled 24 Nov 2026. Source numbers point to `09-Sources.md`.

**About the evidence:** I found very few first-hand descriptions of group life by serving officers that I could open. Officer interviews on JWT and cssforum would not load, and Reddit is blocked to my tools. The group descriptions below therefore rest on official bodies (FPSC, CSA, MOFA), Wikipedia, and one detailed coaching guide [S21]. Pros and cons are labelled **Coaching / opinion** unless an officer source is cited. Before you fill in the form, talk to at least two serving officers of each of your top four groups.

## 2. How allocation works in practice

FPSC does not publish the algorithm. This description is **derived** from Rules 20–21 and FPSC's notice wording [S1, S32], and matches the coaching description [S21].

1. All finally qualified candidates are ranked by total marks (written + viva).
2. Going down the list from the top, each candidate is placed in the **highest group on their preference list that still has a seat** in the right quota cell: their domicile's share, open merit or women or minority.
3. If none of your listed groups has a seat left in your cell, you are not allocated, even though you passed.

**Consequences (derived):**

- **Ordering cannot hurt your chances of a higher choice.** Listing Postal 11th does not reduce your chance of PAS 1st. It only matters if you miss all the groups above it.
- **Listing more groups lowers the risk of not being allocated.** Each year about 135–250 seats go unfilled, almost all in quota cells [S2], while some passers in crowded cells get nothing.
- **Put a group high only if you would truly take it.** If you are allocated a group you then refuse to join, the seat goes to the waiting list (Rule 21) and you lose that cycle's result.

**Merit thresholds:** FPSC publishes no "closing ranks". Coaching estimates (unsourced): top 30–40 for a strong chance at PAS; 60–100 for PSP [S21]. A coaching summary says all of the top 30 in CSS 2025 went to PAS and PSP; I could not verify this [S21]. Treat these as rough folklore.

## 4. How to order your preferences: a method

All Opinion, built on the verified rules above.

1. **Rank by genuine preference.** Your list order cannot reduce your chance of a higher choice (see section 2).
2. **List all 12** unless there is a group you would truly refuse to join. Every extra group lowers the risk of passing but not being allocated.
3. **Apply the medical filters early** (`03-Medical-Board.md`). If your uncorrected eyesight is below the Police standard, PSP may close. Colour blindness closes Railways.
4. **Match the work to you.** For an engineering or IT graduate, groups where digitisation is the live agenda (IRS, Customs, PAS e-governance, PAAS systems audit, FSP economic and tech diplomacy) give your background a use. Be ready to explain this in the viva (`06-Interview-Question-Bank.md` section M).
5. **Weigh life factors honestly:** family, frequent transfers (PAS, PSP), postings abroad (FSP, Commerce and Trade), city life (IRS, OMG, PAAS).
6. **Talk to officers** of your top four groups before the viva. Ask about their first five years, transfers, pay and what they would do differently.
7. **Revise at the viva only for a good reason.** You may revise once (Rule 20(3)). Expect to be asked why. The board may ask why your first choice is first [R5 in the question bank].
