# Psychological assessment

Compiled 24 Nov 2026. Source numbers point to `09-Sources.md`.

## 1. What is verified

**CSS Rules 2019, Rule 14 [S1]:**

- Every candidate called for the viva must undergo a psychological assessment "comprising written tests and group tasks". Its purpose is to assess "abilities, attitude and personality characteristics ... with special regard to their aptitude for the civil services".
- **No separate marks.** The 300 viva marks "also reflect the performance on psychological assessment". In practice, the psychologists' report goes to the viva board alongside your form (the mechanism is not spelled out in the rules).
- Bring CNIC and two recent photographs.
- **If you are absent, you cannot sit the viva.**

**FPSC instructions (CE-2024 and CE-2026 versions), seen only via search summaries and a site quoting them [S3, S9]:**

- The assessment is "one working day".
- **Written tests** collect information about your interests, opinions and preferences.
- **Group activities:** one group discussion, then a **2-minute extempore speech** on a general topic, then a **group problem** similar to everyday work.
- A **brief interview with a psychologist** follows the same day.
- FPSC says no preparation is required and asks you to answer freely and be yourself.
- Report at 8:30 am; arrive about half an hour early for document checks. **Latecomers are not allowed in.** Centre and date changes are refused. FPSC does not pay travel or lodging.
- Bring originals, the signed form with attested copies, the educational profile proforma and undertaking, two photographs, stationery, and proof of extra-curricular work, job experience and publications.

**FPSC Annual Report description (2016 report, via a Scribd summary) [S6]:** The psychological dossier is built from verbal and non-verbal ability tests; projective tests of completion, association and construction; leaderless group discussions; problem-solving exercises; candidates ranking one another; and an interview with a psychologist.

## 2. What candidates report

### The fullest first-hand account: Mehreen Yousaf (Inland Revenue Service) [S10]

Undated, but older than the current one-day format: she describes two days. Her own words: "Psychological Assessment spans two days and is indeed a gruelling activity."

**Day 1** (report 7:30 am; document check; about ten tests with two short breaks):

| Test | Items and time she reports |
|---|---|
| Analytical reasoning and maths (IQ) | 80 MCQs in about 35 minutes: number and letter series, proportion, deduction |
| Figure-sequence completion (IQ) | 65 patterns in 40 minutes |
| Flash words (word association) | 50 words, each shown for 2–3 seconds; write a meaningful sentence at once |
| Sentence completion | 40 stems in 8 minutes; she mentions two such tests |
| Personality questionnaire | 240 statements on an agree–disagree scale |
| Descriptive personality questions | Written answers, for example the strengths and weaknesses of your parents. The panel later referred back to these |
| Situational MCQs | 6 workplace scenarios × 4 questions, 30 minutes |
| Picture story | 4 vague pictures, 5 minutes per story |

**Day 2** (report 8:45 am; groups of 7–8 seated in a semicircle, watched by three psychologists):

| Task | What she reports |
|---|---|
| Group discussion | **In Urdu**, 15 minutes. Her group's topic translates roughly as "we are not proud of our culture". One member summed up at the end |
| Individual speech | **In English**, 2 minutes after 2 minutes' preparation outside the room. Her topic was HEC policy over the past decade. Many candidates failed to finish on time |
| Command task | 15 minutes to write the problem statement, stakeholders and strategy; then 8 minutes chairing a simulated official meeting with the group. Her problem: a bus drivers' strike after a driver was arrested following an accident |
| Psychologist interview | 15–20 minutes, tailored to the candidate, fairly general. It opened with self-introduction; she was asked why she was leaving her original field, about her family, and about psychology and current affairs |
| Peer ranking | At the end of the day, each candidate ranked the others in the group from 1 to 7 |

### Other first-hand accounts

- **A doctor who sat CSS around 2018 [S11]:** two days. Day 1 was "many questions in little time"; he did poorly on geometric shapes and number series. Day 2 was group discussion, command task, public speaking and the psychological interview. He says the interview probes emotional stability with "burning questions", and advises staying calm, disagreeing when necessary and stating your view clearly. He arrived late and received warning calls from FPSC.
- **A Lahore candidate's account of March 2012 (ppscforum; page now offline, seen only via search summary) [S44]:** a group task with 15 minutes' preparation and 8 minutes' delivery, followed by interview questions about family and ambitions. These timings match Mehreen Yousaf's command task.
- **Forum post on a PMS (provincial) psychological interview, 2014 [S16]:** the candidate was asked to draw a human-figure sketch they had skipped in the written test, plus questions on occupation, weaknesses and family. This is a different commission, so it is only an illustration.

### Coaching-site descriptions (not first-hand)

- **cssprep.ai [S19]:** two days, adding an **autobiography** written test and a **command task done as an obstacle course** with planks, ropes and a barrel, led by one "commander". This differs from Mehreen Yousaf's meeting-style command task. The obstacle version may be from older years; I could not confirm which is current.
- **ilmkidunya and JWT guides [S34]:** word association described as 60 words at about 15 seconds each in one account and 50 words in another; sentence completion 40 stems in 8 minutes; groups of 7–8.

### Where the accounts conflict

| Point | Version A | Version B | My reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | One working day (current FPSC instructions [S3, S9]) | Two days (Mehreen Yousaf [S10], 2018 doctor [S11], cssprep.ai [S19]) | **Expect one long day for CSS 2028**, but read your call letter |
| Group task | "Group problem" (FPSC) or command task chaired as a meeting [S10] | Physical obstacle task with planks and barrel [S19] | Prepare for both; the skills (calm leadership, using others' ideas) are the same |
| Word association | 2–3 seconds per word, 50 words [S10] | About 15 seconds per word, 60 words [S34] | Practise at both speeds |
| Language of discussion | Urdu [S10] | Not stated elsewhere; the speech was in English | Be ready to discuss fluently in **both** |

## 3. How each part is assessed (what the sources say the psychologists look for)

| Part | What is reportedly looked for | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| Ability tests | General intelligence and aptitude under time pressure | FPSC annual report description [S6]; Reported [S10, S11] |
| Word association, sentence completion, picture story | Recurring themes: optimism or pessimism, anxiety, initiative, relationships, attitude to authority and duty. The idea is that you project your own wishes, fears and attitudes | Coaching [S19, S34]; this is the standard logic of projective tests |
| Personality questionnaire | Consistency; lie-scale style items catch people "faking good" | Coaching [S19]; consistency checking reported [S10] |
| Group discussion | Confidence, assertiveness, how clearly you speak, knowledge, teamwork, body language; neither dominating nor silent | Coaching [S19, S34]; Reported [S10] |
| Extempore speech | Communication, confidence, finishing on time | Reported [S10]; FPSC (2 min) [S3] |
| Command task / group problem | Leadership, coordination, time management, composure. **Composure matters more than solving the puzzle** | Reported [S10]; Coaching [S19] |
| Psychologist interview | Emotional stability; consistency with your written answers and form; honesty about weaknesses | Reported [S10, S11]; Coaching [S19] |
| Peer ranking | How the group saw you | Reported [S10]; FPSC annual report [S6] |

## 4. Do's and don'ts

**Do** (Reported or Coaching unless marked)

- Be honest and consistent. Inconsistencies between your day-1 answers, day-2 behaviour and interview are said to be caught [S19]. This matches FPSC's own advice to "be yourself" [S3].
- Write positive, active, realistic responses in the projective tests: people solving problems, recovering from setbacks, helping others [S19, S34].
- In the discussion, make two or three solid points, build on others' points by name, and bring quieter members in [S19].
- If you lead the command task, take charge visibly: assign roles, set the time, summarise, decide [S10].
- In the speech, structure it as one opening line, two points and one close, and finish inside 2 minutes [S10].
- Dress formally and arrive rested [S10].

**Don't**

- Don't try to game the tests with "model" answers. Repetitive, perfect positivity looks false. (Coaching [S19]; Opinion)
- Don't dominate, interrupt, or go silent in the discussion [S10, S19].
- Don't argue with or lecture the psychologist; don't try to outsmart them [S19].
- Don't arrive late; latecomers are excluded (FPSC via secondary [S9]). One 2018 candidate who arrived late got warning calls [S11].
- Don't leave blanks on timed tests if you can help it. Being slow is itself information. (Opinion)

## 5. How to practise

| Week | Practice | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Any time from Mar 2028 | Ability tests: one 35-minute number/letter series set and one figure-sequence set a week. Your GRE quant helps | 1 h/week |
| From the written result | Projective drills (sets below), timed: word association at 15 s and then 3 s per word; sentence completion at 12 s per stem; picture stories at 5 min each | 30 min, 3×/week |
| From the written result | Self-mapping on paper: achievements, failures, what you learnt, your parents' and siblings' strengths and weaknesses, why civil service. Advised by cssprep.ai [S19]; also directly useful because Mehreen Yousaf's panel quoted her descriptive answers back to her [S10] | 2 sessions |
| 4 weeks before | Group discussion practice with 4–6 people (study group or online), 15 minutes, half in Urdu and half in English | Weekly |
| 4 weeks before | 2-minute extempore speeches recorded on your phone: topic drawn at random, 2 minutes' preparation | Daily, 10 min |
| 2 weeks before | One mock command task: 15 minutes' planning, 8 minutes chairing a "meeting" with friends playing stakeholders | 2 sessions |

## 6. Practice sets (written by me for this course)

These are **my own** prompts, built to match the formats candidates describe. They are **not** FPSC items.

### 6.1 Word association: 60 prompts

**Instructions:** First at 15 seconds per word, then at 3 seconds. Write one complete sentence for each word.

**What a strong set shows:** varied, concrete sentences that link the word to action, duty, people or learning. Not every sentence needs to be cheerful. Realistic and constructive is the target: *"Failure taught me to test my code before the deadline,"* not *"Failure is the end."* A set full of "I" sentences boasting about yourself reads as self-centred. A set full of slogans ("Pakistan Zindabad") reads as rehearsed.

| | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Mother | 11 Failure | 21 Night | 31 Crowd | 41 Authority | 51 Chance |
| 2 Fear | 12 Friend | 22 Rules | 32 Code | 42 Village | 52 Enemy |
| 3 Money | 13 Exam | 23 Leader | 33 Rain | 43 Bribe | 53 Patience |
| 4 Police | 14 Blood | 24 Alone | 34 Duty | 44 Hospital | 54 Death |
| 5 Father | 15 Team | 25 Order | 35 Anger | 45 Women | 55 Home |
| 6 Power | 16 Abroad | 26 Mistake | 36 Flag | 46 Lie | 56 Victory |
| 7 Weak | 17 Office | 27 Poor | 37 Teacher | 47 Machine | 57 Help |
| 8 Hope | 18 Fight | 28 Justice | 38 Dark | 48 Promise | 58 Risk |
| 9 Future | 19 Dream | 29 Pressure | 39 Love | 49 Boss | 59 Sister |
| 10 Discipline | 20 Delay | 30 Accident | 40 Change | 50 Corruption | 60 Responsibility |

**Words to watch** (they invite telling answers): Fear, Weak, Blood, Alone, Death, Enemy, Dark, Bribe, Lie, Corruption, Anger, Failure. Good responses acknowledge the negative and show how you deal with it: *"Anger is useful only after I have slept on it."*

### 6.2 Sentence completion: 40 stems

**Instructions:** 40 stems in 8 minutes (12 seconds each). Write the first honest completion.

**What strong completions show:** a stable self-image, warm but not idealised family relationships, ownership of mistakes, calm under pressure, and respect for rules alongside initiative. Avoid blaming others, extreme words ("always", "never", "hate") and empty slogans.

13. In a team, I usually ...
14. Pakistan needs ...

16. When I am alone ...

**Stems to think about beforehand** (not to script, but so your honest answer is considered): 7, 8, 15, 19, 30, 37 and 40. Stem 30 matters if you work or study abroad: a panel may ask whether you plan to stay in Pakistan. Stem 40 tests resilience: an answer like *"...I will use the feedback and try again or build my career elsewhere"* reads well.

### 6.3 Picture story: 10 prompts described in words

**Instructions:** Imagine the scene, then write a story in 5 minutes covering what led up to it, what is happening, what the characters feel, and how it ends.

**What a strong story shows:** a clear main character who acts, a realistic problem, effort and cooperation, a believable resolution, and feelings named but not wallowed in. Weak stories are passive ("he waited and hoped"), magical ("suddenly everything was fine"), or end in disaster for no reason.

1. A young man sits alone at a desk late at night, head in his hands, with an open envelope in front of him.
2. A woman in a headscarf addresses a group of older men in a village meeting; some look away.
3. Two people stand by a broken-down car on an empty highway at dusk; one points into the distance.
4. A uniformed officer faces an angry crowd outside a government building; a child stands near the front.
5. An elderly woman hands a folded paper to a clerk behind a counter; a queue stretches behind her.
6. A group of students around a laptop; one has turned away and is looking out of the window.
7. A figure stands at an airport gate holding a boarding pass, looking back over his shoulder.
8. A flooded street; a man carries a bag of supplies on his head; people watch from a rooftop.
9. Two men shake hands across a table while a third person in the room watches without expression.
10. A blank picture. Write a story about anything. (Some projective test batteries include a blank card; I could not confirm FPSC uses one.)

### 6.4 Group discussion: 15 topics

**Instructions:** 15 minutes, 5–7 people. Practise about half in Urdu.

**What a strong contribution shows:** an early, clear point (not necessarily the first word); facts or examples; building on others by name ("as Ali said..."); disagreeing politely; bringing a quiet member in; and, if no one else does, a short summary at the end. The topics are deliberately arguable.

1. Pakistan should make Urdu the medium for all government work, including the CSS exam.
2. Social media has done more harm than good to Pakistani society.
3. Brain drain is good for Pakistan.
4. Women's quotas in the civil service should be abolished; merit alone should decide.
5. Pakistan's universities produce graduates, not skills.
6. Ham apni saqafat par fakhr nahi karte. (We are not proud of our culture.) A topic of this kind was reported by one candidate [S10]; this wording is my adaptation.
7. Artificial intelligence will take more jobs in Pakistan than it creates.
8. Local governments, not provinces, should control schools and hospitals.
9. Cricket gets too much attention compared with other sports.
10. Joint family systems are better than nuclear families.
11. The government should ban smartphones for children under 14.
12. Pakistan's civil service is a colonial legacy that needs replacing, not reforming.
13. Climate change is a bigger threat to Pakistan than terrorism.
14. Remittances make Pakistan lazy about exports.
15. Online classes can replace classroom teaching in rural areas.

### 6.5 Extra: 10 extempore speech topics (2 minutes each)

Structure: one opening line, two points with one fact each, one close.

1. My district in 2040
2. The best public servant I have met
3. Why Pakistan needs a digital ID for every service
4. What the HEC got right and wrong (a topic of this kind was reported [S10])
5. Lessons from working abroad
6. Is cash dead?
7. Water: Pakistan's next crisis
8. Why I read (or don't)
9. The one law I would change
10. What a good team looks like

### 6.6 Extra: 5 command-task or group-problem scenarios

Practise as a 15-minute plan (problem, stakeholders, strategy), then 8 minutes chairing.

1. Transporters strike after a driver is arrested following a fatal accident. Traders and commuters are stranded. (Modelled on the task reported in [S10])
2. A dengue outbreak in two union councils. The hospital is full and residents blame the municipality.
3. A viral video accuses a patwari of demanding bribes. Protest is called for tomorrow outside your office.
4. Exam papers for a provincial board have leaked on WhatsApp the night before the exam.
5. A flood warning for low-lying areas. Some families refuse to leave their livestock and homes.

**What a strong chair does:** sets the purpose and time limit in the first 30 seconds; hears each stakeholder briefly; separates immediate safety steps from longer-term fixes; assigns named actions; closes with a summary and a follow-up date.
