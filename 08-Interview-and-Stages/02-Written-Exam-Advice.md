# Written exam: practical advice from high scorers and officers

Compiled 24 Nov 2026. Source numbers point to `09-Sources.md`. This file covers how to sit the exam. What to study is in `06-Compulsory-Papers/` and `07-Optional-Papers/`, and exam-day logistics are in `01-Plan/Exam-Day-and-Logistics.md`.

## How good is the evidence?

- **Verified rules** come from the CSS Rules 2019 [S1] and `Official-Facts.md` [S2].
- **Officer and topper advice** comes mainly from Jahangir's World Times (JWT) interviews with toppers [S15]. The JWT pages would not load for me, so I saw them only as search summaries. I name the topper where the summary did, and say so when the line could not be tied to one person. Other sources: ProPakistani's interview with Hamza Shafqaat (PAS) [S17], Geo's piece on Maheen Hassan (PAS, CSS 2020) [S18], and Hassan Jahangir's JWT interview (30th, CSS 2014) [S14].
- **Coaching advice** [S37] is used only where it repeats across sites or matches the rules.

## 1. Rules you must design around (Verified)

| Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Miss any paper and you cannot sit the rest (Rule 11(15)) | Turn up to every paper, even one you expect to fail. Your attempt is already used once you sit the first paper |
| 40% in Essay, Précis and Islamic Studies; **120/300 across the three GK papers combined**; 33% in each optional; 600/1,200 overall | A weak GK paper can be made up by the other two GK papers. A weak Essay cannot be made up by anything |
| Marks under 40%/33% in a paper are not counted towards the total (Rule 16(16)) | A 38 in Précis is not "nearly 40". It is a fail, and its marks are not added |
| Blue or black ink only (Rule 16(27)) | Carry three of the same pen |
| Calculators allowed in Applied Maths, Pure Maths, Statistics, Accountancy, Business Administration, Physics and Chemistry; **not** in General Science and Ability (Rule 16(22)) | Your likely optionals (Applied Maths, Statistics) allow one. Practise with the model you will take into the hall |
| MCQs go on a separate OMR sheet, collected after the allotted time (Rule 16(23)) | Ask the invigilator at the start when the MCQ sheet will be collected, and finish the MCQs first |
| One compulsory paper per day since CSS 2026; optionals in two sessions a day | Six days of compulsories, then up to six days of double sessions [S2] |

**CSS 2026 pattern (the most recent) [S2]:** 4 Feb Essay; 5 Feb Précis; 6 Feb General Science and Ability; 7 Feb Current Affairs; 8 Feb Pakistan Affairs; 9 Feb Islamic Studies; 10–15 Feb optionals in morning and afternoon sessions.

## 2. Managing the 12-day window

### Before day 1 (Derived, plus logistics from the rules)

- **Arrive in Pakistan at least 7 days before the Essay.** If you are flying in from abroad, leave room for a delayed flight, jet lag and a visit to the centre the day before. (Opinion)
- **Stay close to the centre.** Long daily commutes over 12 days wear you down. (Opinion)
- **Bring:** admission certificate (several prints), CNIC, the same pens, pencils, ruler, the allowed calculator model for Applied Maths/Statistics, and a watch without smart functions. (Derived from Rule 16 and the admission certificate norms)

### Each evening during the window (Opinion, consistent with topper advice on revision over new learning [S15])

| Evening before | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Essay | Re-read your 5–7 essay theme outlines; sleep by 11 | Write a full practice essay |
| Précis | 20 minutes of vocabulary and idiom cards; one short précis | Learn new grammar rules |
| GSA | Revise formula and definition sheets; one Ability practice set | New science topics |
| Current Affairs / Pakistan Affairs | Your one-page "facts and figures" sheets (dates, numbers, reports) | Reading whole books |
| Islamic Studies | Quranic verses and hadith you plan to quote, with references | — |
| Optionals | Formula sheets (maths/stats); key theorists and data (Gender Studies) | — |

### During each paper (topper and coaching advice)

- **Read the whole paper first** and choose your questions before writing. Several toppers stress planning before writing. (Reported, JWT topper interviews [S15], individual attribution not confirmed)
- **Finish the MCQs first,** then the subjective part. (Coaching [S37]; also forced by the OMR collection rule)
- **Divide time equally** across the subjective questions. Over-running on one answer costs more marks on the next. (Coaching [S37])
- **Keep a reserve question** in mind in case your first choice falls apart halfway through. (Coaching [S37])

## 3. Answer presentation

| Advice | Status |
|---|---|
| Start each answer with a short **outline** or road map, then use headings and sub-headings | Repeated across topper interviews and coaching guides [S15, S37]. This is how most qualifiers describe their scripts |
| Support claims with **facts, data and named sources** (government reports, SBP, Economic Survey, UN reports) | Coaching [S37]. It also matches FPSC's viva guidance that reasoned, fact-backed views score better than bare opinions [S7] |
| **Write clearly.** Examiners read hundreds of scripts. Neat, legible handwriting helps | Opinion. One 2018 qualifier was even asked in his viva whether he had learnt calligraphy [S11] |
| Number every question clearly, as it appears on the paper | Derived |
| Draw diagrams, flowcharts and tables where they fit (GSA, Current Affairs, Gender Studies) | Coaching |
| Avoid padding. Length does not replace analysis | Coaching; matches FPSC's preference for structured views [S7] |

## 4. Essay (the paper that decides most results)

**Verified [S2]:** 87–99% of candidates failed Essay each year from 2019 to 2023.

What officers and toppers say:

- **Brainstorm before you write, for 30–40 minutes,** including choosing the topic. (Reported, JWT topper interview [S15]; individual attribution not confirmed)
- **A fixed skeleton:** introduction, context, exposition, authority (evidence), analysis, conclusion. (Reported, attributed in the search summary to M. Asif Nawaz, PAS, 6th in Pakistan, CSS 2017-18 [S15])
- **Pick a topic whose stance you can defend** without inviting an obvious counter-argument you cannot answer. Use no more than two quotations. (Reported, JWT topper interview [S15])
- **Get your essays checked** by someone who knows CSS marking. (Reported, JWT topper interview [S15])
- **Practise writing as much as possible.** (Reported, Maheen Hassan, PAS [S18]; her line was "Practice writing as much as possible.")
- **Practice is what makes the difference,** and treat each result as feedback rather than a verdict. (Reported, Awaid Irshad Bhatti, 1st in Pakistan, CSS 2017 [S15])
- **Prepare themes, not topics:** 5–7 core themes you can bend to any question. (Coaching [S37])

**What to do in the hall (Derived from the above):** 30–40 min choosing and outlining → 2 h writing → 10–15 min revising. Make sure your thesis is visible in the first paragraph and your outline is visible at the start. Your `06-Compulsory-Papers/1-English-Essay/` pack follows this structure.

## 5. Précis and Composition

- **Do not look back at the passage while writing the précis.** Write from the points you extracted, in your own words. (Reported, JWT topper guidance [S15])
- **About one-third of the original length,** as a rough guide only. (Reported, same [S15])
- **The précis passage often takes about an hour,** so plan the rest of the paper around that. (Coaching, JWT "tips for compulsory papers" [S37])
- **Practise every past précis and get them checked;** use classic essayists as reading practice. (Coaching, a "tips by toppers" compilation [S37])
- **Your edge:** comprehension and vocabulary carry over from the GRE. The risk is grammar and correction questions in the CSS style, so drill those. (Derived)

## 6. GK papers, Islamic Studies and optionals

- **Practise past papers from the last ten years.** (Reported, Hamza Shafqaat, PAS, CSS 2005 [S17])
- **Compulsory subjects carried me even when my optionals scored poorly.** (Reported, Hassan Jahangir, 30th, CSS 2014. He stood 140th on his first attempt with 667 written and 200 interview, and built his second attempt around the compulsory papers [S14])
- **Study compulsory subjects every day,** not in blocks. (Reported, Maleeha Iesar, 1st in Pakistan, CSS 2016 [S15])
- **Optional scoring varies a lot by subject,** and some candidates think it is partly luck. (Opinion, Express Tribune column, May 2026 [S22]. The columnist puts the gap between "high-scoring" and "low-scoring" subjects at 70–90 written marks, but cites no official data.)
- **Optional choice matters before the hall, not in it.** Your choices are analysed in `04-Research/Optional-Subjects-Research.md`.

## 7. Handling a bad paper

| Situation | What to do | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A paper went badly | **Sit every remaining paper.** If you skip one, you cannot sit the rest, and the attempt is used anyway | Verified, Rule 11(15) [S1] |
| A bad GK paper | The three GK papers pass together on 120/300. A strong Current Affairs or Pakistan Affairs paper can carry a weak GSA paper | Verified [S2] |
| A bad Essay | Nothing compensates for it. Still finish the rest: the attempt is used, so get the practice and a marks sheet that shows your level | Verified rule; advice is opinion |
| Panic during a paper | Stop for 60 seconds, re-read the question you are on, write its outline, carry on. A half-structured answer earns more than a blank | Opinion |
| A sense of unfair marking afterwards | Only a recount is allowed (Rs 500 per paper, within a month); there is no re-checking | Verified, Rule 16(28), (30) [S1] |
| Doubts about marking in general | Letters to Dawn have questioned marking in some years (for example Pakistan Affairs in 2022). That is opinion, not evidence, and it changes nothing you can do in the hall | Opinion [S23] |

## 8. Travel and health

All opinion and derived, except where noted.

- **Sleep beats last-minute reading.** Twelve days is a marathon.
- **Eat the same simple food all fortnight.** A stomach upset that makes you miss one paper ends your attempt. That is the real risk behind "food hygiene" advice. (Derived from Rule 11(15))
- **Carry basic medicines** (ORS, paracetamol, antacid, antihistamine).
- **Hand fatigue:** you will write by hand for about 3 hours a day for 12 days. Build stamina with the weekly 3-hour handwritten essay in your schedule. This is one of the two things the course cannot do for you (see `00-START-HERE.md`).
- **Winter:** the exam falls in late Jan to mid Feb. Some halls are cold; dress in layers.
- **Special needs:** extra time (15 minutes per hour) is for visually impaired candidates only. Requests for a helper or computer must be made in the application form before the closing date (Verified, Rule 13(4)–(6) [S1]).
