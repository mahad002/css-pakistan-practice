# Viva voce

Compiled 24 Nov 2026. Source numbers point to `09-Sources.md`. The question bank is in `06-Interview-Question-Bank.md`.

## 1. Verified rules

From the CSS Rules 2019 [S1]:

| Rule | Text in brief |
|---|---|
| 15(1) What the board has | A board interviews you "who shall have before them a record of his career, both academic and extramural". |
| 15(1) What it assesses | Suitability for the services you applied for. It attaches "particular importance to the moral and ethical qualities of the candidates, his intelligence and alertness of mind, his vigour and strength of character and his potential qualities of leadership". It also considers extramural activities (sports, debates, hobbies) and their effect on physique and character. |
| 15(2) Islam and Pakistan | Everyone is expected to have basic knowledge of Islam and Pakistan, and may be questioned on both. Non-Muslims are not asked about Islam. |
| 15(3) Marks | 300 marks. **Fewer than 100 means failing the whole examination.** |
| 14 Psychological assessment | Its result is reflected in the 300 viva marks. |
| 16(29) No re-evaluation | Viva marks cannot be re-evaluated. |
| 18(2) Ties | Ties in total marks go to the higher viva mark. |
| 20(1) Suitability | You will not be considered for any group the board declares you "not suitable" for. |
| 20(3) Preferences | You revise group preferences **once, at the viva**, and that is final. |
| 3(5) Centres | Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore, Multan, Peshawar, Quetta. |

**FPSC instructions, via secondary sources [S3, S4, S7]:**

- The board will not interview anyone who cannot produce original certificates.
- Candidates should be properly dressed.
- Scenario-based questions may be asked to judge problem-solving ability.
- FPSC's guidance for the viva board (an older version, quoted in a 2020 post [S7]) lists the qualities assessed: knowledge, ability, confidence, exposure, leadership, initiative, decision-making, fairness, judgment, critical thinking, innovativeness and communication. It lists the topics: Islam, Pakistan's ideology, challenges facing the Muslim world, current affairs, history, geography, South Asian geopolitics, general knowledge, applied sciences, globalisation and contemporary issues in Pakistan. It asks candidates for original, frank, independent but structured views. Reasoned, fact-backed views score better than bare opinions. It also says "No correct, proper, textbook solution or answer is either expected". Genuineness, balance, honesty, decency and sobriety are valued.

## 2. The panel and format

| Point | What sources say | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Panel size | Four named members plus a chairman who opened, at Islamabad in Dec 2014 [S12]. Old forum accounts: about four members led by a chairman [S16]. Coaching sites: 5–6 [S20], or 5–7 [S36] | Reported (2014, forum) vs coaching. **Not fixed in any FPSC document I could read** |
| Who sits | Chairman usually a senior serving or retired officer or FPSC member; other members from different services; sometimes a subject specialist or psychologist | Coaching [S20]; the 2014 panel included a retired major general [S12] |
| Length | Coaching guesses range from 20–40 to 30–60 minutes [S20, S36]. No first-hand account I opened gives a timed length | **Not verified** |
| Waiting | The 2014 candidate waited about two hours before being called in at 11:30 am [S12] | Reported |
| Language | English; one PMS (provincial) account says a chairman asked everything in Urdu [S16]. Be ready for both | Reported |
| Opening | Usually personal background and the form; the 2014 chairman opened informally with the candidate's home district (Chitral: geography, the Lowari tunnel, Kalash culture) [S12] | Reported; also coaching [S20] |
| Tone | Varies. One 2018 doctor found the panel "pretty accommodating and kind", but says it was not so for everyone [S11]. Another forum candidate was cross-questioned hard by the chairman and held his ground [S16]. Panels may push back just to see your reaction [S20] | Reported |

## 3. What is assessed and how marks are reportedly distributed

**Verified:** 300 marks; minimum 100; psychological assessment folded in. FPSC publishes no breakdown of how the 300 are split.

**Reported viva marks (individual accounts, not averages):**

| Candidate | Viva marks | Source and status |
|---|---|---|
| Husham Ahmed Cheema, FSP, "interview topper" of the 38th Common (CSS 2009) | 229/300 (written 681) | His own account in JWT, via search summary [S13] |
| Hassan Jahangir, CSS 2014 (30th); figures from his first attempt | 200/300 (written 667; 140th; missed allocation narrowly) | His own account in JWT, via search summary [S14] |
| CSS 2022 merit number 1 (unnamed) | 210/300 | Scribd "column practice" file [S35]. **Unverified** |

**Reading (Opinion):** These three figures (200–229) suggest that strong candidates score roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of the 300. A 30–40-mark gap between two candidates is common, and it can move someone several groups up or down. Do not treat this as a statistic: it is three data points. Commentators argue about how much the viva can offset optional-subject luck. A May 2026 Express Tribune column argues that 20–30 extra viva marks cannot make up a 70–90-mark written gap, while admitting some candidates do get through on exceptional interviews [S22]. Letters to Dawn have called the viva partly luck and questioned panel quality [S24]. Those are opinions.

**What the board weighs (combining Rule 15 and FPSC guidance [S1, S7]):**

1. Character and ethics: honesty, fairness, balance
2. Intelligence and alertness: quick, structured thinking; answering the question asked
3. Knowledge: Pakistan, Islam, current affairs, your optional subjects, your own field
4. Leadership and decision-making: scenario questions
5. Communication and composure: clear, calm, not defensive
6. Extramural record: sports, debates, hobbies, work experience
7. The psychologists' report: personality and consistency (Rule 14)

## 4. Types of questions

See `06-Interview-Question-Bank.md` for more than 300 examples. The categories below are those reported in first-hand accounts [S11, S12, S16] and coaching guides [S20].

| Type | Reported examples (first-hand) | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal / form (DAF)** | Handwriting and calligraphy; favourite books (a panel remarked that all his were by women authors); the parent who is a doctor [S11]; present job; why you left an earlier career; even choice of clothing or beard [S16] | Self-knowledge, honesty, poise |
| **Home district and province** | Chitral's geography, the Lowari tunnel, Kalash culture, conversion issues; security and development in the district [S12] | Rootedness; knowing the area you might administer |
| **Education / professional field** | A doctor was asked why cancer is spreading in Pakistan and whether medical ethics are practised [S11]. A development worker was asked why his NGO worked in Hunza but less well in Baltistan and Chitral [S12] | Depth in your own field; applying it to policy. **For you: software engineering and IT** |
| **Current affairs** | Terrorism: causes, solutions, whether the government has failed [S12]; inflation: how to end it and who controls prices [S16]; oil politics and Russia [S11] | Analysis, balance |
| **Pakistan affairs / constitution** | Shimla Agreement pros and cons; should Pakistan give up Kashmir [S12]; can Parliament extend its own tenure [S16]; Constitution of Pakistan [S11] | Facts plus a reasoned national stance |
| **International relations** | RCD and its status; ECO; protectionism [S12]; Middle East crisis; JFK assassination; US history [S11] | Usually linked to optionals |
| **Optional subjects** | Economics: national income, consumption taxes [S12]; were your optionals chosen because they score well [S16] | Whether you actually know them |
| **Group preferences** | Why PAS is your first preference (older blog [S39]); tax evasion and recent initiatives from a member interested in the candidate's first-choice group [S16] | Whether your preference list makes sense for you |
| **Situational / ethical** | FPSC confirms scenario questions are used [S3]; an undated list of reported questions includes tribal elders and politicians pressuring an officer's decisions [S40] | Judgment, integrity |
| **Curveballs** | Your reaction if TV reported a car running on water [S11]; why history is worth studying and why historians disagree [S12] | Composure, common sense, humour |

## 6. Do's and don'ts

**Do**

- Know every line of your form: degree, final-year project, job, hobbies, books listed, district. Inconsistency is the most-cited cause of trouble (Coaching [S20]; consistent with the psychologists' focus on consistency [S10]).
- Answer exactly the question asked, then stop (Reported [S11]).
- Give a view, then reasons, then a fact, then the other side if relevant. FPSC asks for "original, frank ... yet structured views" [S7].
- When your strength comes up, inform the board and keep talking while you have useful things to say (Reported [S11]).
- Read the national newspaper daily from the written result onwards (Coaching [S20, S36]).

**Don't**

- Don't bluff. Dropping a question is survivable (see section 7).
- Don't argue for the sake of it, or flatter the panel.
- Don't criticise institutions in a sweeping, partisan way. Critique policies with evidence instead (Opinion; consistent with FPSC's preference for balance [S7]).
- Don't give memorised speeches. Panels interrupt and probe (Reported [S11, S16]).
- Don't assume your viva will match anyone else's. "Panels vary" (Reported [S11]).

## 7. When you do not know the answer

| Approach | Source |
|---|---|
| Say so plainly ("I'm not certain of that, sir/madam"). Husham Cheema dropped a question, the chairman gave him the answer, he was not embarrassed, and he went on to score 229 | Reported [S13] |
| If you miss a question, look for an aspect of it you can speak to, and ask whether you may address that | Reported, 2018 qualifier [S11] |
| You don't need every detail if you know how events ended; making sense matters more than convincing | Reported [S11] |
| Don't let one drop derail the next answer. The 2014 candidate says a single weak-area question (national income accounting) became the turning point of his interview | Reported [S12] |
| Admit the gap and give the broader context you do know | Coaching [S38] |

**Script to rehearse (Opinion):** "I haven't read enough about that to give a firm answer. What I do know is [related point]. I'll make a note to read up on it." Then stop.

## 8. A 12-week preparation plan

**When:** The written result is likely in Aug–Sep 2028 and your viva somewhere in Oct 2028–Mar 2029. Start this plan the week the written result appears. Do weeks 1–2 in spring 2028, after the written exam, if you can. Allow 1–2 hours on weekdays and 4–5 hours at weekends alongside your job.

| Week | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your form.** Print the submitted form. For every line, write the three most likely questions and your honest answer in bullet points. Gather all original documents (`00-Overview.md` checklist) | Form Q&A sheet; document folder |
| 2 | **Self-profile.** Why civil service; why not tech; strengths and weaknesses with examples; family; failures; your current job. Psychological self-mapping (`04-Psychological-Assessment.md` section 5) | One-page "about me" in bullets |
| 3 | **Home district and province.** Geography, history, economy, notable people, problems (water, schools, health, law and order), development schemes, MNAs and MPAs, the DC's priorities | District fact sheet |
| 4 | **Your field: software engineering and IT policy.** Digital Pakistan, the AI policy, data protection, cyber security, e-governance, IT exports, freelancing, the tech brain drain | IT-policy brief (2 pages) |
| 5 | **Pakistan affairs and constitution.** Key amendments (18th, 26th), federal–provincial issues, CPEC, the economy (IMF programme, budget, inflation, debt), energy | Facts and figures sheet |
| 6 | **International relations.** US, China, India, Afghanistan, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, the Middle East, multilateral bodies. Pakistan's position on each | One line per country: interest, issue, stance |
| 7 | **Optional subjects.** Revise your three or four optionals for oral questions: definitions, applications, current examples (for statistics and maths: how they apply to policy, the census, surveys) | Optional cue cards |
| 8 | **Group preferences.** Research each group (`07-Group-Preferences.md`), decide the final order, and prepare a one-minute reason for each of your top three | Final preference form draft |
| 9 | **Situational and ethical questions.** Practise 30 scenarios aloud using the four-step answer (`06-Interview-Question-Bank.md` section L) | Recorded answers |
| 10 | **Mock interviews 1–2** with a panel of 3–4 (officers, teachers, friends). Record them. Many academies run mocks; the CSS 2024 topper's World Times Institute mock is on YouTube as a model [S41] | Feedback list |
| 11 | **Mock interviews 3–4.** Fix habits: fillers, over-long answers, defensiveness. Refresh current affairs daily | Refined answers |
| 12 | **Taper.** Current affairs only, clothes ready, documents checked twice, travel booked. Sleep | Ready |

**Daily throughout:** 45 minutes of Dawn (editorials, front page, business) and one line in `02-Practice-and-Tracking/Current-Affairs-Log.md`. Panels ask about the week's news.

**The day before:** read the headlines of that day's papers, check your documents, iron your clothes, sleep early.
