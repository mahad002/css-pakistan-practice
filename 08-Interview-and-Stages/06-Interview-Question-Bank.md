# Viva question bank (335 questions)

Compiled 24 Nov 2026. Each question has a short pointer to **what a strong answer covers**. These are not scripted answers: write your own bullet answers in your notes, in your own words.

## How to use this bank

- **[Reported]** means a question of this kind appears in a first-hand account, with the source linked. The wording is a paraphrase. All other questions are my own predictions, based on Rule 15, FPSC's viva guidance [S7] and the patterns in the reported accounts.
- Sources marked "forum, via search summary" come from cssforum.com.pk posts I could not open (S16 in `09-Sources.md`).
- One reported list [S40] is undated and mixes FPSC and provincial commissions. Those items are tagged **[Reported – unverified list]**.
- Bracketed items such as [district] are for you to fill in.
- For a timed drill, pick 10 questions at random each day and answer aloud in 60–90 seconds each.

## A. Opening, personal and form-based (30)

1. **Introduce yourself.** One minute: name, district, education, work, one distinctive thing. Bridge to why you are here. No life story.
2. **Walk us through your form.** Know every entry. Be consistent with what you wrote in the psychological tests.
3. **What does your name mean?** A short, light answer is fine. Panels use it as an ice-breaker.
4. **Tell us about your family.** Warm and factual. Mention what you learnt from your parents. The psychologists may already have your written answers about them [R4 shows panels quote these back].
5. **What do your parents do?** [Reported: a panel connected a parent's profession to the candidate's career choice, R2.] Show respect and any influence on you, without dropping names.
6. **Your handwriting is ... did you ever learn calligraphy?** [Reported, R2.] Answer lightly and honestly. Panels comment on what is in front of them.
7. **What is your present job?** [Reported, R3.] Role, team, one concrete achievement, what it taught you about organisations.
8. **Why did you leave your earlier career path?** [Reported, R3; a version was asked by the psychologist, R4.] Frame it as moving towards public service, not running away from tech.
9. **Your favourite books?** [Reported, R2, R3.] Name two you have truly read. Know the authors, themes and why they matter to you.
10. **All your favourite books are by women authors. Why?** [Reported, R2.] Answer honestly and calmly. They may be testing poise more than taste.
11. **What is the theme of [book you listed]?** [Reported, R3.] Two sentences on the theme, one on its relevance to Pakistan.
12. **Your hobbies?** Rule 15 says the board weighs extramural activities. Be specific (how often, any achievement) and link them to character.
13. **Which sport do you play? Rules of it?** Know the basic rules and Pakistan's standing in it.
14. **Your greatest strength, with an example.** A real story using situation, action, result.
15. **Your greatest weakness.** A real one, with what you are doing about it. Not "perfectionism".
16. **Your biggest failure.** Own it. Show the lesson and the change you made.
17. **Biggest achievement.** Quantify it (users, money saved, team size) and explain your personal role.
18. **Who is your role model?** One person, the specific traits you admire, and how you apply them.
19. **Describe yourself in three words.** Pick words your examples elsewhere support.
20. **What do your friends criticise you for?** An honest, mild flaw, and how you handle it.
21. **What did you do in the gap between graduation and now?** Account for the time with work and learning.
22. **Why did you choose your university?** Merit, programme strength, career logic.
23. **What were your marks, CGPA or class rank?** State them honestly. If they were modest, show growth since.
24. **Did you take part in societies or debates?** Name the roles. Link them to leadership.
25. **Any volunteer or community work?** Concrete details. If none, say what you would do and why.
26. **Languages you speak?** Include regional languages. Panels may switch to Urdu or a regional language.
27. **What newspaper do you read? Today's lead story?** Read that day's paper before the viva. Have a view on the lead story.
28. **What was the last book you read? The last film you watched?** Recent and honest. Avoid naming something you cannot discuss.
29. **How did you prepare for CSS while working full-time?** Discipline, scheduling, sacrifices. Shows time management.
30. **What will you do if you are not selected?** Resilience: try again within the rules, or serve the country through your profession.

## B. Motivation: why civil service, why not tech (20)

31. **Why CSS?** Personal motive plus public purpose plus fit (systems thinking, service delivery). Avoid "power" and "prestige".
32. **You are a software engineer. Why not stay in tech, where you would earn more?** Acknowledge the pay gap honestly. Explain the scale of impact through the state, and that you want to fix systems used by millions.
33. **Isn't this a waste of your technical education?** No: governments need technical literacy (digital ID, e-procurement, data). Give an example of where tech skill would help as an officer.
34. **Would you leave the service for a better offer abroad?** Commitment. Note the training bond as a fact (see `08-After-Allocation.md`) and what keeps you here.
35. **What do you know about the work of a BS-17 officer in your first-choice group?** The concrete first posting and its duties.
36. **What will you change in your first year as an officer?** Modest and realistic: learn first, fix one process (digitising records, public hearing days).
37. **Is the civil service still relevant in the age of AI?** Yes, with change: judgment, accountability and field presence cannot be automated. Use AI for routine work.
38. **Civil servants are seen as corrupt and arrogant. Why join them?** Acknowledge the perception. Say change needs honest people inside. Give an example of an officer you admire.
39. **What is "public service" to you?** A personal definition: accountability to citizens, fairness, service delivery.
40. **Why did you choose CSS over PMS?** Federal scope, all-Pakistan postings, group variety.
41. **Is CSS a fair test of a civil servant?** A balanced view. Mention reform proposals (specialised exams, cluster-based recruitment) as reported, not as fact.
42. **What qualities make a good civil servant?** Integrity, neutrality, empathy, competence. Link each to yourself.
43. **How will your family feel about hardship postings?** Honest discussion; family support.
44. **Who influenced your decision?** Real people and moments.
45. **What did you learn about government while working abroad?** Compare service delivery (for example the e-services of the country where you worked) with Pakistan's, without sneering.
46. **The salary is low. How will you live honestly?** Contentment, budgeting, values. Mention the pay and allowance structure if you know it.
47. **Would you join politics later?** Neutrality: civil servants must stay apolitical.
48. **What is the difference between a bureaucrat and a politician?** Elected mandate vs permanent executive; policy vs implementation; accountability lines.
49. **What would you do if you were the Prime Minister for a day?** One focused, feasible reform, explained well.
50. **If not CSS, what would you be in ten years?** Credible tech-career path. Show CSS is a choice, not a fallback.

## D. Education: software engineering and computer science (25)

[Reported: field-specific questions are standard, for example a doctor asked about cancer and medical ethics, R2.]

71. **What is software engineering? How is it different from computer science?** Engineering discipline (process, quality, maintenance) vs theory.
72. **What was your final-year project?** Problem, your role, technology, result, and how it could serve government.
73. **Explain an algorithm to a layperson.** A simple analogy (a recipe); shows communication.
74. **What is the software development life cycle?** Phases; waterfall vs agile; why government projects often fail at the requirements stage.
75. **Why do government IT projects fail?** Unclear requirements, procurement by lowest bid, no ownership, no maintenance budget, resistance to change.
76. **What is a database? Why does NADRA's database matter?** Central identity records, citizen services, privacy risks.
77. **What is cloud computing? Should government data be on the cloud?** Benefits; data sovereignty; the federal cloud policy (check the current status).
78. **What is open source? Should government use it?** Cost, transparency and security trade-offs.
79. **What is blockchain? Any real use for Pakistan?** Land records and supply chains; be sceptical of hype.
80. **What is machine learning? How is it different from AI?** A plain definition; examples.
81. **What is a large language model?** How it is trained on text, its limits (hallucination, bias), and its uses in government.
82. **What is an API? Why do government systems need them?** Interoperability: one system talks to another (for example FBR and NADRA).
83. **What is software quality? How would you test a government app?** Testing types, user testing, security testing.
84. **Explain encryption.** Public/private keys in plain words; why it protects citizens' data.
85. **What is a data breach? Any famous Pakistani breaches?** Know one or two reported cases accurately, or say you would check.
86. **Is coding a skill every student should learn?** A balanced view: computational thinking vs basic literacy needs.
87. **What is "technical debt"? Does government have it?** Legacy systems, paper-to-digital half-measures.
88. **How do apps like Uber or Careem work?** Platform economy, matching, and regulatory issues.
89. **What is the difference between the internet and the web?** Network vs service.
90. **What are 5G and fibre? Where does Pakistan stand?** Spectrum auction delays and connectivity gaps (check current status).
91. **What did you learn at work that university didn't teach?** Teamwork, deadlines, customers, accountability.
92. **How would you explain your job to a farmer?** Simple, respectful analogy.
93. **What is project management? Tools you used?** Scope, time, cost. Link to managing district schemes.
94. **What is agile? Could government work in sprints?** Iterative delivery; pilot projects.
95. **Which programming languages do you know, and which do you prefer?** Short answer, then pivot to problem-solving.

## E. IT policy, AI governance, e-governance, cyber security, digital Pakistan (35)

**Check every figure and policy status in 2028.** Names and dates change.

96. **What is Digital Pakistan?** The vision of digital services, connectivity and the digital economy. Main initiatives. Gaps in execution.
97. **Pakistan's National AI Policy: what does it say?** Know its approval status and main pillars (skills, research, governance) as of 2028. Name one strength and one gap.
98. **How should Pakistan regulate AI?** Risk-based approach (compare the EU AI Act), sector regulators, keeping innovation alive, local data needs.
99. **Should government use AI to make decisions about citizens?** Assistive use is fine. Human accountability, bias audits, transparency and appeal rights.
100. **AI and jobs in Pakistan?** Risk to BPO and routine jobs; opportunity in services exports; reskilling.
101. **What are deepfakes? How do they threaten elections?** Disinformation; detection; legal response; media literacy.
102. **What is e-governance? Give a Pakistani success story.** For example NADRA, FBR's IRIS, e-Khidmat, the Punjab land record system. What made it work.
103. **Why hasn't e-governance reduced corruption more?** Partial digitisation, discretion still offline, low digital literacy.
104. **How would you digitise a district office?** Start with the citizen's most-used service; process redesign before software; training; feedback.
105. **What is the Personal Data Protection Bill? Has it passed?** Check the status. Main principles: consent, a data authority, breach notification.
106. **Should government have access to citizens' WhatsApp messages?** Security vs privacy, judicial oversight, proportionality.
107. **What is PECA? Is it misused?** The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 and later amendments (check them). Cybercrime vs free-speech concerns. Give a balanced view.
108. **Cyber security threats to Pakistan?** State actors, ransomware, financial fraud, critical infrastructure. National Cyber Security Policy; CERTs.
109. **What would you do if a ministry was hit by ransomware?** Isolate, report to the national CERT, restore from backups, don't pay, communicate, then a lessons-learnt review.
110. **Should Pakistan ban social media platforms during unrest?** Costs to the economy and rights; effectiveness; legal basis. Example: the X/Twitter suspension in 2024 (check the facts).
111. **Internet shutdowns: who decides and with what effect?** PTA's role, court cases, economic losses.
112. **What is the role of the PTA?** Telecom regulation, licensing, content blocking powers.
113. **How can IT exports grow?** Skills, ease of payments, stable internet, tax policy, freelancer support. Know the latest export figure.
114. **Freelancing in Pakistan: opportunities and problems?** Large freelancer base; payment gateway issues (no PayPal); taxation.
115. **Why doesn't PayPal operate in Pakistan?** Regulatory and compliance concerns, FATF history, market size. Alternatives.
116. **What is a digital ID? Is NADRA a model for others?** CNIC, NADRA's international contracts, privacy questions.
117. **What is RAAST?** SBP's instant payment system. Its role in financial inclusion.
118. **Cashless economy: is it possible in Pakistan?** Informality, trust, tax evasion, the gender gap in accounts. A gradual path.
119. **Digital divide in Pakistan?** Rural–urban and gender gaps in phone and internet use. Policies to close them.
120. **Women and technology in Pakistan?** Mobile ownership gap, online harassment, programmes for women coders. Link to gender studies.
121. **Should civil servants be trained in data science?** Yes, basic data literacy. Give an example (dengue tracking in Punjab).
122. **What is open data? Should government publish its data?** Transparency, research, innovation; privacy safeguards.
123. **What is a smart city? Is one feasible in Pakistan?** Safe City projects, traffic and e-challan, cost and maintenance.
124. **Safe City cameras: security or surveillance?** Effect on crime, privacy, data retention rules.
125. **What is the Ignite or National Technology Fund?** Startup support. Check the current name and programmes.
126. **Why do Pakistani tech startups fail?** Funding winter, small market, policy instability, talent drain.
127. **The tech brain drain: how to stop it?** Better pay is limited, so offer remote-work-friendly policy, quality of life, a startup environment. Use your own experience if you have worked abroad.
128. **Should Pakistan build its own large language model in Urdu?** Language inclusion and sovereignty vs cost. Partnerships.
129. **What is the role of the Ministry of IT and Telecom?** Policy, Digital Pakistan, the special technology zones authority. Know the minister's name in 2028.
130. **What are Special Technology Zones?** Incentives for tech firms; results so far.
131. **How can technology reduce corruption in procurement?** E-procurement (e-PADS), transparency, audit trails.
132. **Use of drones by government?** Agriculture, disaster response, security; regulation.
133. **Is TikTok harmful? Should it be banned?** Content concerns vs livelihoods and expression. Pakistan's past bans and reversals.
134. **How should the government handle fake news?** Fact-checking, media literacy, transparent communication. Avoid censorship by default.
135. **Your one tech policy recommendation for the Prime Minister?** One clear, costed, feasible idea, such as a single citizen services app with an API standard.

182. **The Shimla Agreement: pros and cons?** [Reported, R1.] 1972; return of territory and prisoners; bilateralism vs the UN route; LoC.
183. **The significance of the 18th Amendment?** Devolution, the concurrent list abolished, provincial autonomy, NFC links. Criticisms.
184. **The significance of the 19th Amendment?** [Reported – unverified list, R6.] 2011; judicial appointments commission and parliamentary committee changes.
185. **The 26th Amendment: what changed?** 2024; constitutional benches; how the Chief Justice is appointed. Debate on judicial independence. Check later amendments by 2028.
186. **Objectives Resolution: its importance?** 1949; preamble; made a substantive part by the 8th Amendment.
187. **Ideology of Pakistan?** Two-nation theory, Iqbal and Jinnah; relevance today. Balanced.
188. **Quaid-e-Azam's vision: what did he want?** Use the 11 August 1947 speech and others; democracy, minorities, rule of law.
189. **Why did East Pakistan separate?** Political exclusion, economic disparity, the language issue, 1970 election, military action, Indian intervention.
190. **Allama Iqbal's contribution?** Allahabad address 1930; philosophy of selfhood (khudi).
191. **Key features of the 1973 Constitution?** Federal parliamentary system, Islamic provisions, fundamental rights, bicameral legislature.
192. **What are fundamental rights? Name some articles.** Articles 8–28, with examples: 9 (life), 19 (speech), 25 (equality), 25A (education).
193. **Article 25A: is it implemented?** Free compulsory education for ages 5–16. Gaps.
194. **Role of the President vs Prime Minister?** Parliamentary system; the presidency is mostly ceremonial since the 18th Amendment.
195. **What is the Council of Common Interests?** Article 153; federal–provincial disputes; meeting frequency.
196. **Lahore Resolution: what did it actually say?** 1940; "independent states" wording debate.
197. **Pakistan's nuclear programme: why and how?** 1974 and 1998 context, deterrence, command and control.
198. **Why does Pakistan have frequent martial laws?** Weak institutions, political crises, regional security. Stay analytical.
199. **The Indus Waters Treaty: what is it and what is its status?** 1960, World Bank, eastern and western rivers. India's 2025 "abeyance" announcement and its aftermath (check the 2028 status).
200. **Pakistan's founding fathers besides Jinnah and Iqbal?** For example Liaquat Ali Khan, Fatima Jinnah, Sir Syed (earlier), Chaudhry Rahmat Ali.
201. **Aligarh movement: significance?** Modern education, political awakening.
202. **Pakistan's major wars?** 1948, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999, the May 2025 conflict. Concise and factual.
203. **Gilgit-Baltistan's constitutional status?** Provisional arrangements, the Kashmir linkage, demands for provincial status.
204. **What are the principles of policy in the constitution?** Articles 29–40; not enforceable in court.
205. **Why do historians disagree? Is history worth studying?** [Reported, R1.] Sources, perspectives, interpretation; lessons for policy.

## J. Islam and Islamic studies (15)

## L. Situational and ethical (30)

FPSC confirms scenario questions are used [S3]. [Reported – unverified list: tribal elders and politicians pressuring an officer's decision, R6.]

**A four-step structure for every scenario (Opinion):**

1. Immediate safety and the law
2. Facts and stakeholders
3. A fair decision with records kept
4. Follow-up and the systemic fix

Never answer "I would resign" as a first move.

271. **Tribal elders and a local politician pressure you to release a suspect. What do you do?** [Reported – unverified list, R6.] Follow the law, keep records, engage the elders respectfully, inform your seniors, and keep the case transparent.
272. **Your senior orders something illegal. What do you do?** Ask for a written order, point out the rule, decline politely, escalate through the proper channel.
273. **A minister's relative wants a contract that has not met the rules.** Procurement rules (PPRA), documentation, polite refusal, inform your senior.
274. **You find your subordinate taking a bribe.** Inquiry under the efficiency and discipline rules, evidence, due process; fix the process that allowed it.
275. **A flood hits your district at night.** Evacuation, rescue (Rescue 1122, army if needed), relief camps, communication, coordination with the PDMA.
276. **A protest blocks the main highway.** Dialogue first, the protesters' legitimate demands, traffic diversions, proportionate force only as a last resort.
277. **A mob gathers over a blasphemy allegation.** Protect the accused and the community, call police reinforcements, engage religious leaders, follow due process, counter rumours.
278. **Your close friend asks you to bend a rule.** Refuse warmly; explain your duty.
279. **You discover your own mistake caused a loss.** Report it immediately, correct it, accept responsibility, and set up a check so it doesn't recur.
280. **The media publishes a false story about your office.** Verify, issue a factual response, avoid a public fight, fix any real issue it points to.
281. **A dengue outbreak in your district.** Surveillance data, fumigation, hospital surge, public awareness, inter-department coordination.
282. **A powerful landlord encroaches on state land.** Records, notice, legal process, anti-encroachment drive with police, transparency.
283. **Your team resists a new digital system.** Explain the benefits, train, start with champions, run the old and new systems in parallel briefly, listen to feedback.
284. **You have budget for either a school or a road. Which?** Use data (enrolment, distance, usage), consult the community, consider long-term impact. Show the criteria, not just a pick.
285. **A woman employee complains of harassment by a senior.** Workplace harassment law, inquiry committee, protect the complainant, confidentiality, no retaliation.
286. **A citizen insults you publicly in a complaint hearing.** Stay calm, address the substance, don't take it personally; decorum.
287. **You must enforce a smog-season ban on brick kilns that employ poor workers.** Enforce the law, plus mitigation: zigzag technology support, worker relief, phased compliance.
288. **A journalist asks for confidential information.** Refuse politely; refer to the right-to-information process and the official spokesperson.
289. **Two communities clash over water.** Separate them, assure security, hold a jirga or mediation using the records, find a technical fix.
290. **A senior politician's convoy demands a road be cleared during an emergency ambulance run.** The ambulance first; protocol second; explain later.
291. **A colleague leaks exam papers.** Report, cancel or re-hold the exam fairly, investigate, prosecute.
292. **You are offered a costly gift by a businessman.** Refuse or deposit it under the government servants' conduct rules (gifts); keep a record.
293. **A child labourer is found in a factory you are inspecting.** Remove the child safely, enforce the law, link the family to support (school, cash transfer).
294. **A dispute with an officer of another group over jurisdiction.** Check the rules, meet in person, escalate jointly, put the citizen first.
295. **Ghost schools found in your district.** Verify on the ground, use data (biometric attendance), act on salaries, reopen or merge schools.
296. **Polio teams are attacked in your area.** Security, community leaders, religious endorsements, flexible timing, keep vaccinating.
297. **Your orders are ignored by an influential subordinate.** Document it, give a clear instruction in writing, take disciplinary action through due process.
298. **You must choose between loyalty to your boss and truth to an inquiry.** Truth; legal duty.
299. **The government's policy contradicts your personal view.** Implement it faithfully; give dissent through proper channels (notes on file). Neutrality.
300. **If a citizen's need conflicts with a rule, what do you do?** Look for lawful discretion; use available relief schemes; propose a rule change if it is unjust.
