# Quick Revision — English Literature (CSS)

One page per section. Read the night before and on the morning of the paper. Full notes in `03-Notes/`.

---

## Page 1 — Format and strategy

- 100 marks, 3 hours. Part I: 20 MCQs (20 marks, 30 minutes, OMR, no negative marking: attempt all). Part II: attempt **4 of 7**, 20 marks each, no sections.
- Time: 30 min MCQs + 4 × 35 min + 10 min reading and choosing. Plan 5 minutes, write 30.
- **Every year since 2018**: one essay question, one short-story question, at least one theory question. Prepare these first.
- Most asked: *Sons and Lovers* (6), "Lotus-Eater" (6), Russell (5), "Tintern Abbey" (5), Emerson (5), O. Henry (4), *Hamlet* (4), *Waste Land* (4), "Prufrock" (4).
- Answer shape: outline → thesis → 5–7 headed sections → one short quotation and one critic per section → counter-view → judgement.
- Examiners' complaints (CE-2018 report): candidates wrote summaries instead of arguments, could not develop an argument, wrote poor English, lacked critical thinking. **Do not retell the plot.**

## Page 2 — Essays

| Text | Five things to remember |
|---|---|
| **Russell, *Conquest of Happiness* (1930)** | Root of unhappiness = self-absorption (sinner, narcissist, megalomaniac). Part I causes: Byronic gloom, competition, boredom, fatigue, envy (Napoleon→Caesar→Alexander→Hercules), sense of sin, persecution mania, fear of public opinion. Part II: zest, affection, family, work, impersonal interests, effort and resignation. Happy man lives "objectively". Critique: ignores poverty and structures; written for the comfortable |
| **Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946, *Horizon*)** | Language and thought corrupt each other; reversible. Four tricks: dying metaphors, verbal false limbs, pretentious diction, meaningless words. Euphemism: "pacification", "transfer of population", "elimination of unreliable elements". Six rules (metaphor, short word, cut, active, no jargon, break rules rather than be barbarous). Pullum: Orwell uses passives himself |
| **Orwell, "Prevention of Literature" (1946, *Polemic*)** | PEN meeting on *Areopagitica*; intellectuals' complicity with Soviet lies; totalitarianism rewrites history; prose needs honesty, poetry may survive; machine-made literature (→ *1984*) |
| **Emerson, "The Transcendentalist" (1842 lecture)** | Materialist vs idealist; "Idealism as it appears in 1842"; Kant's transcendental forms; defends withdrawn young idealists; capitalist's "quaking foundations" |
| **Emerson, "Self-Reliance" (1841)** | "Trust thyself"; imitation is suicide; society a conspiracy; nonconformist; two terrors = conformity and consistency; "foolish consistency…hobgoblin"; intuition vs tuitions; travel a "fool's paradise". Links: Iqbal's *khudi* (but completed in *millat*), Rumi; Emerson's poem "Saadi" and translations of Hafiz |

## Page 3 — Short stories

| Text | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Maugham, "The Lotus-Eater" (1935)** | Narrator meets Thomas Wilson on Capri (1913); ex-bank manager; 25-year annuity; plans to die at ~60. After annuity: borrows a year, charcoal suicide fails, brain-damaged, lives in landlord's woodshed; found dead overlooking the Faraglioni. Moral: ease sapped his will (muscles on level ground). Title: Homer *Odyssey* 9; Tennyson "Lotos-Eaters"; contrast "Ulysses". Model: John Ellingham Brooks (Selina Hastings) |
| **Chesterton, "A Somewhat Improbable Story" (*Tremendous Trifles*, 1909)** | Frame: City quick-lunch; solemn man. Inner tale: 40 years same walk from Leadenhall Street; street rises like a wave to seek justice; strange figure asks if he is alive. Ends bowing to the mustard pot. Themes: routine, wonder, paradox. Technique: frame narrative |
| **O. Henry, "The Gift of the Magi" (1905; *The Four Million*)** | $1.87; flat $8/week; Jim's pay $30→$20; hair sold to Mme Sofronie for $20; platinum fob chain $21; Jim sells watch for tortoise-shell combs. "They are the magi." Devices: situational irony, twist ending, intrusive narrator, allusion (Sheba, Solomon, Magi), hyperbole, paradox |

## Page 4 — Poetry

| Poem | Core |
|---|---|
| **"Tintern Abbey" (1798)** | 159 lines blank verse; 5 years; three stages (animal movements → appetite → "still, sad music of humanity"); "blessed mood… see into the life of things"; "something far more deeply interfused"; "Abundant recompence"; Dorothy; "Nature never did betray". Abrams (greater Romantic lyric), Levinson (suppression of history), Bate (ecology) |
| **"Resolution and Independence" (1802/07)** | Rhyme royal + alexandrine; hare, dejection, Chatterton and Burns; leech-gatherer like a huge stone or sea-beast; stately speech; Coleridge's critique; Carroll's parody |
| **"Ode to a Nightingale" (May 1819)** | 8 × 10 lines; numbness, wine, "fever, and the fret", "viewless wings of Poesy", "half in love with easeful Death", "immortal Bird", Ruth, "Forlorn!", "Do I wake or sleep?" Negative capability; Vendler |
| **"To Autumn" (Sept 1819)** | 3 × 11 lines; ripeness / harvest worker / music of evening; swallows; acceptance. Hartman; McGann (Peterloo) |
| **"Ulysses" (1833/1842)** | Blank verse monologue; after Hallam's death; Homer + Dante *Inferno* 26; "drink life to the lees", "I am a part of all that I have met", "rust unburnish'd", "To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield". Heroic vs ironic readings |
| **"Lady of Shalott" (1832/42)** | Mirror, web, curse; "half sick of shadows"; Lancelot; "The curse is come upon me"; artist's isolation; gender |
| **"The Second Coming" (1919/20)** | Gyre, falcon; "Things fall apart"; "The best lack all conviction"; *Spiritus Mundi*; sphinx; "rough beast… Bethlehem". *A Vision*; WWI, Russia, Ireland; Achebe |
| **"Dialogue of Self and Soul" (1933)** | Soul: winding stair, escape rebirth. Self: Sato's sword in silk; live it all again; cast out remorse; blessed. Self wins |
| **"Prufrock" (1915/17)** | Dante epigraph; etherised patient; Michelangelo; fog-cat; "Do I dare / Disturb the universe?"; coffee spoons; claws; not Hamlet; mermaids; "we drown". Paralysis, alienation, ageing |
| ***The Waste Land* (1922)** | 434 lines, 5 parts (Burial of the Dead / Game of Chess / Fire Sermon / Death by Water / What the Thunder Said). Pound; Weston, Frazer; Tiresias; Datta, Dayadhvam, Damyata; "fragments I have shored"; "Shantih". Richards, Leavis, Brooks; mythical method |
| **Larkin** | "The Trees" (1967, *High Windows*): renewal as a "trick", begin afresh. "Continuing to Live" (1954): life as habit, the "blind impress"; Rorty. "Maturity" (1951): prime of life = start of decline |
| **Stevens / Whitman** | Stevens: "Postcard" (bones, legacy of perception), "Dry Loaf" (art in wartime), "Continual Conversation" (dialogue with silent reality). Whitman: "As I Ponder'd" (a greater war), "New Person" (beware idealising me), "This Moment" (brothers in other lands) |

## Page 7 — Theory

| School | Names and terms |
|---|---|
| **Structuralism** | Saussure (signifier/signified, arbitrary, langue/parole, difference), Shklovsky (defamiliarisation), Propp (31 functions), Lévi-Strauss (binaries, mythemes), Barthes (*Mythologies*), Greimas, Todorov, Genette, Culler. Critique: ahistorical, reductive. Post: Derrida 1966, Barthes "Death of the Author" 1967, Foucault |
| **Marxism** | Base/superstructure, ideology, class, alienation; Lukács (realism), Gramsci (hegemony), Benjamin (aura), Adorno (culture industry), Althusser (ISAs, interpellation), Macherey (silences), Williams (structures of feeling), Eagleton, Jameson ("Always historicize"). Taine (race, milieu, moment). Limits: determinism, neglects form, socialist realism |
| **Deconstruction** | Derrida 1967: logocentrism, presence, phonocentrism, binaries (reverse, displace), différance, trace, supplement, aporia, no outside-text. de Man, Miller ("Critic as Host"), Johnson ("not destruction… analysis = undo"). Critics: Abrams, Searle |
| **Psychoanalysis** | Freud: unconscious, dreams (condensation, displacement), Oedipus, id/ego/superego, "Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming", "The Uncanny" (Hoffmann), death drive. Jung (archetypes), Jones, Lacan (mirror stage, Imaginary/Symbolic/Real), Bloom, Kristeva. Limits: unfalsifiable, reductive, sexist |
| **Feminism** | Wollstonecraft; Woolf (room, £500, Judith Shakespeare); de Beauvoir ("becomes a woman", Other); Millett; Showalter (feminine/feminist/female; gynocriticism); Gilbert & Gubar (angel/monster, anxiety of authorship); Cixous (écriture féminine); Butler (performativity); hooks, Mohanty, Spivak; Moi. Limits: essentialism, white/Western, reductive |
| **Postcolonialism** | Fanon (*Black Skin*, *Wretched*; pitfalls of national consciousness), Said (*Orientalism*; contrapuntal), Spivak (subaltern), Bhabha (hybridity, mimicry, third space; *Nation and Narration*), Achebe (Conrad), Ngũgĩ, Anderson (imagined communities), Chatterjee (derivative discourse), Ahmad (*In Theory*) |
