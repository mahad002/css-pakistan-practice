# MCQ Bank — English Literature (CSS)

155 MCQs written for this pack. Recent FPSC papers (2024–2026) ask MCQs almost entirely on the **set texts and the six theories**, so this bank does the same, plus a few on periods and terms. Every key has been checked against the notes in `03-Notes/`.

## Periods, Movements and Terms

**Q1.** Which publication is conventionally taken as the start of English Romanticism?
- A) *Lyrical Ballads*
- B) *The Prelude*
- C) *Biographia Literaria*
- D) *Poems in Two Volumes*
**Answer:** A. Wordsworth and Coleridge's *Lyrical Ballads* (1798).

**Q2.** Who coined the label "Theatre of the Absurd" in a 1961 book?
- A) Kenneth Tynan
- B) Albert Camus
- C) Irving Wardle
- D) Martin Esslin
**Answer:** D. Martin Esslin, *The Theatre of the Absurd* (1961).

**Q3.** The term "comedy of menace" was first applied to Pinter's early plays by:
- A) T.S. Eliot
- B) Irving Wardle
- C) Michael Billington
- D) Martin Esslin
**Answer:** B. Irving Wardle used it in 1958.

**Q4.** Which anthology (1956) is most associated with the Movement poets?
- A) *Georgian Poetry*
- B) *New Lines*
- C) *The Faber Book of Modern Verse*
- D) *Des Imagistes*
**Answer:** B. Robert Conquest's *New Lines*.

**Q5.** "Objective correlative" is a term introduced by T.S. Eliot in his essay on:
- A) the Metaphysical poets
- B) *Ulysses*
- C) Dante
- D) *Hamlet*
**Answer:** D. "Hamlet and His Problems" (1919).

**Q6.** A novel tracing the growth of an artist is called a:
- A) Künstlerroman
- B) Epistolary novel
- C) Roman à clef
- D) Picaresque
**Answer:** A. *A Portrait of the Artist* is the classic example.

**Q7.** Unrhymed iambic pentameter is known as:
- A) Rhyme royal
- B) Free verse
- C) Blank verse
- D) Heroic couplet
**Answer:** C. Used in "Tintern Abbey" and "Ulysses".

**Q8.** Jeremy Collier's 1698 pamphlet attacked:
- A) The immorality of the English stage
- B) Elizabethan revenge tragedy
- C) The Puritan closure of theatres
- D) The Romantic poets
**Answer:** A. *A Short View of the Immorality and Profaneness of the English Stage*.

**Q9.** Which year saw the publication of both *The Waste Land* and Joyce's *Ulysses*?
- A) 1915
- B) 1919
- C) 1922
- D) 1929
**Answer:** C. 1922, the high point of modernism.

**Q10.** Ruskin's term for attributing human feelings to nature is:
- A) Defamiliarisation
- B) Epiphany
- C) Negative capability
- D) Pathetic fallacy
**Answer:** D. From *Modern Painters*, vol. 3 (1856).

## Essays

**Q11.** In *The Conquest of Happiness*, Russell says the root of much unhappiness is:
- A) Poverty
- B) Lack of religion
- C) Self-absorption
- D) Bad government
**Answer:** C. He names the sinner, the narcissist and the megalomaniac as self-absorbed types.

**Q12.** Which of these is NOT one of Russell's three self-absorbed types?
- A) The megalomaniac
- B) The sinner
- C) The ascetic
- D) The narcissist
**Answer:** C. The three are the sinner, narcissist and megalomaniac.

**Q13.** Russell's chapter "Byronic Unhappiness" mainly rejects:
- A) Competition in business
- B) The view that unhappiness is a mark of superior wisdom
- C) Religious faith
- D) Romantic love
**Answer:** B. He treats fashionable despair as a mood, not a reasoned conclusion.

**Q14.** In Russell's chapter on envy, Alexander envied:
- A) Achilles
- B) Napoleon
- C) Caesar
- D) Hercules
**Answer:** D. Hercules, "who never existed".

**Q15.** According to Russell, the most general mark of happy people is:
- A) Solitude
- B) Wealth
- C) Zest
- D) Ambition
**Answer:** C. Zest, an appetite for life and wide interests.

**Q16.** Russell's ideal "happy man" lives:
- A) Subjectively, inside his own thoughts
- B) Objectively, with free affections and wide interests
- C) By strict religious discipline
- D) In withdrawal from society
**Answer:** B. From the final chapter, "The Happy Man".

**Q17.** Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" was first published in 1946 in:
- A) *Horizon*
- B) *The Criterion*
- C) *Tribune*
- D) *Polemic*
**Answer:** A. *Horizon*, April 1946.

**Q18.** Which is NOT one of Orwell's six rules?
- A) If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out
- B) Never use a long word where a short one will do
- C) Never begin a sentence with a conjunction
- D) Never use the passive where you can use the active
**Answer:** C. The other three are among his six rules.

**Q19.** Orwell's sixth rule says:
- A) Avoid all metaphors
- B) Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous
- C) Always prefer Anglo-Saxon words
- D) Use foreign phrases sparingly
**Answer:** B. The rule of last resort.

**Q20.** In Orwell's essay, "transfer of population" is an example of:
- A) Dying metaphor
- B) Foreign phrase
- C) Verbal false limb
- D) Political euphemism
**Answer:** D. It disguises driving peasants from their land.

**Q21.** Orwell's "The Prevention of Literature" was prompted by a PEN meeting marking the anniversary of:
- A) Milton's *Areopagitica*
- B) The Magna Carta
- C) Shakespeare's death
- D) Swift's *Gulliver's Travels*
**Answer:** A. The 300th anniversary of *Areopagitica* (1644).

**Q22.** In "The Prevention of Literature", Orwell argues that under totalitarianism:
- A) Prose survives better than poetry
- B) Drama replaces the novel
- C) Both flourish
- D) Poetry may survive better than prose
**Answer:** D. Prose depends more on intellectual honesty about facts.

**Q23.** "The Transcendentalist" was originally:
- A) A preface to *Nature*
- B) A letter to Carlyle
- C) A chapter of *Walden*
- D) A lecture read in Boston in 1842
**Answer:** D. Read at the Masonic Temple, Boston, January 1842.

**Q24.** Emerson defines Transcendentalism as:
- A) German romantic poetry
- B) Idealism as it appears in 1842
- C) Unitarian theology
- D) Materialism in a new form
**Answer:** B. His own definition in the lecture.

**Q25.** Emerson says the term "transcendental" was borrowed from:
- A) Kant
- B) Coleridge
- C) Plato
- D) Hegel
**Answer:** A. Kant's "transcendental forms".

**Q26.** In "Self-Reliance", the two terrors that scare us from self-trust are:
- A) God and the state
- B) Poverty and death
- C) Pride and envy
- D) Conformity and consistency
**Answer:** D. Fear of others' opinion, and reverence for our past acts.

**Q27.** "A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds" comes from:
- A) "The Transcendentalist"
- B) "Self-Reliance"
- C) "The Over-Soul"
- D) "The American Scholar"
**Answer:** B. "Self-Reliance" (1841).

**Q28.** Emerson calls the original source of self-trust:
- A) Intuition
- B) Tradition
- C) Tuition
- D) Revelation through scripture
**Answer:** A. All later teachings are "tuitions".

**Q29.** In "Self-Reliance", Emerson calls travelling (as escape from oneself):
- A) A form of self-reliance
- B) A religious duty
- C) A fool's paradise
- D) The best education
**Answer:** C. The soul is no traveller.

**Q30.** Which Persian poet did Emerson honour with a poem bearing the poet's name?
- A) Firdausi
- B) Saadi
- C) Omar Khayyam
- D) Rumi
**Answer:** B. Emerson's poem "Saadi"; he also translated Hafiz.

## Short Stories

**Q31.** In "The Lotus-Eater", Thomas Wilson had worked as:
- A) A bank manager
- B) A schoolteacher
- C) A civil servant in India
- D) A doctor
**Answer:** A. He managed a bank branch in London.

**Q32.** Wilson financed his life on Capri by buying:
- A) A villa to let
- B) An annuity for 25 years
- C) A pension at 60
- D) Shares in a hotel
**Answer:** B. A 25-year annuity.

**Q33.** How does Wilson attempt suicide when his money runs out?
- A) A pistol
- B) Drowning
- C) Poison
- D) Charcoal fumes
**Answer:** D. He fills his cottage with charcoal fumes, survives with brain damage.

**Q34.** The narrator concludes that Wilson failed to kill himself because:
- A) His friends stopped him
- B) He fell in love
- C) He found religion
- D) Years of ease had weakened his will
**Answer:** D. Without challenge, his will had grown flabby.

**Q35.** The title "The Lotus-Eater" alludes to an episode in:
- A) Dante's *Inferno*
- B) Homer's *Odyssey*
- C) Ovid's *Metamorphoses*
- D) Virgil's *Aeneid*
**Answer:** B. *Odyssey*, Book 9.

**Q36.** "The Lotus-Eater" is set on:
- A) Malta
- B) Sicily
- C) Corfu
- D) Capri
**Answer:** D. Capri, in the Bay of Naples.

**Q37.** Chesterton's "A Somewhat Improbable Story" appears in the collection:
- A) *Orthodoxy*
- B) *Tremendous Trifles*
- C) *The Innocence of Father Brown*
- D) *The Club of Queer Trades*
**Answer:** B. *Tremendous Trifles* (1909), chapter XXXVI.

**Q38.** In Chesterton's story, what happens to the street the man walks every day?
- A) It rises up like a mountain or wave
- B) It floods
- C) It disappears
- D) It is renamed
**Answer:** A. It rises steeply, rebelling against thoughtless use.

**Q39.** At the end of Chesterton's story, the man bows to:
- A) The narrator
- B) The mustard pot
- C) The waiter
- D) His hat
**Answer:** B. He now treats inanimate things with respect.

**Q40.** Chesterton's story uses which narrative technique?
- A) Epistolary form
- B) Second-person narration
- C) Stream of consciousness
- D) A story within a story (frame narrative)
**Answer:** D. A lunch-counter frame contains the stranger's tale.

**Q41.** In "The Gift of the Magi", how much money has Della saved at the start?
- A) $20.00
- B) $8.00
- C) $1.87
- D) $21.00
**Answer:** C. One dollar and eighty-seven cents.

**Q42.** Della sells her hair to:
- A) Madame Sosostris
- B) Madame Sofronie
- C) Mrs Tinckham
- D) Mrs Pearce
**Answer:** B. Madame Sofronie, for $20.

**Q43.** Della buys Jim:
- A) A leather wallet
- B) A gold watch
- C) A platinum fob chain
- D) A set of combs
**Answer:** C. A platinum fob chain for $21.

**Q44.** The dominant device in "The Gift of the Magi" is:
- A) Allegory
- B) Situational irony
- C) Stream of consciousness
- D) Dramatic irony
**Answer:** B. Each gift is made useless by the other's sacrifice.

**Q45.** "The Gift of the Magi" was collected in:
- A) *The Trimmed Lamp*
- B) *Cabbages and Kings*
- C) *Heart of the West*
- D) *The Four Million*
**Answer:** D. *The Four Million* (1906).

## Poetry

**Q46.** "Tintern Abbey" was written on revisiting the Wye after:
- A) Ten years
- B) Five years
- C) Two years
- D) Fifteen years
**Answer:** B. "Five years have past".

**Q47.** "Tintern Abbey" is written in:
- A) Heroic couplets
- B) Ottava rima
- C) Blank verse
- D) Rhyme royal
**Answer:** C. 159 lines of blank verse.

**Q48.** The "dearest Friend" addressed at the end of "Tintern Abbey" is:
- A) Annette Vallon
- B) Mary Hutchinson
- C) Dorothy Wordsworth
- D) Coleridge
**Answer:** C. His sister Dorothy.

**Q49.** Which critic argued that "Tintern Abbey" suppresses social and historical realities?
- A) Jonathan Bate
- B) Marjorie Levinson
- C) M.H. Abrams
- D) Geoffrey Hartman
**Answer:** B. Levinson's New Historicist reading (1986).

**Q50.** Jonathan Bate's *Romantic Ecology* (1991) reads Wordsworth as:
- A) A Christian apologist
- B) A pioneer of ecological thinking
- C) An imperialist
- D) A political radical
**Answer:** B. An ecocritical defence of Wordsworth.

**Q51.** The old man in "Resolution and Independence" earns his living by gathering:
- A) Firewood
- B) Stones
- C) Herbs
- D) Leeches
**Answer:** D. He is the leech-gatherer.

**Q52.** Which two poets does Wordsworth recall in "Resolution and Independence"?
- A) Chatterton and Burns
- B) Milton and Spenser
- C) Keats and Shelley
- D) Gray and Collins
**Answer:** A. Chatterton ("the marvellous Boy") and Burns.

**Q53.** The stanza of "Resolution and Independence" is a variant of rhyme royal ending in:
- A) A refrain
- B) An alexandrine
- C) A couplet in tetrameter
- D) A half-line
**Answer:** B. The seventh line has twelve syllables.

**Q54.** Keats's "negative capability" means the capacity to:
- A) Reject all emotion
- B) Express only negative feelings
- C) Write without rhyme
- D) Remain in uncertainties and doubts without irritably reaching after fact and reason
**Answer:** D. From his letter of December 1817.

**Q55.** "Ode to a Nightingale" has stanzas of:
- A) Eleven lines
- B) Fourteen lines
- C) Ten lines
- D) Eight lines
**Answer:** C. Ten lines each, eighth line shortened.

**Q56.** In "Ode to a Nightingale", the word that "tolls" the poet back to his "sole self" is:
- A) Adieu
- B) Fled
- C) Forlorn
- D) Darkling
**Answer:** C. "Forlorn! the very word is like a bell".

**Q57.** "To Autumn" has how many stanzas?
- A) Three
- B) Eight
- C) Five
- D) Ten
**Answer:** A. Three stanzas of eleven lines.

**Q58.** In stanza 2 of "To Autumn", Autumn is personified as:
- A) A harvest worker (winnower, reaper, gleaner)
- B) A dying king
- C) A young bride
- D) A wandering minstrel
**Answer:** A. Seen on the granary floor, the furrow, by the cider-press.

**Q59.** The most direct source for Ulysses's final voyage in Tennyson's poem is:
- A) Dante's *Inferno*, Canto 26
- B) Chapman's Homer
- C) Virgil's *Aeneid*
- D) Ovid's *Metamorphoses*
**Answer:** A. Dante's Ulysses urges his men beyond the Pillars of Hercules.

**Q60.** Tennyson wrote "Ulysses" shortly after the death of:
- A) Arthur Henry Hallam
- B) Prince Albert
- C) Wordsworth
- D) His father
**Answer:** A. Hallam died in 1833.

**Q61.** "Ulysses" is an example of:
- A) Elegy
- B) Ballad
- C) Sonnet sequence
- D) Dramatic monologue
**Answer:** D. Ulysses speaks; listeners are implied.

**Q62.** The Lady of Shalott sees the world through:
- A) A crystal ball
- B) A mirror
- C) A window
- D) Her dreams
**Answer:** B. Reflections in a mirror, which cracks when she looks out.

**Q63.** Who rides past and causes the Lady of Shalott to look down to Camelot?
- A) Sir Galahad
- B) King Arthur
- C) Sir Gawain
- D) Sir Lancelot
**Answer:** D. Lancelot, singing "Tirra lirra".

**Q64.** In "The Second Coming", the "rough beast" slouches towards:
- A) Bethlehem
- B) Jerusalem
- C) Rome
- D) Byzantium
**Answer:** A. Bethlehem, to be born.

**Q65.** Yeats's theory of historical cycles, set out in *A Vision*, uses the image of:
- A) The wheel of fortune
- B) The ladder
- C) Gyres
- D) The labyrinth
**Answer:** C. Interlocking gyres (cones).

**Q66.** Chinua Achebe took the title of his 1958 novel from:
- A) "Easter, 1916"
- B) *The Waste Land*
- C) "The Second Coming"
- D) "Sailing to Byzantium"
**Answer:** C. "Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold".

**Q67.** In "A Dialogue of Self and Soul", the Self holds:
- A) A cross
- B) A book of prayers
- C) Sato's ancient sword wrapped in silk
- D) A golden bird
**Answer:** C. A Japanese sword given to Yeats by Junzo Sato.

**Q68.** "A Dialogue of Self and Soul" ends with the Self:
- A) Sailing to Byzantium
- B) Affirming willingness to live life again and casting out remorse
- C) Yielding to the Soul
- D) Choosing death
**Answer:** B. The Self wins; "we are blest by everything" (paraphrased).

**Q69.** The epigraph of "Prufrock" is from:
- A) Dante's *Inferno*
- B) The Bible
- C) Homer
- D) Shakespeare's *Hamlet*
**Answer:** A. Guido da Montefeltro in *Inferno* 27.

**Q70.** Prufrock says he has measured out his life with:
- A) Hourglasses
- B) Coffee spoons
- C) Cigarettes
- D) Teaspoons
**Answer:** B. "I have measured out my life with coffee spoons".

**Q71.** "No! I am not Prince Hamlet, nor was meant to be" is spoken by:
- A) Edmund Tyrone
- B) Stephen Dedalus
- C) Tiresias
- D) Prufrock
**Answer:** D. Prufrock casts himself as an "attendant lord".

**Q72.** *The Waste Land* is dedicated to:
- A) Ezra Pound
- B) Jessie Weston
- C) Vivienne Eliot
- D) James Joyce
**Answer:** A. "il miglior fabbro".

**Q73.** Eliot's notes name which book as a major source for the Grail and Fisher King material?
- A) Malory's *Morte d'Arthur*
- B) Wagner's *Parsifal*
- C) Jessie Weston's *From Ritual to Romance*
- D) *The Golden Bough* only
**Answer:** C. Weston's book (1920); he also cites Frazer.

**Q74.** The thunder's three words in "What the Thunder Said" (Datta, Dayadhvam, Damyata) come from:
- A) The Rig Veda
- B) The Bhagavad Gita
- C) The Buddha's Fire Sermon
- D) The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad

**Q75.** In *The Waste Land*, the figure who "sees" the typist scene and unifies the poem, according to Eliot's notes, is:
- A) Stetson
- B) Madame Sosostris
- C) Tiresias

**Q76.** I.A. Richards claimed *The Waste Land* effects a complete separation between poetry and:
- A) All beliefs
- B) Music

**Answer:** A. *Science and Poetry* (1926).

**Q77.** Philip Larkin's "The Trees" appears in the collection:
- A) *The North Ship*
- B) *The Whitsun Weddings*
- C) *High Windows*
- D) *The Less Deceived*
**Answer:** C. *High Windows* (1974).

**Q78.** Which philosopher discussed Larkin's "Continuing to Live" in *Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity*?
- A) Richard Rorty
- B) Bertrand Russell
- C) Jacques Derrida
- D) Iris Murdoch
**Answer:** A. Rorty, in his chapter on the contingency of selfhood.

**Q79.** Wallace Stevens worked for most of his life as:
- A) An insurance company executive
- B) A diplomat
- C) A university professor
- D) A newspaper editor
**Answer:** A. At the Hartford Accident and Indemnity Company.

**Q80.** In Whitman's "As I Ponder'd in Silence", the phantom tells the poet that the one theme of great bards is:
- A) God
- B) Love
- C) Nature
- D) War

**Q81.** Whitman's "This Moment Yearning and Thoughtful" expresses:
- A) Brotherhood with men in other lands
- B) Grief for Lincoln
- C) Fear of foreigners
- D) Love of the sea
**Answer:** A. He imagines yearning men in Germany, China, Russia and elsewhere as brothers.

## Drama

**Q82.** In *Hamlet*, the play-within-the-play is called:
- A) *The Masque of Blackness*
- B) *The Mousetrap*
- C) *Pyramus and Thisbe*
- D) *The Spanish Tragedy*
**Answer:** B. Also called *The Murder of Gonzago*.

**Q83.** A.C. Bradley explained Hamlet's delay chiefly by:
- A) Excess of thought
- B) Melancholy caused by his father's death and mother's remarriage
- C) An Oedipus complex
- D) External obstacles
**Answer:** B. *Shakespearean Tragedy* (1904).

**Q84.** "It is we who are Hamlet" was written by:
- A) Hazlitt
- B) Goethe
- C) Bradley
- D) Coleridge
**Answer:** A. *Characters of Shakespeare's Plays* (1817).

**Q85.** Ernest Jones's *Hamlet and Oedipus* (1949) reads the delay through:
- A) Freud's Oedipus complex
- B) Structuralism
- C) Christian ethics
- D) Marxism
**Answer:** A. Claudius did what Hamlet unconsciously wished.

**Q86.** T.S. Eliot called *Hamlet*:
- A) A political allegory
- B) Shakespeare's masterpiece
- C) A comedy in disguise
- D) Most certainly an artistic failure
**Answer:** D. It lacks an objective correlative, he argued.

**Q87.** Goethe compared Hamlet to:
- A) An oak planted in a costly vase
- B) A caged bird
- C) A ship without a rudder
- D) A flower in winter
**Answer:** A. The vase shatters as the roots grow.

**Q88.** Elaine Showalter's essay "Representing Ophelia" (1985) is an example of:
- A) Archetypal criticism
- B) Marxist criticism
- C) Deconstruction
- D) Feminist criticism
**Answer:** D. It recovers Ophelia's story and its representations.

**Q89.** In *King Lear*, who says "Nothing, my lord"?
- A) Regan
- B) Cordelia
- C) The Fool
- D) Goneril
**Answer:** B. Cordelia refuses to flatter.

**Q90.** In *King Lear*, Gloucester's eyes are put out by:
- A) Albany
- B) Cornwall
- C) Oswald
- D) Edmund
**Answer:** B. Cornwall, with Regan's encouragement.

**Q91.** Nahum Tate's 1681 adaptation of *King Lear*:
- A) Turned it into an opera
- B) Doubled the subplot
- C) Moved it to Rome
- D) Cut the Fool and gave the play a happy ending
**Answer:** D. Lear survives and Cordelia marries Edgar; Samuel Johnson approved.

**Q92.** Jan Kott's essay linking *King Lear* to Beckett is titled:
- A) "King Lear, or Endgame"
- B) "Shakespeare Our Contemporary"
- C) "The Absent Mother"
- D) "Lear's Madness"
**Answer:** A. In *Shakespeare Our Contemporary* (1964).

**Q93.** In *Twelfth Night*, Viola disguises herself as:
- A) Antonio
- B) Sebastian
- C) Cesario
- D) Feste
**Answer:** C. She serves Orsino as Cesario.

**Q94.** Malvolio is tricked into wearing:
- A) A fool's cap

**Q95.** C.L. Barber's "festive" reading of *Twelfth Night* describes a movement:
- A) Through release to clarification
- B) From comedy to tragedy
- C) From order to chaos

**Q96.** The "proviso scene" is a famous feature of:
- A) *The Rivals*

**Q106.** In *The Caretaker*, Mick smashes:

**Q107.** *Waiting for Godot* is subtitled:
