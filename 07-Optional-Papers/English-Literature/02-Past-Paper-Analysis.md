## 2. Paper format (confirmed for every year 2016–2026)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total | 100 marks, 3 hours |
| Part I | 20 MCQs, 1 mark each, compulsory, on a separate OMR sheet, maximum 30 minutes, then collected. No negative marking (stated on the 2026 paper). Cutting or overwriting an option earns nothing |
| Part II | 80 marks. Seven questions (Q2–Q8). **Attempt any four.** All carry 20 marks |
| Sections | None. There are no compulsory questions and no "one from each section" rule in Part II |
| Sub-parts | Occasional (a)/(b) splits, usually 10+10 (2016 Q2, 2017 Q8, 2020 Q2, 2023 Q3, Q5, Q8) |
| Calculator | Not allowed (not on FPSC's list of calculator subjects) |
| Pass mark | 33/100 (FPSC Rule 12; some coaching sites wrongly say 40) |

**The key fact:** the two "10-mark" components (essays and short stories) produce about two of the seven questions every year, and they are the shortest texts on the syllabus. With theory (one or two a year), that is three to four questions from material you can master in about 60 hours.

## 6. Patterns in how questions are framed

1. **A critic's claim plus "discuss" or "elaborate".** Bradley on Hamlet (2017), Hazlitt (2023), de Onís on Maugham (2026), Eliot on Donne (2020). You must agree, disagree or qualify, then prove from the text.
2. **A theory lens on a set text.** Ecocriticism on Tintern (2024), gender on *Hamlet* (2024), masculinity on *Portrait* (2026), Freud on Lawrence (2018). Expect more of these: learn each theory with one set-text application.
3. **Title significance and character sketch** for short texts (Lotus-Eater 2017, 2023, 2025; Gift of the Magi 2016, 2019).
4. **Relevance today** (Emerson 2024, *1984* 2024, Russell 2023, Marx 2022). Prepare one modern example per text, including Pakistan.
5. **Comparison of two set texts** (2022: three comparison questions). Prepare pairs: Russell–Emerson, Hardy–Lawrence, Maugham–Murdoch, Wordsworth–Keats.
6. **Cross-cultural** (Emerson with Iqbal and Rumi, 2025). A Pakistani examiner rewards a well-made link to Iqbal, Rumi or Urdu literature.
7. **Recent papers are harder and more theoretical.** 2025–2026 questions use terms such as "humanist subject", "hegemonic masculinity", "homosociality", "mystery religion". Plain plot summary will not pass.

## 7. Predictions for CSS 2028

These are judgements from the pattern above, not certainties.

| Likelihood | Prediction |
|---|---|
| Near certain | One essay question (Russell or Emerson most likely; Orwell's "Politics" possible) |
| Near certain | One short-story question (Lotus-Eater has appeared six times; O. Henry is due after 2024; Chesterton is under-asked) |
| Near certain | One or two theory questions. Deconstruction and psychoanalysis are "due"; postcolonial and feminist recur every 3–4 years |
| Very likely | *Sons and Lovers* (asked six of the last ten years) |
| Very likely | One Wordsworth or Keats question, and/or one Eliot question |
| Likely | *Hamlet* (gap since 2024) or *Long Day's Journey* (pattern 2016–2021–2025) |
| Possible | First-ever questions on *Heartbreak House*, *Twelfth Night*, Larkin, or *The Way of the World*. The examiner has begun to rotate (Pygmalion first appeared in 2026) |
| Possible | *Scarlet Letter* (2016–2020–2024 pattern points to 2028) |

**Safe core for four answers:** Essays (Russell, both Emerson, Orwell's "Politics") + all three short stories + all six theories + *Sons and Lovers* + "Tintern Abbey" + Eliot's two poems + *Hamlet*. I checked this core against every paper. It gives at least four answerable questions in ten of the eleven years (2023: seven of seven; 2017, 2018, 2025: six; 2020, 2024, 2026: five; 2016, 2019, 2021: four). **The exception is 2022**: the core covers only Russell–Emerson and Marx cleanly, because two questions paired a core text with a non-core one (*Sons and Lovers* with Hardy; Maugham with Murdoch) and the rest were Keats, *King Lear* and "Ulysses". So add **Keats's two odes** (asked 2019, 2021, 2022) and at least a working knowledge of **Hardy and Murdoch** for comparison questions. *Long Day's Journey* is the best next addition after that.
