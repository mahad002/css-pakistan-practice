## 3. What I could not verify

1. **The FPSC website** refused every connection from my tools (certificate error), so I could not open the official syllabus PDF or the official 2023–2026 papers. The syllabus was checked word for word against a copy of the FPSC pages (NOA CSS) and two other transcriptions, which agree. The paper texts come from cssprep.com.pk (2016–2018, 2020–2025), css-vista.com (2017–2019) and vshyne.org (2026). Where two sources covered the same year (2017, 2018), they matched. The 2026 Pygmalion question matches the snippet of FPSC's own PDF. **Open the FPSC papers yourself and check.**
2. **CSS 2025 scoring data** is from a third-party site (cssnorthstar.pk) and cannot be checked against an FPSC document. The 2023 data is official.
3. **High scorers' advice:** I searched but found nothing specific to English Literature (no toppers' interviews or DMC threads); cssforum was not readable (also noted in the research file). The "what examiners want" section rests instead on FPSC's CE-2018 examiner report (read directly) and press reports of the 2016 report.
4. **Project Gutenberg** was rate-limiting me (HTTP 503), so some ebook numbers in `08-Resources.md` are from my own knowledge and are marked "not re-checked". Those marked "checked" were confirmed by search.
5. **Critics' attributions marked [verify]** in the notes: Ray Monk on Russell; Lawrence Buell on Emerson; Emerson's "infinitely repellent particle"; Iqbal's knowledge of Emerson; Anthony Burgess and Ted Morgan on Maugham; Murdoch's exact wording on love; Fred Lewis Pattee on O. Henry; T.S. Eliot's obituary note on Chesterton; Eliot on "Ulysses"; Andrew Motion on "To Autumn"; Isobel Armstrong on "The Lady of Shalott"; Helen Vendler on "The Second Coming"; Eric Bentley on *Heartbreak House*; Harold Bloom on *Long Day's Journey*. Each is a plausible, commonly cited view, but I did not open the source.
6. **Plot details marked [verify]**: the last link in the love network of *Under the Net* (whether Sadie returns Hugo's love); the form of Larkin's "Maturity".
7. **The examiner's identity and marking scheme** are not published; the answer template follows examiner reports and general CSS practice, not an FPSC rubric.

## 4. What is missing or thin

- No full model answer on *Long Day's Journey*, *The Scarlet Letter*, the Keats odes, Marxism or postcolonialism. The notes outline answers to every past question on them; write these yourself as practice in Phase 4.
- Larkin's "Maturity" and Stevens's poems need checking against the texts (see section 3).
- The MCQs are my own, modelled on the 2024–2026 topics. FPSC's actual MCQs for earlier years were not transcribed by any source I could read, so I could not analyse them.
- No audio or video resources beyond the Open Yale courses listed in `08-Resources.md`.
- Quotations from copyrighted texts (Larkin, Stevens, Pinter, Beckett, O'Neill, Murdoch, Maugham, Yeats's "Dialogue") are deliberately absent; build your own short quotation list from your copies.
