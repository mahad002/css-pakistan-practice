## 7.1 Concept and definitions

| Source | Definition (paraphrase where long) |
|---|---|
| **Chartered Institute of Public Relations (UK)** | "The planned and sustained effort to establish and maintain goodwill and mutual understanding between an organisation and its publics" |
| **PRSA** (Public Relations Society of America, 2012) | "A strategic communication process that builds mutually beneficial relationships between organizations and their publics" |
| **Mexican Statement** (World Assembly of PR Associations, 1978) | The art and social science of analysing trends, predicting their consequences, counselling organisational leaders and implementing planned programmes of action that serve both the organisation's and the public's interest |
| **Cutlip, Center and Broom**, *Effective Public Relations* | The management function that establishes and maintains mutually beneficial relationships between an organisation and the publics on whom its success or failure depends |
| **Grunig and Hunt** (1984) | Management of communication between an organisation and its publics |

**Key terms**: **publics** (internal: employees, shareholders; external: customers, media, government, community, NGOs); **stakeholders**; **image** (perception) vs **reputation** (long-term judgement); **goodwill**; **issues management**; **crisis communication**; **corporate social responsibility (CSR)**.

**PR vs related fields**

| | PR | Advertising | Publicity | Propaganda | Marketing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aim | Mutual understanding, reputation | Sell products | Media attention | Persuade to an ideology, often one-sided | Sales and market share |
| Control of message | Partial (earned media) | Full (paid) | Little | Full | Full |
| Cost | Indirect | Paid space | Free (earned) | Varies | Paid |
| Credibility | Higher (third-party) | Lower | Medium | Low | Medium |
| Direction | Two-way (ideally) | One-way | One-way | One-way | Two-way |

**PR and publicity (syllabus item)**: publicity is a *tool* of PR — getting information into the media without paying. PR is broader: research, counselling, relationship management, crisis management, internal communication, community relations. Publicity can be negative and uncontrolled; PR manages it.

## 7.2 Historical development: from press agentry to PR

| Phase | Figures | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient and early | Rulers' inscriptions, religious missions, Mughal court chroniclers; Samuel Adams' Boston Tea Party publicity (1773) | Persuasion and image |
| **Press agentry (19th century)** | **P.T. Barnum** (circus showman): "There's a sucker born every minute" (attributed) | Publicity stunts, exaggeration, truth unimportant |
| **Public information (early 20th century)** | **Ivy Lee**: *Declaration of Principles* (1906) — openness with press; after a Pennsylvania Railroad accident (1906) issued what is considered the first press release; advised the Rockefellers after the Ludlow massacre (1914) | Accurate information to the public; "the public be informed" |
| **WWI** | US Committee on Public Information (**Creel Committee**, 1917) | State propaganda techniques |
| **Scientific persuasion** | **Edward Bernays** (Freud's nephew): *Crystallizing Public Opinion* (1923), *Propaganda* (1928), "The Engineering of Consent" (1947); "Torches of Freedom" campaign (1929) for women smoking | Research-based, two-way asymmetric; "father of PR" |
| **Professionalisation** | PRSA (1947), IPR UK (1948), **IPRA** (1955); **Code of Venice** (1961), **Code of Athens** (1965); Arthur Page's principles (AT&T) | Codes, education, counselling role |
| **Strategic relationship management** | **James Grunig** (Excellence Study, 1992) | Two-way symmetric, dialogue |
| **Digital PR** | Social media, influencers, SEO, online reputation management | Real-time, interactive, data-driven |

### Grunig and Hunt's four models (1984)

| Model | Purpose | Nature | Research | Historical figure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Press agentry/publicity** | Propaganda | One-way; truth not essential | Little | P.T. Barnum |
| **Public information** | Disseminate information | One-way; truth important | Little (readability) | Ivy Lee |
| **Two-way asymmetric** | Scientific persuasion | Two-way; imbalanced effects | Formative and evaluative, to persuade | Edward Bernays |
| **Two-way symmetric** | Mutual understanding | Two-way; balanced effects | Understanding publics | Bernays (later), Grunig, educators |

**"Spin doctors" (2016)**: the term (first used in US media around 1984, about presidential debate handlers) describes PR advisers who "spin" events — interpret them favourably for their clients, often selectively or misleadingly. PR officers are called spin doctors because governments and parties use them to manage the narrative, plant stories, control access and frame failures as successes. Critics say this reduces trust; defenders say presenting a client's case is legitimate if honest. Famous examples: Alastair Campbell (UK, Tony Blair, 1997–2003).

## 7.3 The PR process

- **RACE** (John Marston, 1963): **R**esearch → **A**ction (planning) → **C**ommunication → **E**valuation.
- **ROPE**: Research → Objectives → Programming → Evaluation; **RPIE** (research, planning, implementation, evaluation).
- **Tools**: press releases, press conferences, media briefings, interviews, feature articles, newsletters, annual reports, events, exhibitions, sponsorships, CSR, websites, social media, influencer engagement, lobbying, speeches, open days, crisis plans, intranet for employees.
- **Evaluation**: outputs (coverage), outtakes (awareness), outcomes (attitude/behaviour change); Barcelona Principles (2010, updated 2015, 2020) reject "advertising value equivalency".

## 7.4 Basic methods: press release, press note, press conference

### Press release

- A written statement issued to the media to announce news.
- **Structure**: letterhead and "For immediate release" (or embargo time); **headline**; **dateline** (city, date); **lead** answering 5Ws and 1H; **inverted pyramid** body (most important first); quotes from officials; **boilerplate** (organisation background); contact details; end mark ("###" or "-ends-").
- Qualities: newsworthy, accurate, brief (one page ideally), objective tone, no jargon, timely.
- Media may use, edit or ignore it.

### Press note

- An **official statement of the government** on an important matter (policy, decision, clarification of a controversy); it carries the authority of the government and is expected to be **published without alteration** or editing (2025 MCQ: issued by government to make an important announcement).
- **Handout**: a routine government press release distributed through the PID; can be edited or summarised.

### Press conference

- A meeting where an organisation's spokesperson briefs journalists and answers questions.
- **When**: important or complex news, crisis, need for questions.
- **Planning**: clear purpose; timing (avoid deadlines clashes); venue and technical arrangements; invitations; spokesperson prepared with key messages and likely questions; press kits (release, fact sheet, photos, biographies); live streaming; follow-up.
- **Conduct**: brief opening statement; Q&A managed by moderator; equal access to journalists; avoid "off the record" confusion; never lie.
- Variants: press briefing (regular, e.g. Foreign Office weekly briefing), backgrounder, media tour.

## 7.5 Public relations in Pakistan

### Government machinery

| Body | Role |
|---|---|

| Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson | Weekly briefings; foreign policy communication |
| Ministry/department PROs | Police, WAPDA, NDMA, universities, etc. |
| **Information Group** (an occupational group of the CSS) | Officers staff the PID, MoIB, Pakistani press attaché posts abroad, DEMP — relevant to you as a CSS candidate |

### The PID (2016, 2018)

- **Origins**: in British India, a Bureau of Public Information (1919/1923) and the Principal Information Officer's post; after 1947, the PID in Karachi, then Islamabad, with regional offices (Lahore, Karachi, Peshawar, Quetta) [verify origin dates].
- **Functions**:
  1. Issue press releases, handouts and press notes on government activities.
  2. Arrange press conferences and briefings for the PM, ministers and officials.
  3. **Accreditation** of journalists (federal press cards).
  4. **Media monitoring** and daily digests/analysis of press and TV for the government; rebuttals and clarifications.
  5. **Government advertising**: historically the PID released federal government ads to newspapers and channels (and maintained lists of eligible papers) [verify current role under the advertising policy].
  6. Photo and video coverage; official photographs.
  7. Liaison with foreign media; facilitating visiting journalists.
  8. Feedback to government on public opinion.
  9. Social media and digital outreach (official accounts).
- **Performance in image building (2016)**: *strengths* — reach, institutional memory, accreditation, monitoring; *weaknesses* — seen as a government (party) mouthpiece rather than public information service; reactive; slow on social media; credibility gap; advertising used as reward and punishment; under-trained staff in digital and crisis communication; lack of research and evaluation; turf issues with ISPR and the PM's media team.
- **Reform**: professional, non-partisan public information service; transparent advertising criteria; digital and data analytics; crisis communication cells; RTI compliance; training at the Information Service Academy.

### PR tools, techniques and challenges in Pakistan (2017)

- *Tools*: press releases, conferences, TV talk-show appearances, social media, events, CSR (e.g. telecom and FMCG brands), influencer marketing, sponsorships (PSL cricket).
- *Challenges*: low trust in official information; partisan media; fake news; crisis-prone environment (terrorism, disasters); lack of professional standards and training; PR confused with "media management" and paid content; small budgets in public sector; weak research and evaluation; legal and security constraints; rapid digital change.
- *Private sector*: PR and marketing agencies (local firms and affiliates of global networks), in-house corporate communications departments in banks, telecoms, FMCG, energy.

## 7.6 PR as a tool for governance (2019, 2026)

**Why PR matters for governance.**
1. **Informing citizens** about policies, rights and services (transparency).
2. **Building trust and legitimacy**; explaining difficult decisions (taxes, IMF reforms, subsidies).
3. **Two-way feedback**: understanding public needs; citizen portals and surveys.
4. **Early-warning system** (2019): PR monitors media, social media and stakeholder opinion (environmental scanning, issues management — Howard Chase, 1976) to detect emerging problems (public anger over prices, rumours, protests) before they become crises; advises leadership to act early.
5. **Crisis communication**: disasters, terrorism, pandemics; one voice, speed, accuracy, empathy.
6. **Countering misinformation** with quick, credible facts.
7. **Policy success**: behaviour change campaigns (taxes, health, energy saving).
8. **Accountability**: RTI compliance, proactive disclosure (Article 19-A).
9. **Nation branding and diplomacy**: tourism, investment (SIFC promotions), public diplomacy.

**Pakistani examples**: NCOC daily COVID updates (2020–21) seen as relatively successful; Pakistan Citizen Portal (2018) with millions of complaints; NDMA flood communication; FBR tax campaigns; Ehsaas/BISP information; police community policing pages (e.g. Punjab Safe Cities Authority); failures: rumour-driven crises (petrol shortage panic 2020), slow responses to disinformation.

**Risks**: PR becoming propaganda or "spin"; partisanship; manipulation of media through ads; credibility loss if facts are hidden.

Examples: a telecom company's CSR in floods; a bank's digital-safety awareness; a government ministry's transparency dashboard.

## 7.8 New technologies in PR (2022)

Social media management (X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn); influencer partnerships; live streaming of briefings; chatbots and WhatsApp channels; data analytics and social listening tools; SEO and content marketing; mobile apps; virtual events; AI for drafting and monitoring (with ethics). Pakistani examples: ministries' and police social media accounts; ISPR's digital presence; PSL's digital campaigns; brands' TikTok campaigns; government WhatsApp channels [verify]. Challenges: speed vs accuracy, trolls and bots, privacy, deepfakes, 24/7 crisis exposure.

## 7.9 PR ethics

- **Codes**: IPRA Code of Venice (1961) and Code of Athens (1965) (based on the UN Declaration of Human Rights); PRSA Member Code of Ethics (2000) — values: **advocacy, honesty, expertise, independence, loyalty, fairness**; provisions on free flow of information, competition, disclosure, confidentiality, conflicts of interest. CIPR Code of Conduct.
- **Ethical issues**: lying or "spin"; front groups and **astroturfing** (fake grassroots campaigns); **greenwashing**; undisclosed paid content and advertorials; bribing journalists ("lifafa"); fake reviews and bots; conflicts of interest; privacy and data misuse; manipulating images; withholding information in crises.
- **In Pakistan**: blurred line between PR and paid journalism; government advertising as leverage; troll networks on social media; lack of a PR professional body with enforceable code.
- **Principles to follow**: truth, transparency, accountability, respect for the public interest, disclosure of sponsorship, confidentiality, fairness to competitors.

## 7.10 Model plan: PRO of police department to control street crime (2023)

Use RACE:
1. **Research**: crime data mapping (hotspots, times, types), public perception surveys, social media listening, media audit, stakeholder mapping (citizens, traders, students, media, ride-hailing companies, mobile operators).
2. **Objectives**: e.g. raise reporting of street crime by 30%, raise public trust score, reduce snatching in hotspots by 20% in 12 months, increase use of emergency number 15 and Safe City apps.
3. **Strategy and actions**:
   - *Public awareness*: safety tips campaign (phones, ATMs, motorbikes), on TV, radio, FM, social media, billboards; Urdu and regional languages.
   - *Community policing*: neighbourhood watch, traders' associations, university safety committees, "Police Khidmat Markaz" visits.
   - *Technology*: promote Safe City cameras, emergency apps, women's safety apps; online FIR and e-tag tracking of stolen phones (PTA's DIRBS/IMEI blocking).
   - *Media relations*: regular briefings with real data; quick, factual crime updates; showcase arrests and recoveries (without violating suspects' rights); counter rumours.
   - *Internal communication*: train officers in courteous behaviour and public dealing; reward responsive stations.
   - *Partnerships*: ride-hailing firms, banks, telecoms, schools, influencers.
4. **Evaluation**: crime statistics, reporting rates, surveys, media sentiment, app downloads.
