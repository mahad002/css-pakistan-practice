## I. Introduction and models

- Communication: Latin *communis* (common). Mass communication (Wright 1959): large, heterogeneous, anonymous audience; public, rapid, transient messages; complex organisations; delayed feedback.
- Types: intrapersonal, interpersonal, group, public, mass, organisational, intercultural; business communication (7 Cs).
- Process: source → encoding → message → channel → decoding → receiver → feedback; noise (physical, semantic, psychological); context.
- Model functions (Deutsch): organising, heuristic, predictive, measuring. Evaluation: generality, heuristic value, importance, originality, parsimony, accuracy.
- **Lasswell 1948**: who–what–channel–whom–effect; surveillance, correlation, transmission (+ entertainment, Wright). Linear, no feedback.
- **Shannon-Weaver 1948/49**: source, transmitter, channel, receiver, destination, **noise**; redundancy, entropy; technical/semantic/effectiveness levels; linear, mechanistic.
- **Osgood-Schramm 1954**: circular; encoder–interpreter–decoder. **Schramm**: field of experience overlap.
- **Newcomb 1953**: ABX triangle; strain toward symmetry.
- **Westley-MacLean 1957** (FPSC lists 1976): A advocacy, C channel/gatekeeper, B audience, X events, feedback.
- **Gerbner 1956**: horizontal (perception of event E as E1) + vertical (means/control: statement SE); chain.
- Others: Berlo SMCR 1960; Dance helical 1967; Barnlund transactional 1970; White's "Mr. Gates" 1950.

## II. Theories

- **Eras**: mass society/powerful (1920s–30s) → limited effects (1940s–60s: Lazarsfeld, Hovland, Klapper 1960) → moderate/cultural (1960s–80s: agenda-setting, cultivation, spiral, U&G, McLuhan, hegemony) → meaning-making/digital (1990s–: framing, network society, algorithms).
- **Normative** (Siebert, Peterson, Schramm 1956): authoritarian; libertarian (Milton 1644, Mill 1859); social responsibility (Hutchins Commission 1947: truthful, forum, representative picture, goals and values, full access); Soviet. McQuail (1983): development, democratic-participant. Critiques: Cold War, Western, normative not empirical, ignores commerce (Nerone 1995). Alternatives: Hallin and Mancini 2004 (liberal, democratic corporatist, polarised pluralist); Christians et al. 2009 (monitorial, facilitative, radical, collaborative). Pakistan = hybrid, polarised pluralist.
- **Agents of power**: Marx; Frankfurt School culture industry (1944); **propaganda model** (Herman and Chomsky 1988): ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, anti-communism/fear.
- **Spiral of silence** (Noelle-Neumann 1974): fear of isolation; quasi-statistical sense; ubiquity, cumulation, consonance; hardcore; train test.
- **U&G** (Katz, Blumler, Gurevitch 1973–74; Herzog 1944): active audience; cognitive, affective, personal integrative, social integrative, tension release; McQuail et al.: diversion, relationships, identity, surveillance; "positivist" (surveys, self-report).
- **Hegemony** (Gramsci): consent; Hall encoding/decoding 1973 (dominant, negotiated, oppositional); Gitlin 1980.
- **Diffusion** (Rogers 1962/2003; Ryan and Gross 1943): innovation, channels, time, social system; knowledge → persuasion → decision → implementation → confirmation; innovators 2.5, early adopters 13.5, early majority 34, late majority 34, laggards 16; attributes: relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability; S-curve; critiques: pro-innovation, individual-blame, recall, equality. Pakistan: Green Revolution, polio, family planning, mobile money, solar.
- **Magic bullet**: passive audiences; War of the Worlds 1938 (Cantril 1940). Digital revival debate: misinformation, micro-targeting vs active audiences.
- **Two-step flow**: *The People's Choice* 1944 (Erie County 1940); *Personal Influence* 1955 (Decatur); opinion leaders; multi-step; influencers.
- **McLuhan**: *Gutenberg Galaxy* 1962, *Understanding Media* 1964; medium is the message; extensions of man; hot/cool; four epochs; global village; Innis (time/space bias). Critique: determinism.
- **Others**: agenda-setting (McCombs and Shaw 1972; Cohen 1963; Lippmann 1922); priming; framing (Goffman 1974, Entman 1993); cultivation (Gerbner; mean world); gatekeeping (Lewin 1947, White 1950); dependency (Ball-Rokeach and DeFleur 1976); knowledge gap (Tichenor et al. 1970); Bandura; Berkowitz vs Feshbach; information disorder (Wardle and Derakhshan 2017).
