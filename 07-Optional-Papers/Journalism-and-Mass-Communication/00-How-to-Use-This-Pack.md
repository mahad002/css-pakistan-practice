## 3. The answer template that scores in this subject

Examiners reward three things: **correct theory with its author and date, Pakistani examples with current facts, and balanced judgement on freedom versus responsibility.**

**Step 0 — 2 minutes.** Underline the command words and every part; 2024–2026 questions often have three parts (e.g. "explain the theory, apply it to Pakistan, discuss criticisms").

1. **Outline (5–6 lines).**
2. **Introduction (80–120 words).** A definition with source (Quebral for development communication; CIPR or PRSA for PR; Article 19 for freedom) → context → **thesis**.
3. **Theory/concept section (200–300 words).** Author, year, key terms, a **diagram** (models, spiral of silence, diffusion curve, Westley-MacLean, Gerbner) — diagrams earn marks in this subject.
4. **Pakistan section (250–350 words).** Laws with years (PPO 1963, PEMRA 2002, PECA 2016 and 2025 amendments), institutions (PEMRA, PID, PCP, journalists' commission), dated facts (RSF 153rd in 2026; 142 TV and 240 FM licences, March 2026), and one or two cases (X ban 2024–25, NCOC communication, polio campaigns, talk-show polarisation).
5. **Critical analysis (100–150 words).** Both sides: freedom vs responsibility; state's security concerns vs press rights.
6. **Way forward (80–120 words).** Specific measures naming the actor (Parliament, PEMRA, MoIB, PFUJ/APNS, HEC).
7. **Conclusion (60–80 words).**

**Short notes (10 marks):** definition → origin (who, when) → 3–4 key points or features → Pakistani application → one-line evaluation; about 400–450 words. For 5-mark notes, about 200 words.

## 4. Common mistakes

1. **Theory without Pakistan, or Pakistan without theory.** Almost every question wants both.
2. **Wrong authors and dates** (e.g. confusing Gerbner's 1956 model with cultivation theory; misdating the MacBride report — it is 1980).
3. **Outdated law.** Writing about PECA 2016 without the 2025 amendments, or PEMRA without the 2023 amendment and 2026 developments.
4. **One-sided writing on freedom of expression.** Acknowledge legitimate state concerns (terrorism coverage, disinformation), then argue for narrow, proportionate rules.
5. **No diagrams** for models and theories.
6. **Confusing DC, DSC and development journalism**, or press release and press note.
7. **Political partisanship** in answers on anchors, PTI, PML-N or the establishment. Stay analytical and neutral.
8. **Ignoring the digital turn**: fake news, deepfakes, digital divide and platform regulation now appear every year.
9. **Unequal answers**: four complete answers beat three long ones.
10. **Neglecting MCQs**: they test basic journalism terms (scoop, feature, editorial, teleprompter), agencies (MENA = Egypt) and theorists. Aim for 15+/20.
