# How to Use This Pack — Political Science (CSS, Group I, 200 marks)

Checked 24 Nov 2026. Facts about current politics are dated "as of Nov 2026". Anything I could not confirm is marked **[verify]**.

## 1. The papers at a glance

Confirmed from FPSC's own 2024, 2025 and 2026 papers (links in `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`) and the CE Rules (see `../../04-Research/Official-Facts.md`).

| Item | Paper I | Paper II |
|---|---|---|
| Marks / time | 100 marks, 3 hours | 100 marks, 3 hours |
| Part I | 20 MCQs, 1 mark each, on a separate OMR sheet taken back after **30 minutes**. No negative marking. "All MCQs must be attempted." | Same |
| Part II | 80 marks. Seven questions (Q2–Q8), 20 marks each. **Attempt four: TWO from Section A and TWO from Section B** | 80 marks. Seven questions. **Attempt four: ONE from Section A and THREE from Section B** |
| Section A | Western and Muslim political thought (usually Q2–Q5) | USA/UK/France/Germany + global and regional integration (usually only Q2–Q3) |
| Section B | State, concepts, comparative politics, participation, institutions, forms of government, ideologies, local government (Q6–Q8) | Turkey/Iran/Malaysia/India/China, Muslim nationalism, Pakistan's government and politics, IR and Pakistan's foreign policy (Q4–Q8) |
| Short notes | Often one question is "notes on two of three" (10 marks each) | Sometimes |
| Calculator | Not allowed (only seven listed subjects allow one) | Not allowed |
| Same day | Both papers are usually on the same day (morning and afternoon) [verify on the CSS 2028 date sheet] | |
| Pass mark | 33% in the subject (66/200 across both papers) | |

**Watch the section rule.** Coaching websites often print "attempt any four". The FPSC papers say otherwise. Extra attempts are not marked: if you answer three from Section A in Paper I, one of them is wasted.

**The official syllabus marks** say Paper I Part A = 50, Part B = 50; Paper II Part A = 30, Part B = 70. The question split matches: in Paper II you write only one Part A answer.

**What this means.** Political Science is passable but tight. 60% is rare. A realistic target is **105–125 out of 200** (52–62%). That needs about 14–16 in MCQs per paper and 11–12 out of 20 per long answer. A 13 or 14 on a long answer is excellent.

Why it is still the right Group I choice: 86% of allocated candidates in 2025 took it; it overlaps with Pakistan Affairs, Current Affairs and the Essay; books are everywhere.

## 3. What examiners reward

FPSC publishes no marking scheme. This comes from examiner remarks quoted in the press and FPSC reports, and from the consistent advice of high scorers (see `08-Resources.md`).

| They look for | You show it by |
|---|---|
| Answering the exact question | Headings that use the question's words. If it says "compare", every section compares |
| Understanding, not memory | Explain *why* a thinker said something: his times, his problem, his method |
| Primary concepts and terms | Use the thinker's own terms correctly: *asabiyya*, general will, philosopher-king, *velayat-e faqih*, democratic centralism |
| Critique | Two or three named critics with their actual objection |
| Relevance | Link to today and to Pakistan: Hobbes and security states, Ibn Khaldun and dynastic parties, Montesquieu and the 26th/27th Amendments |
| Structure | Outline, headings, short paragraphs, one diagram |
| Accuracy | Correct dates, book titles, article numbers, current office-holders |

## 4. The answer template (20 marks, 35 minutes)

**Step 0 — Read and plan (3 minutes).** Underline the command word and every part. Write a 6–8 line outline on the first page.

**Step 1 — Outline (shown to the examiner).** Numbered headings. It tells the examiner you have a plan.

**Step 2 — Introduction (100–150 words).**
- Define the key term or place the thinker in one line (dates, city, main work).
- State your thesis: your answer to the question in one sentence.

**Step 3 — Context (80–120 words).** For a thinker: the crisis he faced (Athens' defeat for Plato, English Civil War for Hobbes, Abbasid decline for Mawardi, Mughal collapse for Shah Waliullah). For a country: the constitution and its origin.

**Step 4 — Body (5–7 headings, 600–800 words).** One idea per heading. Each heading: explain → evidence (quote of under 15 words, article number, example) → so what.

**Step 5 — Critical evaluation (150–200 words).** Strengths, then named criticisms. Do not skip this: it is where marks separate.

**Step 6 — Relevance today / Pakistan (100 words).**

**Step 7 — Conclusion (80–120 words).** Restate the thesis as a judgement. No new facts.

**Diagrams that work.** Ibn Khaldun's five-stage cycle; Plato's three classes and three parts of the soul; Aristotle's six-fold table; Easton's input–output systems model; Almond–Verba's three cultures; Iran's power chart; US checks-and-balances triangle; EU institutional map; Pakistan's federal–provincial money flow (NFC).

**Short notes (10 marks, 17 minutes each).** Definition → 3–4 headed points → one example → one-line judgement. About 350–450 words.

## 5. Time per paper

| Block | Minutes |
|---|---|
| MCQs (sheet collected) | 30 |
| Read the paper, choose questions, plan all four | 10 |
| Four answers × 33–35 minutes | 135–140 |
| Check headings, underline key terms, cross out blank pages | 5 |
| **Total** | **180** |

Write the answer you know best first. Never spend more than 40 minutes on one answer: a missing fourth answer costs more than a weak one.

## 6. Common mistakes

1. **Breaking the section rule** (see §1).
2. **Writing a biography.** Life and context get one paragraph, not a page.
3. **Theory with no critique** or critique with no names.
4. **Mixing up thinkers.** Popular sovereignty is Rousseau, not Locke; "man is born free…" is Rousseau; "life… solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short" is Hobbes; the "Aristotle of the 18th century" is Montesquieu.
5. **Out-of-date facts in Paper II.** As of Nov 2026: Iran's Supreme Leader is **Mojtaba Khamenei** (since 8 March 2026); the UK Prime Minister is **Andy Burnham** (since 20 July 2026); the French PM is **Sébastien Lecornu**; Germany's Chancellor is **Friedrich Merz**; Democrats won both houses of the US Congress on 3 November 2026; Turkey has been presidential since 2018; Pakistan's latest amendment is the **27th** (Nov 2025). Re-check all of these in Dec 2027.
6. **Ignoring the "Islamic" half.** Political Concepts (sovereignty, justice, rights) are "Western and Islamic". Give both.
7. **No Pakistan link** in Section B of Paper I. Examiners like it.
8. **Overlong answers.** Twelve pages of filler do not beat eight focused pages.
