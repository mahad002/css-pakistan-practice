**Format note.** Every year from 2016 to 2026 has the same shape. Paper I: attempt four, **two from each section** (2016 and 2017 said "at least two from each section"). Paper II: attempt four, **one from Section A and three from Section B**. Some coaching sites print "attempt any four" — that is wrong; read the FPSC instruction box.

Tags (Paper I): **W** Western thought, **M** Muslim thought, **B** Part B concepts/institutions/ideologies. (Paper II): **C** comparative systems, **G** global and regional integration, **N** Muslim nationalism, **P** Pakistan government and politics, **I** international relations and foreign policy.

### What the counts tell you

1. **Paper I, Section A** is 70% classical and early-modern: Plato, Aristotle, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Montesquieu, Hegel, Marx, Ibn Khaldun. Modern names (Fukuyama, Sartre, Descartes, Bentham) appear about once a year, often as short notes.
2. **Muslim thought** gives one long question every year. Ibn Khaldun alone is half of them.
3. **Paper I, Section B** loves federalism, parties, sovereignty and ideologies (fascism, capitalism, totalitarianism).
4. **Paper II, Section A** (you answer only one): always one Western country (USA, UK, France or Germany) and one integration question (EU, SAARC, IMF/WTO, globalisation).
5. **Paper II, Section B** (you answer three): from 2024 it has shifted towards **foreign policy and IR**. In 2026, five of seven questions were IR or foreign policy. Pakistan's federalism and constitutions remain the other staple.
6. **Repeats are common.** 2021 Paper II was almost wholly repeated from 2016–2019. Hegel's Spirit appeared word for word in 2019 and 2020. Montesquieu's "Aristotle of the 18th century" in 2020 and 2024.
