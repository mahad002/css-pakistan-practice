# PAPER I

## P-I/01 Plato and Aristotle (+ Epicureans, Stoics)
- **Plato** (c. 427–347 BC; Academy c. 387 BC). *Republic*, *Statesman*, *Laws*. Justice = each class doing its own work: rulers (reason, wisdom, gold), auxiliaries (spirit, courage, silver), producers (appetite, temperance, bronze). Philosopher-king; state education (rule at ~50); communism of property and family for guardians only; women equal among guardians; decline: aristocracy → timocracy → oligarchy → democracy → tyranny. *Laws*: rule of law, 5,040 households, mixed constitution. Critics: Aristotle (unity, care), Popper (*Open Society*, 1945).
- **Aristotle** (384–322 BC; Lyceum 335 BC; 158 constitutions). "Man is a political animal"; state natural, prior to individual; exists for the good life. Six-fold table: monarchy/tyranny, aristocracy/oligarchy, polity/democracy; best practicable = **polity** (middle class). Citizen = shares in judging and deliberating. Natural slavery. Distributive vs corrective justice. Rule of law ("reason free from passion"). Revolution: passion for equality; prevent by law, middle class, education. Realist, inductive, father of political science.
- **Epicureans** (Epicurus 341–270 BC): ataraxia, "live unnoticed", justice as a pact not to harm. **Stoics** (Zeno of Citium): natural law, cosmopolis, equality.

## P-I/02 Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, Montesquieu, Rousseau
- **Machiavelli** (1469–1527): *Prince* (1513), *Discourses*. Realism; politics separate from ethics; better feared than loved, never hated; lion and fox; virtù vs fortuna; citizen army; republic preferred in the long run. "Citizen of all states" → realism, raison d'état.
- **Hobbes** (*Leviathan* 1651): selfish, fearful equals; war of all against all; "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short"; laws of nature; covenant of each with each; sovereign not a party; absolute, indivisible; resistance only for self-preservation; "the end of obedience is protection".
- **Locke** (*Two Treatises* 1689): peaceful state of nature with three inconveniences; life, liberty, estate; labour theory of property; consent and trust; legislative/executive/federative; right of revolution; toleration (not Catholics, atheists).
- **Rousseau** (*Social Contract* 1762): "born free… in chains"; property → inequality; total alienation; general will vs will of all; sovereignty inalienable, indivisible, unrepresentable; "forced to be free"; small direct democracy; critics: Talmon.
- **Montesquieu** (*Spirit of the Laws* 1748): republic–virtue, monarchy–honour, despotism–fear; climate; liberty needs separation of legislative, executive, judicial; "power must check power"; "Aristotle of the 18th century" (comparative, empirical); Madison's *Federalist 47*.

## P-I/03 Kant, Bentham, Mill, Hegel
- **Kant**: categorical imperative; republic (not democracy); no right of revolution; *Perpetual Peace* (1795): six preliminary, three definitive articles (republican constitutions, federation of free states, hospitality) → democratic peace.
- **Bentham**: pain and pleasure; greatest happiness of the greatest number; felicific calculus (7 dimensions); "nonsense upon stilts"; law as command; Panopticon.
- **Mill**: higher and lower pleasures ("Socrates dissatisfied"); *On Liberty* (1859): harm principle, tyranny of the majority, four arguments for free speech; *Representative Government* (1861): PR, plural voting, open ballot; *Subjection of Women* (1869).
- **Utilitarianism today**: cost–benefit, QALYs, effective altruism; Rawls as alternative.
- **Hegel**: Spirit; history = progress of consciousness of freedom (one → some → all); dialectic (Aufhebung); family → civil society → state; state as ethical whole; war; Popper's and Marx's critiques. Hegel vs Marx: idea vs matter; state vs withering away.

## P-I/04 Marx, Lenin, Mao, Gramsci

Critique: predictions, determinism, Popper, Bernstein.
- **Lenin**: vanguard (*What Is to Be Done?* 1902); democratic centralism; imperialism (1916) → weakest link; *State and Revolution* (smash the state).
