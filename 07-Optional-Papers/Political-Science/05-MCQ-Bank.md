## Paper I — Classical Greek Thought

**Q1.** In Plato's *Republic*, who defines justice as "the interest of the stronger"?
- A) Glaucon
- B) Cephalus
- C) Thrasymachus
- D) Polemarchus
**Answer:** C. Thrasymachus states this view in Book I; Socrates refutes it.

**Q2.** In Plato's ideal state, the virtue that belongs to the ruling (guardian) class is:
- A) Courage
- B) Wisdom
- C) Temperance
- D) Piety
**Answer:** B. Rulers embody reason and its virtue, wisdom; auxiliaries courage; temperance is shared by all.

**Q3.** Under Plato's scheme of education, philosopher-rulers take up the highest offices at about the age of:
- A) 65
- B) 30
- C) 35
- D) 50
**Answer:** D. After dialectic (30–35) and practical service (35–50), the best rule from about 50.

**Q4.** Plato's communism of property and family applied to:
- A) All citizens
- B) The producing class only
- C) The guardian classes only
- D) Slaves and foreigners
**Answer:** C. Only rulers and auxiliaries were to live without private property or private families.

**Q5.** Which work of Plato describes a "second-best" state of 5,040 households governed by law?
- A) Laws
- B) Apology
- C) Republic
- D) Statesman
**Answer:** A. The *Laws*, his last work, replaces philosopher-kings with the rule of law and a mixed constitution.

**Q6.** In Aristotle's classification, the perverted form of rule by the many is:
- A) Tyranny
- B) Polity
- C) Oligarchy
- D) Democracy
**Answer:** D. Polity is the true form; democracy, rule of the poor for themselves, is its corruption.

**Q7.** Aristotle regarded which form as the best practicable state for most cities?
- A) Monarchy
- B) Polity
- C) Aristocracy
- D) Democracy
**Answer:** B. Polity, a mix of oligarchy and democracy resting on a large middle class.

**Q8.** Aristotle's school in Athens was called the:
- A) Lyceum
- B) Academy
- C) Stoa
- D) Garden
**Answer:** A. He founded the Lyceum in 335 BC; the Academy was Plato's.

**Q9.** Aristotle and his students are said to have studied how many constitutions?
- A) 58
- B) 108
- C) 158
- D) 258
**Answer:** C. Only the *Constitution of Athens* survives from the 158 studies.

**Q10.** For Aristotle, distributive justice allocates offices and honours according to:
- A) Need
- B) Merit or contribution in proportion
- C) Lottery
- D) Strict numerical equality
**Answer:** B. Distributive justice is geometric (proportional); corrective justice is arithmetic.

**Q11.** Aristotle defined a citizen as one who:
- A) Pays taxes to the city
- B) Was born in the city
- C) Owns land in the city
- D) Shares in judicial and deliberative office
**Answer:** D. Citizenship meant participation in the assembly and the courts.

**Q12.** For Epicurus, the goal of life was:
- A) Contemplation of the Forms
- B) Virtue as duty to the state
- C) Ataraxia, freedom from mental disturbance
- D) Glory in war
**Answer:** C. Epicurean pleasure is the absence of bodily pain and mental disturbance.

**Q13.** The founder of Stoicism was:
- A) Zeno of Citium
- B) Diogenes of Sinope
- C) Protagoras
- D) Epicurus
**Answer:** A. Zeno taught at the Stoa Poikile in Athens; Diogenes was a Cynic.

**Q14.** Karl Popper attacked Plato as an enemy of the open society in:
- A) Conjectures and Refutations
- B) The Logic of Scientific Discovery
- C) The Poverty of Historicism
- D) The Open Society and Its Enemies
**Answer:** D. Volume 1 of *The Open Society and Its Enemies* (1945) is devoted to Plato.

## Paper I — Machiavelli to Rousseau

**Q15.** Machiavelli wrote *The Prince* in:
- A) 1469
- B) 1513
- C) 1532
- D) 1559
**Answer:** B. Written in 1513 after his dismissal; published posthumously in 1532.

**Q16.** Machiavelli advised that a prince should combine the qualities of the:
- A) Lion and the fox
- B) Fox and the wolf
- C) Lion and the lamb
- D) Eagle and the lion
**Answer:** A. The fox recognises traps; the lion frightens wolves.

**Q17.** Machiavelli's preference for republics and mixed government is set out mainly in:
- A) The Prince
- B) The Art of War
- C) Discourses on Livy
- D) Florentine Histories
**Answer:** C. The *Discourses* praise Rome's mixed constitution and liberty.

**Q18.** Hobbes's *Leviathan* was published in:
- A) 1642
- B) 1651
- C) 1689
- D) 1588
**Answer:** B. Published in 1651, at the end of the English Civil War.

**Q19.** Hobbes's three principal causes of quarrel are competition, diffidence and:
- A) Property
- B) Religion
- C) Ignorance
- D) Glory
**Answer:** D. *Leviathan*, ch. 13: competition (gain), diffidence (safety), glory (reputation).

**Q20.** In Hobbes's social contract, the sovereign is:
- A) Bound by courts to observe natural law
- B) A party to the contract
- C) Not a party to the contract
- D) Elected by a majority each year
**Answer:** C. Because the sovereign did not covenant, he cannot breach the contract.

**Q21.** Locke's *First Treatise of Government* was written to refute:
- A) Robert Filmer
- B) Thomas Hobbes
- C) Richard Hooker
- D) Jean Bodin
**Answer:** A. Filmer's *Patriarcha* defended divine right descending from Adam.

**Q22.** Which of these is NOT one of Locke's three "inconveniences" of the state of nature?
- A) No established, known law
- B) No impartial judge
- C) No power to enforce judgments
- D) No established religion
**Answer:** D. The three are absence of known law, impartial judge and executive power.

**Q23.** Locke's "federative" power concerns:
- A) Taxation
- B) War, peace, treaties and foreign affairs
- C) The judiciary
- D) Local government
**Answer:** B. Locke distinguished legislative, executive and federative powers.

**Q24.** "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains" opens:
- A) Rousseau's Social Contract
- B) Hobbes's Leviathan
- C) Montesquieu's Spirit of the Laws
- D) Locke's Second Treatise
**Answer:** A. The first line of Rousseau's *Social Contract* (1762).

**Q25.** For Rousseau, the "will of all" differs from the general will because the will of all is:
- A) The will of the legislator
- B) The will of the government
- C) The sum of private wills
- D) The will of the majority party
**Answer:** C. The general will aims at the common good; the will of all adds up private interests.

**Q26.** Rousseau said the English people are free only:
- A) In the courts
- B) During elections of members of Parliament
- C) When Parliament sits
- D) Under the King
**Answer:** B. He held that sovereignty cannot be represented.

**Q27.** Montesquieu's *The Spirit of the Laws* was published in:
- A) 1762
- B) 1721
- C) 1734
- D) 1748
**Answer:** D. 1748; the *Persian Letters* appeared in 1721.

**Q28.** According to Montesquieu, the principle (moving spirit) of despotism is:
- A) Honour
- B) Virtue
- C) Fear
- D) Moderation
**Answer:** C. Republic: virtue; monarchy: honour; despotism: fear.

**Q29.** Montesquieu is called "the Aristotle of the 18th century" mainly because of his:
- A) Comparative, empirical study of laws and governments
- B) Defence of slavery
- C) Theory of philosopher-kings
- D) Belief in direct democracy
**Answer:** A. Like Aristotle, he compared many societies to find general causes.

**Q30.** James Madison called Montesquieu "the oracle" on separation of powers in *Federalist* No.:
- A) 51
- B) 78
- C) 10
- D) 47
**Answer:** D. Federalist 47 discusses "the celebrated Montesquieu".

## Paper I — Kant, Bentham, Mill, Hegel

**Q31.** The first definitive article of Kant's *Perpetual Peace* requires that the civil constitution of every state be:
- A) Monarchical
- B) Republican
- C) Directly democratic
- D) Federal
**Answer:** B. The second calls for a federation of free states; the third for universal hospitality.

**Q32.** The Enlightenment motto *Sapere aude* ("dare to know") is associated with:
- A) Kant
- B) Bentham
- C) Rousseau
- D) Hegel
**Answer:** A. From Kant's essay "What is Enlightenment?" (1784).

**Q33.** Which of these is NOT a dimension of Bentham's felicific calculus?
- A) Intensity
- B) Duration
- C) Quality
- D) Extent
**Answer:** C. Quality of pleasure was Mill's revision; Bentham measured quantity.

**Q34.** Bentham called natural and imprescriptible rights "nonsense upon stilts". He was referring to:
- A) Common law
- B) Natural rights
- C) The principle of utility
- D) Parliamentary sovereignty
**Answer:** B. From *Anarchical Fallacies*, his attack on the French Declaration of Rights.

**Q35.** "Better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied" was written by:
- A) Hume
- B) Bentham
- C) James Mill
- D) J.S. Mill
**Answer:** D. From J.S. Mill's *Utilitarianism* (1861).

**Q36.** Mill's harm principle holds that power may be exercised over an individual against his will only to:
- A) Protect the majority's opinion
- B) Make him happier
- C) Prevent harm to others
- D) Enforce morality
**Answer:** C. *On Liberty* (1859): his own good is not a sufficient warrant.

**Q37.** In *Considerations on Representative Government*, Mill proposed:
- A) Plural votes for the educated
- B) Compulsory voting
- C) Abolition of Parliament
- D) The secret ballot
**Answer:** A. He also backed proportional representation and opposed the secret ballot.

**Q38.** In Hegel's *Philosophy of Right*, which stage of ethical life is the "system of needs" (the antithesis)?
- A) State
- B) Abstract right
- C) Family
- D) Civil society
**Answer:** D. Family (unity) → civil society (particularity) → state (synthesis).

**Q39.** For Hegel, world history is the progress of:
- A) Class struggle
- B) The consciousness of freedom
- C) Material production
- D) The general will
**Answer:** B. From one free (Oriental) to some (Greco-Roman) to all (Germanic-Christian).

- A) Interest

**Q43.** The phrase "withering away of the state" was coined by:

**Q44.** Lenin's theory of the vanguard party is set out in:
- A) April Theses

**Q45.** The theory of hegemony as rule by consent as well as coercion is associated with:
- A) Antonio Gramsci
- B) Jacques Derrida

**Q46.** "Political power grows out of the barrel of a gun" was said by:
- A) Che Guevara

**Q47.** Gramsci's "war of position" means:

**Q48.** Democratic centralism, the organising principle of communist parties, was developed by:
