# 14. Political Ideologies: Capitalism, Marxism, Communism, Socialism, Totalitarianism, Fascism, Nationalism, Islamic Political Ideology

Asked: fascism 2017 (child of early 20th-century Europe), 2019 (enabling conditions: Germany and Italy), 2020 (principles); capitalism and democracy 2021, 2025; Islamic political ideology 2022 (note); nationalism 2024 (note); totalitarianism 2016 (note). Read with `04-Marxist-Thought.md` and `13-Forms-of-Government.md`.

**What is an ideology?** A more or less coherent set of ideas that (1) describes and explains the existing order, (2) sets out a vision of a good society, and (3) explains how to get there (Andrew Heywood). The term was coined by **Destutt de Tracy** (1796) as the "science of ideas"; Marx used it for ruling-class ideas that mask interests; Daniel Bell announced "**the end of ideology**" (1960), Fukuyama "the end of history" (1989).

## 1. CAPITALISM

### 1.1 Meaning
An economic system based on **private ownership of the means of production**, production for **profit**, **market** competition and **wage labour**, with prices set by supply and demand.

### 1.2 Thinkers
- **Adam Smith**, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776): self-interest guided by the "**invisible hand**" of the market produces public benefit; division of labour; free trade; limited state (defence, justice, public works).
- **Laissez-faire** ("let do", "let it be"): the state should withdraw from economic regulation (CSS 2024 MCQ: withdrawal of restrictions). Associated with the Physiocrats (Quesnay) and classical economists (Ricardo).
- **Friedrich Hayek**, *The Road to Serfdom* (1944): central planning leads to tyranny; the market is a discovery process.
- **Milton Friedman**, *Capitalism and Freedom* (1962): economic freedom is a precondition of political freedom.
- **J.M. Keynes**, *General Theory* (1936): markets can fail; the state must manage demand (spending, interest rates) to prevent depressions.

### 1.3 Features
Private property; profit motive; free market and price mechanism; competition; consumer sovereignty; freedom of enterprise and contract; limited government role; wage labour; inequality accepted as incentive.

### 1.4 Types
| Type | Features | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Laissez-faire | Minimal state | 19th-century Britain |
| Welfare / Keynesian | Mixed economy, social security | Post-1945 Western Europe |
| Neoliberal | Privatisation, deregulation, free trade (**Washington Consensus**, 1989) | Thatcher's UK, Reagan's US; IMF programmes |
| State capitalism | State-owned firms in a market | China, Gulf states, Singapore |
| Crony capitalism | Profits via connections | Many developing states |

### 1.5 Merits and demerits
**Merits**: efficiency, innovation, growth, consumer choice, individual freedom, decentralised decisions.
**Demerits**: inequality, exploitation, monopolies, booms and busts (Great Depression 1929; global financial crisis 2008), environmental damage, commodification, influence of money on politics.

### 1.6 Capitalism and democracy (2021, 2025: "two faces of the same coin")

**Arguments that they go together**
1. Both rest on **individual freedom** and choice (market choice and electoral choice).
2. Private property creates centres of power independent of the state (Friedman, Hayek).
3. Capitalist growth creates a **middle class** that demands democracy (Lipset; Barrington Moore: "no bourgeoisie, no democracy").
4. Historically, stable democracies are almost all market economies.
5. Both rely on **rule of law**, contracts, rights, limited government.
6. Both are competitive systems (Schumpeter: democracy is competition for votes like firms compete for customers).

**Arguments that they conflict**
1. Capitalism produces **inequality**; democracy demands **equality** of citizens. Concentrated wealth buys political influence (US *Citizens United* ruling, 2010, allowed unlimited independent corporate political spending).
2. **Thomas Piketty**: returns on capital exceed growth, so wealth concentrates and threatens democracy.
3. **Karl Polanyi**, *The Great Transformation* (1944): unregulated markets provoke social backlash (fascism in the 1930s).
4. **Colin Crouch**, *Post-Democracy* (2004): elections continue, but corporate elites decide.
5. **Capitalism without democracy** works: China, Singapore, Gulf states, Pinochet's Chile, South Korea before 1987.
6. **Democracy without full capitalism**: social democracies with large public sectors (Nordic states).
7. Marxists: "bourgeois democracy" is a façade for class rule.

**Balanced conclusion**: they have historically reinforced each other in the West, but the link is conditional. Democracy tames capitalism (welfare state, regulation), and capitalism funds democracy (growth). When inequality rises too far, populism follows (Trump, Brexit). For Pakistan: crony capitalism and elite capture (elite privilege about 6% of GDP, UNDP 2021 [verify]) weaken both markets and democracy.

## 2. SOCIALISM

### 2.1 Meaning
An ideology that favours **social (collective or state) ownership** or control of major means of production, **equality**, cooperation over competition, and distribution according to need or work.

### 2.2 Types
| Type | Ideas | Thinkers |
|---|---|---|
| **Utopian** | Ideal cooperative communities | Saint-Simon, Charles Fourier, Robert Owen (New Lanark, New Harmony) |
| **Scientific** (Marxism) | Socialism as the outcome of class struggle | Marx, Engels |
| **Fabian** (evolutionary) | Gradual reform through parliament; "inevitability of gradualness" | Fabian Society (1884): Sidney and Beatrice Webb, G.B. Shaw; shaped the British Labour Party |
| **Democratic / social democracy** | Mixed economy, welfare state, within liberal democracy | Bernstein; Scandinavian social democrats |
| **Guild socialism** | Workers' self-governing guilds | G.D.H. Cole |
| **Syndicalism** | Trade unions take over industry by general strike | Georges Sorel |
| **Market socialism** | Social ownership with markets | Yugoslavia (self-management) |
| **Islamic socialism** | Socialism justified through Islamic justice | Nasser, Gaddafi; **Z.A. Bhutto's PPP** (1967 slogan: "Islam is our faith, democracy is our polity, socialism is our economy; all power to the people") |
| **African socialism** | Communal traditions | Julius Nyerere (*ujamaa*) |

### 2.3 Merits and demerits
**Merits**: equality, welfare, security, ends exploitation, planning for needs.
**Demerits**: inefficiency, bureaucracy, weak incentives, threats to liberty (Hayek), failures of central planning (Soviet shortages); Pakistan's nationalisation (1972–74) of industries, banks and insurance damaged private investment.

## 3. MARXISM AND COMMUNISM

- **Marxism**: the theory (historical materialism, class struggle, surplus value, revolution) — full notes in `04-Marxist-Thought.md`.
- **Communism**: (1) the final stage of society in Marx: classless, stateless, "from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs"; (2) the movement and regimes led by communist parties (USSR 1917–91; China; Cuba; Vietnam; North Korea; Laos).
- **Marxism-Leninism**: vanguard party, democratic centralism, dictatorship of the proletariat, planned economy.
- **Differences between socialism and communism**: socialism (lower stage) allows a state and distribution by work; communism abolishes state and classes and distributes by need. Socialism can be democratic and gradual; communism (in practice) meant one-party revolutionary rule.
- **Collapse** of the Soviet bloc (1989–91) and market reforms in China (from 1978) led to claims of the "end of history".

## 4. TOTALITARIANISM

See `13-Forms-of-Government.md` §4 (Arendt; Friedrich and Brzezinski's six features; comparison with authoritarianism).

**As an ideology**: the claim that the state (or party, or race) should control and remake all aspects of life in pursuit of an ultimate goal. Both fascism and Stalinist communism were totalitarian, though from opposite ideologies (right and left).

**Note format (2016)**: definition → origin of term (Gentile, Mussolini) → features (six-point syndrome) → examples (Nazi Germany, Stalin's USSR, Mao's China, North Korea) → causes (WWI, mass society, economic crisis, ideology, technology) → critique (Arendt) → relevance today (digital surveillance).

## 5. FASCISM (2017, 2019, 2020)

### 5.1 Origin
- From Italian *fascio* (bundle, group), recalling the Roman *fasces* (bundle of rods with an axe, symbol of authority).
- **Benito Mussolini** founded the *Fasci di Combattimento* in Milan (March 1919); "**March on Rome**" (October 1922) led the king to make him prime minister; dictatorship from 1925. *The Doctrine of Fascism* (1932, partly written by philosopher Giovanni **Gentile**).
- **Nazism** (National Socialism) in Germany: Adolf **Hitler** became Chancellor on **30 January 1933**; the Enabling Act (March 1933) gave him dictatorial power. *Mein Kampf* (1925).
- Other forms: Franco's Spain (Falange), Salazar's Portugal (Estado Novo), Japan's militarism (debated).

### 5.2 Basic principles (2020)
1. **Ultra-nationalism**: the nation (or race) is supreme; national rebirth (palingenesis, Roger Griffin).
2. **Totalitarian state**: "everything within the state, nothing outside the state, nothing against the state" (Mussolini).
3. **Leader principle** (*Führerprinzip*; *Il Duce*): an infallible, charismatic leader embodies the national will.
4. **Anti-liberalism**: rejects individualism, rights, parliament, pluralism.
5. **Anti-communism** and anti-Marxism; rejects class struggle in favour of national unity.
6. **Anti-democracy** and elitism: rule by the strong; the masses must be led.
7. **Corporatism**: economy organised into corporations of employers and workers under the state (Italy's Charter of Labour, 1927); private property retained but subordinated.
8. **Militarism and glorification of war**: "war is to man what maternity is to woman" (Mussolini, paraphrase); expansion (*Lebensraum*, living space; Italy's new Roman empire, Ethiopia 1935).
9. **Racism and anti-Semitism** (central in Nazism; Nuremberg Laws 1935; the Holocaust killed about six million Jews).
10. **Irrationalism**: action, myth, emotion, will over reason; mass rallies and propaganda.
11. **Social Darwinism**: struggle and survival of the fittest among nations and races.
12. **Cult of youth, violence and the "new man"**.

### 5.3 Why fascism was a "child of the first half of the 20th century" (2017) / enabling conditions (2019)
**Common conditions**
1. **World War I**: trauma, brutalisation, millions of demobilised soldiers used to violence.
2. **Humiliation and resentment**:
   - Germany: **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**: war-guilt clause (Art. 231), reparations, loss of territory, army limited to 100,000; the "stab in the back" myth.
   - Italy: the "**mutilated victory**": promised territories (Fiume, Dalmatia) not received despite being on the winning side.
3. **Economic crisis**: Italy's post-war unemployment and inflation; Germany's **hyperinflation (1923)** and the **Great Depression (1929)** (German unemployment about six million by 1932).
4. **Fear of communism** after the Russian Revolution (1917): Italy's "Red Biennium" (1919–20) factory occupations; strong German communist party. Elites and middle classes turned to fascists as protectors of property.
5. **Weak democratic institutions**: new or fragile democracies; Weimar's proportional representation produced unstable coalitions; **Article 48** emergency powers; Italian liberal governments unstable.
6. **Strong nationalism and weak democracy** (the 2019 question's premise): nationalist myths without democratic traditions.
7. **Mass society and new media**: radio, cinema, mass rallies for propaganda.
8. **Charismatic leaders** and paramilitary organisations (Blackshirts, SA Brownshirts).
9. **Support or tolerance of traditional elites**: King Victor Emmanuel III invited Mussolini; President Hindenburg and conservatives appointed Hitler, believing they could control him.
10. **Middle-class anxiety** (small shopkeepers, peasants, clerks squeezed between big capital and labour).

### 5.4 Germany and Italy compared
| | Italy | Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | Mussolini (PM 1922) | Hitler (Chancellor 1933) |
| Key grievance | Mutilated victory | Versailles, reparations |
| Economic trigger | Post-war crisis, Red Biennium | Hyperinflation 1923, Depression 1929 |
| Route to power | March on Rome; king's invitation | Elections (NSDAP largest party July 1932, 37%); Hindenburg's appointment |
| Racism | Less central until 1938 racial laws | Core ideology; Holocaust |
| Totalitarian reach | Partial (Church, monarchy retained) | Near-total |

### 5.5 Critique and end
Defeat in World War II (1945); genocide, war crimes (Nuremberg trials 1945–46); economic irrationality; suppression of freedom.

### 5.6 Relevance
"Neo-fascism" and far-right populism (Golden Dawn in Greece; debates on AfD in Germany, which won the Saxony-Anhalt state election in September 2026); ultra-nationalism and majoritarianism (critics apply the term to Hindutva in India, citing the RSS's 1930s admiration of European fascism) [frame carefully as a contested label]; Umberto Eco's "Ur-Fascism" (1995) list of 14 features.

## 6. NATIONALISM (2024 note)

### 6.1 Meaning
The belief that the **nation** is the central principle of political organisation; that each nation should have its own state (self-determination); and loyalty to the nation above other loyalties.

### 6.2 Theories of the nation
| School | View | Thinkers |
|---|---|---|
| **Primordialist** | Nations are ancient, natural, based on kinship, language, religion | Clifford Geertz (cultural ties) |
| **Modernist** | Nations are products of modernity: industrialisation, print, state-building | **Ernest Gellner** (*Nations and Nationalism*, 1983: industrial society needs a common culture; "nationalism invents nations"); **Benedict Anderson** (*Imagined Communities*, 1983: print capitalism lets strangers imagine a community); **Eric Hobsbawm** ("invented traditions") |
| **Ethno-symbolist** | Modern nations build on pre-modern ethnic cores, myths and symbols | **Anthony D. Smith** |
| Constructivist | Nations are socially constructed and contested | — |

Ernest **Renan**, "What is a Nation?" (1882): a nation is a soul, a spiritual principle, "a daily plebiscite".

### 6.3 Types of nationalism
- **Civic** (based on citizenship and shared political values: France, USA) vs **ethnic** (based on descent, language, culture: 19th-century Germany).
- **Liberal** (Mazzini: self-determination for all nations, compatible with peace).
- **Conservative** (tradition, order, national identity).
- **Expansionist / chauvinist** (imperialism, fascism).
- **Anti-colonial / liberation** (India, Pakistan, Algeria, Vietnam).
- **Religious nationalism** (Muslim nationalism in India; Zionism; Hindutva).
- **Economic nationalism** (tariffs; "America First").

### 6.4 History in brief
American and French revolutions (18th c.) → Napoleonic wars → 1848 → unifications of Italy (1861) and Germany (1871) → collapse of empires after WWI and Wilson's **Fourteen Points** (1918) → anti-colonial movements and decolonisation after 1945 → break-up of the USSR and Yugoslavia (1991) → today's populist nationalism.

### 6.5 Merits and demerits
**Merits**: unity and solidarity; source of legitimacy; liberation from foreign rule; democracy and self-government; motivates sacrifice for common goods.
**Demerits**: exclusion of minorities; aggression and war (WWI, WWII); chauvinism and racism; secessionist conflicts; divides humanity (Iqbal's critique of territorial nationalism; Tagore's critique).

### 6.6 Muslim nationalism and Pakistan
Two-nation theory: Muslims of India as a separate nation by religion, culture, history and law (Sir Syed, Iqbal, Jinnah's Lahore address, 22 March 1940). Pakistani nationalism today combines Islam, the Pakistan Movement and anti-India sentiment; challenged by ethnic sub-nationalisms (Bengali in 1971; Baloch, Sindhi, Pashtun). See Paper II files 11 and 14.

## 7. ISLAMIC POLITICAL IDEOLOGY (2022 note)

### 7.1 Sources
Quran, Sunnah, consensus (ijma), analogy (qiyas), ijtihad; the practice of the Madinah state and the Rightly Guided Caliphs.

### 7.2 Principles
1. **Tawhid**: oneness of God; sovereignty belongs to Allah.
2. **Risalat**: the Prophet's guidance as the model.
3. **Khilafat**: humans as God's vicegerents; authority as a trust (*amanah*).
4. **Shura**: consultation in collective affairs (42:38; 3:159).
5. **'Adl**: justice—legal, social and economic.
6. **Equality** and brotherhood (49:10, 49:13).
7. **Rule of law**: rulers under the shari'a.
8. **Accountability** of rulers to God and the people.
9. **Protection of rights**: life, property, honour, faith, intellect, lineage (*maqasid*).
10. **Welfare state**: zakat, *bayt al-mal*, care of the needy.
11. **Rights of non-Muslims** (Charter of Madinah; *dhimma*).
12. **Amr bil ma'ruf**: promote good, forbid evil.
13. **Peace and just war**: treaties, jihad under strict rules.

### 7.3 Islamic economic principles
Prohibition of **riba** (interest) and *gharar* (excessive uncertainty); zakat and *sadaqat*; inheritance law spreading wealth; condemnation of hoarding (*ihtikar*); legitimate private property with social obligations; profit-and-loss sharing (*mudaraba*, *musharaka*).

### 7.4 Islamic ideology compared with other ideologies

| Point | Islamic | Capitalism | Socialism/Communism | Fascism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sovereignty | God | People/market | Proletariat/party | Nation/leader |
| Property | Private with social duties | Private, unlimited | Collective/state | Private under state |
| Basis | Revelation and reason | Reason, self-interest | Materialism | Myth, will, nation |
| Individual | Dignified, responsible to God and community | Autonomous | Subordinate to class/collective | Subordinate to state |
| Community | Ummah (faith-based) | Market society | Class | Nation/race |
| Morality | Divine and fixed in principle | Relative, private | Class-based | Power and struggle |

### 7.5 Modern thinkers
- **Iqbal**: spiritual democracy; ijtihad by assembly (file 07).
- **Syed Abul A'la Maududi** (1903–79), founder of Jamaat-e-Islami (1941): *Islami Riyasat*, *Khilafat-o-Malukiyat* (on the decline from caliphate to kingship); "theo-democracy"; sovereignty of God; an ideological state run by those who accept its ideology; influential on the Objectives Resolution and on Zia-era Islamisation.
- **Sayyid Qutb** (1906–66), Muslim Brotherhood: *Milestones* (1964): modern societies (including Muslim ones) are in *jahiliyya*; *hakimiyya* of God; more radical.
- **Ali Shariati** and **Ayatollah Khomeini** (*velayat-e faqih*, 1970) in Iran.
- **Modernists**: Fazlur Rahman, Rashid Ghannouchi (Tunisia's Ennahda: Muslim democracy).

### 7.6 Critiques and debates
Who interprets? (ulama vs elected assembly); compatibility with democracy and human rights; sectarian divisions; militant misuse (ISIS's "caliphate", 2014–19); gap between ideal and the record of Muslim states.

### 7.7 Pakistan
Objectives Resolution (Art. 2A); Islamic provisions (Arts. 2, 31, 227–231, 203); Zia's Islamisation (Hudood Ordinances 1979, Federal Shariat Court 1980, zakat 1980); the Federal Shariat Court's riba judgment (2022) and the 2028 target.
