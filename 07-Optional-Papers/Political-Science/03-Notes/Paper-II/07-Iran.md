# 07. Political System of Iran

Asked: Guardian Council (note) 2018; leadership structures of Iran and Turkey and their effect on policy 2025. MCQs: Iran's parliament is unicameral (2025). **Major change in 2026**: Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed in US–Israeli strikes on 28 February 2026 and his son **Mojtaba Khamenei** was named Supreme Leader on **8 March 2026**. Facts as of **24 Nov 2026**; this is the fastest-moving country in the syllabus, so re-check in late 2027.

---

## 1. Background

- **Pahlavi monarchy**: Reza Shah (1925–41) and Mohammad Reza Shah (1941–79). The 1953 CIA–MI6-backed coup removed elected PM Mohammad Mosaddegh (who had nationalised oil). The Shah's "White Revolution" (1963), SAVAK repression, inequality and Westernisation fuelled opposition.
- **Islamic Revolution (1978–79)**: mass protests led by **Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini** (in exile since 1964); the Shah left on 16 January 1979; Khomeini returned on 1 February; the monarchy fell on 11 February 1979.
- **Referendum** (30–31 March 1979): Islamic Republic proclaimed (1 April 1979).
- **Hostage crisis** (1979–81); **Iran–Iraq war** (1980–88, about half a million or more dead).
- Khomeini's theory: **velayat-e faqih** (guardianship of the Islamic jurist), set out in lectures in Najaf (1970): in the absence of the Hidden Twelfth Imam, a just, qualified jurist should hold political authority.

---

## 2. The Constitution

- Adopted by **referendum in December 1979**; revised by referendum in **July 1989** (after Khomeini's death): abolished the post of **Prime Minister**, strengthened the presidency, removed the requirement that the Supreme Leader be a *marja'* (source of emulation, the highest clerical rank), and constitutionalised the **Expediency Council**.
- **177 articles**. A **hybrid** of theocratic and republican elements:
  - **Theocratic**: sovereignty belongs to God (Art. 2, 56); all laws must be based on Islamic criteria (Art. 4); **Twelver Ja'fari Shi'ism** is the state religion (Art. 12); velayat-e faqih (Art. 5, 107–112).
  - **Republican**: elected President, parliament, Assembly of Experts, local councils; popular sovereignty exercised "within God's sovereignty" (Art. 56); separation of powers (Art. 57) under the Leader's supervision.
- Recognised minorities: Zoroastrians, Jews and Christians (Art. 13), with reserved parliamentary seats.

---

## 3. The Supreme Leader (Rahbar)

### 3.1 Selection
Chosen and supervised (in theory, removable) by the **Assembly of Experts** (Art. 107, 111).

### 3.2 Powers (Art. 110)
- Determines the **general policies** of the system and supervises their execution.
- **Commander-in-chief** of all armed forces (regular army and IRGC); declares war and peace; mobilises forces.
- Appoints and dismisses: the **six clerical members of the Guardian Council**; the **head of the judiciary**; the head of state radio and television (IRIB); the chief of the joint staff; the commander of the **IRGC**; other military commanders.
- Resolves disputes between the three branches; resolves problems through the **Expediency Council**.
- Signs the decree formalising the elected President; can **dismiss the President** after a Supreme Court ruling or a parliamentary vote of incompetence (Banisadr was dismissed in 1981).
- Calls referendums; pardons.
- Controls vast economic foundations (*bonyads*) and the office (*beit*) of the Leader.

### 3.3 Holders
| Leader | Years |
|---|---|
| **Ruhollah Khomeini** | 1979–89 |
| **Ali Khamenei** | 1989–**28 February 2026** (killed in US–Israeli strikes) |
| **Mojtaba Khamenei** | Named by the Assembly of Experts on **8 March 2026** |

Mojtaba Khamenei (born 1969), a mid-ranking cleric with close ties to the IRGC, had long been discussed as a possible successor; critics call the succession quasi-hereditary, which the revolution's ideology rejected. By **August 2026** reports said he had **not appeared in public** since taking office and President Pezeshkian said communication with him was "very difficult" (The National, citing Reuters).

---

## 4. The Assembly of Experts (Majlis-e Khobregan)

- **88 clerics** directly elected for **eight years** (last elections March 2024); candidates vetted by the Guardian Council.
- Elects the Supreme Leader, can supervise and dismiss him; in practice deferential. Its choice of Mojtaba Khamenei in March 2026 was its second succession decision ever.

---

## 5. The President

- Directly elected for **four years**; at most **two consecutive** terms; two-round system.
- **Second-highest official**; head of the executive; implements the constitution; chairs the cabinet and the Supreme National Security Council; signs treaties after parliamentary approval; prepares the budget.
- Ministers need a **vote of confidence** from the Majlis; the Majlis can impeach ministers and question the President.
- Limited: no control over the armed forces, judiciary, state media or foreign and nuclear strategy without the Leader's approval.
- Presidents: Banisadr (1980–81), Rajai (1981), Khamenei (1981–89), Rafsanjani (1989–97), Khatami (1997–2005, reformist), Ahmadinejad (2005–13), Rouhani (2013–21, moderate; JCPOA), Raisi (2021–24; killed in a helicopter crash on 19 May 2024), **Masoud Pezeshkian** (reformist-leaning; elected July 2024, run-off against Saeed Jalili; turnout about 50%).

---

## 6. The Legislature: Majlis (Islamic Consultative Assembly)

- **Unicameral** (CSS 2025 MCQ); **290 members**; four-year term; direct elections (multi-member and single-member districts, two rounds).
- **Five reserved seats** for religious minorities: Zoroastrians 1, Jews 1, Assyrian and Chaldean Christians 1, Armenian Christians 2.
- **Powers**: legislation; budget; approve treaties; vote of confidence in and impeachment of ministers; investigate any matter.
- **Limits**: every bill goes to the **Guardian Council**; candidates are vetted (thousands disqualified in 2020 and 2024).
- **2024 elections** (March and May): conservatives (principlists) dominate; turnout about **41%**, the lowest since 1979. Speaker: **Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf**.

---

## 7. The Guardian Council (Shura-ye Negahban) (2018 note)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Composition | **12 members**: **6 Islamic jurists (faqihs)** appointed by the **Supreme Leader**; **6 lawyers** nominated by the **head of the judiciary** and approved by the **Majlis** |
| Term | Six years; half renewed every three years |
| Powers | (1) **Reviews all legislation**: the six faqihs decide compatibility with Islam (by majority of faqihs), all twelve decide compatibility with the constitution; a bill rejected goes back to the Majlis (Art. 94–96). (2) **Interprets the constitution** (three-quarters majority). (3) **Supervises elections** (Art. 99) and **vets candidates** for the presidency, Majlis and Assembly of Experts ("approbatory supervision"). |
| Chair | Ayatollah **Ahmad Jannati** (since 1992; born 1927) [verify still in post] |
| Significance | The key "filter" of the system: keeps elections within the regime's limits (e.g. disqualified former President Ahmadinejad and ex-speaker Ali Larijani in 2021 and 2024); blocks reformist legislation |
| Disputes | When the Majlis insists on a bill the Council rejects, the **Expediency Council** decides |

---

## 8. Other Institutions

- **Expediency Discernment Council** (created by Khomeini in 1988; constitutional since 1989): members appointed by the Leader; arbitrates between Majlis and Guardian Council; advises the Leader on general policies. Chair: Sadegh Amoli Larijani [verify].
- **Judiciary**: head appointed by the Leader for five years (**Gholam-Hossein Mohseni-Ejei** since 2021); Supreme Court; **Revolutionary Courts** (security, drugs, political cases); **Special Clerical Court** (answerable to the Leader). Criticised for executions (Iran had among the highest numbers of executions in the world in 2024–25) and political trials.
- **Supreme National Security Council**: chaired by the President; includes military chiefs and the Leader's representatives; decisions need the Leader's approval. Secretary: **Ali Larijani** (from August 2025) [verify].
- **Armed forces**: the regular army (**Artesh**) and the **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps** (IRGC, created 1979): ground, air, navy, aerospace and the **Quds Force** (external operations); the **Basij** militia. The IRGC is also a major economic and political actor.
- **Local councils** (elected since 1999).

## 9. Leadership Structures of Iran and Turkey and Policy Formulation (2025)

| Point | Iran | Turkey |
|---|---|---|
| System | Theocratic republic (hybrid) | Presidential republic (since 2018) |
| Top authority | **Supreme Leader** (unelected by people directly; chosen by the Assembly of Experts; life tenure) | **President** directly elected for five years |
| Legitimacy | Religious (velayat-e faqih) + electoral | Electoral + nationalist/Islamic-conservative |
| Head of government | President (elected) but subordinate to the Leader | President (also head of state) |
| Dual structure | Elected institutions (President, Majlis) **and** appointed clerical-military bodies (Leader, Guardian Council, judiciary, IRGC) | Single, centralised executive |
| Filters on elections | Guardian Council vets all candidates | Elections open but on an uneven field; opposition leaders prosecuted |
| Military | IRGC loyal to the Leader; powerful in economy and foreign policy | Army brought under civilian (presidential) control after 2016 |
| Legislature | Majlis (290), checked by Guardian Council | Grand National Assembly (600), weakened after 2018 |
| Judiciary | Head appointed by the Leader | Senior judges largely appointed by the President |
| **Policy formulation** | **Multiple veto players and factions** (principlists, reformists, IRGC, clergy); strategic decisions (nuclear, regional "axis of resistance", relations with the US) made by the Leader and the SNSC; Presidents shape tactics (Rouhani's JCPOA; Pezeshkian's outreach) | **Highly centralised and personalised**: Erdoğan and a small circle decide; quick, flexible policy shifts (e.g. reconciliation with Gulf states and Israel in 2021–22, then rupture over Gaza; Syria after Assad's fall in December 2024) |
| Effect on policy | Continuity and ideological consistency; slow, contested change; elected officials can be overruled | Speed and flexibility but unpredictability; policy tied to one leader |
| Accountability | Leader not accountable to voters | President accountable at elections every five years |
| Succession | Tested in 2026 under war; produced a hereditary-looking choice | Erdoğan's term ends 2028; succession unclear |

**Similarities**: both are Muslim-majority states with strong executives; both mix religion and nationalism in legitimacy; both are regional powers with ambitions in the Middle East and Central Asia; both limit pluralism (Iran far more than Turkey).

**Conclusion**: Iran's structure produces policy through a contested, layered process anchored by an unelected Leader; Turkey's produces policy through an elected but highly personalised presidency. Iran is ideological continuity with slow change; Turkey is pragmatic flexibility with weak checks.

## 10. Political Factions

Iran has weak formal parties and loose **factions**:
- **Principlists** (*osulgarayan*, conservatives): loyal to the Leader; strong in the Majlis, judiciary, IRGC.
- **Reformists** (*eslahtalaban*): Khatami, Pezeshkian; greater social freedom, engagement with the West; many candidates disqualified.
- **Moderates / pragmatists**: Rafsanjani, Rouhani.
- Hardline groups (e.g. Paydari Front).
