# 09. Political System of India

India appears mainly in comparisons; prepare it as a full system too, since a stand-alone question (federalism, secularism, Modi era) is overdue. Facts as of **24 Nov 2026**.

---

## 1. Background

- British rule (East India Company from 1757; Crown from 1858); Government of India Acts **1919** (dyarchy) and **1935** (federal scheme, provincial autonomy): the 1935 Act supplied much of the later constitution's text.
- **Indian National Congress** (1885); independence and **Partition** on **15 August 1947**.
- **Constituent Assembly** (December 1946–November 1949); **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee; Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution (December 1946).

---

## 2. The Constitution

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **26 November 1949** (Constitution Day); in force **26 January 1950** (Republic Day) |
| Length | The **longest written national constitution**: originally 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules; now about 470 articles and **12 schedules** |
| Preamble | "Sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic" ("socialist" and "secular" added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, during the Emergency); justice, liberty, equality, fraternity |
| Sources | 1935 Act (federal structure, emergency powers), UK (parliamentary system), US (fundamental rights, judicial review), Ireland (Directive Principles), Canada (strong centre), Australia (concurrent list), Weimar (emergency) |
| Fundamental Rights | Part III (Arts. 12–35): equality, freedoms, against exploitation, religion, cultural and educational rights, **constitutional remedies (Art. 32**, which Ambedkar called "the heart and soul" of the constitution) |
| Directive Principles | Part IV: non-justiciable social and economic goals |
| Fundamental Duties | Part IV-A (added 1976) |
| Amendment (Art. 368) | Mostly by a special majority of Parliament (two-thirds present and voting, majority of membership); federal provisions also need ratification by half the states. **106 amendments** by 2023 (106th: one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, to take effect after the next census and delimitation) |
| **Basic structure doctrine** | *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973): Parliament can amend but not destroy the basic structure (democracy, secularism, federalism, judicial review, rule of law…) |

**Major amendments**: 1st (1951, land reform, Ninth Schedule); 42nd (1976, "mini-constitution" of the Emergency); 44th (1978, restored liberties, made "armed rebellion" the ground for a national emergency); 52nd (1985, anti-defection law, Tenth Schedule); **73rd and 74th** (1992, panchayats and municipalities); 101st (2016, Goods and Services Tax); 103rd (2019, 10% reservation for economically weaker sections); 104th (2020, ended Anglo-Indian nominated seats).

## 3. Federalism: a "quasi-federation"

- Art. 1: India is a "**Union of States**" (indestructible union of destructible states: Parliament can create or reorganise states by simple law, Art. 3).
- **28 states and 8 union territories** (after Jammu and Kashmir became two union territories in 2019).
- **Seventh Schedule**: Union List, State List, Concurrent List; residuary powers with the Union.
- **Unitary features** (K.C. Wheare called it "quasi-federal"): strong centre; single constitution and citizenship; integrated judiciary; all-India services (IAS, IPS); governors appointed by the centre; **President's Rule** (Art. 356; used over 100 times, limited by *S.R. Bommai*, 1994); emergency provisions; centre's control of major taxes; Planning Commission (replaced by NITI Aayog, 2015).
- **Cooperative federalism**: GST Council (2017); Finance Commission (16th, report 2025 [verify]); Inter-State Council.
- **Article 370**: special status of Jammu and Kashmir **abrogated on 5 August 2019**; the state divided into two union territories (J&K and Ladakh); upheld by the Supreme Court in **December 2023**; J&K assembly elections held in 2024 (National Conference won). Pakistan rejects the move as a violation of UN resolutions.
- Tensions: opposition-ruled states vs governors (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Punjab, West Bengal); language (Hindi imposition debates); delimitation fears in southern states; financial devolution.

## 4. The Executive

### 4.1 President
- **Nominal head**; executive power vested in him (Art. 53) but exercised on the **advice of the Council of Ministers** (Art. 74; binding since the 42nd Amendment; can return advice once, 44th Amendment).
- **Election**: indirect, by an **electoral college** of elected members of both houses of Parliament and of state legislative assemblies, by **single transferable vote**, with weighted votes.
- Five-year term; re-eligible.
- Powers: appoints PM, ministers, judges, governors, CAG, Election Commissioners; summons and prorogues Parliament, dissolves the Lok Sabha; assents to bills (suspensive veto; **pocket veto** in practice); **ordinances** (Art. 123); pardons (Art. 72); supreme commander; **emergency powers**: national (Art. 352; used in 1962, 1971 and the internal Emergency of **1975–77**), state (Art. 356), financial (Art. 360, never used).
- Impeachment by Parliament for "violation of the Constitution" (two-thirds of each house; never used).
- **Current: Droupadi Murmu** (since July 2022; first tribal woman President).
- **Vice President**: ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; **C.P. Radhakrishnan** (elected September 2025 after Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned in July 2025).

### 4.2 Prime Minister and Council of Ministers
- Real executive; PM appointed by the President as leader of the Lok Sabha majority; collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha (Art. 75).
- PMs: Nehru (1947–64), Shastri, Indira Gandhi (1966–77, 1980–84), Morarji Desai, Rajiv Gandhi, V.P. Singh, Narasimha Rao (1991 reforms), Vajpayee (1998–2004), Manmohan Singh (2004–14), **Narendra Modi** (since **26 May 2014**; third term from **9 June 2024**).

## 5. The Legislature: Parliament (Sansad)

| House | Composition | Term | Powers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) | **543** elected by **FPTP** in single-member constituencies (seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Tribes) | Five years (dissolvable) | Money bills (Art. 110) only here; no-confidence votes; dominant in joint sittings |
| **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) | Up to **250**: 238 elected by state assemblies (STV), 12 nominated for arts, science, literature, social service | Permanent; one-third retire every two years; six-year terms | Can delay money bills only **14 days**; equal on constitutional amendments; special powers under Arts. 249 and 312 |

- **Anti-defection law** (Tenth Schedule, 1985): members who defy party whips or switch parties can be disqualified by the Speaker.
- **Electoral bonds** scheme (2018) struck down by the Supreme Court in **February 2024** as unconstitutional.

## 6. The Judiciary

- **Integrated judiciary**: Supreme Court (34 judges), 25 High Courts, subordinate courts.
- **Judicial review**: Arts. 13, 32, 226; basic structure doctrine; **Public Interest Litigation** (PIL, from the late 1970s: Justices Bhagwati and Krishna Iyer) made the Indian Supreme Court one of the most powerful courts in the world.
- **Appointments**: the **collegium system** (the CJI and senior judges recommend; from the Second Judges Case 1993 and Third 1998); the **National Judicial Appointments Commission** (99th Amendment, 2014) was struck down in **2015**.
- **Chief Justice of India: Surya Kant** (53rd CJI; sworn in **24 November 2025**; retires **9 February 2027**).
- Landmark cases: *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973); *ADM Jabalpur* (1976, Emergency, later regretted); *Minerva Mills* (1980); *Shah Bano* (1985); *Indra Sawhney* (1992, 50% reservation cap); *S.R. Bommai* (1994); *Vishaka* (1997, workplace harassment); *Puttaswamy* (2017, privacy); *Navtej Johar* (2018, decriminalised homosexuality); Ayodhya (2019); Article 370 (2023); electoral bonds (2024).
- **Election Commission of India** (Art. 324): conducts elections for Parliament, state legislatures, President and Vice President; the 2023 law changed the appointment committee (removing the CJI), criticised by the opposition; controversy over the **Special Intensive Revision** of voter rolls (Bihar 2025, then nationwide) [verify].

## 7. Parties and Elections

### 7.1 Party system evolution
| Phase | Character |
|---|---|
| 1952–67 | "**Congress system**" (Rajni Kothari): one-party dominance |
| 1967–89 | Congress weakened in states; Janata government (1977–79) after the Emergency |
| 1989–2014 | **Coalition era**: regional parties as kingmakers (NDA under Vajpayee; UPA under Manmohan Singh) |
| 2014– | **BJP dominance** under Modi (majorities in 2014: 282; 2019: 303) |
| 2024– | BJP short of a majority (240) but leads the **NDA coalition** (293), relying on the TDP and JD(U) |

### 7.2 Main parties
- **Bharatiya Janata Party** (BJP, 1980; successor of the Jana Sangh, 1951; ideological parent the **RSS**, 1925): Hindu nationalist (**Hindutva**), pro-business, centralising.
- **Indian National Congress** (1885): centre-left, secular; Nehru–Gandhi family; leader of opposition in the Lok Sabha: **Rahul Gandhi** (since 2024).
- Regional parties: Trinamool Congress (West Bengal), DMK and AIADMK (Tamil Nadu), **TVK** (actor Vijay's party, Tamil Nadu, 2024), Samajwadi Party, BSP (Uttar Pradesh), Shiv Sena factions, NCP factions, TDP (Andhra Pradesh), JD(U) (Bihar), AAP (Delhi, Punjab), Left parties (CPI(M)).
- **INDIA bloc** (2023): opposition alliance (234 seats in 2024).

### 7.3 Elections
- **2024 general election** (April–June; about 640 million votes, the world's largest election): BJP **240**, Congress **99**; NDA 293; INDIA 234.
- **Bihar** (November 2025): NDA won a large majority.
- **May 2026 state elections**: **BJP won West Bengal** for the first time, ending Mamata Banerjee's 15-year rule (she lost her own seat); **TVK** became the largest party in **Tamil Nadu** (Vijay to become Chief Minister); Congress-led **UDF** won **Kerala** (ending Left rule in every state); BJP kept **Assam**; NDA kept Puducherry. Seat totals vary across reports; check the Election Commission's figures.

## 8. Secularism and Democracy: Debates

- **Indian secularism**: equal respect for all religions (*sarva dharma sambhava*), not strict separation; personal laws by religion; state support to religious institutions.
- **Concerns** raised by critics (Freedom House rates India "Partly Free" since 2021; V-Dem calls it an "electoral autocracy" since 2018): the Citizenship (Amendment) Act 2019 (rules notified March 2024), proposals for a National Register of Citizens, communal violence (Delhi 2020), bulldozer demolitions, sedition and UAPA cases, press freedom, the **Waqf (Amendment) Act 2025**, the Ram temple consecration at Ayodhya (January 2024) by the PM.
- **Government's view**: development for all ("sabka saath, sabka vikas"); correcting minority appeasement; strong national security.
- **Strengths of Indian democracy**: regular, competitive elections with alternation at state and national levels (BJP lost its majority in 2024); independent Election Commission (contested); vibrant federal politics; active judiciary; civilian control of the military since 1947 (contrast with Pakistan).
