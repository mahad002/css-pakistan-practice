# 10. Political System of China

Asked: China and Malaysia political economy 2017, 2021 (see `08-Malaysia.md` §8.2); powers and functions of the National People's Congress 2020; characteristics of China's system vs the USSR's 2022. MCQs: democratic centralism (2025). Facts as of **24 Nov 2026**.

---

## 1. Background

- End of the Qing dynasty (1911 revolution; Republic of China 1912; Sun Yat-sen; Kuomintang).
- **Communist Party of China (CPC)** founded **July 1921** in Shanghai.
- Civil war, the **Long March** (1934–35), war against Japan (1937–45), communist victory: **People's Republic of China proclaimed by Mao Zedong on 1 October 1949**; the KMT retreated to Taiwan.
- Mao era: land reform, collectivisation, **Great Leap Forward** (1958–62; famine), **Cultural Revolution** (1966–76).
- **Deng Xiaoping** (paramount leader 1978–early 1990s): "**reform and opening up**" from December 1978; special economic zones (Shenzhen 1980); household responsibility system; "socialism with Chinese characteristics"; Tiananmen crackdown (4 June 1989); "**socialist market economy**" (1992–93).
- Jiang Zemin (1989–2002), Hu Jintao (2002–12), **Xi Jinping** (since November 2012).

---

## 2. The Constitution

- Four constitutions: **1954**, 1975, 1978, and the current **1982** constitution (amended 1988, 1993, 1999, 2004, **2018**).
- **Art. 1**: "a socialist state under the **people's democratic dictatorship** led by the working class and based on the alliance of workers and peasants". Since 2018: "the **leadership of the Communist Party of China** is the defining feature of socialism with Chinese characteristics".
- **Art. 3**: state organs apply the principle of **democratic centralism** (CSS 2025 MCQ).
- Preamble: guided by Marxism-Leninism, Mao Zedong Thought, Deng Xiaoping Theory, the "Three Represents" (Jiang), the Scientific Outlook on Development (Hu) and **Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era** (added 2018).
- **2018 amendment**: removed the **two-term limit** on the President and Vice President; created the **National Supervisory Commission** (anti-corruption agency covering all public officials).
- Rights are listed (Arts. 33–56) but are subordinate to state and party interests; no independent constitutional court.
- Formally a **unitary** state: 22 provinces (Beijing claims Taiwan as a 23rd), 5 autonomous regions (Tibet, Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia, Guangxi, Ningxia), 4 municipalities (Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin, Chongqing), 2 special administrative regions (**Hong Kong** 1997, **Macau** 1999: "one country, two systems").

---

## 3. The Party-State: the CPC above all

**The key point**: China's formal state institutions (NPC, State Council, courts) are led by the **Communist Party**. Party and state are parallel and interlocked: the top party leaders hold the top state posts. "**The Party leads everything**" (Xi, 2017).

### 3.1 Party structure
| Body | Composition and role |
|---|---|
| **National Party Congress** | About 2,300 delegates; meets every **five years** (20th Congress October 2022; **21st due in 2027**); elects the Central Committee; amends the party constitution |
| **Central Committee** | About 200 full members; meets in plenary sessions ("plenums") about once a year; the **Fourth Plenum (October 2025)** adopted proposals for the 15th Five-Year Plan |
| **Politburo** | About 24 members |
| **Politburo Standing Committee (PSC)** | **7 members**: the real top leadership: **Xi Jinping** (General Secretary), **Li Qiang** (Premier), **Zhao Leji** (NPC Chairman), **Wang Huning** (CPPCC Chairman), **Cai Qi**, **Ding Xuexiang**, **Li Xi** (discipline) |
| **General Secretary** | Xi Jinping since November 2012; third term from 2022 |
| **Central Military Commission** | Commands the **People's Liberation Army** (a party army); chaired by Xi |
| **Central Commission for Discipline Inspection** | Anti-corruption and party discipline; Xi's campaign has punished over a million officials since 2012, including senior generals (e.g. two defence ministers in 2023–24; CMC vice-chairman He Weidong expelled in October 2025 [verify]) |
| Members | About **100 million** (largest party in the world) [verify latest count] |

### 3.2 Democratic centralism
Lenin's principle (see `../Paper-I/04-Marxist-Thought.md`): discussion inside the party, then strict obedience to decisions; the individual obeys the organisation, the minority the majority, the lower level the higher, the whole party the Central Committee. Factions banned.

### 3.3 Nomenklatura
The party appoints and promotes all senior officials in the state, army, state enterprises, universities and media.

## 4. State Institutions

### 4.1 President (State Chairman)
**Xi Jinping** since March 2013; third term from March 2023 (possible after the 2018 amendment). Largely ceremonial in law; powerful because he is also General Secretary and CMC Chairman. Vice President: Han Zheng.

### 4.2 State Council (central government)
Headed by the **Premier**: **Li Qiang** (since March 2023). Vice premiers, state councillors, ministers. Runs the administration and economy; drafts the budget and five-year plans.

### 4.3 National People's Congress (NPC) (2020)

**Composition**
- The **"highest organ of state power"** (Art. 57); unicameral.
- About **2,900 deputies** (2,977 elected in 2023; the 2026 session had 99 fewer than in 2023 after removals), **indirectly elected** by provincial-level people's congresses and the armed forces for **five-year** terms. Only the lowest-level (county and township) congresses are directly elected.
- Meets **once a year for about a week in March** (the "**Two Sessions**", with the CPPCC).
- **Standing Committee** (about 170 members) meets every two months and exercises most powers between sessions; Chairman **Zhao Leji** (third-ranked leader).

**Powers and functions (Arts. 62–63)**
1. **Amend the constitution** (two-thirds majority) and supervise its enforcement.
2. **Enact and amend basic laws** (criminal, civil, state organs).
3. **Elect** the President and Vice President; **decide** on the Premier (nominated by the President) and, on the Premier's nomination, vice premiers and ministers; elect the Chairman of the Central Military Commission, the head of the National Supervisory Commission, the President of the **Supreme People's Court** and the Procurator-General.
4. **Remove** these officials.
5. **Examine and approve** the national economic and social development plan (e.g. the **15th Five-Year Plan, 2026–30, adopted 12 March 2026**, with a 2026 growth target of 4.5–5%) and the state budget.
6. Approve the establishment of provinces and SARs and their systems (e.g. the Hong Kong Basic Law; in 2020 it decided on the Hong Kong **National Security Law**; in 2021 it overhauled Hong Kong's electoral system).
7. Decide questions of **war and peace**.
8. Supervise the State Council, courts and procuratorate.
9. The Standing Committee **interprets the constitution and laws** (including the Hong Kong Basic Law).

**Role in practice**
- Often called a "**rubber stamp**": the CPC decides; NPC votes are near-unanimous (in 2018 the term-limit removal passed 2,958 to 2 with 3 abstentions).
- But not wholly empty: it gives legitimacy, consults experts, revises draft laws in committees, and its Standing Committee has become an active law-making body; occasional dissent votes on officials' reports.
- The 2026 session adopted the **National Development Planning Law**, codifying the five-year plan system.

### 4.4 Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC)
A "**united front**" advisory body: the CPC, eight small "democratic parties" (e.g. the Revolutionary Committee of the Kuomintang, China Democratic League), mass organisations, ethnic and religious representatives, Hong Kong and Macau delegates. Chairman: **Wang Huning**. China calls its system "**multi-party cooperation and political consultation under the leadership of the CPC**", not a one-party system; in reality the minor parties accept CPC leadership.

### 4.5 Judiciary
**Supreme People's Court**, local courts; **Supreme People's Procuratorate**. Courts are not independent: they answer to people's congresses and are guided by the party's **Political and Legal Affairs Commission**. "Rule by law" (law as a tool of the party) rather than "rule of law" (Xi's "law-based governance" framework, 2014).

## 5. Ideology and Legitimacy

- Official ideology: **socialism with Chinese characteristics**; "**whole-process people's democracy**" (Xi's term, 2019/2021): China's claim to a different kind of democracy based on consultation and outcomes.
- **Performance legitimacy**: growth (average about 9–10% a year from 1978 to 2010), poverty reduction (about 800 million people), infrastructure.
- **Nationalism**: "**national rejuvenation**" (the "Chinese Dream", 2012), the "century of humiliation", Taiwan reunification goal.
- **Control**: censorship (the "Great Firewall"), surveillance, repression in **Xinjiang** (Uyghur detention camps condemned by the UN human rights office in 2022) and **Tibet**, Hong Kong after 2020.

---

## 6. Main Characteristics of China's Political System (2022)

1. **Leading role of the CPC** (constitutionally entrenched).
2. **Democratic centralism**.
3. **Fusion of party and state**; nomenklatura.
4. **Unitary state** with ethnic autonomous regions and SARs.
5. **People's congress system** (NPC as highest organ) instead of separation of powers.
6. **Socialist market economy**: state-owned enterprises dominate strategic sectors alongside a large private sector.
7. **Party control of the military** (PLA is the party's army: "the Party commands the gun").
8. **Consultative "multi-party cooperation"** (CPPCC).
9. **Limited rights**; no independent judiciary or free press.
10. **Centralisation of power under Xi** since 2012: "core" leader (2016); term limits removed (2018); leading small groups chaired by Xi.

## 7. China vs the Soviet Union (2022)

| Point | People's Republic of China | USSR (1922–91) |
|---|---|---|
| Ideology | Marxism-Leninism + Mao Thought, Deng Theory, Xi Thought: pragmatic, "Chinese characteristics" | Marxism-Leninism: more doctrinaire |
| Party | CPC; democratic centralism | CPSU; democratic centralism |
| Economy | **Socialist market economy** since 1978–92: markets, private enterprise, foreign investment, global integration (WTO 2001) | **Command economy**: central planning (Gosplan), almost no private enterprise |
| Reform strategy | **Economic reform first, political control kept** ("crossing the river by feeling the stones") | Gorbachev's **glasnost** (openness) and **perestroika** (restructuring) loosened political control alongside economic change; the system collapsed |
| State structure | **Unitary**; autonomous regions without secession rights | **Federation** of 15 union republics with a constitutional right to secede (Art. 72, 1977 constitution), which was used in 1991 |
| Nationalities | Han about 91%; minorities controlled | Russians about half; many large nationalities with territorial republics |
| Leadership succession | After Deng: collective leadership, term limits and retirement norms (now weakened under Xi) | Gerontocracy and lifetime rule (Brezhnev); succession crises |
| Legislature | NPC (about 2,900) | Supreme Soviet (bicameral: Soviet of the Union and Soviet of Nationalities) |
| Foreign policy | Integration into the world economy; avoids alliances; Belt and Road | Cold War bloc leader; Warsaw Pact; arms race |
| Outcome | Survived and grew into the world's second-largest economy | Collapsed in December 1991 |

**Why China survived**: lessons drawn from the Soviet collapse (the CPC studies it closely), growth-based legitimacy, gradual reform, firm control of army and media, nationalism, and decentralised experimentation.
