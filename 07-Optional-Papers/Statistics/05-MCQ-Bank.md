## Descriptive Statistics

**Q1.** Statistics as a discipline is mainly concerned with
- A) single individuals
- B) isolated facts
- C) particular cases
- D) aggregates of facts
**Answer:** D. Statistics studies groups (aggregates), not individuals.

**Q2.** A five-point Likert scale (strongly disagree … strongly agree) measures a variable that is
- A) continuous
- B) ordinal
- C) ratio-scaled
- D) interval with a true zero
**Answer:** B. The categories are ordered but the gaps are not measured.

**Q3.** A numerical value that summarises a sample, such as the sample mean, is called a
- A) parameter
- B) constant
- C) statistic
- D) variate
**Answer:** C. A parameter describes a population.

**Q4.** If a constant c is added to every observation, which measure is unchanged?
- A) mean
- B) median
- C) mode
- D) standard deviation
**Answer:** D. Change of origin shifts averages but not spread.

**Q5.** The sum of absolute deviations Σ|x − a| is least when a is the
- A) mean
- B) median
- C) mode
- D) geometric mean
**Answer:** B. A basic property of the median.

**Q6.** The sum of squared deviations Σ(x − a)² is least when a is the
- A) mean
- B) median
- C) mode
- D) harmonic mean
**Answer:** A. Property of the arithmetic mean.

**Q7.** In a moderately skewed distribution the mean is 40 and the median 36. The mode is about
- A) 44
- B) 38
- C) 28
- D) 32
**Answer:** C. Mode ≈ 3(36) − 2(40) = 28.

**Q8.** The median of 52, 90, 88, 61, 75, 82, 75, 83, 88, 86 is
- A) 82
- B) 82.5
- C) 78.5
- D) 83
**Answer:** B. Sorted, the 5th and 6th values are 82 and 83.

**Q9.** The sample standard deviation (divisor n − 1) of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 is
- A) 1.414
- B) 2
- C) 2.5
- D) 1.581
**Answer:** D. s² = 10/4 = 2.5, s = 1.581.

**Q10.** For any set of positive numbers that are not all equal,
- A) AM > GM > HM
- B) HM > GM > AM
- C) GM > AM > HM
- D) AM = GM = HM
**Answer:** A. Equality holds only when all values are equal.

**Q11.** The correct average of speeds over equal distances is the
- A) arithmetic mean
- B) geometric mean
- C) harmonic mean
- D) median
**Answer:** C. 30 and 60 mph give 40 mph.

**Q12.** The coefficient of variation is
- A) σ/x̄²
- B) (σ/x̄) × 100%
- C) x̄/σ
- D) σ² × 100
**Answer:** B. A unit-free measure of relative spread.

**Q13.** In a positively skewed distribution
- A) mean < median < mode
- B) mean = median = mode
- C) mode > mean
- D) mean > median > mode
**Answer:** D. The long right tail pulls the mean up.

**Q14.** A distribution with β₂ = 3 is called
- A) mesokurtic
- B) leptokurtic
- C) platykurtic
- D) bimodal
**Answer:** A. Same kurtosis as the normal curve.

**Q15.** Bowley's coefficient of skewness always lies between
- A) 0 and 1
- B) −3 and 3
- C) −1 and 1
- D) 0 and ∞
**Answer:** C. |Q₃ + Q₁ − 2Md| ≤ Q₃ − Q₁.

**Q16.** The weighted mean of the first n natural numbers, with weights equal to the numbers themselves, is
- A) (n + 1)/2
- B) (2n + 1)/3
- C) n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6
- D) (2n + 1)/6
**Answer:** B. Σk²/Σk = [n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6]/[n(n + 1)/2].

## Basic Probability

**Q17.** The probability of a sure event is
- A) 0
- B) 0.5
- C) 1
- D) undefined
**Answer:** C. P(S) = 1.

**Q18.** For mutually exclusive events A and B, P(A ∪ B) equals
- A) P(A) + P(B)
- B) P(A)P(B)
- C) P(A) + P(B) − P(A)P(B)
- D) 1
**Answer:** A. P(A ∩ B) = 0.

**Q19.** A and B are independent with P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5. P(A ∩ B) is
- A) 0.9
- B) 0.1
- C) 0.45
- D) 0.2
**Answer:** D. 0.4 × 0.5.

**Q20.** The conditional probability P(A | B) equals
- A) P(A)/P(B)
- B) P(A ∩ B)/P(B)
- C) P(A ∩ B)/P(A)
- D) P(A)P(B)
**Answer:** B. Definition, with P(B) > 0.

**Q21.** Bayes' theorem is used to find
- A) prior probabilities
- B) the mean of a distribution
- C) posterior (revised) probabilities
- D) the number of permutations
**Answer:** C. It updates probabilities after evidence.

**Q22.** The number of ways of choosing 3 people from 10 is
- A) 720
- B) 120
- C) 30
- D) 1000
**Answer:** B. ¹⁰C₃ = 120.

**Q23.** The number of arrangements of 3 letters chosen from A, B, C, D is
- A) 4
- B) 12
- C) 64
- D) 24
**Answer:** D. ⁴P₃ = 24.

**Q24.** Two fair dice are thrown. P(sum = 7) is
- A) 1/6
- B) 1/12
- C) 7/36
- D) 1/9
**Answer:** A. Six favourable outcomes out of 36.

**Q25.** The complement of an event A has probability
- A) P(A)
- B) 1/P(A)
- C) 1 − P(A)
- D) 0
**Answer:** C. P(A) + P(Aᶜ) = 1.

**Q26.** E(aX + b) equals
- A) aE(X) + b
- B) a²E(X)
- C) aE(X)
- D) E(X) + b²
**Answer:** A. Linearity of expectation.

**Q27.** Var(aX + b) equals
- A) aVar(X) + b
- B) a²Var(X)
- C) a²Var(X) + b
- D) Var(X)
**Answer:** B. Adding b does not change spread.

**Q28.** By Chebyshev's inequality, the proportion of any distribution within two standard deviations of the mean is at least
- A) 50%
- B) 95%
- C) 68%
- D) 75%
**Answer:** D. 1 − 1/2² = 3/4.

**Q29.** A CDF is F(x) = 0 for x < 2, K(x − 2) for 2 ≤ x < 6, 1 for x ≥ 6. K equals
- A) 1/4
- B) 1/2
- C) 2
- D) 1/6
**Answer:** A. K(6 − 2) = 1.

**Q30.** X has CDF F(x) = 1 − e^{−2x}, x ≥ 0. The mean of X is
- A) 2
- B) −0.5
- C) 0.5
- D) 1
**Answer:** C. Exponential with rate 2 has mean 1/2.

## Probability Distributions

**Q31.** For a binomial distribution with n = 20 and p = 0.3, the mean and variance are
- A) 6 and 4.2
- B) 6 and 6
- C) 4.2 and 6
- D) 6 and 1.8
**Answer:** A. np = 6, npq = 4.2.

**Q32.** For a binomial distribution the variance is always
- A) greater than the mean
- B) equal to the mean
- C) zero
- D) less than the mean
**Answer:** D. npq < np since q < 1.

**Q33.** A distribution whose mean equals its variance is the
- A) binomial
- B) normal
- C) Poisson
- D) uniform
**Answer:** C. Both equal λ.

**Q34.** The Poisson distribution approximates the binomial when
- A) n is small and p is large
- B) n is large and p is small
- C) p = 0.5
- D) n = 1
**Answer:** B. With np = λ moderate.

**Q35.** For a Poisson variable with mean 2, P(X = 0) is about
- A) 0.1353
- B) 0.2707
- C) 0.5
- D) 0.0183
**Answer:** A. e^{−2}.

**Q36.** The hypergeometric distribution applies to
- A) sampling with replacement
- B) counts of rare events in time
- C) waiting times
- D) sampling without replacement from a finite population
**Answer:** D. Draws are dependent.

**Q37.** In a normal distribution
- A) mean > median > mode
- B) mean = median = mode
- C) mode > mean
- D) median is undefined
**Answer:** B. The curve is symmetric and unimodal.

**Q38.** The area under the normal curve within μ ± 1.96σ is
- A) 68%
- B) 90%
- C) 95%
- D) 99%
**Answer:** C. 2Φ(1.96) − 1 = 0.95.

**Q39.** The kurtosis coefficient β₂ of a normal distribution is
- A) 0
- B) 3
- C) 1
- D) 2
**Answer:** B. Mesokurtic.

**Q40.** If X ~ N(50, 100), P(X > 50) is
- A) 0.5
- B) 0.1
- C) 0.68
- D) 0.05
**Answer:** A. 50 is the mean; the curve is symmetric.

**Q41.** The standard normal variable Z has
- A) mean 1, variance 0
- B) mean 0, variance 0
- C) mean 1, variance 1
- D) mean 0, variance 1
**Answer:** D. Z = (X − μ)/σ.

**Q42.** The number of independent trials needed to obtain the first success follows the
- A) binomial distribution
- B) Poisson distribution
- C) geometric distribution
- D) normal distribution
**Answer:** C. P(X = k) = q^{k−1}p.

**Q43.** A binomial distribution is symmetric when
- A) p = 0.5
- B) n is large
- C) p < 0.5
- D) p = 1
**Answer:** A. Otherwise it is skewed.

**Q44.** A continuity correction is needed when
- A) using the t-table
- B) approximating a discrete distribution by a continuous one
- C) computing the mode
- D) sampling with replacement
**Answer:** B. E.g. P(X ≤ k) ≈ Φ((k + 0.5 − np)/√(npq)).

## Regression and Correlation

**Q45.** The least-squares slope of Y on X is
- A) S_xy/S_yy
- B) S_xx/S_xy
- C) S_xy/S_xx
- D) S_yy/S_xx
**Answer:** C. b = S_xy/S_xx.

**Q46.** The least-squares regression line always passes through
- A) the origin
- B) (x̄, ȳ)
- C) (0, ȳ)
- D) the first data point
**Answer:** B. From the first normal equation.

**Q47.** For a least-squares line with an intercept, the residuals sum to
- A) zero
- B) one
- C) n
- D) the SSE
**Answer:** A. Σeᵢ = 0.

**Q48.** The correlation coefficient r always lies between
- A) 0 and 1
- B) −∞ and ∞
- C) 0 and ∞
- D) −1 and 1
**Answer:** D. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

**Q49.** If b_yx = 0.8 and b_xy = 0.45, the correlation coefficient is
- A) 0.36
- B) 0.6
- C) 1.25
- D) 0.625
**Answer:** B. r = √(0.8 × 0.45) = 0.6 (positive, as both slopes are).

**Q50.** r² is called the
- A) coefficient of determination
- B) regression coefficient
- C) coefficient of variation
- D) standard error
**Answer:** A. Proportion of variation in Y explained by X.

**Q51.** The correlation coefficient is unchanged by
- A) squaring the data
- B) taking logarithms
- C) change of origin and (positive) change of scale
- D) adding an outlier
**Answer:** C. It is unit-free.

**Q52.** In the regression of Y on X, Y is the
- A) independent variable
- B) dependent (response) variable
- C) regressor
- D) instrument
**Answer:** B. X is the regressor (explanatory variable).

**Q53.** The degrees of freedom for testing the slope in simple linear regression with n pairs are
- A) n
- B) n − 1
- C) 2
- D) n − 2
**Answer:** D. Two parameters are estimated.

**Q54.** The partial correlation r₁₂.₃ measures the association between X₁ and X₂
- A) ignoring X₃
- B) with the linear effect of X₃ removed
- C) together with X₃
- D) only when X₃ = 0
**Answer:** B. Net association, X₃ held constant.

**Q55.** The multiple correlation coefficient R₁.₂₃ lies between
- A) −1 and 1
- B) 0 and 1
- C) 0 and ∞
- D) −1 and 0
**Answer:** B. It is the correlation of X₁ with its best linear prediction.

**Q56.** The significance of all partial regression coefficients together is tested by
- A) the F-test
- B) the z-test
- C) the χ² test
- D) the t-test
**Answer:** A. Overall regression F = MSR/MSE.

**Q57.** If r = 0 for two variables, then
- A) they are independent
- B) there is no linear relationship
- C) the regression slope is undefined
- D) one variable is constant
**Answer:** B. A strong curved relation can still exist.

**Q58.** Spearman's rank correlation coefficient is
- A) 1 − 6Σd²/[n(n² − 1)]
- B) Σd²/n
- C) 1 − Σd/n
- D) 6Σd²/(n² − 1)
**Answer:** A. d = difference in ranks.

## Non-Parametric Methods

**Q59.** Non-parametric tests are also called
- A) exact tests
- B) likelihood tests
- C) distribution-free tests
- D) large-sample tests
**Answer:** C. They need few assumptions about the population.

**Q60.** The sign test is based on the
- A) normal distribution with σ known
- B) binomial distribution with p = ½
- C) Poisson distribution
- D) F distribution
**Answer:** B. Under H₀, + and − signs are equally likely.

**Q61.** The Wilcoxon signed-rank test uses
- A) only the signs of the differences
- B) the ranks of the absolute differences, with their signs
- C) the original values
- D) runs of signs
**Answer:** B. More powerful than the sign test.

**Q62.** The Mann–Whitney U test compares
- A) two independent samples
- B) paired observations
- C) several related samples
- D) one sample with a median
**Answer:** A. Rank-sum test for two groups.

**Q63.** The Kruskal–Wallis test is the non-parametric alternative to
- A) the paired t-test
- B) the χ² test
- C) regression
- D) one-way ANOVA
**Answer:** D. It compares k independent groups using ranks.

**Q64.** The runs test is used to test
- A) normality
- B) equality of variances
- C) randomness of a sequence
- D) independence in a contingency table
**Answer:** C. Too few or too many runs suggest non-randomness.

**Q65.** The number of runs in the sequence A A B B B A B A A is
- A) 4
- B) 5
- C) 6
- D) 9
**Answer:** B. AA | BBB | A | B | AA.

**Q66.** The Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic is
- A) Σ(O − E)²/E
- B) the number of runs
- C) the sum of ranks
- D) the largest absolute difference between two distribution functions
**Answer:** D. D = max|Fₙ(x) − F₀(x)|.

**Q67.** The Friedman test is the non-parametric analogue of
- A) the randomised complete block (two-way) ANOVA
- B) the one-sample t-test
- C) simple regression
- D) the F-test for two variances
**Answer:** A. Ranks within blocks.

**Q68.** When the assumptions of a parametric test hold, a non-parametric test is generally
- A) more powerful
- B) equally powerful in every case
- C) less powerful
- D) invalid
**Answer:** C. It discards information about magnitudes.

## Sampling and Sampling Distributions

**Q69.** Sampling error arises because
- A) of interviewer mistakes
- B) only part of the population is observed
- C) of coding errors
- D) of non-response
**Answer:** B. It is absent in a complete census.

**Q70.** Non-sampling errors can occur in
- A) sample surveys only
- B) censuses only
- C) neither
- D) both sample surveys and censuses
**Answer:** D. E.g. response and processing errors.

**Q71.** The number of samples of size 2 that can be drawn without replacement from a population of 6 is
- A) 15
- B) 30
- C) 36
- D) 12
**Answer:** A. ⁶C₂ = 15.

**Q72.** The number of ordered samples of size 2 drawn with replacement from a population of 6 is
- A) 15
- B) 36
- C) 12
- D) 30
**Answer:** B. Nⁿ = 6² = 36.

**Q73.** The standard error of the sample mean (infinite population) is
- A) σ/n
- B) σ²/n
- C) σ/√n
- D) √σ/n
**Answer:** C. SE = σ/√n.

**Q74.** The finite population correction (N − n)/(N − 1) can be ignored when
- A) the sampling fraction n/N is small
- B) n = N
- C) sampling is without replacement and n/N is large
- D) the population is normal
**Answer:** A. It is then close to 1.

**Q75.** Stratified random sampling is most useful when the population
- A) is homogeneous
- B) has no sampling frame
- C) is very small
- D) consists of distinct subgroups that are internally homogeneous
**Answer:** D. Strata should be similar within, different between.

**Q76.** Selecting every 10th name from a list after a random start is
- A) cluster sampling
- B) quota sampling
- C) systematic sampling
- D) snowball sampling
**Answer:** C. Interval k = N/n.

**Q77.** The central limit theorem states that, for large n, the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately
- A) normal, whatever the population shape
- B) uniform
- C) the same as the population
- D) Poisson
**Answer:** A. With mean μ and SE σ/√n.

**Q78.** The sum of the frequencies of a sampling distribution of means equals
- A) the population size
- B) the sample size
- C) the number of possible samples
- D) one
**Answer:** C. Each possible sample contributes one value.

**Q79.** Increasing the sample size mainly reduces
- A) non-response
- B) sampling error
- C) bias in the frame
- D) measurement error
**Answer:** B. SE falls like 1/√n.

**Q80.** Snowball sampling is an example of
- A) probability sampling
- B) stratified sampling
- C) multistage sampling
- D) non-probability sampling
**Answer:** D. Respondents recruit further respondents.

**Q81.** Population variance σ² = 9, N = 6, samples of n = 2 without replacement. Var(x̄) is
- A) 4.5
- B) 3.6
- C) 9
- D) 1.8
**Answer:** B. (9/2)(4/5) = 3.6.

**Q82.** For samples from a normal population, (n − 1)s²/σ² follows
- A) t with n − 1 df
- B) F with (n, n − 1) df
- C) χ² with n − 1 df
- D) the standard normal
**Answer:** C. Basis of the variance test.

## Statistical Inference

**Q83.** A Type I error is
- A) rejecting H₀ when it is true
- B) accepting H₀ when it is false
- C) accepting H₀ when it is true
- D) choosing the wrong test
**Answer:** A. Its probability is α.

**Q84.** The probability of a Type II error is denoted by
- A) α
- B) 1 − α
- C) 1 − β
- D) β
**Answer:** D. Not rejecting a false H₀.

**Q85.** The power of a test is
- A) α
- B) 1 − β
- C) β
- D) 1 − α
**Answer:** B. Probability of rejecting a false H₀.

**Q86.** The level of significance of a test is the
- A) probability of a Type II error
- B) p-value
- C) maximum probability of a Type I error the tester accepts
- D) confidence level
**Answer:** C. Fixed before the data are seen.

**Q87.** A paired t-test on n pairs has degrees of freedom
- A) 2n − 2
- B) n
- C) 2n − 1
- D) n − 1
**Answer:** D. One difference per pair.

**Q88.** The paired t-test is appropriate when
- A) the two samples are dependent (before/after on the same units)
- B) the samples are independent and large
- C) variances are known
- D) the data are categorical
**Answer:** A. Differences remove unit-to-unit variation.

**Q89.** The critical value of z for a two-sided test at the 5% level is
- A) 1.645
- B) 2.576
- C) 1.96
- D) 2.326
**Answer:** C. 2.5% in each tail.

**Q90.** The t statistic can take values in the range
- A) 0 to ∞
- B) −∞ to ∞
- C) −1 to 1
- D) 0 to 1
**Answer:** B. It is symmetric about 0.

**Q91.** Which is a composite hypothesis?
- A) μ = 50, σ = 5
- B) p = 0.5 for a binomial with n = 10
- C) μ > 50
- D) λ = 3 for a Poisson variable
**Answer:** C. It does not fix a single distribution.

**Q92.** An unbiased estimator of the population variance σ² is
- A) Σ(x − x̄)²/n
- B) Σ(x − x̄)²/(n + 1)
- C) Σ|x − x̄|/n
- D) Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1)
**Answer:** D. Divisor n − 1.

**Q93.** Which is NOT a property of every consistent estimator?
- A) it converges in probability to the parameter
- B) bias (if any) tends to 0 as n → ∞
- C) it always attains the Cramér–Rao lower bound
- D) its variance tends to 0 (under the usual sufficient conditions)
**Answer:** C. Attaining the bound is efficiency, not consistency.

**Q94.** In a χ² goodness-of-fit test with k classes and m parameters estimated from the data, the df are
- A) k
- B) k − 1 − m
- C) k − m
- D) k + m − 1
**Answer:** B. One df lost for the total, one per estimated parameter.

**Q95.** The degrees of freedom for a χ² test of independence in a 3 × 4 contingency table are
- A) 12
- B) 7
- C) 11
- D) 6
**Answer:** D. (3 − 1)(4 − 1) = 6.

**Q96.** For a fixed sample size, increasing α will
- A) increase β
- B) leave β unchanged
- C) decrease β
- D) make the test invalid
**Answer:** C. A larger rejection region means fewer Type II errors.

**Q97.** A test gives p = 0.03. At α = 0.05 you should
- A) reject H₀
- B) not reject H₀
- C) accept H₁ with certainty
- D) increase α
**Answer:** A. p ≤ α.

**Q98.** Other things equal, a confidence interval becomes narrower when
- A) the confidence level increases
- B) σ increases
- C) the sample size increases
- D) the population is skewed
**Answer:** C. Width ∝ 1/√n.

## Design of Experiments

**Q99.** The three basic principles of experimental design are
- A) randomisation, replication and local control
- B) sampling, estimation and testing
- C) blocking, coding and tabulation
- D) mean, median and mode
**Answer:** A. Fisher's principles.

**Q100.** Local control in an experiment is achieved by
- A) replication
- B) randomisation
- C) increasing the error df
- D) blocking (grouping similar units)
**Answer:** D. Removes block-to-block variation from error.

**Q101.** In a one-way ANOVA with N observations, the total degrees of freedom are
- A) N
- B) N − 1
- C) N − k
- D) k − 1
**Answer:** B. One df used for the grand mean.

**Q102.** The error degrees of freedom in an RCBD with b blocks and t treatments are
- A) bt − 1
- B) b + t − 2
- C) (b − 1)(t − 1)
- D) b(t − 1)
**Answer:** C. Total − blocks − treatments.

**Q103.** In a two-way ANOVA with factor A at 3 levels and B at 4 levels, the interaction df are
- A) 12
- B) 5
- C) 11
- D) 6
**Answer:** D. (3 − 1)(4 − 1) = 6.

**Q104.** The F statistic in ANOVA for treatments is
- A) MS(treatment)/MS(error)
- B) SS(treatment)/SS(total)
- C) MS(error)/MS(treatment)
- D) SS(error)/df
**Answer:** A. Compared with F(df_T, df_E).

**Q105.** Experimental error is
- A) a mistake in calculation
- B) variation among experimental units receiving the same treatment
- C) the difference between treatment means
- D) bias caused by randomisation
**Answer:** B. Due to uncontrolled factors.

**Q106.** The least significant difference (LSD) between two treatment means, each based on r observations, is
- A) t·√(MSE/r)
- B) t·√(2MSE/r)
- C) F·MSE
- D) 2t·MSE/r
**Answer:** B. t at error df times the SE of a difference.

**Q107.** A Latin square design controls variation from
- A) one source
- B) no source
- C) three sources
- D) two sources (rows and columns)
**Answer:** D. Each treatment appears once in each row and column.

**Q108.** A completely randomised design is most appropriate when
- A) experimental units are homogeneous
- B) there is a fertility gradient
- C) there are two nuisance factors
- D) treatments cannot be randomised
**Answer:** A. Otherwise block.

**Q109.** A significant F in a one-way ANOVA shows that
- A) all treatment means differ
- B) at least one treatment mean differs from the others
- C) the variances are unequal
- D) the experiment was badly designed
**Answer:** B. Follow up with a multiple-comparison test.

**Q110.** If the relative efficiency of an RCBD with respect to a CRD is 1.6, then
- A) blocking was ineffective
- B) the CRD is more precise
- C) a CRD would need about 60% more replicates for equal precision
- D) the treatments are not significant
**Answer:** C. RE > 1 means blocking paid off.

## Vital Statistics, National Income and Index Numbers

**Q111.** The infant mortality rate is 45 per 1000 live births and there were 250,000 live births. The number of infant deaths is
- A) 10,000
- B) 12,500
- C) 11,250
- D) 13,750
**Answer:** C. 45 × 250 = 11,250.

**Q112.** The crude death rate is
- A) deaths per 1000 live births
- B) deaths in the year per 1000 mid-year population
- C) deaths per 100 females
- D) deaths under one year per 1000 births
**Answer:** B. "Crude" because it ignores age structure.

**Q113.** The total fertility rate (five-year age groups, ASFRs as proportions) is
- A) ΣASFR
- B) 5 × ΣASFR
- C) ΣASFR/5
- D) births/women aged 15–49

**Q114.** A net reproduction rate of exactly 1 means that the population
