## Section I-a. Concepts and scope (files 01–02)
- **Criminology:** *crimen* + *logos*; Topinard 1879; Garofalo 1885. Sutherland: making, breaking, reacting to laws → sociology of law, etiology, penology.
- **Fathers:** Beccaria (classical, 1764); Lombroso (modern/positivist, 1876); Mendelsohn (victimology); John Augustus (probation).
- **Terms:** actus reus, mens rea, strict liability, mala in se/prohibita, dark figure, index crimes (fraud is not one), UCR (1930), NIBRS, victim surveys.
- **Scope:** statistics, sociology of law, etiology, typologies, penology, victimology, juvenile, policing/investigation/forensics, prevention, transnational crime.
- **Crime vs deviance vs sin vice evil;** norms (folkways, mores, laws — Sumner), values.
- **Crime as social problem:** harm, fear, cost; perspectives — functionalist, conflict, interactionist, constructionist (moral panic — Cohen 1972).
- **Security:** physical, social, economic; human security (UNDP 1994).
- **Pakistan significance:** terrorism (GTI 2025: 2nd), street crime, GBV, juvenile, police reform, prisons (161%, 75% under-trial, Oct 2026), FATF, cyber.
- **Typology:** occasional, habitual (CrPC s.110), professional (Sutherland 1937), white-collar (Sutherland 1939/1949), organised (UNTOC 2000; 3+ persons), corporate (Clinard and Yeager 1980).

## Section I-b. Theories (file 03)
- **Classical:** Beccaria (certainty > severity), Bentham (felicific calculus, panopticon). Neo-classical: mitigation.
- **Positivist:** Lombroso atavism → Goring 1913 refuted; Ferri, Garofalo. Phrenology (Gall), physiognomy (Lavater).
- **Biological:** Sheldon mesomorph 1949; Jukes, Kallikaks; twins (Christiansen), adoption (Mednick 1984); XYY; MAOA × maltreatment (Caspi 2002). Nature × nurture.
- **Psychological:** Freud id/ego/superego; Bowlby (44 thieves); Eysenck; Kohlberg; Bandura Bobo doll 1961; psychopathy (Hare).
- **Social disorganisation:** Park–Burgess zones 1925; Shaw–McKay 1942; collective efficacy (Sampson 1997); broken windows (Wilson–Kelling 1982).
- **Strain:** Durkheim anomie (1893, 1897); **Merton 1938** — conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism, rebellion; Cohen 1955; Cloward–Ohlin 1960; Agnew GST 1992; Messner–Rosenfeld 1994.
- **Control:** Reckless containment 1961; Sykes–Matza neutralisation 1957 (5 techniques), drift 1964; **Hirschi 1969** — attachment, commitment, involvement, belief; Gottfredson–Hirschi 1990 self-control.
- **Learning:** Tarde imitation; **Sutherland 1939/1947** (9 propositions, excess of definitions); Burgess–Akers 1966; Akers social learning.
- **Labelling:** Tannenbaum 1938 (dramatisation of evil); Lemert 1951 (primary/secondary); **Becker 1963** (*Outsiders*, moral entrepreneurs); Goffman stigma 1963; Braithwaite 1989 reintegrative shaming.
- **Conflict:** Marx; Bonger 1916; Vold 1958; Quinney 1970; Chambliss 1973. Left realism (Lea–Young 1984, relative deprivation, square of crime); right realism (Wilson 1975).
- **Symbolic interactionism:** Mead, Blumer, Cooley — meanings, labels, identities → hate crime, polarisation.
- **Choice:** rational choice (Cornish–Clarke 1986); routine activity (Cohen–Felson 1979: offender + target − guardian); situational prevention (Clarke); crime pattern (Brantinghams).
- **Life-course:** Sampson–Laub 1993; Moffitt 1993.
- **Legitimacy:** Tyler 1990 procedural justice.
- **Islamic:** fitrah, maqasid (din, nafs, 'aql, nasl, mal); hudud (zina, qazf, sariqa, hirabah, shurb), qisas, diyat, ta'zir; doubt averts hudud; Umar's famine suspension; Hudood 1979, Qisas–Diyat 1990/1997, Women's Protection 2006, honour law 2016, whipping abolished 1996.

## Section II-a. Juvenile delinquency (file 04)
- **Juvenile:** under 18 (JJSA 2018). PPC s.82–83: under 10 no offence; 10–14 maturity test **[verify]**.
- Legal vs behavioural; delinquent vs **status offender**; chronic status offender; parens patriae.

## Section II-b. Juvenile justice (file 05)
- **History:** House of Refuge 1825; *Ex parte Crouse* 1838; Augustus 1841; **first juvenile court Chicago 1899**; Borstal 1902; *Kent* 1966, **Gault 1967**, Winship 1970; JJDPA 1974; Beijing Rules 1985; CRC 1989 (Pakistan 1990); Riyadh and Havana 1990; Roper 2005.
- **Pakistan:** JJSO 2000 → **JJSA 2018** — under 18; minor/major/heinous; Juvenile Justice Committees (diversion); juvenile courts; no death penalty/hard labour; observation homes; female juveniles by female officers; legal aid; probation; rehabilitation centres; confidentiality.
- **Process:** intake → detention hearing → pretrial (waiver) → adjudication → disposition → aftercare. Roles: prosecutor, defence, judge, probation officer.
- **Gaps:** few courts, JJCs not functional, juveniles in adult jails, age disputes, untrained police.

## Section II-c. Criminal justice system (file 06)
- Packer (1964): crime control vs due process. Formal vs informal agencies (jirga).
- **Police history:** kotwal → Napier's Sindh Police 1843 → Police Commission 1860 → **Police Act 1861** → Fraser Commission 1902–03 → Police Rules 1934 → **Police Order 2002** (PSCs, complaints authorities, investigation separated) → 2004 dilution → provincial divergence (KP Act 2017; Sindh revived PO 2019) → CTDs, Safe City, **CCD Punjab 2025**.
- **Challenges:** politicisation, colonial culture, resources, pay, weak investigation, terrorism, urban crime, trust, encounters, gender, coordination.
- **Courts:** magistrates, sessions, HC, SC, FSC, ATCs, accountability, anti-rape special courts, military courts (civilians — SC CB 5–2, May 2025). Problems: backlog, low conviction, witnesses, under-trials.
- **Prosecution services:** KP 2005, Punjab 2006, Sindh 2009.
- **Prisons:** Prisons Act 1894; Prison Rules 1978; Sindh Prisons and Corrections Act 2019; **Oct 2026: 110,513 vs 68,832 (160.6%), 82,988 under-trial (75%), 28,000+ CNSA.**
- **Probation:** Ordinance 1960; CrPC s.562; Punjab Act 2019; KP Act 2021. **Parole:** 1926 Act; Maconochie, Crofton, Elmira 1876.
