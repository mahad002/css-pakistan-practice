# MCQ Bank — Criminology

Questions written for this pack, grouped by syllabus area. FPSC Criminology MCQs (2024–2026) test founders and dates, theory terms, juvenile justice history, police law and cyber terms; several had "None of these" as the correct answer. Answers and one-line explanations follow each question.

## Basic Concepts and Understanding Criminology

**Q1.** The word "criminology" comes from the Latin *crimen* and the Greek:
- A) Logia/logos
- B) Logic
- C) Logistic
- D) Locus
**Answer:** A. "Study of crime".

**Q2.** The term "criminology" was first used by:
- A) Cesare Beccaria
- B) Paul Topinard
- C) Cesare Lombroso
- D) Howard Becker
**Answer:** B. Topinard (1879); Garofalo popularised it in 1885.

**Q3.** *Criminologia* (1885), which popularised the term, was written by:
- A) Enrico Ferri
- B) Adolphe Quetelet
- C) Raffaele Garofalo
- D) Charles Goring
**Answer:** C. Garofalo also developed the idea of "natural crime".

**Q4.** Sutherland's three divisions of criminology are the sociology of law, criminal etiology and:
- A) Victimology
- B) Forensics
- C) Psychiatry
- D) Penology
**Answer:** D. Penology studies punishment and corrections.

**Q5.** Penology deals with the study of:
- A) Causes of crime
- B) Victims' rights
- C) Forensic evidence
- D) Punishment and prisons
**Answer:** D. Etiology studies causes.

**Q6.** The guilty mind or criminal intent is called:
- A) Actus reus
- B) Mala in se
- C) Mens rea
- D) Corpus delicti
**Answer:** C. Actus reus is the guilty act.

**Q7.** *Actus reus* means:
- A) Criminal intent
- B) Criminal act
- C) Defence
- D) Body of the crime
**Answer:** B. The physical element of the offence.

**Q8.** Crimes that are "wrong in themselves", such as murder, are called:
- A) Mala in se
- B) Mala prohibita
- C) Strict liability offences
- D) Status offences
**Answer:** A. Mala prohibita are wrong only because prohibited.

**Q9.** Liability without proof of criminal intent is known as:
- A) Strict liability
- B) Vicarious liability
- C) Mens rea
- D) Mala in se
**Answer:** A. Common in regulatory offences such as food adulteration.

**Q10.** "UCR" in US crime statistics stands for:
- A) Universal Crime Rate
- B) Uniform Crime Reports
- C) Unified Criminal Register
- D) Urban Crime Review
**Answer:** B. FBI data collected since 1930.

**Q11.** Which of the following is NOT a Part I index crime in the FBI's UCR?
- A) Burglary
- B) Larceny-theft
- C) Fraud
- D) Motor vehicle theft
**Answer:** C. Fraud is a Part II offence.

**Q12.** Crime that is never reported or recorded is called the:
- A) Grey area
- B) Hidden curriculum
- C) Crime funnel
- D) Dark figure of crime
**Answer:** D. It limits official statistics.

**Q13.** Non-conformity to norms accepted by a significant number of people is called:
- A) Criminality
- B) Recidivism
- C) Degeneracy
- D) Deviance
**Answer:** D. Deviance is broader than crime.

**Q14.** W.G. Sumner divided norms into folkways and:
- A) Laws only
- B) Values
- C) Mores
- D) Rituals
**Answer:** C. Mores carry strong moral sanction; laws are formal norms.

**Q15.** The "father of victimology" is usually considered to be:
- A) Edwin Sutherland
- B) Benjamin Mendelsohn
- C) Albert Cohen
- D) John Augustus
**Answer:** B. Hans von Hentig is also a pioneer.

**Q16.** The UNDP Human Development Report that introduced "human security" was published in:
- A) 1990
- B) 1994
- C) 2000
- D) 2005
**Answer:** B. Freedom from fear and freedom from want.

**Q17.** Stanley Cohen's concept describing exaggerated public alarm about a group seen as a threat is:
- A) Moral panic
- B) Anomie
- C) Labelling
- D) Drift
**Answer:** A. *Folk Devils and Moral Panics* (1972).

## Crime and Criminals

**Q18.** A person frequently convicted of crimes and presumed dangerous is a(n):
- A) Occasional criminal
- B) Habitual criminal
- C) Professional criminal
- D) White-collar criminal
**Answer:** B. Repetition is the defining feature.

**Q19.** *The Professional Thief* (1937) was written by:
- A) Cesare Lombroso
- B) Robert Merton
- C) Edwin Sutherland
- D) Travis Hirschi
**Answer:** C. Based on the account of a thief, "Chic Conwell".

**Q20.** The term "white-collar crime" was coined by:
- A) Walter Reckless
- B) Albert Cohen
- C) Donald Cressey
- D) Edwin Sutherland
**Answer:** D. In his 1939 address to the American Sociological Society.

**Q21.** White-collar crime has low visibility mainly because it:
- A) Involves only small sums
- B) Is committed by the poor
- C) Is always violent
- D) Goes undetected within normal business transactions
**Answer:** D. Victims are often unaware.

**Q22.** The UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime is also called the:
- A) Vienna Convention
- B) Merida Convention
- C) Palermo Convention
- D) Budapest Convention
**Answer:** C. Adopted in 2000; Merida is UNCAC, Budapest is cybercrime.

**Q23.** Under UNTOC, an organised criminal group has at least:
- A) Two persons
- B) Three persons
- C) Five persons
- D) Ten persons
**Answer:** B. Structured, existing for a period, committing serious crimes for material benefit.

**Q24.** *Corporate Crime* (1980), a classic study, was written by:
- A) Clinard and Yeager
- B) Shaw and McKay
- C) Cloward and Ohlin
- D) Lea and Young
**Answer:** A. Marshall Clinard and Peter Yeager.

**Q25.** CrPC section 110 allows magistrates to require security for good behaviour from:
- A) Habitual offenders
- B) Juveniles
- C) Public servants
- D) Witnesses
**Answer:** A. Security bonds for habitual offenders.

## Theoretical Perspectives

**Q26.** *On Crimes and Punishments* was published by Cesare Beccaria in:
- A) 1738
- B) 1764
- C) 1789
- D) 1799
**Answer:** B. Founding text of the classical school.

**Q27.** The "father of modern (positivist) criminology" is:
- A) Cesare Beccaria
- B) Edwin Sutherland
- C) Cesare Lombroso
- D) Jeremy Bentham
**Answer:** C. *L'uomo delinquente* (1876).

**Q28.** Lombroso claimed that criminals were driven by:
- A) Social interaction
- B) Labelling
- C) Economic inequality
- D) Biological failings (atavism)
**Answer:** D. The "born criminal" as an evolutionary throwback.

**Q29.** *The English Convict* (1913), which challenged Lombroso, was written by:
- A) Earnest Hooton
- B) William Sheldon
- C) Henry Goddard
- D) Charles Goring
**Answer:** D. Found no distinct physical criminal type.

**Q30.** The hedonistic (felicific) calculus is associated with:
- A) Émile Durkheim
- B) Sigmund Freud
- C) Jeremy Bentham
- D) Karl Marx
**Answer:** C. People weigh pleasure against pain.

**Q31.** The study of skull shape to infer character is:
- A) Physiognomy
- B) Phrenology
- C) Somatotyping
- D) Craniometry only
**Answer:** B. Franz Joseph Gall; physiognomy reads facial features.

**Q32.** William Sheldon's body type most linked with delinquency was the:
- A) Mesomorph
- B) Endomorph
- C) Ectomorph
- D) Somatomorph
**Answer:** A. Muscular build (1949).

**Q33.** According to Freud, criminality arises from imbalance among the:
- A) Id, ego and superego
- B) Body fluids
- C) Genes and hormones
- D) Peers and family
**Answer:** A. Psychoanalytic theory.

**Q34.** The Bobo doll experiments on learned aggression were conducted by:
- A) B.F. Skinner
- B) Albert Bandura
- C) Hans Eysenck
- D) John Bowlby
**Answer:** B. Social learning through modelling (1961).

**Q35.** The concept of anomie was introduced into sociology by:
- A) Auguste Comte
- B) Karl Marx
- C) Émile Durkheim
- D) Max Weber
**Answer:** C. In *The Division of Labour* (1893) and *Suicide* (1897).

**Q36.** Merton's "Social Structure and Anomie" was published in:
- A) 1925
- B) 1938
- C) 1955
- D) 1969
**Answer:** B. Strain between goals and means.

**Q37.** In Merton's typology, people who accept cultural goals but use illegitimate means are:
- A) Conformists
- B) Ritualists
- C) Rebels
- D) Innovators
**Answer:** D. E.g. thieves and fraudsters.

**Q38.** In Merton's typology, drug addicts and vagrants who reject both goals and means are:
- A) Innovators
- B) Ritualists
- C) Retreatists
- D) Rebels
**Answer:** C. They withdraw from society.

**Q39.** "People commit crime due to blocked opportunities" is the core idea of:
- A) Labelling theory
- B) Strain theory
- C) Social control theory
- D) Routine activity theory
**Answer:** B. Blocked legitimate means produce strain.

**Q40.** General Strain Theory (1992) was developed by:
- A) Robert Agnew
- B) Albert Cohen
- C) Richard Cloward
- D) Steven Messner
**Answer:** A. Adds negative emotions and loss of positive stimuli.

**Q41.** *Delinquent Boys* (1955), on status frustration and subcultures, was written by:
- A) Albert Cohen
- B) Walter Miller
- C) Lloyd Ohlin
- D) David Matza
**Answer:** A. Lower-class boys reject middle-class standards.

**Q42.** Differential opportunity theory (1960) was proposed by:
- A) Shaw and McKay
- B) Cloward and Ohlin
- C) Sykes and Matza
- D) Burgess and Akers
**Answer:** B. Criminal, conflict and retreatist subcultures.

**Q43.** The concentric zone model of the city was developed by:
- A) Shaw and McKay
- B) Wilson and Kelling
- C) Park and Burgess
- D) Cohen and Felson
**Answer:** C. Chicago School, 1925.

**Q44.** *Juvenile Delinquency and Urban Areas* (1942), the classic statement of social disorganisation theory, was written by:
- A) Wilson and Kelling
- B) Sampson and Laub
- C) Lea and Young
- D) Shaw and McKay
**Answer:** D. Delinquency stayed high in the same areas as populations changed.

**Q45.** The Broken Windows theory (1982) is associated with:
- A) Beccaria
- B) Hirschi
- C) Becker
- D) Wilson and Kelling
**Answer:** D. Disorder invites serious crime.

**Q46.** Hirschi's social bond theory includes attachment, commitment, involvement and:
- A) Opportunity
- B) Strain
- C) Belief
- D) Learning
**Answer:** C. *Causes of Delinquency* (1969).

**Q47.** *A General Theory of Crime* (1990), stressing low self-control, was written by:
- A) Sampson and Laub
- B) Gottfredson and Hirschi
- C) Cornish and Clarke
- D) Burgess and Akers
**Answer:** B. Self-control formed early by parenting.

**Q48.** Containment theory was proposed by:
- A) Walter Reckless
- B) Travis Hirschi
- C) Albert Reiss
- D) Ivan Nye
**Answer:** A. Inner and outer containment (1961).

**Q49.** "Denial of the victim" and "condemnation of the condemners" are:
- A) Techniques of neutralisation
- B) Modes of adaptation
- C) Elements of the social bond
- D) Stages of labelling
**Answer:** A. Sykes and Matza (1957).

**Q50.** Differential association theory was proposed by:
- A) Ronald Akers
- B) Edwin Sutherland
- C) Gabriel Tarde
- D) Robert Burgess
**Answer:** B. 1939; final nine propositions in 1947.

**Q51.** In the mid-1960s Burgess and Akers presented their differential ______ theory.
- A) Association
- B) Opportunity
- C) Reinforcement
- D) Identification
**Answer:** C. Differential association–reinforcement (1966).

**Q52.** "Dramatisation of evil" was described by:
- A) Edwin Lemert
- B) Howard Becker
- C) Erving Goffman
- D) Frank Tannenbaum
**Answer:** D. *Crime and the Community* (1938).

**Q53.** Primary and secondary deviance were distinguished by:
- A) Howard Becker
- B) Kai Erikson
- C) John Braithwaite
- D) Edwin Lemert
**Answer:** D. 1951.

**Q54.** *Outsiders* (1963), a key work of labelling theory, was written by:
- A) Albert Cohen
- B) Sigmund Freud
- C) Howard Becker
- D) Erving Goffman
**Answer:** C. Introduced "moral entrepreneurs".

**Q55.** The view that deviance is not a quality of an act but the result of others applying a label is:
- A) Control theory
- B) Labelling theory
- C) Functionalist theory
- D) Strain theory
**Answer:** B. Becker's formulation.

**Q56.** Reintegrative shaming was proposed by:
- A) John Braithwaite
- B) Howard Zehr
- C) Lawrence Sherman
- D) Ronald Clarke
**Answer:** A. *Crime, Shame and Reintegration* (1989).

**Q57.** *Criminality and Economic Conditions* (1916), an early Marxist work, was written by:
- A) Willem Bonger
- B) Richard Quinney
- C) William Chambliss
- D) Austin Turk
**Answer:** A. Capitalism fosters egoism.

**Q58.** "Relative deprivation" and the "square of crime" are central to:
- A) Right realism
- B) Left realism
- C) Classical school
- D) Biological positivism
**Answer:** B. Lea and Young (1984).

**Q59.** Routine activity theory requires a motivated offender, a suitable target and:
- A) Weak laws
- B) Poverty
- C) Absence of a capable guardian
- D) Peer pressure
**Answer:** C. Cohen and Felson (1979).

**Q60.** Rational choice theory in criminology is associated with:
- A) Shaw and McKay
- B) Glueck and Glueck
- C) Park and Burgess
- D) Cornish and Clarke
**Answer:** D. *The Reasoning Criminal* (1986).

**Q61.** Environmental criminology focuses on:
- A) Forest protection
- B) Climate change
- C) Pollution offences only
- D) Crime locations and patterns
**Answer:** D. Brantinghams' crime pattern theory.

**Q62.** Adolescence-limited and life-course-persistent offenders were distinguished by:
- A) Sampson and Laub
- B) Marvin Wolfgang
- C) Terrie Moffitt
- D) Robert Agnew
**Answer:** C. 1993.

**Q63.** In Islamic criminal law, fixed punishments prescribed by the Qur'an and Sunnah are called:
- A) Ta'zir
- B) Hudud
- C) Diyat
- D) Qisas
**Answer:** B. For offences such as theft, zina and qazf.

**Q64.** Discretionary punishments set by the state in Islamic law are:
- A) Ta'zir
- B) Hudud
- C) Qisas
- D) Arsh
**Answer:** A. Most offences fall here.

**Q65.** "Qazf" in Islamic law means:
- A) False accusation of zina
- B) Highway robbery
- C) Drinking alcohol
- D) Apostasy
**Answer:** A. A hadd offence.

**Q66.** The Qisas and Diyat Ordinance was first promulgated in Pakistan in:
- A) 1979
- B) 1990
- C) 2006
- D) 2016
**Answer:** B. Made permanent by Act in 1997.

## Juvenile Delinquency and Juvenile Justice

**Q67.** Under the Juvenile Justice System Act 2018, a juvenile is a person who at the time of the offence is below:
- A) 14 years
- B) 16 years
- C) 18 years
- D) 21 years
**Answer:** C. Aligned with the UN CRC.

**Q68.** An act illegal only because of the actor's age, such as truancy, is a(n):
- A) Felony
- B) Index crime
- C) Misdemeanour
- D) Status offence
**Answer:** D. Running away and curfew violations are other examples.

**Q69.** Children who keep committing status offences despite repeated intervention are called:
- A) Life-course persistent offenders
- B) Recidivists
- C) Professional delinquents
- D) Chronic status offenders
**Answer:** D. Repeated failure of family, school and agency intervention.

**Q70.** *Parens patriae* means the state is the:
- A) Prosecutor of every child
- B) Owner of public property
- C) Ultimate guardian of every child
- D) Judge of last resort
**Answer:** C. Basis of juvenile court jurisdiction.

**Q71.** The first specialised correctional institution for youth in the United States was the:
- A) Borstal institution
- B) New York House of Refuge
- C) Chicago juvenile court
- D) Elmira Reformatory
**Answer:** B. Opened in 1825.

**Q72.** The world's first juvenile court was established in 1899 in:
- A) Cook County (Chicago), Illinois
- B) London
- C) Boston
- D) New York City
**Answer:** A. A rehabilitative, informal court.

**Q73.** The US Supreme Court case that gave juveniles due-process rights such as counsel and notice was:
- A) *In re Gault*
- B) *Miranda v. Arizona*
- C) *Terry v. Ohio*
- D) *Mapp v. Ohio*
**Answer:** A. 1967.

**Q74.** The UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Administration of Juvenile Justice (1985) are known as the:
- A) Riyadh Guidelines
- B) Beijing Rules
- C) Havana Rules
- D) Tokyo Rules
**Answer:** B. Riyadh covers prevention; Havana covers deprivation of liberty.

**Q75.** The UN Guidelines for the Prevention of Juvenile Delinquency (1990) are the:
- A) Beijing Rules
- B) Bangkok Rules
- C) Riyadh Guidelines
- D) Mandela Rules
**Answer:** C. Focus on family, education and community.

**Q76.** Pakistan ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child in:
- A) 1989
- B) 1990
- C) 2000
- D) 2018
**Answer:** B. The CRC was adopted in 1989.

**Q77.** The Borstal system for young offenders began in 1902 in:
- A) Scotland
- B) India
- C) Ireland
- D) England
**Answer:** D. At Borstal, Kent.

**Q78.** Pakistan's first national juvenile justice law, replaced in 2018, was the:
- A) Juvenile Justice System Ordinance 2000
- B) Youthful Offenders Act 1983
- C) Children Act 1955
- D) Reformatory Schools Act 1897
**Answer:** A. JJSO 2000.

**Q79.** Under JJSA 2018, the bodies that handle diversion of juveniles are called:
- A) Public Safety Commissions
- B) Juvenile Justice Committees
- C) Dispute Resolution Councils
- D) Probation Boards
**Answer:** B. Headed by a judicial magistrate.

**Q80.** Marvin Wolfgang's Philadelphia cohort study found that a small group of chronic offenders committed:
- A) More than half of offences
- B) About 10% of offences
- C) About a quarter of offences
- D) All violent offences
**Answer:** A. About 6% of boys committed over half the offences.

**Q81.** "Scared Straight" programmes for juveniles have been found to:
- A) Increase offending
- B) Greatly reduce offending
- C) Have no effect at all
- D) Eliminate drug use
**Answer:** A. Meta-analyses show harmful effects.

**Q82.** The "father of probation" is:
- A) William Blackstone

**Q83.** The "mark system", a forerunner of parole, was introduced by:

## Criminal Investigation

**Q109.** "Every contact leaves a trace" is the principle of:
- A) Alphonse Bertillon
- B) Francis Galton
- C) Hans Gross
- D) Edmond Locard
**Answer:** D. Locard's exchange principle.

**Q110.** The fingerprint classification system adopted in British India in 1897 is the:
- A) Bertillon system
- B) Vucetich system
- C) Henry system
- D) Galton system
**Answer:** C. Developed with Azizul Haque and Hem Chandra Bose.

**Q111.** DNA profiling was developed in 1984–85 by:
- A) Kary Mullis
- B) Alec Jeffreys
- C) Francis Crick
- D) Edmond Locard
**Answer:** B. First used to convict Colin Pitchfork (1988).

**Q112.** Forensic pathology deals mainly with:
- A) Dead bodies
- B) DNA analysis only
- C) Fingerprints
- D) Handwriting
**Answer:** A. Cause and manner of death.

**Q113.** The crime-scene search pattern best suited to a small open area searched by one person is the:
- A) Spiral
- B) Grid
- C) Zone
- D) Strip

**Q114.** The search pattern in which a strip search is repeated at right angles is the:
- A) Wheel
- B) Grid
- C) Link
- D) Spiral

**Q115.** A criminal's "signature" refers to:
- A) Fingerprints
- B) A legal signature on documents
- C) Unique ritualistic behaviour beyond what the crime requires
- D) The modus operandi
**Answer:** C. MO is functional; signature meets psychological needs.

**Q116.** Predictive policing relies on:
- A) Astrology
- B) Guesswork
- C) Confessions
- D) Data analytics
**Answer:** D. Algorithms forecast crime locations or risks.

**Q117.** Crime mapping shows mainly the:
- A) Arrest rates
