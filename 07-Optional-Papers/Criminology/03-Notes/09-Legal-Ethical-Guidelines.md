# XI. Legal and Ethical Guidelines for Investigators

Asked as gender-based crime investigation (2017, 2022). Stop and frisk, arrest, search and seizure appear in MCQs and as parts of investigation answers. Combine US landmark cases (the syllabus is US-textbook based) with Pakistani law.

---

## 1. Why legal and ethical guidelines matter

- Protect constitutional rights: life and liberty (Art. 9), safeguards on arrest and detention (Art. 10), fair trial (Art. 10A), dignity and privacy of home (Art. 14), protection against self-incrimination (Art. 13), equality (Art. 25).
- Ensure evidence is admissible and reliable.
- Prevent abuse, torture, wrongful convictions.
- Build public trust and police legitimacy (procedural justice).

## 2. Stop and frisk operations

- **Meaning:** a brief stop of a person on reasonable suspicion of criminal activity, with a pat-down of outer clothing for weapons.
- **US:** ***Terry v. Ohio* (1968)** — police may stop and frisk on **reasonable suspicion** (less than probable cause) that a person is armed and involved in crime; the frisk is limited to weapons. Later: "plain feel" (*Minnesota v. Dickerson*, 1993). New York's stop-and-frisk programme was ruled unconstitutional in practice (*Floyd v. City of New York*, 2013) for racial profiling.
- **UK:** stop and search under PACE 1984 (reasonable grounds); Section 60 (no suspicion in designated areas).
- **Pakistan:** no single statute titled "stop and frisk"; police checkpoints (nakas), snap checking and body searches are done under general police powers (Police Order 2002 duties; CrPC s.54 arrest without warrant on reasonable suspicion; Police Rules) and anti-terrorism laws **[verify specific provisions]**. Women must be searched only by women (CrPC s.52).
- **Ethical issues:** profiling by ethnicity, dress or class (e.g. Pashtun and Afghan profiling complaints); harassment and bribery at checkpoints; need for reasons, courtesy, recording.

## 3. Arrest procedures

**US standards:** arrest requires **probable cause**; warrants preferred; ***Miranda v. Arizona* (1966)** — before custodial interrogation suspects must be told of the right to remain silent, that statements can be used against them, the right to a lawyer, and the right to appointed counsel.

**Pakistan:**
| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Art. 10(1) | Person arrested must be informed of the grounds as soon as possible; right to consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice |
| Art. 10(2) | Must be produced before a magistrate within **24 hours** (excluding travel time); no detention beyond without magistrate's authority |
| Art. 10(3)–(9) | Preventive detention safeguards (review boards; maximum periods) |
| CrPC s.46 | How arrest is made (touching or confining body; force only if resisting; no right to cause death except for offences punishable with death or life imprisonment) |
| CrPC s.54 | Police may arrest without warrant in cognizable offences, on reasonable complaint, credible information or reasonable suspicion |
| CrPC s.60–61 | Person arrested taken before magistrate; not detained over 24 hours |
| CrPC s.167 | Remand by magistrate, maximum **15 days** in total **[verify]** |
| CrPC s.50 | Inform arrested person of grounds and right to bail in bailable offences |
| CrPC s.52 | Search of women only by women |
| JJSA 2018 | Juveniles: inform guardian and probation officer; no lock-ups with adults; female juveniles handled by female officers |
| ATA 1997 s.11EEEE (as amended Aug 2025) | Preventive detention of terrorism suspects for up to **three months**, with a three-year sunset clause ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1938651)) |
| Torture and Custodial Death Act 2022 | Torture by public servants criminalised; custodial deaths investigated by FIA (or a judicial magistrate) **[verify]** |

**Ethical guidelines:** use minimum necessary force (UN Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms by Law Enforcement Officials, 1990; UN Code of Conduct for Law Enforcement Officials, 1979); record arrest (arrest memo, time); inform family; no handcuffing of juveniles and women without need; no illegal detention ("private custody" in police stations); no "encounters" — extrajudicial killing is murder (Art. 9).

## 4. Search and seizure

**US:** Fourth Amendment — protection against unreasonable searches and seizures; warrants on probable cause; **exclusionary rule** — illegally obtained evidence excluded (*Weeks v. US*, 1914 federal; ***Mapp v. Ohio*, 1961** for states); exceptions: consent, search incident to arrest, plain view, exigent circumstances, automobile exception, good-faith exception (*US v. Leon*, 1984). Digital: *Riley v. California* (2014) — warrant needed to search a phone.

**Pakistan:**
| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Art. 14 | Dignity of man and privacy of home inviolable (subject to law) |
| CrPC s.96–99 | Search warrants issued by courts |
| CrPC s.102 | Police power to seize property suspected stolen or connected with offence |
| **CrPC s.103** | Search of a place to be made in the presence of **two or more respectable inhabitants of the locality** as witnesses; list of items seized signed by witnesses |
| CrPC s.165 | Search by the investigating officer without warrant, recording reasons in writing, when delay would defeat the purpose |
| CrPC s.51 | Search of arrested persons |
| CNSA 1997; Customs Act 1969 | Special search powers for narcotics and smuggling |
| PECA 2016 (amended 2025) | Search and seizure of data and devices, with warrants from courts; preservation orders |
| Investigation for Fair Trial Act 2013 | Warrants for surveillance and interception in terrorism/serious cases **[verify]** |

**Pakistani courts** do not apply a strict exclusionary rule; illegally obtained evidence may be admitted if relevant, but courts discount recoveries made without independent witnesses (s.103 violations) **[verify case law]**.

**Ethical issues:** night raids; harassment of women (chadar and chardiwari — sanctity of home); planting evidence; theft during searches; privacy of digital data.

## 5. Legal and ethical guidelines for investigating gender-based crimes (2017, 2022)

**Gender-based crimes:** rape and sexual assault, domestic violence, honour killings, acid attacks, forced marriage, harassment (workplace, online), trafficking for sexual exploitation, child sexual abuse, crimes against transgender persons.

### Legal framework in Pakistan
| Law | Key points |
|---|---|
| PPC s.375–376 (rape) as amended by the Protection of Women Act 2006, Criminal Law (Amendment) (Offences relating to Rape) Act 2016, and Anti-Rape Act 2021 | Rape defined; gang rape; marital rape not expressly criminalised **[verify]** |
| **Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021** | Special courts; **Special Sexual Offences Investigation Units (SSOIUs)**; Anti-Rape Crisis Cells in hospitals; medico-legal examination within 6 hours of report **[verify]**; sex offenders register (NADRA); in-camera trials; trial within four months; ban on the "two-finger" virginity test; victim's identity protected; legal aid. **Anti-Rape (Amendment) Bill 2026** passed by the National Assembly (May 2026) to make sexual assault and child abuse offences non-bailable — Senate passage and assent **[verify]** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/05/13/national-assembly-passes-bill-declaring-arrests-under-child-abuse-cases-non-bailable/)) |
| Lahore High Court, *Sadaf Aziz v. Federation* (January 2021) | Virginity (two-finger) tests declared illegal and unconstitutional |
| Criminal Law (Amendment) (Offences in the name or pretext of Honour) Act 2016 | Honour killers cannot escape punishment by forgiveness of heirs (mandatory life imprisonment) **[verify]** |
| Acid Control and Acid Crime Prevention Act 2011 (PPC s.336A–B) | Acid attacks punished |
| Prevention of Anti-Women Practices (Criminal Law Amendment) Act 2011 | Forced marriage, vani/swara, depriving women of inheritance |
| Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010 (amended 2022) | Inquiry committees; ombudsperson |
| Provincial domestic violence laws | Sindh 2013; Balochistan 2014; Punjab Protection of Women against Violence Act 2016; KP 2021; ICT (Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2025 **[verify]**) |
| ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025 | Minimum marriage age 18 in Islamabad (assent May 2025) ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1914268)) |
| Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2018 | Partly struck down by the Federal Shariat Court in May 2023 **[verify status of appeal]** |
| PECA 2016 s.21–24 | Offences against dignity, modesty; cyber stalking; sharing intimate images |

### Guidelines for investigators
1. **Victim-centred and trauma-informed:** believe and respect the victim; avoid re-traumatisation; allow a support person.
2. **Female officers** to record statements of women and girls; women police stations and gender protection units.
3. **Privacy and confidentiality:** do not disclose identity; in-camera proceedings; no media leaks.
4. **Prompt medico-legal examination** by trained (female) medico-legal officers with consent; no virginity tests.
5. **Forensic evidence:** DNA kits, chain of custody, timely dispatch to labs.
6. **Register FIR without delay or bargaining**; no pressure to compromise or marry the offender.
7. **Protection orders and shelters** (Dar-ul-Aman; Violence Against Women Centres, Multan model 2017).
8. **Avoid victim-blaming questions** (character, dress, past sexual history).
9. **Child victims:** child-friendly interviewing, presence of guardian/psychologist, Zainab Alert procedures.
10. **Interagency coordination:** police, health, prosecution, social welfare, legal aid.
11. **Prevent informal settlement** through jirgas in rape and honour cases.
12. **Ethical neutrality and non-discrimination** — including transgender victims.
13. **Training and accountability** for officers who obstruct cases.

**Pakistani examples:** Motorway gang-rape case (September 2020) led to the Anti-Rape Ordinance and Act 2021; Zainab case (2018) led to the Zainab Alert Act 2020 and showed the power of DNA evidence; Noor Mukadam murder (2021) — forensic and CCTV evidence secured conviction; Qandeel Baloch (2016) — honour killing case under the 2016 law (the brother's later acquittal on appeal in 2022 raised questions about forgiveness provisions **[verify]**).

## 6. General ethics for investigators

- Integrity, impartiality, accountability, respect for human rights, transparency, professionalism.
- Avoid corruption, fabrication, planting evidence, selective investigation, leaking to media.
- **Noble-cause corruption** — bending rules to convict someone believed guilty — is still misconduct.
- Codes: UN Code of Conduct for Law Enforcement Officials (1979); Police Order 2002 code of conduct; Punjab Police Rules.
