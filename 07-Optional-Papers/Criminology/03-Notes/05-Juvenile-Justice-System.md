# VI. Juvenile Justice System

Asked 2017 (Pakistan's juvenile justice system and reforms), 2021 (juvenile probation services), 2022 (history of juvenile justice and role of juvenile courts), 2026 (UNICEF/UNODC and juvenile justice plan). Know the US model (the syllabus is US-textbook based — Siegel) **and** Pakistan's JJSA 2018.

## 1. History of juvenile justice

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| Pre-19th c. | Children over 7 treated as adults under common law (*doli incapax* below 7; rebuttable presumption 7–14) |
| 1825 | **New York House of Refuge** — first specialised institution for juveniles in the US |
| 1838 | *Ex parte Crouse* (Pennsylvania) — court upheld state power under **parens patriae** |
| 1841 | **John Augustus**, Boston shoemaker, begins bailing and supervising offenders — "father of probation" |
| 1850s | Reform schools; "child savers" movement |
| **1899** | **First juvenile court, Cook County (Chicago), Illinois** — rehabilitative, informal, civil not criminal |
| 1902 | **Borstal** system begins at Borstal, Kent (England) — training for young offenders (16–21) |
| 1908 | England's Children Act — juvenile courts |
| 1966–70 | US Supreme Court due-process cases: *Kent v. US* (1966, waiver hearings), ***In re Gault* (1967)** — juveniles have rights to notice, counsel, confrontation, protection against self-incrimination; *In re Winship* (1970) — proof beyond reasonable doubt |
| 1974 | US Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention Act — deinstitutionalise status offenders, separate juveniles from adults |
| 1985 | **UN Beijing Rules** (Standard Minimum Rules for the Administration of Juvenile Justice) |
| 1989 | **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC)** — Art. 37 and 40 on juvenile justice; Pakistan ratified in **1990** |
| 1990 | **Riyadh Guidelines** (prevention) and **Havana Rules** (juveniles deprived of liberty) |
| 2005 | *Roper v. Simmons* — US bans death penalty for crimes committed under 18 |

## 2. Pakistan's juvenile justice laws

| Law | Points |
|---|---|
| Reformatory Schools Act 1897; Punjab/Sindh Borstal Acts (1926) | Colonial institutions for young offenders **[verify Borstal Act years]** |
| Sindh Children Act 1955; Punjab Youthful Offenders Ordinance 1983 | Provincial juvenile laws, poorly implemented |
| **Juvenile Justice System Ordinance 2000 (JJSO)** | First national law: juvenile courts, no death penalty for juveniles, legal aid, separate custody. Weak implementation; disputes about age; Lahore High Court struck it down in 2004 and Supreme Court restored it (2005) **[verify]** |
| **Juvenile Justice System Act 2018 (JJSA)** | Replaced JJSO; aligned with CRC |
| Provincial child protection laws | Punjab Destitute and Neglected Children Act 2004; Sindh Child Protection Authority Act 2011; KP Child Protection and Welfare Act 2010; Balochistan Child Protection Act 2016 **[verify years]** |
| ICT Child Protection Act 2018; Zainab Alert, Response and Recovery Act 2020 | Child protection system and missing-children response in Islamabad |
| National Commission on the Rights of the Child Act 2017 | NCRC — monitoring |

### JJSA 2018 — key provisions (learn these)
1. **Juvenile** = under 18 at the time of offence.
2. **Classification of offences:** minor (punishable up to 3 years), major (more than 3 up to 7 years), heinous (more than 7 years or death) **[verify thresholds]**.
3. **Diversion:** alternatives to formal proceedings for minor and some major offences — through **Juvenile Justice Committees** (JJCs) headed by a judicial magistrate with a public prosecutor, a member of the bar, a probation officer and a civil-society member **[verify composition]**; options include restorative measures, apology, compensation, community service, counselling.
4. **Juvenile Courts:** exclusive jurisdiction; trial separate from adults; in-camera, informal procedure.
5. **No death penalty or hard labour** for juveniles; no handcuffs or fetters.
6. **Age determination:** by medical/documentary evidence; benefit of doubt to the child.
7. **Arrest:** police must inform guardian and probation officer within 24 hours; juveniles kept in **observation homes**, not police lock-ups; **female juveniles** dealt with by female police officers only.
8. **Bail:** a juvenile accused of minor or major offences is entitled to bail, subject to exceptions; heinous offences — bail discretionary (and generally for juveniles under 16) **[verify]**.
9. **Legal aid** at state expense.
10. **Probation and parole** for juveniles; **Juvenile Rehabilitation Centres** (replacing borstals) for those sentenced.
11. **Confidentiality:** publication of a juvenile's identity is prohibited.
12. **Time limits:** inquiry and trial to be completed within set periods (e.g. six months) **[verify]**.
13. **Rules:** federal and provincial rules required to make the Act work.

### Implementation gaps
- Few dedicated juvenile courts — sessions courts designated; judges and prosecutors not trained.
- JJCs not notified or not functional in many districts.
- Observation homes and rehabilitation centres too few; juveniles held in adult prisons and police stations.
- Age disputes (no birth certificate); juveniles tried as adults; past death sentences of persons who were juveniles at the offence (e.g. Shafqat Hussain, executed 2015 despite age claims **[verify]**).
- Police unaware of the law; custodial abuse.
- Probation officers overloaded.
- Weak data; little coordination between police, courts, probation and social welfare.

## 3. Role of police in juvenile justice

- First point of contact; discretion to warn, refer, divert or arrest.
- Must follow child-friendly procedures: inform guardian and probation officer, no lock-up with adults, no handcuffs, female officers for girls, no coercive interrogation.
- Juvenile/child protection units in police (Punjab Police Child Protection Units; Gender and Child Protection Units in some districts **[verify names]**).
- Prevention: school liaison, community policing, Police Assistance Lines (Khidmat Markaz).
- Challenges: lack of training, corruption, using juveniles as informers, pressure to show arrests.

## 4. Juvenile court process

**US model (Siegel):**
1. **Intake** — screening by a probation/intake officer: dismiss, divert, informal probation, or file a petition.
2. **Detention hearing** — whether the juvenile is held pending adjudication.
3. **Pretrial** — plea bargaining, waiver (transfer) to adult court for serious offences, bail.
4. **Adjudication (trial)** — judge (rarely jury) decides whether the juvenile is "delinquent".
5. **Disposition (sentencing)** — based on a pre-disposition (social history) report: dismissal, fine/restitution, probation, community service, placement in a residential facility, secure correctional institution.
6. **Aftercare** — supervision after release.

**Pakistan (JJSA 2018):** arrest → information to guardian/probation officer → observation home → JJC diversion (where eligible) or juvenile court → trial with legal aid → sentence (probation, rehabilitation centre, fine, community service) → rehabilitation and reintegration.

### Roles
| Actor | Role |
|---|---|
| **Prosecutor** | Screens cases; decides whether to file petition or divert; represents the state; balances child's welfare and public safety |
| **Defence counsel** | Protects rights (*In re Gault*); ensures fair process, age claims, bail, alternatives; legal aid in Pakistan |
| **Juvenile judge** | Ensures due process and the child's best interests; decides adjudication and disposition; supervises rehabilitation; *parens patriae* |
| **Juvenile probation officer** | Intake screening; social investigation report on family, school, environment; supervises probationers; links to services; reports to the court |

## 5. Juvenile correctional institutions

- **Types:** detention centres (pre-trial), shelter care, group homes, foster care, training schools/reformatories, boot camps (evidence weak), wilderness programmes, secure youth institutions.
- **Pakistan:** Borstal Institutions and Juvenile Jails (e.g. Bahawalpur and Faisalabad in Punjab **[verify]**); Youthful Offenders Industrial School, Karachi; juvenile wards in central prisons; under JJSA, Juvenile Rehabilitation Centres.
- **Problems:** overcrowding, mixing with adults, abuse, poor education and vocational training, staff shortages, stigma.

## 6. Probation and non-punitive alternatives

- **Juvenile probation:** conditional release under supervision of a probation officer; the most common disposition worldwide. Conditions: school attendance, curfew, counselling, no contact with certain peers, community service.
- **Juvenile probation services in Pakistan (2021):** under the Probation of Offenders Ordinance 1960 and JJSA 2018; Punjab Probation and Parole Service Act 2019; KP Probation and Parole Act 2021; Sindh still largely under older laws. Problems: very few officers for large caseloads, low budgets, poor training, weak link to social services.
- **Non-punitive alternatives:**
  - Diversion (warnings, cautions, referral to services).
  - **Restorative justice** — victim–offender mediation, family group conferencing (New Zealand's Children, Young Persons and Their Families Act 1989).
  - Community service.
  - Restitution/compensation.
  - Counselling and treatment (Multisystemic Therapy).
  - Education and skills programmes.
  - Electronic monitoring/home detention (limited).
- **Why alternatives:** labelling theory, lower recidivism, cheaper, protects children's rights (CRC Art. 37: detention as last resort and for the shortest time).

## 7. Improving Pakistan's juvenile justice system (2017, 2026)

1. Notify and train **dedicated juvenile courts** in every district; specialised prosecutors and judges.
2. Make **Juvenile Justice Committees** functional; track diversion numbers.
3. Build **observation homes and rehabilitation centres**; end detention of children in adult prisons.
4. **Birth registration** (NADRA's CRMS) and quick age assessment.
5. **Police training** and child protection units in every district.
6. Expand **probation services**; recruit social workers; reasonable caseloads.
7. **Legal aid** through provincial legal aid authorities.
8. **Data system** for juvenile cases (case management, disaggregated statistics).
9. **Coordination** between police, courts, social welfare, education, health.
10. **Partnerships:** UNICEF (child protection systems, birth registration, justice for children), UNODC (criminal justice reform, prisons, trafficking), NGOs (SPARC, Sahil, LAD).
11. **Prevention:** schooling, family support, community programmes (file 04).

## 8. UNODC and UNICEF — plan for Pakistan (2026 question link)

See file 10 for the organisations. A strategic plan: (1) diagnostic assessment with UNODC/UNICEF (legal gaps, institutional capacity, data); (2) costed provincial juvenile justice action plans; (3) UNICEF support for birth registration, child protection case management and social workforce; (4) UNODC support for police and prosecutor training, prison reform (Nelson Mandela Rules), and anti-trafficking (Global Programme against Trafficking in Persons; Pakistan's TIP and SOM Acts 2018); (5) diversion and restorative pilots in selected districts with evaluation; (6) shared data platform; (7) annual joint review with NCRC; (8) sustainability through provincial budgets.
