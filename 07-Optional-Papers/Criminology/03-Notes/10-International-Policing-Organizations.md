# XII. International Policing and Criminal Justice Monitoring Organizations

One question almost every year in Section III: INTERPOL (2018, 2020, 2021), EUROPOL (2021), human trafficking and international bodies (2023), IPA (2024), UNODC and UNICEF (2026). Learn each organisation's founding, headquarters, mandate, tools, Pakistan link, and limits.

## 1. INTERPOL (International Criminal Police Organization)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origins | First International Criminal Police Congress, Monaco, **1914**; **International Criminal Police Commission (ICPC)** founded in **Vienna, 1923**; renamed INTERPOL 1956 |
| Headquarters | **Lyon, France** (General Secretariat); Global Complex for Innovation in **Singapore** (2014–15) |
| Members | **196** member countries **[verify current count]**; Pakistan a member since 1952 **[verify]** |
| Governance | General Assembly; Executive Committee; Secretary General |
| Constitution | Art. 3: strictly forbidden to undertake intervention or activities of a political, military, religious or racial character |
| National Central Bureaus (NCBs) | Each member has one; **Pakistan's NCB is in the FIA, Islamabad** |
| Communication | **I-24/7** secure global police network |
| Databases | Nominal data, fingerprints, DNA, stolen and lost travel documents (SLTD), stolen motor vehicles, stolen works of art, firearms (iARMS), child sexual exploitation images (ICSE) |
| **Notices** | **Red** (arrest/locate for extradition), **Blue** (information on a person), **Green** (warning about a criminal), **Yellow** (missing persons), **Black** (unidentified bodies), **Orange** (imminent threat), **Purple** (modus operandi), **INTERPOL–UN Security Council Special Notice** (sanctions), **Silver** (asset tracing — pilot launched January 2025) **[verify]** |
| Crime areas | Terrorism, organised crime, cyber crime, drugs, trafficking in persons, financial crime and anti-corruption, environmental crime, maritime piracy |
| Financial crime | Anti-money laundering; Global Rapid Intervention of Payments (I-GRIP, 2022) to stop fraudulent transfers **[verify]**; Silver Notice |

**How far has INTERPOL reduced organised crime? (2018)**
- *Successes:* information sharing across 196 countries; Red Notices lead to arrests and extraditions; global operations (e.g. Operation Pangea against illicit medicines; Operation Lionfish against drug trafficking; HAECHI against cyber-enabled financial crime) seize assets and arrest thousands; databases checked billions of times a year; training and capacity building.
- *Limits:* no executive powers — cannot arrest; depends on members' cooperation and capacity; political abuse of Red Notices by authoritarian states (reforms since 2015, Notices and Diffusions Task Force); slow extradition processes; weak NCBs in developing countries; organised crime adapts (crypto, encryption); funding gaps.
- *Verdict:* a force multiplier, not a solution; effective where national agencies are strong.

**INTERPOL and money laundering — Pakistan (2020):** the FIA NCB uses Red Notices to seek fugitives accused of financial crimes; Silver Notices and I-GRIP help trace assets; cooperation with FMU and FATF processes. Limits: extradition treaties (Pakistan has few — with the UK only a memorandum, not a full treaty **[verify]**); political cases; evidentiary standards.

## 2. EUROPOL (European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origins | Europol Drugs Unit 1993/94; Europol Convention 1995; operational **1 July 1999**; became an EU agency in 2010; current regulation 2016 |
| Headquarters | **The Hague, Netherlands** |
| Members | EU member states; partnerships with non-EU states and INTERPOL |
| Mandate | Serious international crime and terrorism affecting two or more EU states |
| Tools | Europol Information System; SIENA secure exchange; analysis projects; **European Cybercrime Centre (EC3, 2013)**; European Counter Terrorism Centre (2016); European Migrant Smuggling Centre; Financial and Economic Crime Centre; IOCTA and SOCTA threat assessments |
| Powers | Analysis and support; joint investigation teams; no arrest powers |

### INTERPOL vs EUROPOL (2021)
| | INTERPOL | EUROPOL |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Global (196 countries) | EU (regional) |
| Legal base | Its own constitution (international organisation) | EU law |
| Founded | 1923 | 1999 (operational) |
| HQ | Lyon | The Hague |
| Focus | Information exchange, notices, databases | Analysis, operational support, threat assessment, EU policy |
| Politics | Neutral; Art. 3 bans political cases | EU priorities (EU Policy Cycle / EMPACT) |
| Powers | No arrests | No arrests; joint investigation teams |

## 3. UNODC (United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime)

- **Founded:** 1997 (merger of UN Drug Control Programme and Centre for International Crime Prevention); named UNODC in 2002. **HQ:** Vienna.
- **Mandate:** drugs, organised crime, corruption, terrorism prevention, crime prevention and criminal justice reform; guardian of the **UN conventions**: drug conventions (1961, 1971, 1988), **UNTOC (Palermo, 2000)** with its protocols on **trafficking in persons**, **smuggling of migrants** and **firearms**; **UNCAC (2003)**.
- **Products:** World Drug Report (annual), Global Report on Trafficking in Persons, UN Standard Minimum Rules (Nelson Mandela Rules), technical assistance.
- **Pakistan:** UNODC Country Office Pakistan — country programme supporting police and prosecution training, prison reform, border management, counter-narcotics (with ANF), anti-trafficking and migrant-smuggling laws (Pakistan's Prevention of Trafficking in Persons Act 2018 and Prevention of Smuggling of Migrants Act 2018 drafted with UNODC support **[verify]**), counter-terrorism legal frameworks, maritime crime, drug-use surveys (Drug Use in Pakistan 2013 — about 6.7 million drug users **[verify]**).

## 4. UNICEF (United Nations Children's Fund)

- **Founded:** 1946 (UN International Children's Emergency Fund); HQ New York.
- **Criminal-justice relevance:** child protection; **justice for children** (children in conflict with the law, child victims and witnesses); birth registration; violence against children; child marriage; child labour; children in armed conflict; promotes the CRC, Beijing Rules, Riyadh Guidelines, Havana Rules.
- **Pakistan:** supports birth registration (with NADRA and provinces), child protection case management systems, juvenile justice reform and JJSA implementation, social-welfare workforce, child protection units, campaigns against child marriage; emergency child protection in floods.

**2026 question — strategic plan for Pakistan:** see file 05 §8 (assessment, provincial action plans, training through UNODC, child protection systems through UNICEF, diversion pilots, data, NCRC review, budget ownership).

## 5. IPA (International Police Association) (2024)

- **Founded:** **1 January 1950** by **Arthur Troop**, a British police sergeant. Motto: *"Servo per Amikeco"* (Esperanto: "Service through Friendship").
- **Nature:** world's largest police **friendship association** — a non-governmental organisation of serving and retired police officers, independent of rank, gender, race, language or religion. Over 70 national sections and several hundred thousand members **[verify numbers]**. Holds consultative status with the UN Economic and Social Council and the Council of Europe **[verify]**.
- **Mission/aims:** develop cultural relations, friendship and mutual understanding among police officers; promote international cooperation in social, cultural and professional fields; exchange of experience; improve the image of police; support the rule of law and human rights.
- **Activities:** International Youth Gatherings; scholarships (Arthur Troop Scholarship); IBZ Gimborn education centre in Germany (seminars); professional exchanges; travel and hospitality network ("IPA Houses"); humanitarian aid; World Congress.
- **Contribution to international police relations:** informal networks that ease formal cooperation; shared training; trust-building; promotes professional standards.
- **Limits:** no operational role; membership skewed to Europe; depends on volunteers. **Pakistan:** an IPA national section **[verify whether Pakistan has an active section]**.

## 6. UNAFEI (UN Asia and Far East Institute for the Prevention of Crime and the Treatment of Offenders)

- **Founded:** **1962** by agreement between the UN and the Government of **Japan**; located in **Tokyo (Akishima/Fuchu area)** **[verify location]**; run by Japan's Ministry of Justice.
- **Role:** training and research for criminal-justice officials of Asia-Pacific and other regions — international training courses and seminars on corrections, juvenile justice, corruption, organised crime, victims, community-based treatment; publishes the Resource Material Series.
- Member of the UN Crime Prevention and Criminal Justice Programme Network. Pakistani police, judges, prosecutors and prison officers have attended its courses.

## 8. How international bodies control human trafficking (2023)

- **Legal standards:** Palermo Trafficking Protocol (2000) — definition: act (recruitment, transport…), means (force, deception, abuse of vulnerability), purpose (exploitation). The "3Ps" (prevention, protection, prosecution) + partnership.
- **UNODC:** Global Report on Trafficking in Persons; model laws; training; Global Programme against Trafficking in Persons; Blue Heart Campaign.
- **INTERPOL:** notices (yellow for missing children), databases, global operations rescuing victims (e.g. Operation Liberterra, 2023).
- **EUROPOL:** European Migrant Smuggling Centre; joint action days.
- **IOM:** victim assistance, return and reintegration.
- **ILO:** forced labour conventions (C29 and 2014 Protocol).
- **US TIP Report:** tier rankings (Pakistan has usually been on Tier 2 or the Tier 2 Watch List **[verify latest]**).
- **Pakistan:** Prevention of Trafficking in Persons Act 2018; Prevention of Smuggling of Migrants Act 2018; FIA's Anti-Human Trafficking Circles; national action plan on TIP and SOM; crackdowns after migrant boat tragedies (Greece, June 2023; others). **Limits:** corruption, porous borders, demand in destination countries, weak victim protection, conflation of smuggling with trafficking.
