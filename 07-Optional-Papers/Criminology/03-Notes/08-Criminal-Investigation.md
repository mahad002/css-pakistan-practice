# IX–X. Criminal Investigation and Techniques of Investigation

The most reliable Section III topic: principles of investigation (2016, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2024), importance and effectiveness (2025), forensic investigation (2022), crime scene and search patterns (2023), interviewing and interrogation (2016, 2021).

---

## 1. Meaning and objectives

- **Criminal investigation:** the lawful process of collecting, preserving, analysing and presenting information and evidence to establish whether a crime occurred, identify the offender, and support prosecution.
- **Objectives:** establish that a crime was committed (corpus delicti); identify and locate the offender; recover property; gather admissible evidence; protect the innocent; present the case to court; prevent further crime.
- **Importance in the CJS (2025):** investigation is the foundation of prosecution and trial; weak investigation causes acquittals, wrongful convictions and loss of public trust. In Pakistan, low conviction rates are widely blamed on poor investigation — reliance on confessions, delayed FIRs, poor scene handling, witnesses turning hostile.

## 2. Principles of criminal investigation

1. **Legality** — act within the Constitution (Arts. 9, 10, 10A, 13, 14) and law (CrPC, Qanun-e-Shahadat, Police Rules).
2. **Objectivity and impartiality** — follow evidence, not assumptions or pressure; avoid tunnel vision (confirmation bias).
3. **Promptness** — speed at the scene (the "golden hour"); evidence degrades; witnesses forget.
4. **Thoroughness/completeness** — leave nothing unexamined; record everything.
5. **Preservation of the crime scene and evidence integrity** — **chain of custody**.
6. **Locard's exchange principle** — every contact leaves a trace (Edmond Locard, who set up a police laboratory in Lyon in 1910).
7. **Documentation** — notes, photographs, sketches, case diary (CrPC s.172).
8. **Use of science and technology** — forensics, databases, digital evidence.
9. **Confidentiality** — protect information, victims, informants.
10. **Respect for human rights** — no torture or coerced confessions (inadmissible; Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act 2022).
11. **Teamwork and coordination** — with forensic experts, prosecutors, other agencies.
12. **Victim and witness care** — trauma-informed; protection.
13. **Supervision and review** — senior officers review progress.
14. **Ethics and integrity.**

**The 3 I's of investigation** (O'Hara / Osterburg and Ward): **Information** (from people and records), **Interrogation/interviewing**, **Instrumentation** (forensic science and technology).

**How these principles contribute to success (2024):** admissible, reliable evidence; correct identification; fewer wrongful convictions; higher conviction rates; public trust.

## 3. Manual of preliminary investigation (first responder's duties)

1. Respond promptly and safely; assess danger.
2. Render aid to the injured; call medical help.
3. Arrest the suspect if present (with legal authority).
4. **Secure and protect the scene** — cordon, log of entries/exits, prevent contamination.
5. Identify, separate and record witnesses; obtain initial statements.
6. Note details: time, weather, lighting, positions, smells, statements made (*res gestae*).
7. Preserve fragile evidence.
8. Inform supervisors, investigation officer and forensic teams.
9. Register FIR (s.154 CrPC) — accurately, without delay.
10. Maintain notes for the case diary.
(Punjab Police Rules 1934, Chapter 25 lay down investigation procedure **[verify chapter]**.)

## 4. Crime scene and search patterns (2023)

**Why the crime scene matters:** it holds physical evidence linking suspect, victim and place (Locard); establishes corpus delicti and modus operandi; corroborates or contradicts statements; reconstructs events; supports forensic analysis. Mishandling contaminates or destroys evidence.

**Crime scene processing:** secure → initial walk-through → document (photograph, video, sketch, notes) → search → collect, package, label → chain of custody → release scene.

**Search patterns:**
| Pattern | Use |
|---|---|
| **Strip/line** | Large open outdoor areas; searchers walk parallel lines |
| **Grid (double strip)** | Thorough; strip search repeated at right angles |
| **Spiral** (inward or outward) | Small, open areas without obstacles; single searcher |
| **Zone/quadrant/sector** | Indoor scenes, houses, vehicles — divide into zones |
| **Wheel/ray** | Circular areas; searchers move outward from centre — rarely used |
| **Link** | Follows logical links between items of evidence |

## 5. Forensic investigation (2022)

**Forensic science (criminalistics):** application of science to legal questions.
| Branch | Work |
|---|---|
| Fingerprints (dactyloscopy) | Unique ridge patterns; Henry classification system (1897, developed in Bengal with Azizul Haque and Hem Chandra Bose); AFIS databases; NADRA biometric database used for identification |
| DNA profiling | Alec Jeffreys (1984–85); first criminal use: Colin Pitchfork case (UK, 1987–88). Pakistan: Zainab murder case (Kasur, 2018) solved through DNA matching by the Punjab Forensic Science Agency |
| Forensic pathology | Cause, manner and time of death; autopsy (dead bodies — 2026 MCQ) |
| Toxicology | Drugs, poisons |
| Serology | Blood and body fluids |
| Ballistics/firearms | Matching bullets and weapons |
| Questioned documents | Handwriting, forgery |
| Digital forensics | Phones, computers, cloud data |
| Forensic odontology, anthropology, entomology | Teeth, bones, insects |
| Polygraph and narco-analysis | Deception detection (psychophysiological); limited evidentiary value; narco tests raise rights issues |
| Voice and video analysis | Audio/video authentication — growing with deepfakes |

**Pakistan's forensic system:** **Punjab Forensic Science Agency (PFSA)**, Lahore — Act 2007, operational 2012, among the best-equipped labs in the region; Sindh Forensic Science Agency and DNA lab (Karachi University) **[verify]**; KP forensic lab Peshawar; Balochistan facilities limited; FIA forensic labs; National Forensic Science Agency (Islamabad) **[verify]**. **Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984, Art. 164:** courts may allow evidence from modern devices or techniques. **Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021:** DNA and medical examination within set times; Special Sexual Offences Investigation Units.

**Hurdles to forensic investigation in Pakistan (2022):**
1. Few labs; long backlogs; distance from districts.
2. Poor crime-scene management — evidence contaminated before forensic teams arrive.
3. Lack of trained investigators and forensic scientists.
4. Weak chain of custody; evidence lost in *malkhanas* (police evidence rooms).
5. Courts' reliance on ocular (eyewitness) evidence; limited judicial familiarity with forensics.
6. Funding and equipment gaps; maintenance.
7. Legal gaps — no national DNA database law; privacy concerns.
8. Delays in sample dispatch; poor coordination between police and labs.
9. Corruption and tampering.
10. Medico-legal officers' shortage, especially female MLOs for sexual-assault cases.

**Remedies:** forensic labs in every province and regional sub-labs; mobile crime-scene units; crime-scene officer cadre; standard operating procedures; a DNA database law with safeguards; judicial training; digital evidence tracking; accreditation (ISO/IEC 17025).

## 6. Intelligence operations

- **Criminal intelligence:** information collected and analysed to understand crime threats and target offenders.
- **Intelligence cycle:** direction/planning → collection → processing/collation → analysis → dissemination → feedback.
- **Sources:** HUMINT (informants, undercover officers), SIGINT (communications), OSINT (open sources, social media), financial intelligence (FMU suspicious transaction reports), surveillance.
- **Use:** organised crime, terrorism, trafficking (file 11 on intelligence-led policing).
- **Ethics:** legal authorisation for surveillance (Investigation for Fair Trial Act 2013 — warrants for interception **[verify]**); informant management; privacy.

## 7. Database investigation

- Use of databases to identify, link and trace: NADRA (CNIC, biometrics, family trees), vehicle registration, mobile SIM records (biometric verification since 2015), criminal records (Punjab Police's Criminal Record Management System; Hotel Eye; tenant registration), FMU, passport/immigration (FIA's IBMS), Interpol databases (I-24/7, stolen and lost travel documents, nominal data), AFIS.
- **Benefits:** speed, linking cases, locating suspects.
- **Risks:** data errors, privacy, misuse, data leaks (no comprehensive data protection law yet **[verify]**).

## 8. Electronic investigation

- Investigation of digital evidence and cyber crime: seizing and imaging devices (write-blockers, hash values), mobile forensics, call detail records (CDRs) and geo-fencing, CCTV and Safe City footage, social-media analysis, cloud data requests (mutual legal assistance), cryptocurrency tracing (blockchain analytics).
- **Law:** PECA 2016 (amended 2025) — procedural powers for preservation, search and seizure of data; Qanun-e-Shahadat Art. 164; Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002.
- **Agencies:** NCCIA (replacing FIA's cyber crime wing under the PECA amendment, 2025), provincial CTDs, Punjab Safe Cities Authority.

## 9. Gathering information from persons; interviewing and interrogation (2016, 2021)

| Interview | Interrogation |
|---|---|
| With victims, witnesses, informants (non-suspects) | With suspects believed to have information/guilt |
| Non-accusatory, cooperative | Accusatory (traditionally) |
| Purpose: gather information | Purpose: obtain truthful account/admission, test alibis |
| Open questions, rapport | Structured; legal safeguards (right to silence, counsel) |

**Techniques for interviewing:**
- Build rapport; open-ended questions; active listening; avoid leading questions.
- **Cognitive interview** (Fisher and Geiselman, 1984): reinstate context; report everything; recall in different orders; change perspectives — improves recall without increasing errors.
- Special care: child witnesses (child-friendly rooms, support persons), victims of sexual violence (female officers, privacy), vulnerable persons.

**Interrogation models:**
- **Reid technique** (Inbau and Reid): behavioural analysis interview + nine-step accusatory interrogation (direct confrontation, theme development, handling denials, overcoming objections, keeping attention, handling passive mood, alternative question, oral confession, written statement). **Criticism:** psychological pressure produces false confessions (e.g. Central Park Five, 1989).
- **PEACE model** (England and Wales, 1992): **P**reparation and planning; **E**ngage and explain; **A**ccount, clarification and challenge; **C**losure; **E**valuation. Investigative, non-coercive, information-gathering — now international best practice; the **Méndez Principles on Effective Interviewing (2021)** promote rapport-based interviewing.
- **Strategic use of evidence (SUE)** — disclose evidence strategically to test accounts.

**Legal framework in Pakistan:**
- Art. 13(b): no person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.
- Art. 14(2): no torture for extracting evidence.
- **Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984 Art. 37–39:** confession caused by inducement, threat or promise is irrelevant; confession to a police officer cannot be proved; confession in police custody not admissible unless made before a magistrate. **Art. 40:** information leading to discovery of a fact is admissible.
- **CrPC s.161** (police examination of witnesses — statements not signed, not substantive evidence), **s.162** (use limited to contradiction), **s.164** (confession or statement recorded by a magistrate with safeguards).
- **Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act 2022** — criminalises torture; FIA to investigate complaints against police.

**Gathering information — sources:** victims, witnesses, informants, suspects, records, open sources, community; techniques: canvassing (door-to-door), tip lines (Punjab's 15/1122 helplines), rewards, media appeals.

## 10. Criminal investigation analysis

- **Crime analysis:** tactical (patterns, series, hot spots), strategic (long-term trends), administrative.
- **Crime mapping and hot-spot analysis** — GIS; predictive policing (data analytics — 2026 MCQ).
- **Link analysis/network analysis** — relationships between suspects, phones, accounts.
- **Offender profiling:** FBI Behavioral Science Unit (1970s) — organised vs disorganised offenders; **signature** (ritualistic behaviour that fulfils the offender's psychological needs, unlike modus operandi, which is functional — 2026 MCQ); investigative psychology (David Canter); **geographic profiling** (Kim Rossmo) — likely residence from crime locations.
- **Case linkage** — comparing MO and signature across crimes.
- **Timeline and event analysis; financial analysis.**

## 11. Making investigation more effective in Pakistan (2025)

1. Separate, specialised, well-resourced investigation wing (Police Order 2002 principle).
2. Training in scientific investigation, interviewing (PEACE), digital forensics.
3. Forensic labs and crime-scene units in every district.
4. Prosecutor–investigator cooperation from the start.
5. Reduce reliance on confessions; end torture; video-record interviews.
6. Witness protection.
7. Digital case management and evidence tracking.
8. Adequate investigation budgets (no "self-financing" by officers).
9. Accountability: supervision, performance indicators, complaints authorities.
10. Legal reform: modernise the Qanun-e-Shahadat for digital evidence; DNA database law.
