# VII. The Criminal Justice System

Asked: crime-control agencies (2016), probation (2016), probation and parole (2017 note), police history (2018, 2022), criminal courts (2021), police challenges (2023, 2025). Pakistan facts checked 24 Nov 2026.

---

## 1. The criminal justice system (CJS)

- **Components:** police (law enforcement) → prosecution → courts → corrections (prisons, probation, parole). Supporting: forensics, legal aid, victim services.
- **Models (Herbert Packer, 1964):**
  - *Crime control model* — efficiency, speed, presumption of guilt in practice, "assembly line".
  - *Due process model* — rights, reliability, presumption of innocence, "obstacle course".
- **Systems view:** the parts are interdependent but often act as a "non-system" — poor coordination, conflicting goals.
- **Formal vs informal agencies:**
  - *Formal:* police, prosecution, courts, prisons, probation, specialised agencies (FIA, ANF, NAB, CTD, NCCIA).
  - *Informal:* family, community elders, *jirga* and *panchayat*, religious leaders, NGOs, private security. Jirgas give quick, cheap settlement but often violate women's rights (vani/swara, honour killings) and due process; the Supreme Court in 2019 declared jirga/panchayat decisions that conflict with law unconstitutional **[verify]**; Sindh High Court banned jirgas in 2004 **[verify]**. ADR under the Alternative Dispute Resolution Act 2017 and provincial laws gives a lawful channel.

## 2. Police and its role

### Functions
Prevention and detection of crime; maintenance of public order; investigation; protection of life and property; traffic; counter-terrorism; VIP security; service delivery (complaints, character certificates); intelligence.

### History of police in the subcontinent and Pakistan (2018, 2022)
| Period | Development |
|---|---|
| Mughal | Kotwal in towns, faujdar in districts, village watchmen (chowkidar) |
| East India Company | Cornwallis (1793) — daroga system under district magistrates; Bombay and Madras experiments; **Sindh Police (1843)** organised by Charles Napier on the Royal Irish Constabulary model — a separate, disciplined police under its own officers |
| **1857 uprising** | Exposed weak control; need for a uniform police |
| **Police Commission 1860** | Recommended a civil police under the district magistrate |
| **Police Act 1861** | Colonial, paramilitary, control-oriented police; SP under the DM (dual control); designed to suppress rebellion, not serve citizens |
| Police Rules | Punjab Police Rules 1934 (still widely applied) |
| Indian Police Commission 1902–03 (Fraser) | Criticised corruption and low pay; created the Indian Police (Service) officer cadre and CID |
| 1947 | Pakistan inherits the 1861 system; Police Service of Pakistan |
| 1950s–90s | Many commissions (1951, 1961, 1970, 1985 **[verify years]**), little change; police politicised and militarised in conflict areas |
| **Police Order 2002** (Musharraf) | Replaced 1861 Act: operational autonomy; **Public Safety Commissions** (national, provincial, district) for oversight; **Police Complaints Authorities**; separation of **investigation** from **watch and ward**; National Police Management Board; DPO reports to elected Nazim; fixed tenure for police chiefs. Amended in 2004 to dilute it |
| After 2009 | Provinces given power over police after the 18th Amendment (2010): **Sindh** and **Balochistan** reverted to the 1861 Act in 2011; Sindh revived a modified Police Order 2002 in 2019; **KP Police Act 2017** (strong IGP autonomy; Regional Police Complaint Authorities; Safety Commissions); **Punjab** continues with an amended Police Order 2002; Islamabad under Police Order 2002 **[verify Balochistan's current law]** |
| 2014–present | Counter-Terrorism Departments (CTDs) in provinces; Safe City projects (Punjab Safe Cities Authority 2016); Motorway Police (1997) as a model of professional policing; Punjab **Crime Control Department (CCD)**, early 2025 |

### Challenges police face (2023, 2025 — repeat question)
1. **Colonial structure and culture** — control over service; "thana culture", custodial torture.
2. **Political interference** — postings and transfers by politicians; police used against opponents; short IGP tenures.
3. **Under-resourcing** — low police-to-population ratio; poor vehicles, equipment, forensic support; small investigation budgets.
4. **Low pay and poor working conditions** — long hours, no shifts, poor housing → corruption and low morale.
5. **Corruption** — TI Pakistan's surveys repeatedly rank police among the most corrupt sectors **[verify latest NCPS ranking]**.
6. **Weak investigation** — reliance on confessions, poor crime-scene handling, weak forensics → low conviction rates.
7. **Terrorism and militancy** — police are frontline targets in KP and Balochistan; casualties high.
8. **Rapid urbanisation and street crime** — Karachi, Lahore; mobile snatching; katcha-area dacoits with heavy weapons.
9. **Low public trust** — non-registration of FIRs; citizens avoid police.
10. **Legal framework** — outdated laws and rules; Police Order not fully implemented; oversight bodies (Public Safety Commissions) never functional in many provinces.
11. **Human-rights concerns** — encounters and extrajudicial killings: HRCP documented hundreds of deaths in alleged encounters by Punjab's CCD between April and December 2025 ([Al Jazeera, Feb 2026](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/18/pakistans-punjab-police-kill-900-people-in-eight-months-whats-going-on); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1948812/deadly-surge-in-police-encounters-in-punjab-alarming-hrcp)); custodial deaths (Torture and Custodial Death Act 2022 assigns investigation to FIA).
12. **Coordination** — between police, prosecution, CTDs, intelligence agencies, Rangers, FC.
13. **Gender** — very few women officers; women victims reluctant to report.
14. **Technology gap** — cyber crime, crypto, AI-enabled fraud.

### Reforms (way forward)
- Full implementation of Police Order 2002 principles: operational autonomy with democratic oversight (Public Safety Commissions), fixed tenure, independent complaints authorities.
- Separate, specialised and well-funded **investigation wing**; forensic capacity (PFSA model in each province).
- Community policing and police–public liaison (file 11).
- Merit-based recruitment and promotion; better pay and shifts; welfare.
- Training in human rights, scientific investigation, gender sensitivity.
- Technology: CCTV, Safe City, crime mapping, digital FIR, e-challan, data analytics.
- Accountability: body cameras, judicial inquiries into encounters, implementation of the Torture Act 2022.
- Increase women in police; gender and child protection desks.
- Coordinated criminal justice committees at district level.
- Examples of good practice: Motorway Police; KP's Dispute Resolution Councils (DRCs, 2014) for minor disputes; Punjab's Khidmat Markaz and Police Station Record Management System; Sindh's IGP-led reforms **[verify specifics]**.

## 3. Trial and conviction of offenders

### Criminal courts in Pakistan
| Court | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| Magistrates (Judicial Magistrates of Section 30, first class, second class) | Minor offences; first-class magistrates can sentence up to 3 years **[verify]** |
| Sessions Courts (Sessions Judge, Additional Sessions Judges) | Serious offences, murder; can pass death sentence (confirmation by High Court) |
| High Courts | Appeals, revisions, confirmation of death sentences, bail, writs |
| Supreme Court | Final appeal (criminal) |
| Federal Shariat Court | Appeals in Hudood cases |
| Special courts | Anti-Terrorism Courts (ATA 1997); CNSA special courts; Accountability Courts (NAB); Banking courts; Anti-Corruption courts; Customs and Taxation; Gender-Based Violence courts (Lahore 2017 model, expanded); **Special courts under the Anti-Rape Act 2021**; juvenile courts; Model Criminal Trial Courts (2019 initiative) |
| Military courts | For offences under the Army Act; civilians tried in 2015–19 under the 21st Amendment; civilians accused in the 9 May 2023 riots tried after the Supreme Court Constitutional Bench restored the relevant Army Act provisions (5–2, May 2025), with a direction to legislate a right of appeal ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1909300)) |

### Procedure (CrPC 1898)
FIR (s.154) → investigation by police (s.156–173) → arrest and remand (s.167; production before magistrate within 24 hours — Art. 10) → challan/report under s.173 → cognizance → charge → prosecution evidence → statement of accused (s.342) → defence evidence → arguments → judgment → appeal.

### Problems of criminal courts (2021)
1. **Delays and backlog** — over two million cases pending across Pakistan's courts **[verify latest Law and Justice Commission figure]**; frequent adjournments.
2. **Low conviction rates** — weak investigation, hostile witnesses, poor forensic evidence, compromise in compoundable offences.
3. **Witness problems** — no effective protection (Sindh Witness Protection Act 2013; Punjab Witness Protection Act 2018 — weakly implemented).
4. **Shortage of judges** and support staff; poor infrastructure.
5. **Prosecution–police gap** — prosecutors not involved early; poor coordination.
6. **Corruption and influence.**
7. **Under-trial detention** — three-quarters of prisoners await trial (NCHR/JPP, Oct 2026).
8. **Misuse of bail and stay orders.**
9. **Language and complexity** — English procedures, poor legal aid.
10. **Parallel informal justice** (jirgas).

**Reforms:** case-management systems and e-courts; model courts (2019); time limits; plea bargaining (limited); ADR for minor cases; witness protection; forensic labs; more judges; legal aid authorities (Legal Aid and Justice Authority Act 2020 **[verify]**); National Judicial (Policy Making) Committee decisions; CrPC amendments (e.g. the Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Act 2026 on sections 513–514 passed by the National Assembly in March 2026 **[verify content and Senate status]**).

## 4. Role of prosecutors

- Represent the state; review police investigations; decide whether evidence supports prosecution; conduct trials; advise police; protect victims' interests and the accused's rights (minister of justice, not just advocate).
- **Pakistan:** independent prosecution services created in the 2000s — KP (2005), Punjab Criminal Prosecution Service (Constitution, Functions and Powers) Act 2006, Sindh Criminal Prosecution Service Act 2009, Balochistan (2003) **[verify]**; Prosecutor-General in each province; federal prosecutors for FIA and NAB (NAB Prosecutor-General).
- **Problems:** prosecutors excluded from investigation; heavy caseloads; low pay; political pressure; poor training; weak case-screening → weak cases reach court.
- **Reforms:** prosecutor-led or prosecutor-guided investigation; case screening; training; performance indicators (conviction rates, timelines); digital case files.

## 5. Prisons, probation and parole

### Prisons — the situation in 2026
- **Legal framework:** Prisons Act 1894; Prisoners Act 1900; Pakistan Prison Rules 1978 (provincial versions); **Sindh Prisons and Corrections Service Act 2019** (amended 2025) and Sindh Prison Rules (reformative focus); Punjab and KP rules **[verify Punjab's new Prisons Act status]**; Federal Ombudsman's prison reform implementation (Supreme Court-directed) **[verify]**.
- **Data (Annual Prisons Data Report 2026, National Commission for Human Rights and Justice Project Pakistan, October 2026):** **110,513 prisoners** against capacity of **68,832** — occupancy **160.6%**; **82,988 under-trial prisoners (75.09%)** — more than total capacity; **28,000+ held under the Control of Narcotic Substances Act** ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2634153/prisons-operating-at-161-capacity); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/pakistan/three-in-four-prisoners-in-pakistan-await-trial-jails-operate-at-161-percent-capacity-report-3003563)). Punjab alone held 74,773 prisoners in 45 prisons against capacity of 39,204 (about 191%) in September 2026 (HRCP figures reported by Tribune).
- **Problems:** overcrowding; under-trial majority; poor health care (TB, hepatitis, HIV); drug use inside prisons; corruption; violence; mixing of first offenders and hardened criminals ("universities of crime"); radicalisation inside prisons; juveniles and women in poor conditions; death-row population large; mentally ill prisoners; lack of education and vocational training; inadequate staff training.
- **Reforms:** bail reform and speedy trials for under-trials; plea bargaining for petty cases; non-custodial sentences; probation and parole expansion; separate facilities for juveniles, women, first offenders; health care through health departments; education (AIOU programmes in prisons), vocational training and fair wages; de-radicalisation; independent monitoring (Board of Visitors, NCHR); implementing the **UN Nelson Mandela Rules (2015)** and **Bangkok Rules (2010)** for women prisoners; Sindh's corrections model as a template.

### Probation
- **Meaning:** a court sentence in which the offender, instead of imprisonment, is released under supervision of a probation officer on conditions (good conduct, reporting, residence, treatment).
- **Origins:** John Augustus (Boston, 1841); Massachusetts probation law (1878); England Probation of Offenders Act 1907.
- **Pakistan:** **Probation of Offenders Ordinance 1960** and Rules 1961; **CrPC s.562** (release on probation of good conduct — admonition or bond); **Punjab Probation and Parole Service Act 2019** (a unified service under a Director General, parole boards with independent members) ([Punjab Prisons](https://prisons.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/THE%20PUNJAB%20PROBATION%20AND%20PAROLE%20SERVICE%20ACT%202019.pdf)); **KP Probation and Parole Act 2021**; Sindh still relies largely on older laws ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1459272) **[verify current Sindh position]**).
- **Conditions of probation (2016 question):** under the 1960 Ordinance the court may release an offender on probation for one to three years on a bond, with conditions: keep the peace and be of good behaviour; appear when called; live where directed; abstain from intoxicants; avoid bad company; attend school/work/treatment; report to the probation officer; compensation to victims. The court considers age, character, antecedents, physical and mental condition, nature of offence, and extenuating circumstances. Breach → offender can be sentenced for the original offence. Offences punishable with death or life imprisonment are excluded **[verify the exact exclusion; a 2025 MCQ asked whether a s.392 PPC robbery offender can be released on probation]**.
- **Advantages:** avoids prison stigma and contamination; cheaper (a fraction of prison cost); maintains family and job; reduces overcrowding; rehabilitative.
- **Problems in Pakistan:** few probation officers (each with very large caseloads), courts rarely use it, lack of awareness, weak supervision, no link to treatment and employment services.

### Parole
- **Meaning:** conditional early release of a convicted prisoner after serving part of the sentence, under supervision; recall on breach.
- **Origins:** Alexander Maconochie's "mark system" (Norfolk Island, 1840); Walter Crofton's Irish system (ticket of leave, 1850s); Elmira Reformatory, New York (1876, Zebulon Brockway) — indeterminate sentences and parole.
- **Pakistan:** **Good Conduct Prisoners' Probational Release Act 1926** and Rules 1927; Punjab 2019 and KP 2021 laws created parole boards.
- **Probation vs parole:** probation is a court sentence *instead of* prison; parole is release *from* prison by an executive/parole board after part of the sentence. Both are community supervision.
- **Remission:** sentence reductions for good conduct and on special occasions (e.g. presidential remissions on national days).

## 6. Role of crime-control agencies (2016)

Federal: FIA, ANF, NAB, NCCIA (cyber, 2025), Intelligence Bureau, Counter-Terrorism under NACTA coordination, Pakistan Coast Guards, Maritime Security Agency, Customs, Frontier Corps and Rangers (civil armed forces in aid of civil power), Motorway Police (NHMP), Railway Police, Islamabad Police. Provincial: police, CTDs, Special Branch, Anti-Corruption Establishments, Excise and Taxation (narcotics), Levies (Balochistan, merged into police in many districts **[verify]**). Roles: prevention, detection, investigation, intelligence, border control, financial investigation. Problems: overlap, rivalry, poor data sharing — NACTA's coordination role and the National Action Plan sought to fix this.
