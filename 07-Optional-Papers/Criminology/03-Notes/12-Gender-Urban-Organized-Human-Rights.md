## 1. Gender and crime in urban and rural Pakistan

### Theory
- **Gender gap:** men commit most crime everywhere (especially violent crime); women more often victims of sexual and domestic violence.
- **Explanations:** socialisation (masculinity norms — "doing gender", Messerschmidt), opportunity and control (girls more supervised — Hagan's power-control theory, 1985), strain (Agnew: men respond with anger), biology (testosterone, contested).
- **Chivalry hypothesis** (Pollak, 1950): women treated leniently by justice system — contested; Pakistani women prisoners often face harsher social stigma.
- **Feminist criminology:** patriarchy shapes both women's victimisation and the justice system's response; intersectionality (class, caste, religion).
- **Women as offenders in Pakistan:** a small share of prisoners (about 1–2% **[verify]**); offences: drug carrying (often coerced), hudood-related cases historically, theft, murder in domestic contexts; many with children in prison.

### Women as victims — patterns
| Rural | Urban |
|---|---|
| Honour killings (karo-kari in Sindh, siyahkari in Balochistan) | Domestic violence in nuclear families under economic stress |
| Exchange marriages (watta satta), vani/swara (giving girls to settle disputes) | Street harassment, sexual harassment at work and on transport |
| Forced and child marriage | Cyber harassment, blackmail, non-consensual sharing of images (PECA s.21) |
| Deprivation of inheritance; land disputes | Acid attacks (also rural), stalking |
| Jirga decisions; panchayat-ordered punishments | Rape and sexual assault (Motorway case 2020; Noor Mukadam 2021) |
| Bonded labour (brick kilns); trafficking | Trafficking for domestic work and sex work; online exploitation |
| Limited access to police and courts; long distances; female officers rare | Better access but low trust; social stigma prevents reporting |

**Data sources:** HRCP annual *State of Human Rights* reports (hundreds of honour killings each year **[verify latest figure]**); Sustainable Social Development Organisation; Aurat Foundation's violence data; Punjab Women Protection Authority.

**Laws and institutions:** see file 09 §5 (Anti-Rape Act 2021, honour-killing law 2016, domestic violence laws, harassment law 2010, ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025). Women police stations (since 1994), gender protection units, Violence Against Women Centre (Multan, 2017), women's protection authorities, Ombudsperson for harassment, National Commission on the Status of Women.

**Way forward:** enforce existing laws; female police and medico-legal officers; shelters; legal aid; end jirga settlements in rape and honour cases; sensitise police and courts; women's economic empowerment; data collection; awareness through media and religious scholars.

## 2. Crime and urbanisation

- **Urbanisation in Pakistan:** about 39% urban in the 2023 census **[verify]**, though many "rural" areas are peri-urban; Karachi and Lahore megacities; rapid growth of informal settlements.
- **Why urbanisation raises crime:** anonymity and weaker informal control (Durkheim, Wirth's "Urbanism as a Way of Life", 1938); social disorganisation in transition zones (Chicago School); inequality and visible wealth (relative deprivation); more targets and fewer guardians (routine activity); unemployment among migrant youth; drug markets; gangs; overstretched police and services; land mafias.
- **Typical urban crimes:** street crime, robbery and dacoity, vehicle theft, burglary, extortion (bhatta), target killings, kidnapping for ransom, cyber fraud, drug peddling, land grabbing.
- **Karachi example:** political-ethnic violence and target killings (1990s–2013); extortion and Lyari gang war; Rangers-led Karachi operation from 2013 reduced target killings and kidnappings sharply **[verify percentages]**; street crime remains high (CPLC data on mobile snatching and vehicle theft).
- **Controlling robberies, dacoities and street crime (2023):** see file 11 §4 (hot spots, Safe City, rapid response, CPTED, community policing, IMEI blocking, prosecution, youth programmes, weapons control, coordination). For **katcha-area dacoits** (riverine belt of Sindh and south Punjab): joint police–Rangers operations, roads and connectivity, removing political patronage, development and education, bounty and surrender schemes.

## 3. Organised crime and white-collar crime (Section IV angle)

- **Transnational organised crime threats to Pakistan:** Afghan opiates and methamphetamine transit; arms smuggling; human smuggling routes to Iran, Turkey and Europe; fuel and goods smuggling from Iran and Afghanistan (costing billions in lost revenue **[verify]**); trade-based money laundering; cyber-enabled fraud (call-centre scams, crypto scams).

- **White-collar crime in Pakistan:** tax evasion (narrow tax base), corruption in procurement, banking fraud, fake housing schemes, sugar/wheat/fertiliser hoarding, power-sector theft and circular debt, NAB plea bargains (criticised as legitimising corruption).
- **Responses:** FIA, ANF, NAB, FBR's intelligence wing, Customs, SECP, Competition Commission; UNTOC and UNCAC; FATF compliance; asset recovery; regional cooperation.

## 4. Human rights abuses and protection — children, women, minorities

### Children
- **Abuses:** child sexual abuse (Kasur scandal 2015 — hundreds of videos; Zainab case 2018); Sahil's *Cruel Numbers* records thousands of reported cases each year (over 4,000 in recent years **[verify latest]**); child labour (domestic workers — Rizwana and Tayyaba cases); child marriage; trafficking; street children; juveniles in adult prisons; corporal punishment; madrassa abuse cases.
- **Laws:** JJSA 2018; ICT Child Protection Act 2018; Zainab Alert, Response and Recovery Act 2020 (ZARRA agency for missing children); ICT Domestic Workers Act 2022 **[verify]**; Punjab Domestic Workers Act 2019; child marriage restraint laws (Sindh 2013; ICT 2025; Punjab 1929 Act with amendments — age 16 for girls **[verify]**); PPC amendments 2016 on child pornography and sexual abuse (s.292A–C, s.377A–B); National Commission on the Rights of the Child (2017).
- **Protection:** child protection units and bureaus; helplines (1121 in Punjab **[verify]**); UNICEF support; CRC reporting.

### Women
See §1 and file 09.

### Minorities (religious and others)
- **Abuses:** misuse of blasphemy laws (PPC s.295–298) and mob violence (Gojra 2009; Joseph Colony 2013; Jaranwala 2023; Sargodha 2024 **[verify]**); forced conversions and marriages of Hindu and Christian girls (especially in Sindh); attacks on places of worship; discrimination in jobs (sanitation work reserved for Christians in adverts); violence against Ahmadis; transgender persons' killings.
- **Protection:** Art. 20 (freedom of religion), Art. 36 (protection of minorities), Art. 25; Supreme Court's **2014 Jillani judgment** (Suo Motu Case 1 of 2014) directing a national commission for minorities and a special police force for worship places **[verify]**; National Commission for Minorities (2020, statutory status debated); 5% job quota for minorities; Punjab's protection measures after Jaranwala.

### Role of civil society and NGOs
- **Documentation and monitoring:** HRCP (since 1987), Sahil, SPARC, Aurat Foundation, Justice Project Pakistan (death row, prisons), Digital Rights Foundation (cyber harassment helpline), Center for Social Justice (minorities).
- **Legal aid and litigation:** AGHS Legal Aid Cell (Asma Jahangir), Legal Aid Society.

- **Advocacy:** law reform (Anti-Rape Act, child marriage, torture law); public awareness.

**Statutory human-rights institutions:** National Commission for Human Rights (Act 2012) — co-authored the Oct 2026 prison data report; NCSW; NCRC; provincial commissions; Ministry of Human Rights.

## 5. Declining trust in institutions and deviance (2026)

- **Concepts:** **legitimacy** — belief that authorities have the right to govern and should be obeyed; **procedural justice** (Tom Tyler, *Why People Obey the Law*, 1990): people obey when treated fairly (voice, neutrality, respect, trustworthy motives), more than from fear of punishment.
- **How distrust produces deviance:**
  - *Non-cooperation:* not reporting crime, refusing to testify, avoiding FIRs → impunity.
  - *Self-help and vigilantism:* mob justice, lynchings, private militias, jirgas.
  - *Rule-breaking as resistance:* tax evasion ("why pay to a corrupt state"), power theft, violent protests.
  - *Anomie* (Durkheim/Merton): norms lose force when institutions seem unfair; people innovate.
  - *Conflict theory:* perceived elite capture and selective justice delegitimise law.
  - *Control theory:* weakened "belief" bond (Hirschi).
  - *Neutralisation:* "condemnation of the condemners".
- **Pakistani context:** low trust in police and courts; perceptions of selective accountability; political polarisation; economic hardship and inflation; misinformation; extrajudicial killings and enforced disappearances; delays in justice.
- **Restoring trust:** procedural justice training for police; transparency (body cameras, data publication); fair and speedy trials; independent oversight of police; consistent rule of law for elites; community policing; responsive grievance systems; reducing corruption; inclusive politics; credible communication.
