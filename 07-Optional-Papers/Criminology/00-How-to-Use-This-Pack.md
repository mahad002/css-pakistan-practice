## 3. What examiners reward

1. **Definition + theory + Pakistan.** Start with a precise definition (with author), use a named theory, then apply it to Pakistani evidence (laws, cases, data, institutions).
2. **Current Pakistani facts.** Prisons at about 161% occupancy with 75% under-trial (NCHR/JPP, Oct 2026); Anti-Rape Act 2021; JJSA 2018; ATA amendment 2025; PECA amendment 2025 and the NCCIA; Punjab's CCD and encounter controversy; FATF exit (2022); GTI 2025 rank 2.
3. **Theories applied to new problems** — 2026 asked about misinformation, polarisation, AI, deepfakes and trust in institutions. Practise applying strain, labelling, conflict, social learning, routine activity and symbolic interactionism to unfamiliar topics.
4. **Structure:** headings, short paragraphs, a table or flowchart (criminal justice process, juvenile court process, Merton's typology, stages of money laundering, search patterns).
5. **Balance:** for police encounters, military courts or PECA, present the state's case and the rights-based critique, then a reasoned view.
6. **Way forward** with named institutions (provincial police, NCCIA, FIA, ANF, FMU, JJCs, probation services, NACTA).

## 4. The answer template

**Plan (3 minutes):** underline command words and parts; outline 5–7 headings.

1. **Introduction (80–120 words):** context → definition with author → thesis answering the question.
2. **Conceptual frame (150–200 words):** key concepts; theory with author and year; a table if comparing.
3. **Main body (450–600 words, 4–6 headings):** each heading a claim; point → evidence (theory, law, case, statistic) → link to question.
4. **Pakistan section (150–200 words)** if not woven in: laws, institutions, cases, data.
5. **Critical analysis (100–150 words):** limits, counter-arguments.
6. **Way forward (80–120 words):** 5–8 specific measures.
7. **Conclusion (50–80 words).**

**Short notes (10 marks, ~450 words):** definition → origin/author → features → Pakistani example/law → one criticism → conclusion. **5-mark definitions** (2020, 2025 style): 120–150 words each — definition, author, one example.

## 6. Common mistakes

1. Preparing only "easy" topics and finding a section with nothing you can answer.
2. Answering with Western examples only; no Pakistani laws or data.
3. Confusing founders: Beccaria vs Lombroso; Durkheim (anomie) vs Merton (strain); Lemert (primary/secondary deviance) vs Becker (*Outsiders*); Shaw–McKay vs Park–Burgess.
4. Mixing up probation (court sentence instead of prison) and parole (early release from prison).
5. Treating JJSO 2000 as current — it was replaced by JJSA 2018.
6. Writing theory without critique.
7. Ignoring new laws: ATA amendment 2025, PECA amendment 2025, ICT child marriage law 2025, Anti-Rape amendment bill 2026.
8. Moralising instead of analysing (e.g. on terrorism or blasphemy-related violence).
9. Unequal answers; leaving the fourth answer short.
10. Neglecting MCQs — FPSC repeats founders, dates, cyber terms and police-law articles.
