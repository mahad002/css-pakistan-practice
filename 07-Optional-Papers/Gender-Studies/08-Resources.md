# Resources — Gender Studies

Keep it small. The notes in `03-Notes/` cover the syllabus; use these to deepen, update figures and check facts. **[FREE]** = free to read online; **[OFFICIAL]** = government, UN or other primary source.

## 1. Core books (buy or borrow one or two)

| Book | Why | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| Rosemarie Tong (and Tina Fernandes Botts), *Feminist Thought: A More Comprehensive Introduction* (5th ed., 2017 or later) | On the FPSC list; the standard account of every feminist school | Read the chapters on liberal, radical, Marxist/socialist, psychoanalytic and postmodern feminism only (section III). One chapter a week in Mar–Apr 2027 |
| Naila Kabeer, *Reversed Realities: Gender Hierarchies in Development Thought* (1994) | On the FPSC list; best critique of WID/WAD/GAD and empowerment | Chapters 1–3 and the empowerment chapter for section V |
| Caroline Moser, *Gender Planning and Development* (1993) | On the FPSC list; triple roles, practical/strategic needs, policy approaches | Chapters 1–4 only |
| Khawar Mumtaz and Farida Shaheed, *Women of Pakistan: Two Steps Forward, One Step Back?* (1987) | On the FPSC list; the classic history of Pakistan's women's movement to the 1980s | Read for section IV (Pakistan); add post-1987 events from the notes |
| Harriet Bradley, *Gender* (2nd ed., 2013) | On the FPSC list; short, clear introduction to theory | Use for sections I–II |
| A CSS guide (e.g. Caravan or JWT Gender Studies books) | Past-paper orientation | Use only to check question coverage; do not copy their answers. Many contain errors and outdated data |

## 3. Pakistani scholars worth citing (read short pieces, not whole books)

Rubina Saigol (state of the discipline; textbooks and gender); Farzana Bari (women in politics; women's studies); Farida Shaheed and Khawar Mumtaz (movement history; Shirkat Gah); Afiya Shehrbano Zia (*Faith and Feminism in Pakistan*, 2018); Saba Gul Khattak (gender and development; NGO critiques) [verify title of specific works before citing]; Nighat Said Khan (*Locating the Self*, 1998); Riffat Hassan (Islamic feminist theology); Asma Barlas (*Believing Women in Islam*, 2002).

## 4. How to use resources in the study plan

1. **Feb–Jun 2027**: notes + one core book chapter a week.
2. **Jul–Sep 2027**: official sources for figures; build your own one-page "numbers sheet" from the WEF, PBS and Economic Survey.
3. **Oct 2027–Jan 2028**: past papers and model answers; update figures from the June 2027 Economic Survey and the 2027 Global Gender Gap Report.
4. Never cite a figure you have not seen in an official or reputable source. If unsure, write "about" or leave it out.
