## Paper format (confirmed from the 2016–2026 papers)

- 100 marks, 3 hours.
- **Part I:** Q1, 20 MCQs × 1 mark, on a separate OMR sheet, taken back after **30 minutes**. No negative marking (stated on the 2024–2026 papers). Options are usually A, B, C and "D) None of these".
- **Part II:** Q2–Q8, seven questions of 20 marks. **Attempt any four.** All parts of a question must be answered in one place. Q8 is often "short notes" (two × 10 marks), sometimes "any two of three".
- No calculator (Gender Studies is not on FPSC's calculator list).

## What changed in 2024–2026

1. **Longer, layered questions.** 2016–2023 questions were one line ("Discuss WID, WAD, GAD"). From 2024 they name a theory **and** a Pakistani case (Fathalla's "maternity death road"; the Punjab 2016 Act; world-system theory and "gendered globalisation"; queer theory and the Transgender Act).
2. **Current data is now examined.** 2026 asked directly about the WEF Global Gender Gap Report 2025 and GSMA mobile gender gap figures. You must carry dated numbers.
3. **Off-syllabus theory.** Intersectionality (2025, 2026), ecofeminism (2026), language (2020), masculinity studies (2026). The 2026 MCQs also tested Foucault, Spivak, Kandiyoti, Collins, standpoint theory and "glass cliff".
4. **Section I has vanished since 2023.** Do not drop it; it was asked seven times and could return.
5. **Case studies come as full questions** (Sharmeen 2020, Malala 2025). Mukhtaran Mai is the one not yet set alone.

## MCQ pattern

- 2024–2026 MCQs mix theorists and books (Butler, de Beauvoir, Friedan, Cixous, Showalter, Connell, West and Zimmerman), UN milestones (Beijing 1995, CEDAW, SDG 5), Pakistan facts (harassment law 2010, first Constituent Assembly women members, NA reserved seats, honour-killing law 2016, GGGR rank) and concepts (glass ceiling, glass cliff, sticky floor, androgyny, practical vs strategic needs).
- Several have "D) None of these" as the right answer (2026 Q16 sati reform; Q20 matrix of domination, Collins was not listed). Read every option.
