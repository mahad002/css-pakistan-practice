## 3. The answer template that scores in this subject

Gender Studies examiners want three things together: **a concept defined with its theorist, Pakistani evidence, and a balanced judgement.** Use this for every 20-mark answer.

**Step 0 — 2 minutes.** Underline the command word (discuss, critically analyse, evaluate, compare) and each part. In 2024–2026 most questions have two or three parts; give each part its own heading.

1. **Outline (5–6 lines).** Numbered headings. Shows structure at a glance.
2. **Introduction (80–120 words).** Context sentence → definition with source (e.g. "DEVAW 1993 defines…", "Kabeer defines empowerment as…") → **thesis** answering the question.
3. **Theory section (150–250 words).** The relevant theory/theorists, with dates and key terms. One small table (e.g. WID/WAD/GAD, waves) saves time and earns marks.
4. **Pakistan section (250–350 words).** Laws with years, institutions, two or three dated figures (GGGR 2026, LFS 2024–25, Census 2023), one case (Mukhtaran Mai, Qandeel Baloch, Aurat March, 2022 floods).
5. **Critical analysis (100–150 words).** Strengths and weaknesses; the opposing view stated fairly (especially on contested topics: Aurat March, Transgender Act, quotas, religious debates).
6. **Way forward (80–120 words).** Five or six specific measures, each naming who acts (Parliament, NCSW, ECP, provincial governments, HEC).
7. **Conclusion (60–80 words).** Restate the judgement in fresh words.

**Signals of a strong answer:** correct names and dates (Boserup 1970, Moser 1993, Crenshaw 1989); one diagram or table; Pakistani laws with years; dated data with sources; neutral tone on contested issues; equal length for all four answers.

**Short notes (10 marks each):** definition → origin/theorist → 3–4 key points → Pakistani example → one-line evaluation. About 400–450 words each.

## 4. Common mistakes

1. **Writing everything about a topic instead of answering the parts.** 2024–26 questions are layered ("theory X AND Pakistan Y AND law Z").
2. **No theorist or date.** "Feminists say…" earns little; "Heidi Hartmann's dual-systems theory (1979)" earns marks.
3. **Out-of-date data.** Citing the 2018 ranking when the 2026 report is out. Pakistan was 148/148 in 2025 and 143/145 in 2026.
4. **One-sided or polemical writing** on religion, the Aurat March, transgender rights or quotas. State both views, then judge. Avoid emotive language.
5. **Invented statistics or quotations.** Examiners know the standard ones. If unsure, write "about" or omit.
6. **Ignoring Pakistan.** Almost every question rewards Pakistani laws and cases, even theory questions.
7. **Ignoring men and masculinities** in "gender" questions; mixing up "sex" and "gender".
8. **Confusing WID/WAD/GAD** or the waves. Learn the comparison tables cold.
9. **Skipping the off-syllabus areas** (intersectionality, ecofeminism, digital gap). They were three of seven questions in 2026.
10. **Neglecting MCQs.** 2026 MCQs tested Foucault, Kandiyoti, Spivak, Collins and "glass cliff". Aim for 14+/20.

- Aurat March 2027 developments; any major GBV case.
