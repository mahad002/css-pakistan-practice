## I. Introduction

- **Gender studies**: interdisciplinary study of how gender is constructed and how it shapes power, identity and inequality. Gender is social, relational, a power system, intersectional; knowledge is gendered.
- **Women's studies (1970s, San Diego State 1970)** → **gender studies (1980s–90s)**: from women to gender relations; adds men, masculinities, queer theory, intersectionality. Oakley (1972), Rubin (1975), Scott (1986).
- **GS vs WS**: subject (women vs gender system), men (absent vs studied), theory (liberal/radical/Marxist vs + constructionism, postmodern, queer), aim (visibility vs transformation). Critics: "gender" can depoliticise.
- **Multidisciplinary**: sociology, anthropology (Mead), psychology (Bem, Gilligan), economics (Waring), politics (Pateman), history (Scott), law (MacKinnon), literature (Showalter, Cixous), biology (Fausto-Sterling), development (Moser, Kabeer), media (Mulvey).
- **Autonomy vs integration**: Bowles and Klein (1983). Autonomy = own departments (protects knowledge; risk of ghetto). Integration = mainstream into all disciplines (reach; risk of co-optation). Answer: **dual strategy**.
- **Pakistan**: Women's Studies centres 1989 (QAU, Karachi, Punjab, Peshawar, Balochistan); renamed gender studies; CSS optional since 2016 (8,245 candidates in 2025). Problems: funding, Western syllabi, donor dependence, "Western" stigma, weak research, little policy link. Saigol, Bari, Khan (*Locating the Self*).

## II. Social construction

- **de Beauvoir (1949)**: "becomes a woman". **Berger and Luckmann (1966)**: habit → institution → appears natural.
- **Mead (1935)**: Arapesh, Mundugumor, Tchambuli. **Money (1955)** gender role. **Stoller (1968)**. **Oakley (1972)** sex vs gender. **Ortner (1974)** nature/culture. **West and Zimmerman (1987)** doing gender. **Lorber (1994)** gender as institution.
- **Is sex constructed?** Yes: intersex (Fausto-Sterling 1993, 2000), one-sex to two-sex (Laqueur 1990), Butler (sex "always already gender"), measurement choices, third genders. No/partly: reproductive binary, medicine, gender-critical feminists, religious law (khunsa). **Biosocial conclusion.**
- **Queer theory**: de Lauretis (1990), Butler (*Gender Trouble* 1990, performativity), Sedgwick (1990), Foucault (1976, repressive hypothesis). Anti-essentialism, heteronormativity, deconstruction. Critics: abstract, ignores material, Western.
- **Masculinities (Connell)**: hegemonic, complicit, subordinated, marginalised; emphasised femininity; patriarchal dividend. Kimmel, Halberstam, hooks, Bem (androgyny).
- **Origins of roles**: biological, psychoanalytic (Chodorow), social learning (Bandura), cognitive (Kohlberg), functionalist (Parsons), feminist, interactionist, post-structuralist.
- **Pakistan**: son preference, textbooks, dramas, honour codes; Transgender Act 2018 → FSC 2023.

- **Third (1990s)**: Rebecca Walker 1992; intersectionality (Crenshaw 1989); diversity, identity, riot grrrl.
- **Fourth (2012–)**: digital; #MeToo 2017; Pakistan Aurat March 2018.
- **UN**: CSW 1946; Mexico City 1975 (Decade 1976–85); CEDAW 1979 (in force 1981; Pakistan 1996); Copenhagen 1980; Nairobi 1985 (Forward-Looking Strategies); Vienna 1993; DEVAW 1993; Beijing 1995 (12 areas, mainstreaming); UNSCR 1325 (2000); UN Women 2010; SDG 5 (2015); Beijing+30 (2025).

## V. Gender and development

- **Concepts**: triple roles (Moser); practical vs strategic (Molyneux 1985, Moser); empowerment = resources, agency, achievements (Kabeer 1999); feminisation of poverty (Pearce 1978); second shift (Hochschild 1989); unpaid care (Waring 1988).
- **Colonial**: civilising mission, "white men saving brown women" (Spivak), Orientalism (Said 1978), codified custom (Punjab customary law), male land titles, sati 1829, Sarda Act 1929, Chatterjee's inner/outer; coloniality (Quijano, Lugones), post-development (Escobar 1995), Mohanty.
- **Capitalist**: public/private split, family wage, social reproduction, reserve army, housewifisation (Mies), Federici; counter: jobs empower (Bangladesh garments).
- **Theories**: Modernisation (Rostow 1960; women = tradition; Boserup's critique) · Dependency (Frank 1966; Elson and Pearson "nimble fingers") · World-system (Wallerstein 1974; core, semi-periphery, periphery; household subsidy; care chains; feminisation of labour) · Structural functionalism (Parsons and Bales 1955; instrumental/expressive).
- **WID** (1970s; Boserup; Percy 1973; liberal; integrate women; critique: status quo, ignores relations and reproduction) · **WAD** (late 1970s; Marxist; unequal integration; critique: ignores patriarchy) · **GAD** (1980s; DAWN 1984; socialist; relations, empowerment, strategic needs, mainstreaming; critique: hard to implement, diluted).
- **Moser's policy approaches**: welfare, equity, anti-poverty, efficiency, empowerment.
- **SAPs**: austerity, user fees, privatisation, devaluation; Elson (1991) male bias; women as shock absorbers; UNICEF 1987. Pakistan: 2024 EFF ~US$7bn; BISP as floor.
- **Globalisation**: + jobs, norms, ICT, remittances; − feminised labour, Rana Plaza 2013, Baldia 2012, care chains, trafficking, backlash, digital divide, climate.
