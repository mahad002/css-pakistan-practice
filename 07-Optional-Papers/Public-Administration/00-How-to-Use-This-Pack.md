## 3. What examiners reward in this subject

1. **Theory + thinker + Pakistan.** Almost half the questions end "with reference to Pakistan". Every answer needs at least one named theory or thinker (Weber, Simon, Hood, Riggs, Lipsky) **and** concrete Pakistani evidence (an article of the Constitution, an institution, a reform, a figure).
2. **Answer the parts.** Recent papers split questions into (a) and (b). Give each part its own heading and roughly equal space.
3. **Comparison tables.** TPA vs NPM vs NPS; development administration vs administrative development; open vs closed systems; line-item vs performance vs PPBS vs ZBB. A neat table earns marks and saves time.
4. **Diagrams described simply:** policy cycle, Maslow's pyramid, Blake–Mouton grid, Hersey–Blanchard quadrants, budget cycle flowchart, Arnstein's ladder, Riggs' prism (fused → prismatic → diffracted).
5. **Balanced critique.** For each theory give two strengths and two criticisms with names (Merton on Weber; Simon on the principles; Schick on NPM).
6. **Current facts.** The 2024–26 civil service reforms, Conduct Rules 2026, contributory pension, rightsizing, PIA privatisation, Punjab LG Act 2025, the 26th and 27th Amendments.

## 4. The answer template

**Read and plan (3 minutes).** Underline the command word (discuss, compare, critically evaluate) and each part. Write a 5–8 line outline.

1. **Outline** (5–7 numbered headings).
2. **Introduction (80–120 words):** context sentence → definition with a thinker → **thesis** that answers the question.
3. **Conceptual/theoretical frame (150–200 words):** definitions, the theory, its author and year. A table if comparing.
4. **Main body (450–600 words, 4–6 headings):** each heading a claim. Under each: point → evidence (thinker, case, Pakistani example, article, figure) → one line linking back to the question.
5. **Pakistan application (150–200 words):** if not already woven in. Institutions, laws, reforms, data.
6. **Critical analysis (100–150 words):** limits, counter-arguments, your judgement.
7. **Way forward (80–120 words):** 4–6 concrete measures naming the institution that would act (Establishment Division, CCI, FPSC, provincial governments, PAC).
8. **Conclusion (50–80 words):** restate the judgement; forward-looking line.

**Short notes (Q8, 10 marks each, ~450 words):** definition → origin/thinker → features (bullets) → Pakistani example → one criticism → one-line conclusion.

## 5. Time per question

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| MCQs | 30 min (fixed) |
| Choose four questions, outline all four | 8–10 min |
| Each answer | 33–35 min (about 900–1,100 words, 6–8 pages) |
| Final check | 5 min |

Write the strongest answer first. Never leave the fourth answer short — four complete answers beat three long ones.

## 6. Common mistakes

1. Writing everything you know about "bureaucracy" when the question asks about bureaucracy **and democracy in Pakistan**.
2. Theory with no Pakistan, or Pakistan with no theory.
3. Using outdated facts (e.g. calling the 1964 conduct rules current, or the 26th Amendment the latest).
4. Wrong attributions (Fayol's 14 principles to Taylor; POSDCORB to Fayol; "satisficing" to Lindblom). The 2026 MCQs tested exactly these.
5. Skipping part (b) of a two-part question.
6. No headings or one-word headings.
7. Ignoring budgeting because it looks technical — nine questions in eleven years.
8. Vague "way forward" ("government should take steps").
9. Ignoring MCQ preparation. 20 easy marks.
10. Memorised guidebook answers. Examiners notice identical scripts.

## 8. Keeping it current

This pack is frozen at 24 Nov 2026. Before the exam, check:

- whether the September 2026 civil service reform package was approved and what was implemented (Establishment Division notifications);
- Punjab local government elections under the 2025 Act;
- Budget 2027–28 and Economic Survey 2026–27 figures;
- CPI 2026 (published Feb 2027) and the next UNDP HDR;
- any 28th Amendment and court restructuring;
- PIA handover outcome and further privatisations.
