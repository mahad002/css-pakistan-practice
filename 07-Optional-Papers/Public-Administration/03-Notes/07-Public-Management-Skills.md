# VII. Public Management Skills

Asked 2017 (public service motivation; barriers to innovation), 2018 and 2020 (rational decision model), 2019 (administrative buffering note), 2021 (MBO note), 2023 (distributed leadership), 2025 (delegation). Short-note favourites live here.

---

## 1. Planning

- **Meaning:** deciding in advance what to do, how, when and by whom (Koontz and O'Donnell). The first management function (Fayol's *prévoyance*; P in POSDCORB).
- **Process:** set objectives → analyse environment (SWOT) → develop premises/forecasts → generate alternatives → evaluate → choose → formulate derivative plans → budget → implement and review.
- **Types:** strategic, tactical, operational; standing plans (policies, procedures, rules) vs single-use plans (programmes, projects, budgets); contingency plans.
- **Management by Objectives (MBO) (2021 note):** Peter Drucker, *The Practice of Management* (1954). Superiors and subordinates jointly set specific objectives; periodic review; appraisal against results. Steps: set organisational goals → departmental goals → individual objectives agreed → action plans → periodic review → appraisal and feedback. **Benefits:** clarity, participation, motivation, objective appraisal. **Limits:** time-consuming; easy-to-measure goals crowd out important ones; rigid; needs trust and data. Used in US government under Nixon (1973). Pakistan link: performance agreements with ministries and KPI-based PERs.
- **Barriers to planning in government:** political short-termism; uncertainty; poor data; rigid budgets; resistance to change.

## 2. Decision making (asked 2018, 2020)

**Types:** programmed (routine, rules) vs non-programmed (novel, judgement) — Simon. Strategic, tactical, operational. Individual vs group.

**Rational-comprehensive model — steps:**
1. Identify and define the problem.
2. Set goals and rank values/criteria.
3. Identify all alternatives.
4. Assess the consequences of each alternative.
5. Compare alternatives against criteria.
6. Choose the option that maximises goals.
7. Implement.
8. Monitor and evaluate.
(The "four basic stages" in 2020: define the problem; generate alternatives; evaluate and choose; implement and review.)

**Critique:**
- Information is incomplete and costly.
- Human cognitive limits.
- Goals are vague and conflicting in government; values cannot be ranked neatly.
- Time pressure.
- Sunk costs and political commitments.
- Ignores power and bargaining.
- **Simon:** *bounded rationality* — people intend to be rational but are limited; they **satisfice** (choose the first acceptable option) rather than optimise. Simon received the Nobel Prize in economics in 1978.
- **Lindblom:** incrementalism ("muddling through", 1959).
- **Etzioni:** mixed scanning (1967).
- **Cohen, March and Olsen:** garbage can (1972).

**Heuristics and biases (Kahneman and Tversky):** availability, representativeness, anchoring, framing, confirmation bias, overconfidence, groupthink (Janis, 1972 — Bay of Pigs). Heuristics help under time pressure (for the better) but cause systematic errors (for the worse). Remedies: devil's advocate, checklists, data, pre-mortems, diverse teams. Behavioural public policy/"nudge" (Thaler and Sunstein, 2008).

**Group decision techniques:** brainstorming, nominal group technique, Delphi technique, dialectical inquiry.

**Pakistan:** decisions centralised; risk-averse due to NAB/audit; often driven by crisis or court orders; weak use of data. Remedies: delegated financial powers, decision deadlines, protection for good-faith decisions (NAB amendments 2022 narrowed liability for procedural lapses **[verify]**).

## 3. Conflict management

- **Views:** traditional (conflict is bad), human relations (natural), interactionist (some conflict is functional and drives innovation).
- **Sources:** scarce resources, goal differences, role ambiguity, personality, communication failure, status, interdependence. In government: centre–province, ministry–ministry, generalist–specialist, union–management.
- **Thomas–Kilmann modes (1974):** competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating (assertiveness × cooperativeness).
- **Mary Parker Follett:** domination, compromise, **integration** (best — both sides' interests met).
- **Techniques:** negotiation (Fisher and Ury's principled negotiation — separate people from problem; focus on interests; invent options; objective criteria), mediation, arbitration, superordinate goals, structural changes, rules.
- **Pakistan:** CCI for interprovincial conflict; IRSA for water; grievance mechanisms for employees.

## 4. Leading

See file 02 §6 for theories. Skills: setting vision, building teams, coaching, motivating, modelling integrity, managing upward (politicians) and outward (stakeholders). Distributed leadership (2023): followers exercise leadership through initiative and expertise; organisations enable it by delegation, trust and information sharing.

## 5. Communication

- **Process:** sender → encoding → channel → decoding → receiver → feedback; noise at every step (Shannon–Weaver model, 1949).
- **Directions:** downward, upward, horizontal (Fayol's gangplank), diagonal; formal and informal (grapevine).
- **Barriers:** hierarchy and status, jargon, information overload, filtering, language (English vs Urdu and regional languages), distrust, physical distance, poor listening.
- **Improving:** plain language; two-way channels; open-door policy; digital tools (e-Office); feedback loops; public communication strategies.
- **Government communication with citizens:** transparency, RTI, social media, crisis communication (NDMA alerts), citizen charters.

## 6. Administrative buffering (2019 note)

- **Meaning:** protecting the organisation's core activities (its "technical core") from disturbances in the environment, so that work can proceed steadily. Comes from James D. Thompson, *Organizations in Action* (1967).
- **Techniques:**
  1. **Buffering** in the narrow sense — stockpiles and reserves (inventory, contingency funds, standby staff).
  2. **Smoothing/levelling** — spreading demand (appointment systems, staggered tax deadlines).
  3. **Forecasting/anticipating** — planning for predictable fluctuations (budget for floods).
  4. **Rationing** — limiting service when demand exceeds supply (queues, triage).
  5. **Boundary-spanning units** — PR, legal, liaison offices that absorb outside pressure.
- **In public management:** managers also buffer staff from political pressure and frequent policy shifts — a senior officer shields line staff from interference so they can deliver.
- **Risks:** isolation from citizens; rigidity; hiding problems; over-insulation reduces responsiveness.
- **Pakistan examples:** contingency funds; NDMA stockpiles; SBP reserves (economic buffer); secretaries shielding departments from day-to-day political demands; Citizen Facilitation Centres separating the public interface from back offices.

## 7. Managing change

- **Lewin (1947):** unfreeze → change (move) → refreeze. Force-field analysis: driving vs restraining forces.
- **Kotter (1996), eight steps:** create urgency; build a guiding coalition; form vision; communicate vision; empower action (remove obstacles); generate short-term wins; consolidate gains; anchor changes in culture.
- **ADKAR (Prosci):** awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement.
- **Resistance — reasons:** fear of loss (power, jobs), uncertainty, habit, distrust, poor communication, vested interests. **Kotter and Schlesinger (1979)** — six ways to handle resistance: education and communication; participation; facilitation and support; negotiation; manipulation and co-optation; explicit and implicit coercion.
- **Public-sector specifics:** political cycles, legal constraints, unions, many stakeholders, risk aversion.
- **Pakistan:** e-Office rollout; FBR digitisation (resistance from traders and staff); pension reform; rightsizing; Punjab land record computerisation (resisted by patwaris).

## 8. Managing diversity

- **Meaning:** recognising and using differences — gender, ethnicity, language, religion, disability, age, region — for fairness and better performance.
- **Approaches:** affirmative action/quotas; equal opportunity; inclusion (valuing differences).
- **Benefits:** representativeness and legitimacy (representative bureaucracy); creativity; better service to diverse citizens.
- **Challenges:** stereotypes, tokenism, conflict, resentment of quotas.
- **Pakistan:** regional quotas; 10% women quota; minorities 5%; disabled quota (2%) **[verify]**; Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010; transgender persons' rights law 2018 (partly struck down by the Federal Shariat Court in 2023 **[verify]**).

## 9. Stress management

- **Sources:** workload, role conflict and ambiguity, political pressure, threats of transfer, accountability fears, poor work–life balance, low pay, security risks (police, district officers in conflict areas).
- **Effects:** burnout (Maslach: exhaustion, depersonalisation, reduced efficacy), absenteeism, errors, corruption risk.
- **Individual strategies:** time management, exercise, delegation, mindfulness, social support.
- **Organisational strategies:** realistic workloads, clear roles, participation, employee assistance programmes, flexible hours, fair transfer policies, recognition.

## 10. Delegation

See file 06 §5(b) for principles and steps. Add: delegation develops subordinates, frees managers for strategy, speeds decisions, and raises motivation (Herzberg's responsibility motivator).

## 11. Public Service Motivation (PSM) (asked 2017)

- **Definition:** "an individual's predisposition to respond to motives grounded primarily or uniquely in public institutions" (Perry and Wise, 1990).
- **Three motive types (Perry and Wise):** rational (participation in policy making, commitment to a programme), norm-based (desire to serve the public interest, loyalty, social equity), affective (patriotism of benevolence, commitment from conviction).
- **Perry's four dimensions (1996) — "requisite ingredients":**
  1. Attraction to public policy making.
  2. Commitment to the public interest/civic duty.
  3. Compassion.
  4. Self-sacrifice.
- **Findings:** high PSM is linked to choosing public jobs, higher performance and job satisfaction, and lower corruption tendency; extrinsic rewards (performance pay) can "crowd out" intrinsic motivation (Frey).
- **Implications:** recruit for values (situational judgement tests, interviews); socialise through training; design meaningful jobs; leaders model service; recognise contributions; avoid over-reliance on financial incentives.
- **Pakistan:** low pay and politicisation erode PSM; examples of strong PSM — Rescue 1122, lady health workers, polio workers; the CSS psychological assessment and viva could test PSM.

## 12. Creativity, problem solving and innovation (2017 innovation question)

- **Creativity:** generating new and useful ideas. **Innovation:** implementing them to create value.
- **Problem-solving steps:** define → analyse causes (fishbone/Ishikawa, 5 Whys) → generate options → evaluate → implement → review. Techniques: brainstorming, lateral thinking (de Bono), design thinking (empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test).
- **Public sector innovation:** new or improved services, processes, organisational forms or policies that create public value (OECD).
- **Factors that hamper procedural innovation in Pakistan:** rigid rules and audit focus on compliance; fear of NAB; no budget for experimentation; hierarchy; frequent transfers (no ownership); lack of skills and data; political short-termism; weak incentives; legal hurdles in procurement (PPRA rules); resistance from vested interests.
- **Enablers:** innovation labs (UK Nudge Unit; Pakistan: PITB in Punjab, KP IT Board); leadership support; safe-to-fail pilots; challenge funds; partnership with universities and startups; digital public infrastructure (NADRA, Raast payments); recognition awards (Prime Minister's Public Service Awards **[verify]**).
- **Examples:** NADRA CNIC and NSER; Punjab e-Khidmat Markaz; Rescue 1122 (2004); FBR's POS integration; Sehat Sahulat card; Safe City projects.

## 13. Issues of public management

- Measuring performance where outputs are intangible.
- Balancing accountability with flexibility.
- Political interference and short tenure.
- Resource constraints and austerity.
- Managing networks and contractors.
- Digital transformation and data protection.
- Ethics and corruption.
- Crisis management (floods, pandemics, terrorism).
- Citizen expectations shaped by private-sector service standards.
- Climate adaptation as a cross-cutting management task.
