# IX. Organization of Provincial and Local Government

Asked 2020 (NRB 5D model) and 2025 (district government functions; local government issues; post-devolution challenges). Punjab's new Local Government Act 2025 and still-pending Punjab polls make a 2028 question likely.

---

## 1. Governance structure of provincial administration

**Constitutional framework (Part IV of the Constitution):**
- **Governor** (Art. 101): appointed by the President on the PM's advice; acts on the advice of the Chief Minister (Art. 105).
- **Chief Minister and Provincial Cabinet** (Arts. 129–132): executive authority of the province is exercised in the name of the Governor by the provincial government — the CM and ministers. CM must command a majority in the Provincial Assembly (Art. 130).
- **Provincial Assembly** (Art. 106): legislates on provincial and residual subjects (Art. 142(c)); passes the provincial budget (Arts. 120–124).
- **Advocate-General** (Art. 140).
- **Provincial Public Service Commission** (Art. 242).
- **High Court** (Art. 192).
- **Emergency provisions** (Arts. 232–234): governor's rule, as imposed in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on 11 October 2026.

**Administrative hierarchy:**
Province → Divisions (Commissioner) → Districts (Deputy Commissioner) → Tehsils/Talukas/sub-divisions (Assistant Commissioner) → revenue circles (qanungo) and patwar circles (patwari).

## 2. Organisation of the provincial secretariat

- **Chief Secretary** (BS-22, usually PAS): head of the provincial bureaucracy; secretary to the cabinet; coordinates departments; principal link with the federal government; heads the Services and General Administration Department (S&GAD).
- **Additional Chief Secretaries** (BS-21/22) in some provinces for home, planning or general coordination.
- **Departments** (Home, Finance, Planning and Development, Health, School Education, Agriculture, Irrigation, Local Government, etc.): each headed by an administrative **Secretary**, who is principal accounting officer for the department. Below: Additional Secretary, Deputy Secretary, Section Officer (the "section" is the basic unit that handles files).
- **Planning and Development Board/Department:** chairman (BS-22) prepares the Annual Development Programme (ADP).
- **Board of Revenue:** Senior Member BoR; land revenue, land records (Punjab Land Records Authority).
- **Rules of Business** (e.g. Punjab Government Rules of Business 2011; Sindh Government Rules of Business 1986): allocate subjects, define the role of ministers, secretaries, cabinet, and inter-departmental consultation.
- **Secretariat functions:** policy formulation, legislation drafting, budgeting, coordination, monitoring of attached departments; it should not execute field work.

**Issues:** over-centralisation in secretariats; ministers and secretaries clash over postings; Chief Secretary and IGP often changed; S&GAD overloaded with transfers.

## 3. Provincial authorities and agencies and their relationship with departments

**Types:**
1. **Attached departments** — execute policy under a department: e.g. Director General Health Services, Directorate of Agriculture Extension, Police (IGP under Home Department in policy terms).
2. **Autonomous bodies/authorities** created by statute with boards: development authorities (LDA, KDA, PDA), WASAs, Punjab Food Authority, Punjab Safe Cities Authority, Rescue 1122 (Punjab Emergency Service), PDMAs, regulatory authorities (Punjab Healthcare Commission), Punjab Information Technology Board (PITB), Punjab Land Records Authority, Punjab Revenue Authority, Sindh Revenue Board, KP Revenue Authority.
3. **Public sector companies** (under companies law): e.g. waste management companies, Punjab's "56 companies" criticised in 2017–18 for cost and accountability **[verify]**.
4. **Provincial universities and boards of education.**

**Relationship:** the administrative department controls policy, budget grants, appointments to boards, and oversight; the authority has operational autonomy under its Act. **Problems:** overlapping mandates (e.g. development authority vs municipal corporation in cities); weak boards; capture by the department or politicians; parallel structures that weaken local governments; audit and accountability gaps. **Good practice:** clear statutes, independent boards, performance agreements, transparency.

## 4. Local government — constitutional basis

- **Article 32:** the State shall encourage local government institutions composed of elected representatives, with special representation for peasants, workers and women.
- **Article 140A** (18th Amendment, 2010): each province shall by law establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to elected representatives of local governments. Elections are held by the **Election Commission of Pakistan** (Art. 140A(2), Art. 219).
- **Elections Act 2017, s.219(4):** ECP must hold elections within 120 days of expiry of the term of local governments.
- **Supreme Court** has repeatedly ordered provinces to hold LG elections (2013–15) and to give them real powers (e.g. the 2022 judgment on Sindh LG law **[verify]**).

## 5. History and post-devolution local governance

| Period | System | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| 1959 | Ayub's Basic Democracies | 80,000 "Basic Democrats"; union councils; also an electoral college for the President; controlled by bureaucrats |
| 1979 | Zia's Local Government Ordinances | Non-party elections; districts and municipal bodies; legitimacy for military rule |
| 2001 | Musharraf's **Local Government Ordinance (LGO) 2001** (NRB) | Three tiers: district/city district, tehsil/town, union; elected Nazims; DCO under Nazim; magistracy separated from executive; police oversight by public safety commissions |
| 2009–10 | LGO protection lapsed; provinces revive commissionerate system | Elected bodies dissolved |
| 2013 | New provincial Acts (Punjab, Sindh, KP 2013; Balochistan 2010) | Elections 2013–15 after Supreme Court orders |
| 2019 | Punjab LG Act 2019 (PTI); KP LG amendments 2019 (district tier abolished; tehsil and village/neighbourhood councils) | Punjab bodies dissolved in 2019; restored by Supreme Court order in 2021 **[verify]** |
| 2021–23 | KP LG elections (Dec 2021, Mar 2022); Balochistan (2022); Sindh (2022–23; Karachi mayor elected June 2023) | |
| 2022 | Punjab LG Act 2022 | Repealed in 2025 before elections |
| Oct 2025 | **Punjab Local Government Act 2025** | See §7 |

### The NRB "5D" model (asked 2020)
The Devolution of Power Plan (2000) by the National Reconstruction Bureau rested on five Ds:
1. **Devolution of political power** — elected Nazims and councils at union, tehsil and district.
2. **Decentralisation of administrative authority** — district departments placed under the district government.
3. **Deconcentration of management functions** — powers moved from provincial secretariat to district offices.
4. **Diffusion of the power–authority nexus** — checks and balances (Zila council, Public Safety Commissions, Citizen Community Boards, ombudsman, monitoring committees).
5. **Distribution of resources to districts** — Provincial Finance Commission awards; formula-based transfers.

**Achievements:** first serious devolution; elected local leaders with budgets; women's representation (33% seats); Citizen Community Boards; some improvement in service delivery; magistracy separated.
**Criticisms:** imposed by a military government to build a local power base and bypass parties; non-party elections; provinces bypassed (district governments linked to the centre); bureaucracy demoralised (DC office abolished); police oversight weak; fiscal dependence (over 90% of district funds from transfers **[verify]**); elite capture; lack of constitutional protection after 2009 (Sixth Schedule protection expired), so provinces dismantled it.

## 6. District government and administration

**Under LGO 2001:** District Nazim (head), Naib Nazim (speaker of Zila Council), Zila Council (legislative), District Coordination Officer (DCO, principal civil servant), Executive District Officers (EDOs) for departments (health, education, finance and planning, works, agriculture, revenue, etc.), District Police Officer (DPO), District Public Safety Commission, Zila Mohtasib, District Accounts Office.

**Today (commissionerate system restored):** the **Deputy Commissioner** heads district administration — revenue collection and land records, law and order coordination (with DPO), price control, disaster management (DDMA), coordination of provincial departments in the district, elections support, development monitoring, protocol. **District Police Officer** under the Police Order/Police Act. Elected local governments where they exist handle municipal services (sanitation, water, streets, local roads, markets, parks), and sometimes primary education and health (varies by province).

**Functions of district government (2025):** planning and development; municipal services; primary and secondary education and basic health (depending on law); agriculture extension; local roads; revenue (property tax share, fees); regulation (markets, building control); disaster response; social welfare; record of births and deaths (CRMS with NADRA).

## 7. City district government

- **LGO 2001:** large cities (Karachi, Lahore, Faisalabad, Rawalpindi, Multan, Gujranwala, Peshawar, Quetta and later others **[verify list]**) became **City District Governments** — City Nazim, City District Council, Towns (instead of tehsils) with Town Nazims, union councils; integrated municipal and district functions; e.g. Karachi's city government under Mustafa Kamal (2005–10) built major infrastructure.
- **Now:**
  - **Karachi:** Karachi Metropolitan Corporation (KMC) under the Sindh LG Act 2013, mayor (elected June 2023), town municipal corporations; many functions (water board, building control, solid waste) held by provincial bodies — a frequent complaint.
  - **Lahore:** Metropolitan Corporation Lahore; under the 2025 Act, a new structure of municipal corporations and town corporations was being delimited (reports of 10 town corporations proposed for Lahore, Aug 2026 **[verify]**).
  - **Islamabad:** Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad (ICT LG Act 2015); CDA overlaps.
- **Issues:** fragmentation between provincial authorities (LDA, WASA, KDA, SBCA) and city governments; no unified metropolitan authority; weak own-source revenue; mayors without control of police or planning.

## 8. Punjab Local Government Act 2025 (latest)

- Signed by the Governor in October 2025; repealed the Punjab LG Act 2022; the ECP withdrew its election schedule under the 2022 law ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1950748)).
- Structure: **municipal corporations, tehsil councils and municipal (town) committees**; the **district council system abolished** across Punjab (effective 13 October 2025, as reported).
- **Punjab Local Government (Election) Rules 2026** notified (March 2026) for **party-based** elections.
- Delimitation reported complete on 19 Nov 2026 ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2626179/punjab-completes-local-govt-delimitation)). **Election date not confirmed as of 24 Nov 2026 — check.**
- Punjab last held LG elections in 2015; its elected bodies were dissolved in 2019 — so Pakistan's largest province has had no elected local government for most of the period since.

## 9. Issues and challenges of local governance (2025)

1. **Irregular elections** — provinces delay polls; repeated new laws reset the clock (Punjab: 2013, 2019, 2021 ordinance, 2022, 2025 laws).
2. **Weak constitutional protection** — Art. 140A lacks detail on functions, finances and tenure (proposals for a constitutional amendment listing LG powers have been made **[verify]**).
3. **Provincial reluctance** — MPAs see local governments as rivals for development funds and patronage.
4. **Fiscal dependence** — low own-source revenue (property tax poorly collected); Provincial Finance Commission awards irregular.
5. **Parallel bodies** — authorities and companies take over municipal functions.
6. **Bureaucratic control** — DCs and provincial departments dominate; LG employees transferred by province.
7. **Capacity gaps** — few trained staff; poor planning and accounting.
8. **Elite capture and patronage** — biradari politics.
9. **Women's and minorities' representation** reserved but often nominal.
10. **Urbanisation** — fast-growing cities without metropolitan governance.
11. **Political instability** — governments dissolve LGs of rival parties.
12. **Accountability** — weak audit and citizen oversight.

## 10. Way forward

- Constitutional amendment to protect local governments: fixed tenure, no dissolution without cause, timely elections, guaranteed share of provincial resources.
- Regular Provincial Finance Commission awards with formula-based transfers and performance grants.
- Transfer of municipal authorities (WASA, development authorities, waste companies) to elected city governments.
- Own revenue: property tax reform with digital cadastre; local fees.
- Capacity building: LG service cadre, training, digital accounting.
- Clear division of functions between province, district and city.
- Citizen participation: participatory budgeting, community boards, social audit.
- Metropolitan governance for Karachi and Lahore.
- International lessons: Indonesia's "big bang" decentralisation (2001); India's 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) giving panchayats and municipalities constitutional status; Kerala's People's Plan (1996).
