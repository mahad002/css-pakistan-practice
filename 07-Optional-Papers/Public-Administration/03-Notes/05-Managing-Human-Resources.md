# V. Managing Human Resources

Asked 2020 (HRM in public enterprises), 2024 (political neutrality; HRM shortfalls in Pakistan) and 2025 (spoils vs merit; open and closed systems). Rising in importance. Tie every answer to Pakistan's rules and the 2024–26 reforms.

---

## 1. Spoils system versus merit system

### Spoils system
- **Meaning:** public jobs given as rewards to party supporters; officials replaced when government changes.
- **Origin of the phrase:** US Senator William Marcy (1832): "to the victor belong the spoils". President Andrew Jackson (1829) defended rotation in office as democratic — any citizen could do government work, and rotation prevents an entrenched elite.
- **Arguments for:** political responsiveness; loyalty to elected leaders; democratic rotation.
- **Arguments against:** incompetence, corruption, instability, waste; loss of continuity; politicised policing and justice.
- **End in the US:** after a disappointed office-seeker assassinated President Garfield (1881), Congress passed the **Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act (1883)** — competitive exams and a Civil Service Commission. **Hatch Act (1939)** limited political activity of federal employees.

### Merit system
- **Meaning:** recruitment, promotion and pay based on ability, qualifications and performance, tested openly and fairly.
- **UK roots:** **Northcote–Trevelyan Report (1854)** — open competitive exams, a unified service, promotion on merit, division between intellectual and mechanical work. Civil Service Commission 1855; open competition generalised 1870.
- **India:** Macaulay Report (1854) → ICS by competitive exam from 1855.
- **Elements:** open advertisement; competitive selection by an independent body (FPSC, Art. 242); equal opportunity; political neutrality; tenure security; performance-based promotion; protection against arbitrary dismissal.
- **Criticisms:** exam-based merit tests knowledge, not management ability; can produce an unrepresentative elite; tenure protection can protect poor performers; rigidity.

### Pakistan: between spoils and merit (2025 question)
- **Merit institutions:** FPSC (CSS, general recruitment), provincial PSCs; Civil Servants Act 1973; Appointment, Promotion and Transfer Rules 1973.
- **Spoils practices:** ad hoc and contract appointments outside the PSC; political recruitment in BS-1 to 15 posts (e.g. "jobs for votes"), in SOEs (PIA, Steel Mills, Railways) and in the police; regularisation of ad hoc staff by Acts; transfers and postings as political rewards; lateral entry of 1973–77 later criticised as political.
- **Courts:** the Supreme Court has repeatedly struck down appointments outside merit and protected tenure **[cite specific cases only if verified]**.
- **Quotas:** federal posts are filled 7.5% on merit (all-Pakistan) and the rest by province/region: Punjab 50%, Sindh 19% (rural 11.4%, urban 7.6%), KP 11.5%, Balochistan 6%, Gilgit-Baltistan and ex-FATA 4%, AJK 2%; plus women 10% and minorities 5% within the totals **[verify current notification]**. Supporters call quotas representative bureaucracy; critics call them a dilution of merit.

## 2. Personnel administration versus human resource management

| Personnel administration (traditional) | Human resource management |
|---|---|
| Administrative, rule-bound, reactive | Strategic, proactive, linked to organisational goals |
| Employees as cost | Employees as asset/capital |
| Focus on compliance: hiring, pay, records, discipline | Focus on performance, development, motivation, culture |
| Centralised in a personnel department | Line managers share HR responsibility |
| Collective, uniform treatment | Individual, flexible contracts and pay |
| Short-term | Long-term, workforce planning |

**Strategic HRM (SHRM):** aligns HR practices with organisational strategy (Fombrun, Tichy and Devanna 1984 "Michigan model"; Beer et al. 1984 "Harvard model").

**Pakistan:** the Establishment Division still works mainly as a personnel department (postings, seniority, rules). The September 2026 reform package proposes renaming and restructuring it into a **Human Resource and Organisation Development Division** ([Minute Mirror, 20 Sep 2026](https://minutemirror.com.pk/pakistan-govt-finalises-rs11bn-civil-service-reform-package-632839/)) — a direct move from personnel administration to HRM. Status: proposal, not yet implemented **[check]**.

## 3. Closed (career) versus open (position) systems

| | Closed / career system | Open / position system |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | At the bottom of a cadre, usually young graduates | At any level, by lateral entry |
| Basis | Rank-in-person (rank stays with the officer) | Rank-in-position (rank attaches to the job) |
| Career | Lifelong, promotion within cadre | Job-to-job; mobility to and from private sector |
| Examples | France (ENA/INSP), Japan, Pakistan's CSS cadres, India's IAS | USA (mostly), New Zealand, UK Senior Civil Service partly, Netherlands |
| Strengths | Esprit de corps, continuity, neutrality, institutional memory | Specialist skills, fresh ideas, flexibility, competition |
| Weaknesses | Generalism, complacency, insularity, seniority over merit | Politicisation risk, loss of continuity, weaker loyalty |

**Pakistan:** a closed career system through CSS occupational groups, with limited openings: lateral entry (1973–77), MP-scales (Management Position scales, 1990s **[verify]**) and Special Professional Pay Scale for technocrats; contract hiring of advisers. The **2026 package** proposes a **National Executive Service** in which BS-20 and above posts would be filled by competition for five-year performance contracts, open to serving officers, private professionals and academics — a shift toward an open system at the top **[proposal]**.

## 4. Functions of HRM

1. **Human resource planning** — forecast needs; workforce analysis.
2. **Job analysis and job design** — job descriptions, specifications; classification.
3. **Recruitment** — attracting candidates (FPSC adverts, e-recruitment).
4. **Selection** — tests, interviews, psychological assessment (CSS: MPT screening since 2022, written exam, psychological assessment, viva).
5. **Placement and induction** — Common Training Program (CTP) at the Civil Services Academy, Lahore; specialised academies (Police, Foreign Service, FBR).
6. **Training and development** — mid-career courses at National School of Public Policy (NSPP): Mid-Career Management Course (MCMC), Senior Management Course (SMC), National Management Course (NMC). The 2026 package proposes merging institutes into a National University of Public Administration and shortening NMC from 18 to 10 weeks.
7. **Performance management/appraisal** — PER (Performance Evaluation Report, formerly ACR); 2026 package proposes twice-yearly assessment.
8. **Compensation and benefits** — Basic Pay Scales 1–22, allowances, pension. Pay commissions (most recent federal Pay and Pension Commission 2020 **[verify]**). Disparity allowances.
9. **Promotion and career management** — Central Selection Board (BS-20 and 21) and Departmental Promotion Committees; High Powered Selection Board for BS-22 **[verify]**.
10. **Discipline** — Civil Servants (Efficiency and Discipline) Rules 2020.
11. **Employee relations** — associations; no right to strike for most civil servants.
12. **Separation** — retirement at 60; directory (compulsory) retirement after 20 years' qualifying service on poor performance (Civil Servants (Directory Retirement from Service) Rules 2020 **[verify]**); pension.

## 5. Political neutrality and 19th-century reforms (asked 2024)

- **Origin:** Northcote–Trevelyan (UK 1854) and Pendleton (US 1883) separated appointment from party politics; Wilson's dichotomy gave the theory.
- **Meaning:** officials serve any lawful government loyally; do not use office for party goals; keep out of party politics; give frank advice; implement decisions impartially.
- **Statement (a): "employees should not use their positions to advance political goals."** Supported by Conduct Rules (2026 rules forbid joining or supporting political parties or criticising government policy publicly); Pakistan problem: officials used in elections, rallies, and against opponents.
- **Statement (b): "PA is not a neutral managerial set-up; it involves political choices."** Supported by Appleby, Waldo, Svara; discretion means officials choose values. Reconciliation: *partisan* neutrality (no party bias) differs from *value* neutrality (impossible). Officials should be politically impartial but committed to constitutional values and the public interest.
- **Pakistan measures:** fixed tenure (Supreme Court in the Anita Turab case, 2013 **[verify]**); independent selection boards; Conduct Rules 2026; transfers only with recorded reasons; election-period transfer bans by ECP.

## 6. HRM in Pakistan's public sector: issues and challenges (2020, 2024)

**Recruitment and selection:** FPSC is respected but slow (CSS cycle about 18–24 months); the 2026 package targets six months. Many lower posts filled outside commissions; ad hoc and contract hiring; bans on recruitment followed by mass political hiring; quota system debates.

**Training:** pre-service CTP good; mid-career training linked to promotion rather than skills; little training for lower staff; limited specialisation.

**Performance evaluation:** PER/ACR inflated ("outstanding" for most); confidential, so no feedback; not linked to targets; seniority dominates promotion. 2026 proposals: biannual evaluation, ministry targets, 5% of employee-related expenditure for performance incentives, promotion weighted to performance, leadership and skills.

**Pay and incentives:** low real pay at junior and middle levels; huge disparities between ministries and "special pay" bodies (e.g. FBR, Secretariat allowances, judiciary); weak link between pay and performance; pension burden — now addressed for new hires by a **contributory pension scheme** for federal civil employees appointed on or after 1 July 2024 (employee 10%, government 12% under the 2025 rules; SRO 1728(I)/2025) ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1946440); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/10/04/govt-rolls-out-contribution-based-pension-scheme/)).

**Accountability and discipline:** E&D Rules 2020 slow; fear of NAB and audit causes risk aversion; weak protection for whistle-blowers.

**Postings and transfers:** political interference; short tenures; OSD (Officer on Special Duty) used as punishment.

**Generalist–specialist divide:** PAS dominance over technical posts; specialists' career ceilings. 2026 package: 11 professional cadres (HR, law, engineering, science and technology, IT and telecom, energy, climate, agriculture and forestry, education, health, and others) **[proposal]**.

**HR in public-sector enterprises (2020):** over-staffing (PIA, Steel Mills, Railways); political hiring; unionisation and resistance to reform; boards without independence (addressed in the SOE (Governance and Operations) Act 2023 **[verify]**); losses requiring bailouts. Government's role: owner (policy, appointing independent boards), regulator, and eventually seller (privatisation).

**Gender and diversity:** women under-represented at senior grades despite 10% quota; harassment law 2010; few day-care facilities.

**Way forward:** strategic HRM unit (the proposed HR and Organisation Development Division); competency frameworks; merit-based postings with fixed tenures; open competition for senior posts; pay reform with market benchmarks for specialists; performance contracts; digital HR systems (HRMIS); independent civil service commission to protect neutrality.
