## 1. Historical background

### Colonial origins
- **East India Company:** covenanted civil servants; Cornwallis (1793) separated revenue from commerce and banned private trade by officials; the Collector-Magistrate emerged.
- **Haileybury College (1806)** trained Company servants.
- **Charter Act 1853 and Macaulay Report (1854):** patronage replaced by open competition; first ICS exam in London, 1855.
- **Indian Civil Service (ICS)** under the Crown after 1858 — the "steel frame". Key features: a small elite (about 1,000–1,200 officers at its height); generalists; district officers combined revenue, magistracy and coordination; high pay and prestige; insulated from local politics.
- **Indianisation:** Aitchison Commission (1886–87) — created Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate services; Islington Commission (1912–15); Montagu–Chelmsford reforms (1919); Lee Commission (1923–24) — 50:50 British–Indian recruitment goal; Public Service Commission set up 1926 (Government of India Act 1919); Federal Public Service Commission under the Government of India Act 1935.
- **Punjab model:** the "non-regulation" province — paternal, personal rule by the Deputy Commissioner, strong revenue records; this style shaped Pakistan's district administration.

## 2. Bhutto's 1973 reforms (core exam content)

**Why:** CSP seen as an arrogant elite; colonial "heaven-born" status; the PPP's populist agenda; bureaucracy blamed for Ayub-era inequality.

**Main measures:**
1. **Abolished service cadres/classes** (CSP, PSP labels, Class I–IV) and the reserved posts for CSP.
2. **Unified grading:** a single National Pay Scale of 22 grades (BPS 1–22).
3. **Occupational groups** in place of services: e.g. District Management Group, Tribal Areas Group, Secretariat Group, Police Group, Foreign Affairs, Accounts, Income Tax, Customs and Excise, Commerce and Trade, Information, Postal, Railways, Military Lands and Cantonments **[verify exact initial list]**.
4. **Lateral entry:** about 1,300 or more appointments of outsiders at senior levels between 1973 and 1977 **[verify number]** — intended to bring talent; criticised as political.
5. **Removal of constitutional guarantees:** the 1973 Constitution did not carry over the protections civil servants had enjoyed; terms now set by Acts (Civil Servants Act 1973).
6. **Common training:** joint pre-service training for all groups at the Academy (later the Common Training Program).
7. **Unified seniority and career planning.**

**Assessment:** broke the CSP monopoly and increased mobility, but politicised the service, ended security of tenure (making officers dependent on politicians), and did not fix performance or specialisation.

## 3. The structure of the civil service today

### Levels of government
- **Federal civil servants** (Civil Servants Act 1973).
- **Provincial civil servants** (provincial Civil Servants Acts 1973–74), including Provincial Management Service (Punjab PMS), Provincial Civil Service (Sindh, KP, Balochistan), provincial secretariat services, technical cadres.
- **Local government employees.**
- **Employees of autonomous bodies and SOEs** (not "civil servants" under the Act).

### The CSS occupational groups (12)
Recruited through FPSC's CSS exam at BS-17:

| Group | Main work |
|---|---|
| Pakistan Administrative Service (PAS) | District, provincial and federal administration; secretaries |
| Police Service of Pakistan (PSP) | Senior police posts |
| Foreign Service of Pakistan (FSP) | Diplomacy |
| Inland Revenue Service (IRS) | Income tax, sales tax (FBR) |
| Pakistan Customs Service (PCS) | Customs (FBR) |
| Pakistan Audit and Accounts Service (PA&AS) | Audit and accounts (AGP, CGA) |
| Office Management Group (OMG) / Secretariat Group | Federal secretariat |
| Commerce and Trade Group (CTG) | Trade officers |
| Information Group | Government information and media |
| Military Lands and Cantonments Group (MLCG) | Cantonment boards, military lands |
| Postal Group | Pakistan Post |
| Railways (Commercial and Transportation) Group | Pakistan Railways |

**All-Pakistan Services:** PAS and PSP officers serve in both federal and provincial posts. A share of senior provincial posts is reserved for them under the inter-provincial arrangement of 1954 as revised in 1993 **[verify percentages]**. Provinces (especially Sindh and KP) have challenged this arrangement since the 18th Amendment.

### Grades and posts
- **BPS 1–16:** support staff (clerks, assistants, superintendents); BPS 16 Section Officer level in some cadres.
- **BPS 17:** entry for CSS officers (Assistant Commissioner, ASP, Section Officer, Assistant Director).
- **BPS 18–19:** Deputy Secretary, Deputy Commissioner, SSP/DPO.
- **BPS 20:** Joint Secretary, Commissioner, DIG.
- **BPS 21:** Additional Secretary, Additional Chief Secretary in provinces, Additional IG.
- **BPS 22:** Federal Secretary, Chief Secretary, IGP.
- **Ex-cadre posts:** filled from any group or by specialists.
- **Promotion boards:** Departmental Promotion Committees (up to BS-19), **Central Selection Board** (BS-20, BS-21), PM decides BS-22 on a panel **[verify]**.

## 4. Management of the civil service

- **Recruitment:** CSS (MPT screening since 2022 → written exam → medical → psychological assessment → viva → allocation by merit, quota and preference). Other posts via FPSC general recruitment and provincial PSCs.
- **Training:** CTP (about 9–12 months **[verify]**), specialised training academies, then MCMC (for BS-19 promotion), SMC (BS-20), NMC (BS-21).
- **Performance evaluation:** PER (annual; reporting and countersigning officers; graded). Proposed: biannual assessment and ministry targets.
- **Promotion:** seniority-cum-fitness at lower levels; selection on merit for higher; PER scores, training reports, integrity reputation.
- **Transfers and postings:** Establishment Division and provincial S&GADs; tenure policies (usually three years) often breached.
- **Discipline:** E&D Rules 2020; OSD status.
- **Pay:** BPS with allowances; disparity allowances; special pay in some bodies.
- **Pension:** defined benefit for old employees; contributory for those appointed from 1 July 2024.

## 5. Institutional and cultural context

**Institutional context:** parliamentary federal system; 18th Amendment devolution; strong military role (civil–military "hybrid" arrangements, SIFC 2023, 27th Amendment); powerful courts and NAB; IMF conditionality; weak local governments; coalition politics.

**Cultural context (asked 2024):**
- **Colonial and viceregal legacy** — officers as rulers.
- **Patron–client politics** — biradari, feudal and tribal networks; MNAs and MPAs seek favourable officers in their constituencies (the "thana–kacheri" politics).
- **Politicisation:** transfers as rewards and punishments; officers identified with parties.
- **Generalist elitism** and inter-service rivalry (PAS vs others; generalists vs specialists).
- **Risk aversion** — fear of NAB, audit and courts produces "decision paralysis".
- **Low trust** between citizens and state; *sifarish* and rent-seeking.
- **Gender norms** limiting women's field postings.
- **Weak performance culture.**

**How politicians use civil servants (2024 Q7):** posting pliant DCs and police officers to control local power; using officers in elections; development schemes routed through favoured officials; victimising officers who refuse. Effects: nepotism and favouritism; low morale; collapse of neutral service delivery. Remedies: fixed tenure; independent boards for senior postings; ECP control of transfers during elections (already practised); legal protection for officers following law; e-administration that removes discretion (online land records, e-procurement, digital licences); transparency of postings; citizen feedback.

## 6. Civil service reform — the latest package (status Nov 2026)

| Area | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Status | Finalised and presented on 19 Sep 2026; needs formal approval; reports said no constitutional amendment needed; implementation committee with a 120-day target once approved. **No confirmation of cabinet approval found as of 24 Nov 2026 — check** |
| Cost | About Rs11bn |
| Cadres | 11 specialised professional cadres: HR, law, engineering, science and technology, IT and telecom, energy, climate, agriculture and forestry, education, health and others |
| Senior posts | **National Executive Service:** BS-20 and above filled by open competition for five-year contracts with performance targets; open to serving officers, private professionals and academics |
| Structure | Federal decision tiers cut from five to three |
| Establishment Division | Renamed and restructured as Human Resource and Organisation Development Division |
| CSS exam | Continues; MPT pass mark 33% → 40%; analytical reasoning added; negative marking removed; compulsory papers in English or Urdu; recruitment by specialisation; fully digital; cycle of six months or less |
| Performance | Twice-yearly assessment; ministry annual targets; 5% of employee-related expenditure for performance incentives; promotion weighted to performance, leadership and skills over ACRs |
| Training | Training institutes merged into a National University of Public Administration; NMC cut from 18 to 10 weeks |

**Already in force (not proposals):** Conduct Rules 2026; contributory pension (new hires since July 2024); rightsizing decisions (2024–25); E&D Rules 2020.

**Critical analysis:**
- *Positives:* specialisation addresses the generalist problem (NCGR made the same point in 2008); NES opens top posts to talent; performance pay and fewer tiers target delay; faster recruitment.
- *Risks:* contract-based senior posts could be politicised (who selects?); provinces may resist changes to All-Pakistan Services; Rs11bn is small relative to the task; past packages (NCGR 2008, Task Force 2019) stalled at implementation; performance systems can be gamed without good data; Urdu option raises questions of standard; pay reform not addressed.
- *Conditions for success:* political ownership across parties; an independent body for NES selection; legal backing (amendments to Civil Servants Act); phased pilots; transparent metrics.

## 7. Reform strategy: people vs organisation and methods (2016 question)

- **Personnel side:** merit recruitment; specialisation; training; pay; performance management; ethics.
- **Organisation and methods (O&M) side:** structures, procedures, delegation, rules of business, IT, process re-engineering.
- **Balance:** reforming people without changing procedures produces frustrated talent; changing procedures without capable people produces paper reforms. Sequence: quick process wins (digitisation, delegation) + longer people reforms (cadres, pay, appraisal), both with political protection.

## 8. Civil service neutrality in development administration (2021 question)

- Neutral, competent officials give continuity across governments — essential for long projects (dams, health programmes).
- Neutrality ≠ indifference: development administration needs **committed** officials (to development goals), but **impartial** between parties.
- Pakistan obstacles: politicised transfers; patronage hiring; officers aligned with parties; military influence.
- Measures: fixed tenure for key posts; transparent, rules-based postings; Conduct Rules enforcement; independent civil service commission; protection for officers who refuse unlawful orders; performance contracts focusing on results, not loyalty.

## 9. Role of the civil service in good governance

Policy advice; implementation; regulation; service delivery; crisis response (NDMA, floods, COVID-19's NCOC); revenue collection; maintaining rule of law; continuity; protecting public resources; interface with citizens. Weaknesses limit good governance: World Bank government-effectiveness scores for Pakistan are low **[cite the latest WGI percentile only after checking]**; CPI 2025 score 28/100, rank 136 of 182 ([Profit/Pakistan Today, 10 Feb 2026](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/02/10/pakistan-improves-one-spot-to-136th-on-2025-corruption-perceptions-index)).

## 10. Gender and civil service

- **Policy:** 10% quota for women in federal posts (introduced in the 2000s **[verify year]**); women also compete on open merit; maternity leave; Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010 and ombudsperson; day-care centres in some offices.
- **Situation:** women are a growing share of CSS allocations (they often top the merit list), but remain few in senior posts, field postings (DC, DPO) and in the police; "glass ceiling" in promotion; socio-cultural barriers to transfers; harassment; lack of facilities.
- **Why it matters:** representative bureaucracy (passive → active representation); better services for women (women police stations since 1994; women's protection centres; lady health workers).
- **Measures:** gender audits; mentoring; flexible postings for couples; enforcing harassment law; gender-disaggregated HR data; women in selection boards; gender-responsive budgeting.
