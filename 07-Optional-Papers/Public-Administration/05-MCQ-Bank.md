# MCQ Bank — Public Administration

156 questions written for this pack, grouped by syllabus section. FPSC MCQs often include "None of these" as option D; practise reading every option. Answers and one-line explanations follow each question.

## I. Concepts, Approaches and Context

**Q1.** "The Study of Administration", the essay usually taken as the founding text of the discipline, was written by:
- A) Woodrow Wilson
- B) Frank Goodnow
- C) Leonard D. White
- D) Luther Gulick
**Answer:** A. Wilson published it in 1887 in Political Science Quarterly.

**Q2.** *Politics and Administration* (1900), which divided government into the expression and execution of the state's will, was written by:
- A) Max Weber
- B) Frank Goodnow
- C) Paul Appleby
- D) Dwight Waldo
**Answer:** B. Goodnow developed the politics–administration dichotomy.

**Q3.** The first textbook of public administration, *Introduction to the Study of Public Administration* (1926), was written by:
- A) W.F. Willoughby
- B) Herbert Simon
- C) L.D. White
- D) Henri Fayol
**Answer:** C. Leonard D. White's book appeared in 1926.

**Q4.** POSDCORB was coined by:
- A) Lyndall Urwick
- B) Henri Fayol
- C) F.W. Taylor
- D) Luther Gulick
**Answer:** D. Gulick used it in *Papers on the Science of Administration* (1937).

**Q5.** In POSDCORB, the letter "S" stands for:
- A) Supervising
- B) Strategising
- C) Scheduling
- D) Staffing
**Answer:** D. Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting.

**Q6.** *The Administrative State* (1948), which argued that administration is full of political values, was written by:
- A) Robert Dahl
- B) Chester Barnard
- C) Dwight Waldo
- D) Vincent Ostrom
**Answer:** C. Waldo challenged the value-free view of administration.

**Q7.** The Minnowbrook Conference of 1968 gave rise to:
- A) New Public Management
- B) New Public Administration
- C) Public Choice Theory
- D) The Human Relations School
**Answer:** B. Minnowbrook stressed relevance, values and social equity.

**Q8.** The core value most associated with New Public Administration is:
- A) Social equity
- B) Efficiency
- C) Economy
- D) Hierarchy
**Answer:** A. Frederickson and others put social equity at the centre.

**Q9.** The seven doctrines of New Public Management were set out by:
- A) Christopher Hood
- B) Osborne and Gaebler
- C) Janet and Robert Denhardt
- D) R.A.W. Rhodes
**Answer:** A. Hood's 1991 article "A Public Management for All Seasons?".

**Q10.** "Steer rather than row" is a principle from:
- A) *The New Public Service*
- B) *Reinventing Government*
- C) *Administrative Behavior*
- D) *Bureaucracy and Representative Government*
**Answer:** B. Osborne and Gaebler (1992) — catalytic government.

**Q11.** "Serve citizens, not customers" is a principle of:
- A) New Public Management
- B) Traditional Public Administration
- C) New Public Service
- D) Scientific Management
**Answer:** C. The Denhardts' New Public Service.

**Q12.** R.A.W. Rhodes described governance as:
- A) Rowing without steering
- B) Management by objectives
- C) The one best way
- D) Governing without government
**Answer:** D. Rhodes (1996) linked governance to self-organising networks.

**Q13.** Which of the following is NOT one of the four classical types of market failure?
- A) Public goods
- B) Externalities
- C) Information asymmetry
- D) Rent-seeking
**Answer:** D. Rent-seeking is a form of government failure; the classical market failures are public goods, externalities, natural monopoly and information asymmetry.

**Q14.** John Rawls' "difference principle" allows inequalities only if they:
- A) Increase total utility
- B) Are approved by a majority
- C) Benefit the least advantaged
- D) Reward merit alone
**Answer:** C. From *A Theory of Justice* (1971).

**Q15.** The Islamic institution of market supervision that inspired the name of Pakistan's ombudsman is:
- A) Diwan
- B) Hisbah (muhtasib)
- C) Bait-ul-Mal
- D) Shura
**Answer:** B. The Wafaqi Mohtasib takes its name from the muhtasib.

**Q16.** The *Arthashastra*, an ancient treatise on statecraft and administration, is attributed to:
- A) Kautilya
- B) Todar Mal
- C) Akbar
- D) Al-Mawardi
**Answer:** A. Kautilya (Chanakya), adviser to Chandragupta Maurya.

## II. Classical and Contemporary Theories

**Q17.** Weber's three types of legitimate authority are:
- A) Traditional, charismatic and legal-rational
- B) Political, economic and social
- C) Formal, informal and technical
- D) Autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire
**Answer:** A. Bureaucracy is the purest form of legal-rational authority.

**Q18.** Weber's analysis of bureaucracy appears in:
- A) *The Functions of the Executive*
- B) *Economy and Society*
- C) *The Prince*
- D) *Administrative Behavior*
**Answer:** B. Published after his death, in 1922.

**Q19.** "Trained incapacity" and "goal displacement" in bureaucracy were described by:
- A) Elton Mayo
- B) Chester Barnard
- C) Robert Merton
- D) Mary Parker Follett
**Answer:** C. Merton, "Bureaucratic Structure and Personality" (1940).

**Q20.** *The Principles of Scientific Management* (1911) was written by:
- A) Henri Fayol
- B) Henry Gantt
- C) Frank Gilbreth
- D) F.W. Taylor
**Answer:** D. Taylor proposed time study and the "one best way".

**Q21.** Fayol's five functions of management are planning, organising, commanding, coordinating and:
- A) Budgeting
- B) Staffing
- C) Reporting
- D) Controlling
**Answer:** D. Fayol, *Administration Industrielle et Générale* (1916).

**Q22.** "Unity of command" means:
- A) All units pursue one plan
- B) Authority equals responsibility
- C) An employee receives orders from one superior only
- D) Decisions are made at the top
**Answer:** C. One of Fayol's fourteen principles.

**Q23.** Fayol's "gangplank" allows:
- A) Appeals to the chief executive
- B) Direct horizontal communication between officials at the same level
- C) Delegation of authority downwards
- D) Lateral entry from outside
**Answer:** B. It short-circuits the scalar chain with superiors' consent.

**Q24.** The Hawthorne studies were carried out at:
- A) Western Electric
- B) Bethlehem Steel
- C) Ford Motor Company
- D) Midvale Steel
**Answer:** A. Western Electric's Hawthorne Works near Chicago, 1924–32.

**Q25.** The bank wiring observation room experiment showed that:
- A) Informal groups set their own output norms
- B) Lighting raises output
- C) Piece-rates maximise output
- D) Rest pauses reduce output
**Answer:** A. Workers restricted output to group norms.

**Q26.** The "acceptance theory of authority" and the "zone of indifference" are associated with:
- A) Herbert Simon
- B) Chester Barnard
- C) Max Weber
- D) Luther Gulick
**Answer:** B. *The Functions of the Executive* (1938).

**Q27.** In Maslow's hierarchy, the highest need is:
- A) Esteem
- B) Belonging
- C) Self-actualisation
- D) Safety
**Answer:** C. Physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualisation.

**Q28.** According to Herzberg, salary is a:
- A) Motivator
- B) Growth need
- C) Higher-order need
- D) Hygiene factor
**Answer:** D. Its absence dissatisfies, but it does not motivate by itself.

**Q29.** Theory X and Theory Y were proposed by:
- A) William Ouchi
- B) David McClelland
- C) Victor Vroom
- D) Douglas McGregor
**Answer:** D. *The Human Side of Enterprise* (1960).

**Q30.** In Vroom's expectancy theory, "valence" means:
- A) The belief that effort leads to performance
- B) The belief that performance leads to reward
- C) The value a person places on a reward
- D) Comparison with co-workers
**Answer:** C. Motivation = expectancy × instrumentality × valence.

**Q31.** "Leaders are born, not made" reflects:
- A) Contingency theory
- B) Trait theory
- C) Path–goal theory
- D) Situational theory
**Answer:** B. The earliest phase of leadership theory.

**Q32.** In Hersey and Blanchard's model, the style used for followers with high readiness is:
- A) Delegating
- B) Telling
- C) Selling
- D) Participating
**Answer:** A. S4 delegating suits able and willing followers.

**Q33.** House's path–goal theory identifies four styles: directive, supportive, participative and:
- A) Achievement-oriented
- B) Laissez-faire
- C) Transactional
- D) Charismatic
**Answer:** A. Leaders clear the path to goals.

**Q34.** The LPC (least preferred co-worker) scale belongs to:
- A) Blake and Mouton's grid
- B) Fiedler's contingency model
- C) Ohio State studies
- D) Burns' transforming leadership
**Answer:** B. Fiedler (1967).

**Q35.** In the Managerial Grid, the 9,9 style is called:
- A) Country club
- B) Impoverished
- C) Team management
- D) Middle of the road
**Answer:** C. High concern for both people and production.

**Q36.** Transforming (transformational) leadership was first described by:
- A) Robert Greenleaf
- B) Fred Fiedler
- C) Rensis Likert
- D) James MacGregor Burns
**Answer:** D. *Leadership* (1978); developed further by Bernard Bass.

**Q37.** Public choice theory assumes that bureaucrats:
- A) Pursue the public interest
- B) Follow tradition
- C) Act from charisma
- D) Maximise their own utility
**Answer:** D. Rational, self-interested actors.

**Q38.** The budget-maximising bureaucrat model is associated with:
- A) Anthony Downs
- B) Patrick Dunleavy
- C) William Niskanen
- D) Vincent Ostrom
**Answer:** C. *Bureaucracy and Representative Government* (1971).

**Q39.** *The Calculus of Consent* (1962) was written by:
- A) Downs and Tullock
- B) Buchanan and Tullock
- C) Simon and March
- D) Osborne and Gaebler
**Answer:** B. A founding text of public choice.

**Q40.** Mintzberg's term for an organisation such as a hospital or university, where trained professionals control the operating core, is:
- A) Professional bureaucracy
- B) Machine bureaucracy
- C) Adhocracy
- D) Simple structure
**Answer:** A. One of his five configurations (1979).

**Q41.** Under Article 153, the Council of Common Interests is chaired by the:
- A) Prime Minister
- B) President
- C) Chairman Senate
- D) Minister for Inter-Provincial Coordination
**Answer:** A. Members are the PM, the four Chief Ministers and three federal nominees.

**Q42.** After the 18th Amendment, the CCI must meet at least once every:
- A) 30 days
- B) 90 days
- C) 60 days
- D) Six months
**Answer:** B. Article 154(3).

**Q43.** Under the 7th NFC Award, the weight given to population in horizontal distribution among provinces is:
- A) 50%
- B) 72%
- C) 82%
- D) 100%
**Answer:** C. Population 82%, poverty 10.3%, revenue 5%, inverse population density 2.7%.

**Q48.** Kingdon's multiple streams framework includes the problem, policy and ______ streams.

**Q57.** The OECD-DAC criterion added in 2019 is:
- A) Coherence

**Q58.** The Paris Declaration on Aid Effectiveness (2005) includes the principle of:

- A) Aaron Wildavsky
- B) Allen Schick

**Q60.** Allen Schick's three orientations of budgeting are control, management and:
- A) Evaluation

**Q61.** The budget system that classifies spending by objects such as salaries and supplies is:
- A) Performance budgeting
- B) Zero-base budgeting

**Q62.** Performance budgeting was promoted in the US by the:
- A) Brownlow Committee

**Q63.** PPBS was introduced in the US Department of Defense in 1961 by:
- A) Jimmy Carter

**Q64.** Zero-base budgeting was developed by Peter Pyhrr at:
- A) Texas Instruments

**Q65.** In ZBB, the units that are ranked for funding are called:
- A) Decision packages

**Q66.** Budgeting based on last year's base plus a small change is called:

**Q67.** Participatory budgeting began in:

**Q68.** The principle that all revenues go into a common fund rather than being earmarked is:

**Q75.** Musgrave's three functions of the budget are allocation, distribution and:

**Q99.** Law made by an executive authority under powers given by an Act is called:

**Q104.** The concept of "buffering" the technical core of an organisation comes from:

**Q105.** Groupthink was described by:

**Q112.** Robert Katz's three managerial skills are technical, human and:
- A) Conceptual
