# Everyday Science: Fact Sheets

Part of the 50-mark General Knowledge section (about 16 to 22 questions). This is the easiest part of General Knowledge to improve quickly, and your engineering background covers most of the physics and computing.

## 2. Physics

### Motion and force

- Scalars have size only (speed, mass, time, temperature, energy). Vectors have size and direction (velocity, force, acceleration, momentum, weight).
- Newton's laws: (1) inertia; (2) force = mass × acceleration; (3) action and reaction are equal and opposite.
- g = 9.8 m/s². Weight is greatest at the poles, less at the equator, zero at the Earth's centre. Gravity on the Moon is one-sixth of the Earth's.
- Mass does not change with place; weight does.
- Pressure = force ÷ area. Atmospheric pressure falls with height, so water boils below 100 °C on mountains. A pressure cooker raises the boiling point.
- Archimedes' principle: a body in a fluid is pushed up by a force equal to the weight of fluid it displaces. Ice floats because it is less dense than water.

### Heat

- Conduction (solids), convection (liquids and gases), radiation (no medium needed; how the Sun's heat reaches us).
- 0 °C = 32 °F = 273 K. 100 °C = 212 °F. −40 °C = −40 °F. Absolute zero = −273.15 °C.
- Water is densest at 4 °C. Normal body temperature is 37 °C (98.6 °F).
- Evaporation causes cooling. Dark, dull surfaces absorb and emit heat best; shiny surfaces reflect it.

Penetrating power of radiation: alpha (stopped by paper) < beta (stopped by aluminium) < gamma (needs thick lead or concrete).

### Electricity and energy

- Ohm's law: V = IR. Power = VI.
- Conductors: metals (silver is best, copper is used). Insulators: rubber, glass, plastic. Semiconductors: silicon, germanium.
- AC changes direction; mains supply in Pakistan is about 220–230 V at 50 Hz. DC flows one way (batteries).
- Transformer: changes AC voltage (step-up or step-down). Generator: mechanical to electrical energy. Motor: electrical to mechanical. Rectifier: AC to DC.
- Fuse: thin wire of low melting point. Bulb filament: tungsten. Heater element: nichrome. Lightning conductor: copper rod to earth.
- Fission (splitting heavy nuclei: reactors, atom bomb). Fusion (joining light nuclei: Sun, hydrogen bomb).
- Renewable: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, tidal, biomass. Non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, uranium.

Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K. Water-soluble: B group and C.

Minerals: iron (anaemia), iodine (goitre), calcium (weak bones and teeth), fluoride (tooth decay). Tea with meals lowers iron absorption; vitamin C raises it.

| Cause | Diseases |
|---|---|
| Virus | Polio, measles, influenza, dengue, hepatitis, AIDS (HIV), rabies, smallpox, COVID-19 |
| Bacterium | Tuberculosis, typhoid, cholera, tetanus, diphtheria, whooping cough, leprosy, plague |
| Protozoan | Malaria (Plasmodium), amoebic dysentery |
| Fungus | Ringworm, athlete's foot |

- A virus can multiply only inside living cells and is not killed by antibiotics. A bacterium is a complete single cell.
- Malaria: female Anopheles mosquito. Dengue: Aedes mosquito. Typhoid, cholera, hepatitis A and E: dirty food and water. Hepatitis B and C: blood. Rabies: bite of an infected animal.
- Endemic: always present in a place. Epidemic: sudden rise in one area. Pandemic: spread across countries.
- Wild polio remains endemic only in Pakistan and Afghanistan.
- Hepatitis A prevention: vaccination, clean water, hand washing.
- Arsenic in well water causes skin lesions and cancers over years.
- Organs affected: hepatitis and jaundice (liver), pneumonia and TB (lungs), typhoid (intestines), diabetes (pancreas), goitre (thyroid), glaucoma and cataract (eyes), arthritis (joints), meningitis (brain covering), eczema (skin).
- ECG: heart. EEG: brain. ORS: dehydration.

## 7. Earth and space

- Solar system: 8 planets in order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
- Mercury: nearest and smallest. Venus: hottest and brightest; Morning and Evening Star. Mars: Red Planet. Jupiter: largest. Saturn: rings. Neptune: farthest.
- Pluto became a dwarf planet in 2006 because it has not cleared its orbit of other objects.
- Rotation (24 hours) gives day and night. Revolution (365¼ days) with the 23.5° tilt gives the seasons. When the southern hemisphere tilts towards the Sun (December), Australia has summer. Distance from the Sun is not the cause.
- Solar eclipse: Moon between Sun and Earth (new moon). Lunar eclipse: Earth between Sun and Moon (full moon).
- Tides: mainly the Moon's pull.
- Nearest star: the Sun; next, Proxima Centauri. Our galaxy: the Milky Way.
- Sputnik 1 (1957); Yuri Gagarin, first man in space (1961); Neil Armstrong, first on the Moon (20 July 1969); Halley's Comet every 76 years (last 1986).
- Pakistan: SUPARCO; Badr-1 (first satellite, 1990); PakSat-1R (communication satellite, 2011).
- Atmosphere, bottom to top: troposphere (weather), stratosphere (ozone layer), mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere.
- Earth's layers: crust, mantle, outer core (liquid), inner core (solid; iron and nickel). Evidence: the behaviour of earthquake waves and the magnetic field.
- Earthquakes: focus underground, epicentre above it; measured by seismograph.

Environment in the applied style:

Air pollutants: carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), ground-level ozone, lead. Nitrogen and oxygen are not pollutants.
