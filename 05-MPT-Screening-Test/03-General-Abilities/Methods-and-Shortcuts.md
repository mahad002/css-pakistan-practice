# General Abilities: Methods and Shortcuts

60 marks, the largest section. Target 48. The level is school (SSC) maths plus standard reasoning. No calculator. In the real paper about half the questions are quantitative and half are reasoning.

The practice file follows the same order as this file.

---

## 1. Working rules

1. Read the last line of the question first. It tells you what to find.
2. Estimate before you calculate. Often only one option is the right size.
3. Use the options. For "find x" questions, putting the options back into the equation is often faster than solving.
4. Check units: minutes and hours, cm and m, m/s and km/h.
5. Write one line of working. Mental slips cost more marks than slow hands.
6. Two minutes is the limit. Mark, guess, move on.

## 2. Arithmetic and number

- **Order of operations (BODMAS):** brackets, powers, division and multiplication (left to right), addition and subtraction (left to right).
- **HCF and LCM:** for two numbers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. LCM uses the highest power of every prime; HCF uses the lowest power of the common primes.
- **Divisibility:** by 2 (last digit even); 3 (digit sum divisible by 3); 4 (last two digits); 5 (ends in 0 or 5); 6 (by 2 and 3); 8 (last three digits); 9 (digit sum divisible by 9); 11 (difference of alternate digit sums is 0 or a multiple of 11).
- **Indices:** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ; aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ; (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ; a⁰ = 1; a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ; a^(1/2) = √a. To solve 27ᵏ = 9, write both as powers of 3: 3³ᵏ = 3², so k = 2/3.
- **Difference of squares:** a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b). Example: 53² − 47² = 6 × 100 = 600.
- **Fractions of a whole:** if 1/3 is spent on rent and 1/4 on food, the part left is 1 − 1/3 − 1/4 = 5/12. Whole = amount left ÷ fraction left.

## 3. Percentages

- x% of N = N × x ÷ 100. "What percentage is a of b?" = a ÷ b × 100.
- If p% of a number is v, the number is v × 100 ÷ p.
- **Successive changes multiply.** Up 20% then down 10%: 1.20 × 0.90 = 1.08, a net rise of 8%. Formula: a + b + ab/100 (use a minus sign for a fall).
- Up x% then down x% always gives a net fall of x²/100 %.
- If A is p% more than B, then B is p ÷ (100 + p) × 100 % less than A. (25% more ↔ 20% less; 50% more ↔ 33⅓% less.)
- **After a sale:** "sold 25%, 1,500 left" means 75% = 1,500, so the whole is 2,000.
- **Elections (two candidates):** margin % = winner % − loser %. Total = margin ÷ margin %.
- **Pass marks:** pass marks = marks obtained + marks short. Maximum = pass marks ÷ pass %.
- **Growth for n years:** P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ.

## 4. Profit, loss, discount and interest

- Profit % and loss % are always on **cost price**: profit ÷ CP × 100.
- SP = CP × (100 + profit %) ÷ 100. CP = SP × 100 ÷ (100 + profit %).
- Discount is on **marked price**: SP = MP × (100 − d) ÷ 100.
- Two discounts a% and b% equal one discount of a + b − ab/100.
- "CP of x articles = SP of y articles": profit % = (x − y) ÷ y × 100. A negative answer is a loss.
- "Sold at a loss of l%; Rs k more would give a gain of g%": CP = k × 100 ÷ (l + g).
- **Simple interest:** SI = P × R × T ÷ 100. Amount = P + SI. Money doubles in 100 ÷ R years.
- **Compound interest:** Amount = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ. For 2 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)².

## 5. Ratio and proportion

- To divide an amount in the ratio a : b : c, one part = amount ÷ (a + b + c).
- If the ratio is a : b and the difference is d, one part = d ÷ (a − b).
- To join A : B and B : C, make the B terms equal (use their LCM).
- **Direct proportion** (more items, more cost): multiply across. **Inverse proportion** (more men, fewer days): keep the product constant.
- **Mixtures:** find the actual amounts first. When water is added, the milk does not change.
- **Map scale:** real distance = map distance × scale.

## 6. Averages

- Average = total ÷ number. Total = average × number. Most questions are solved by comparing two totals.
- New item added: new item = new total − old total.
- One person replaced and the average of n people rises by x: new person = old person + n × x.
- Two groups: combined average = (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂). It lies nearer the average of the bigger group.
- **Average speed over equal distances:** 2uv ÷ (u + v), never the simple mean.
- Average of consecutive numbers = the middle number (or the mean of the first and last).

## 7. Ages

Let the younger age be x and write one equation.

- "A is k times B; in t years their ages add up to S": kx + x + 2t = S.
- Ratio a : b with sum S: one part = S ÷ (a + b).
- "Father is k times the son now and will be m times after t years": kx + t = m(x + t).
- The **difference** between two people's ages never changes. Use it as a check.

## 8. Algebra

- Linear equations: expand brackets, collect x on one side.
- Simultaneous equations: multiply to match one coefficient, then subtract.
- Quadratic x² − (sum)x + (product) = 0: find two numbers with that sum and product. Watch the signs: x² + 7x + 10 = 0 has roots −2 and −5.
- Identities:
  - (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², so a² + b² = (a + b)² − 2ab
  - (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
  - (a + b)² − (a − b)² = 4ab
  - If x + 1/x = k, then x² + 1/x² = k² − 2. If x − 1/x = k, then x² + 1/x² = k² + 2.
- Completing the square: x² + 2ax + c = (x + a)² + (c − a²).
- Consecutive numbers: n, n+1, n+2. Consecutive even or odd numbers: n, n+2, n+4. The sum of three of them is 3 × the middle one.
- Inequalities: when you multiply or divide by a negative number, reverse the sign. Count integers carefully at the ends (< excludes, ≤ includes).

## 9. Geometry and mensuration

| Shape | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | 2(l + w) | l × w |
| Square (side s, diagonal d) | 4s | s² = d²/2 |
| Triangle | sum of sides | ½ × base × height |
| Circle (radius r) | 2πr | πr² |
| Semicircle | πr + 2r | πr²/2 |
| Trapezium | sum of sides | ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height |

| Solid | Volume | Surface |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid | l × w × h | 2(lw + wh + lh) |
| Cube | s³ | 6s² |
| Cylinder | πr²h | curved 2πrh; total 2πr(r + h) |
| Cone | ⅓πr²h | curved πrl |
| Sphere | 4/3 πr³ | 4πr² |

- 1 m³ = 1,000 litres. 1 litre = 1,000 cm³.
- Angles of a triangle add up to 180°. Angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360°.
- An isosceles triangle has two equal angles. If one angle is 68°, the others are either 68° and 44°, or 56° and 56°.
- Polygon with n sides: sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 360° ÷ n. Diagonals = n(n − 3)/2.
- Pythagoras: hypotenuse² = sum of the squares of the other two sides.
- If every side grows by p%, the area grows by (1 + p/100)² − 1. Sides up 10% → area up 21%; sides up 20% → area up 44%; sides doubled → area ×4, volume ×8.
- A wheel covers one circumference (πd) per turn. Turns = distance ÷ πd.
- Ring or track: area = π(R² − r²). Outer radius = inner radius + width.

## 10. Time and work

- If A takes a days and B takes b days, together they take ab ÷ (a + b) days.
- A and B together take t days and A alone takes a days: B alone takes at ÷ (a − t) days.
- **Man-days:** men × days is constant for the same job. M₁D₁ = M₂D₂.
- "x workers take d₁ days; with g absent they take d₂ days": d₁x = d₂(x − g).
- Pipes are the same idea: a filling pipe adds 1/a per hour, a drain subtracts 1/b per hour.

## 11. Speed, distance and time

- Distance = speed × time.
- km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18. m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5. (36 km/h = 10 m/s; 54 = 15; 72 = 20; 90 = 25.)
- A train passing a **pole** covers its own length. Passing a **platform or bridge**, it covers its own length plus the platform's.
- Moving towards each other: add the speeds. Moving the same way: subtract them.
- Catching up: time = gap ÷ difference of speeds.
- Boats: downstream = boat + current; upstream = boat − current. Boat = (down + up)/2; current = (down − up)/2.
- Races: in the winner's time the loser covers L × (winner's time ÷ loser's time).

## 12. Sets, probability and statistics

- Two overlapping groups: n(A or B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(both). Neither = total − n(A or B).
- Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ all outcomes. P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
- Two dice give 36 outcomes. Ways to get each total: 2→1, 3→2, 4→3, 5→4, 6→5, 7→6, 8→5, 9→4, 10→3, 11→2, 12→1.
- Three coins give 8 outcomes: exactly two heads in 3 of them.
- Mean = total ÷ count. Median = middle value after sorting (mean of the two middle values if the count is even). Mode = most frequent value. Range = largest − smallest.
- Handshakes or league matches among n: n(n − 1)/2. Knockout among n teams: n − 1 matches.

## 13. Data interpretation

- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ **old** × 100.
- A ratio in lowest terms: divide both numbers by their HCF.
- For "which year had the biggest rise", write the year-to-year differences in a row before answering.
- Round figures to compare quickly, then compute only the option you need.

## 14. Number and letter series

Test these patterns in order:

1. **Differences.** Write the gaps. Constant gap (arithmetic series) or gaps that themselves grow by a fixed amount (2, 6, 12, 20, 30 has gaps 4, 6, 8, 10).
2. **Ratios.** Each term × 2, × 3 or ÷ 2.
3. **Multiply and add.** 3, 7, 15, 31 is "× 2 + 1".
4. **Squares and cubes**, sometimes plus or minus a constant: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26 is n² + 1.
5. **n(n + 1):** 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42.
6. **Sum of the two before** (Fibonacci type): 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13.
7. **Two series mixed** in alternate places: 3, 30, 6, 27, 9, 24.
8. **Primes:** 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13.

Letter series: write the position numbers (A=1 ... Z=26). Useful anchors: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25. For letter groups such as PQS, QRT, RSU, track each position separately.

## 15. Analogies and odd one out

- Number analogies use the same rules as series: square, cube, square ± 1, × k + c.
- For "odd one out", name the property three items share (planet, prime, perfect square, cat family, string instrument) and find the one that lacks it.

## 16. Coding and decoding

- **Shift codes:** each letter moves a fixed number of places forward or back. Compare the first letter of the word and of its code to find the shift, then check the second letter.
- **Reverse alphabet:** A↔Z, B↔Y. The two positions add up to 27.
- **Number codes:** A=1 ... Z=26, with the word's value as a sum, or each letter replaced by its number.
- **Substitution by example:** line up the given word and its code letter by letter and reuse the pairs.

## 17. Directions, clocks and calendars

- Draw a small sketch with north at the top. Right turn = clockwise. Net north-south and east-west movements combine by Pythagoras.
- Turn questions: add right turns and subtract left turns in degrees from the starting direction.
- **Clock angle:** hour hand at 30h + 0.5m degrees, minute hand at 6m degrees. Take the difference, and if it is over 180° subtract it from 360°.
- **Mirror image of a clock:** subtract the time from 12:00 (treat 12:00 as 11:60).
- **Days:** divide the number of days by 7 and count the remainder forward. An ordinary year moves the weekday forward by 1, a leap year by 2.
- Days in months: 31 for Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec; 30 for Apr, Jun, Sep, Nov; Feb 28 or 29. A year is a leap year if divisible by 4 (century years only if divisible by 400).
- **Bus timetable:** time now = next departure − (interval − minutes since the last bus).

## 18. Ranking and ordering

- Position from both ends: total = left rank + right rank − 1.
- Rank from the bottom = total − rank from the top + 1.
- Ordering clues ("A is taller than B"): write a chain A > B and attach each new clue to it.
- Dictionary order: compare letter by letter from the left; the first different letter decides.

## 19. Blood relations

- Draw a small family tree: generations in rows, a line between spouses.
- Decode phrases first: "my mother's only child" = me; "my father's only son" = me (if the speaker is male) or my brother; "my grandfather's only son" = my father.
- Check the gender of the person asked about before choosing "uncle" or "aunt", "nephew" or "niece".

## 20. Syllogisms and logic

- Draw circles. "All A are B": A inside B. "No A is B": separate circles. "Some A are B": overlapping circles.
- A conclusion is valid only if it is true in **every** possible drawing.
- Safe moves: All A are B → Some B are A. All A are B, all B are C → All A are C. All A are B, no B is C → No A is C. Some A are B, all B are C → Some A are C.
- Traps: "All A are B" does **not** give "All B are A". Two "some" statements give no conclusion.
- "If P then Q" is equivalent to "If not Q then not P" (contrapositive). It is not equivalent to "If Q then P" or "If not P then not Q".
- "Only if" marks a necessary condition: "shortlisted only if experienced" means no experience → not shortlisted.
- Negations: not "all are" = "at least one is not"; not "some are" = "none is".
- Two things rising together do not prove that one causes the other. Look for a third factor.

## 21. Arrangement puzzles

1. Draw the slots (a row, a circle, a table of days).
2. Place the fixed facts first ("X is at the left end", "Y is on Wednesday").
3. Then place linked pairs ("B is immediately right of A").
4. Use "not" clues last to decide between the remaining options.
5. In a circle facing the centre, a person's left is clockwise. Two people facing each other have opposite lefts and rights.

## 22. Data sufficiency

You are asked whether the statements are **enough** to answer, not for the answer itself.

1. Test statement I alone. 2. Test statement II alone. 3. Only then combine them.

A yes/no question is answered if the statements force a definite "yes" or a definite "no". Extra information that is not needed (such as the length of a train when distance and time are given) does not make a statement necessary.
