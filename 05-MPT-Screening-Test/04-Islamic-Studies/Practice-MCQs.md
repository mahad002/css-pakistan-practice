# Islamic Studies: Practice MCQs

Written for the MPT (20 marks). The set mixes the classic fact style of the 2022 to 2026 papers with the concept and application style of the CSS 2027 paper. Where Pakistani exam books use a conventional figure that scholars count differently, the question follows the convention used in FPSC keys and the notes explain the difference.

Total questions: **250**. Each question has one correct option. The answer key with one-line explanations is at the end of this file.

## Answer Key

**1. C** (114). The Quran has 114 Surahs arranged in 30 Paras (Juz).  
**2. A** (7). Seven Manzils allow the Quran to be completed in a week.  
**3. A** (Hira). The first five verses of Surah Al-Alaq were revealed in Cave Hira in 610 CE.  
**4. A** (Al-Alaq). "Iqra" (Read) opens Surah Al-Alaq, the first revelation.  
**5. A** (Al-Baqarah). Al-Baqarah has 286 verses and 40 Rukus.  
**6. A** (Al-Kawthar). Al-Kawthar has only three verses.  
**7. C** (At-Tawbah). Surah At-Tawbah (Bara'ah), the ninth Surah, has no Bismillah at its start.  
**8. A** (An-Naml). It opens the Surah and appears again in the letter of Hazrat Sulaiman (27:30).  
**9. C** (Yaseen). A well-known Hadith describes Surah Yaseen as the heart of the Quran.  
**10. D** (Ar-Rahman). Surah Ar-Rahman carries this title.  
**11. D** (Al-Fatihah). Al-Fatihah is also called As-Sab'ul-Mathani and Ash-Shifa.  
**12. B** (Al-Ikhlas). Al-Ikhlas states pure Tawhid and is described as one-third of the Quran.  
**13. C** (Al-Baqarah). It is verse 255 of Surah Al-Baqarah, in the third Para.  
**14. A** (Al-Baqarah). Verse 282 of Al-Baqarah is the longest verse.  
**15. A** (6). Yunus, Hud, Yusuf, Ibrahim, Muhammad and Nuh.  
**16. A** (25). Twenty-five prophets are named in the Quran.  
**17. A** (Hazrat Musa (AS)). Hazrat Musa is named about 136 times.  
**18. A** (4). The name Muhammad occurs four times and Ahmad once (Surah As-Saff).  
**19. C** (Hazrat Maryam). Maryam is named, and the 19th Surah bears her name.  
**20. D** (Hazrat Zaid bin Harithah). Zaid is named in Surah Al-Ahzab (33:37).  
**21. A** (29). Twenty-nine Surahs open with letters such as Alif-Lam-Mim.  
**22. D** (Hazrat Abu Bakr). After many Huffaz were martyred at Yamama, Abu Bakr ordered the compilation on Umar's advice.  
**23. D** (Hazrat Zaid bin Thabit). Zaid bin Thabit, a chief scribe of revelation, did this work.  
**24. B** (Hazrat Usman). Usman united the Ummah on one standard script and reading.  
**25. D** (23 years). Roughly 13 years in Makkah and 10 in Madinah.  
**26. A** (Bani Israel). The 17th Surah is called both Al-Isra and Bani Israel.  
**27. B** (Yusuf). The phrase occurs at the start of Surah Yusuf.  
**28. C** (Al-Kahf). Surah Al-Kahf is the 18th Surah.  
**29. A** (Al-Anfal). Al-Anfal 8:41 lays down the fifth (Khums).  
**30. A** (At-Tawbah). At-Tawbah 9:60 lists the eight heads of Zakat.  
**31. C** (An-Nisa). An-Nisa verses 11, 12 and 176 fix the heirs' shares.  
**32. C** (Al-Baqarah). Al-Baqarah 2:183–185 made fasting obligatory in 2 AH.  
**33. D** (Al-Fath). Surah Al-Fath was revealed on the return from Hudaybiyyah.  
**34. C** (Badr). The term is used for Badr in Surah Al-Anfal.  
**35. D** (Al-Fil). Surah Al-Fil recalls the destruction of the army in the Year of the Elephant.  
**36. A** (Al-Hadid). Al-Hadid means iron.  
**37. A** (Hoopoe (Hud-hud)). The Hud-hud brought news of the Queen of Saba.  
**38. C** (Al-Mujadilah). Every verse of the 58th Surah contains the name Allah.  
**39. D** (14). Hanafis count 14; Shafi'is count 15 with a second Sajdah in Surah Al-Hajj.  
**40. A** (Shah Waliullah). Shah Waliullah's Persian translation is called Fath-ur-Rahman.  
**41. A** (Shah Rafiuddin). Shah Rafiuddin made the literal one and his brother Shah Abdul Qadir the idiomatic one (Muzih-ul-Quran).  
**42. A** (Syed Abul Ala Maududi). Maududi's commentary runs to six volumes.
