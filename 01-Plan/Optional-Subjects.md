# Optional Subjects: 600 Marks

Updated 23 Nov 2026 after checking FPSC data. Full research, the comparison of every subject and all source links are in [04-Research/Optional-Subjects-Research.md](../04-Research/Optional-Subjects-Research.md).

**Status: not chosen yet.** You can change your mind until the written-exam application closes (~late Nov 2027), but the packs get built for whichever combination you confirm.

## The rules (FPSC CE Rules 2019, Appendix-I)

- You take exactly **600 marks** of optional subjects and need **33% in each**.
- **Group I:** one 200-mark subject (Accountancy & Auditing, Economics, Computer Science, Political Science, International Relations).
- **Group II:** one 200-mark subject (Physics, Chemistry) or up to two 100-mark subjects (Applied Maths, Pure Maths, Statistics, Geology).
- **Groups III–VII:** one 100-mark subject from each group you use.
- No other forbidden pairs. You don't have to take a history subject.
- **Not confirmed:** whether you may skip Group I. The rule says a Group I subject "can be opted", and the allocation tables suggest a handful of candidates did skip it, but FPSC doesn't say so outright.

## What changed: my first recommendation was wrong

I first suggested Computer Science + Governance & Public Policies + History of USA + Gender Studies + Criminology. The data doesn't support three of those:

| Subject | CSS 2023 (FPSC Annual Report) | CSS 2025 (third-party figures, said to be FPSC data) |
|---|---|---|
| Computer Science | 221 appeared, 55% failed, 1 candidate scored 60%+, none allocated | Mean 31.5%; 4 of 177 passed the written exam |
| History of USA | 68% failed | Mean 34% |
| Governance & Public Policies | 14% failed | Mean 29% (it collapsed between the two years) |

Computer Science is the surprise. The papers cover 12 areas, including compiler construction, image processing and computer architecture, and are marked hard. A software engineer should expect about 70–100 out of 200, and falling under 66 fails the whole exam.

## Recommended combination

| Group | Subject | Marks | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Political Science** | 200 | Passable in every year with data. Taken by 61% of allocated candidates in 2023 and 86% in 2025. Overlaps with Pakistan Affairs, Current Affairs and the Essay. |
| III | **Public Administration** | 100 | Best 2025 record in its group (median 58%). |
| V | **Gender Studies** | 100 | 2025 median 59%. Short syllabus. A coaching site reports stricter marking in 2026. |
| VI | **Criminology** | 100 | 2025 median 61%. Short syllabus. It had one strict year (40% failed in 2023). |
| VII | **Journalism & Mass Communication** | 100 | Top non-language subject in both years (76% scored 60%+ in 2023; 2025 median 72%). Small pool of about 350 candidates. |

Estimated score: **310–400 out of 600, about 350 likely**. That range is an estimate from two years of data, not a published figure.

## How sure this is

- **Solid:** the rules, the syllabus and the CSS 2023 subject statistics, all from FPSC documents.
- **Medium:** the CSS 2025 figures. They come from a coaching site that says they're FPSC data; FPSC has published no annual report after 2023.
- **Weak:** anything about CSS 2026 marking, which is coaching-site opinion.
- **Subjects swing 20–30 points between years.** Re-check the picks when FPSC publishes its 2024 or 2025 annual report, and again in Oct 2027 before you lock the form.
