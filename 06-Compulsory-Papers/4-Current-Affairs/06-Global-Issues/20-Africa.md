# Africa in World Affairs

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Global issues – international security; political economy; terrorism. **Past papers:** none directly; useful for short notes and for examples in questions on resources (2026), terrorism, migration, UN reform and great-power rivalry.
> **Search limit:** only the Sudan war and the Global Terrorism Index were checked. Other 2026 items are marked **[verify]**.

## 1. Basic facts

- 54 UN member states (55 in the African Union with Western Sahara); about 1.5 billion people, expected to reach 2.5 billion by 2050; the world's youngest population (median age about 19).
- **African Union (AU):** Addis Ababa; successor (2002) to the Organisation of African Unity (1963); Agenda 2063; a permanent member of the G20 since 2023. Chairperson of the AU Commission: Mahmoud Ali Youssouf of Djibouti (elected February 2025) **[verify]**.
- **AfCFTA:** the African Continental Free Trade Area (trading since 2021), the largest by number of members.
- **Resources:** about 30% of the world's mineral reserves, including most cobalt (DR Congo), platinum and manganese (South Africa), plus gold, copper, lithium, oil and gas; 60% of the best solar potential.
- **Regional bodies:** ECOWAS, SADC, EAC, IGAD.

## 2. Conflicts and security

| Conflict | Position |
|---|---|
| **Sudan** | Civil war since 15 April 2023 between the army (General al-Burhan) and the Rapid Support Forces (General "Hemedti" Dagalo). The RSF took **El Fasher on 26 October 2025** after an 18-month siege, with mass killings reported. Estimates of deaths range from 150,000 to 400,000; over 14 million displaced; famine confirmed in several areas; about 25 million face acute hunger. The army holds Khartoum and roughly 60% of the country; the RSF runs a rival government in Darfur and Kordofan. Outside backers are alleged on both sides (the UAE for the RSF, which it denies; Egypt and others for the army). No lasting ceasefire ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_civil_war_(2023%E2%80%93present))). The world's largest displacement and hunger crisis. |
| **Sahel** | Coups in Mali (2020, 2021), Burkina Faso (2022) and Niger (2023). The juntas expelled French and US forces, left ECOWAS (January 2025) and formed the Alliance of Sahel States with Russian security help. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel) control wide areas. Burkina Faso, Niger, Nigeria and Mali rank just behind Pakistan on the Global Terrorism Index 2026 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1984501)). |
| **DR Congo** | The Rwanda-backed M23 took Goma and Bukavu (early 2025). A US-brokered peace agreement between DR Congo and Rwanda was signed in Washington (June 2025), tied to minerals deals; a Qatar-mediated framework with M23 followed; fighting continued **[verify 2026]**. |
| **Horn of Africa** | Ethiopia's Tigray war (2020–22); Ethiopia–Eritrea tension; al-Shabaab in Somalia; the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (inaugurated September 2025) and Egypt's objections; Somaliland's recognition by Israel (December 2025) **[verify]**. |
| **Libya** | Divided between rival governments since 2014. |
| **Coups** | Nine or more successful coups since 2020, including Gabon (2023), Madagascar (2025) and Guinea-Bissau (2025) **[verify]**. |

## 4. Development themes

1. **Debt:** over 20 countries in or near distress; restructurings in Zambia, Ghana and Ethiopia; more spent on interest than on health.
2. **Climate injustice:** under 4% of global emissions; severe droughts, floods and cyclones; COP32 will be in Addis Ababa (2027).
3. **Youth and jobs:** 10–12 million new job-seekers a year; migration pressure; Gen-Z protests (Kenya 2024; Madagascar and Morocco 2025).
4. **Resource curse:** minerals fund wars in DR Congo and Sudan (see `04-Pakistan-Domestic/23`, part B).
5. **Health:** aid cuts hit HIV, malaria and nutrition programmes.
6. **Voice:** demand for two permanent Security Council seats (Ezulwini Consensus); AU in the G20.
7. **Successes:** Botswana, Mauritius, Rwanda's growth, Kenya's mobile money (M-Pesa), the AfCFTA.

## 5. Pakistan and Africa

- "Engage Africa" policy (2019): new missions, trade conferences; trade is about $4–5bn, mostly with Kenya (tea), South Africa and Egypt **[verify]**.
- A long record of UN peacekeeping in DR Congo, Sudan, South Sudan, the Central African Republic, Liberia and Sierra Leone.
- Defence sales and training; shared positions in the G77 and on climate finance.

## 7. Model answer outline (Question 1)

- **Intro:** a continent of 1.5 billion with a third of the world's minerals. Thesis: outside competition gives African states choices but also fuels conflict; outcomes depend on governance and unity.
- **Players and tools** (table); **arenas:** minerals, ports, security, votes at the UN.
- **Conflicts as proxy fields:** Sudan, Sahel, DR Congo.
- **Map:** outline of Africa marking Sudan, the Sahel belt, DR Congo, the Horn and Djibouti.
- **African agency:** AU, AfCFTA, multi-alignment.
- **Conclusion:** partnership that adds value in Africa will outlast extraction.
