## 1. Background

- Donald Trump began his second term on 20 January 2025. **"America First"** rests on four ideas: sovereignty over multilateral commitments; trade deficits as losses; allies must pay more; personal, transactional deal-making between leaders.
- Key figures: Vice President J. D. Vance; Secretary of State Marco Rubio; envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner; Defence (renamed "War") Secretary Pete Hegseth; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.
- First-year moves (2025): withdrawal from the WHO and the Paris Agreement and from several UN bodies; dismantling of USAID; a tariff offensive; pressure on NATO allies to spend 5% of GDP; direct talks with Russia; strikes on Iran (June 2025); the Gaza plan (October 2025). **[Details from 2025 reporting; verify specifics you quote.]**

The IMF still projects world growth of 3.1% for 2026, crediting AI investment for offsetting trade and war shocks ([IMF WEO, October 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/10/13/world-economic-outlook-october-2026)).

## 4. Domestic limits

- **Courts:** the tariff rulings.
- **Congress:** the House voted three times to end the Iran war; the Senate did not ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)).
- **Midterms, 3 November 2026:** Democrats won both chambers (early counts: a House majority and about 54 Senate seats), a result linked to living costs and opposition to Trump ([NPR](https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-11-03/democrats-win-full-control-of-congress-ushering-in-a-new-era-of-divided-government); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/campaigns/4791815/takeaways-2026-midterms/)). Expect investigations, limits on war funding and tariff legislation in 2027–28.

## 5. Assessment (analysis)

| Supporters say | Critics say |
|---|---|
| Allies now pay more for defence; hostages released and a Gaza ceasefire achieved; adversaries deterred by unpredictability | Wars of choice (Iran) without legal authority; alliances weakened; rules replaced by coercion |
| Tariffs brought partners to the table and revenue to the Treasury | Tariffs are a tax on consumers, were ruled unlawful and damaged trust in US commitments |
| Realism about a multipolar world: deal with Xi and Putin directly | Great-power bargains over smaller states' heads invite aggression |

## 6. Implications for Pakistan

1. **Gains:** a tariff edge over India; access to the White House; a mediator's profile; US action against the BLA.
2. **Costs:** the oil shock from the Iran war; aid cuts (health, education, refugees); remittance and export risks; reliance on one man's goodwill.
3. **After the midterms:** Congress may revive democracy and rights conditions.
4. **Lesson:** build institutional ties and economic value; keep options open with China, the Gulf and Europe (GSP+).
