## 1. Background

- **Definition problem.** There is no agreed UN definition; a Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism has been stuck since 1996 over whether to exclude liberation movements and state acts. Working definition: violence or its threat against civilians by non-state actors for political ends.
- **David Rapoport's four waves:** anarchist (1880s), anti-colonial (1920s), new left (1960s), religious (1979 onwards). Some add a fifth: far-right and lone-actor terrorism.
- **Since 2001:** the 9/11 attacks; the "war on terror" (Afghanistan 2001, Iraq 2003); al-Qaeda's franchises; ISIS's "caliphate" in Iraq and Syria (2014–19); the shift of jihadist violence to Africa's Sahel and to the Afghanistan–Pakistan region.
- **UN framework:** Security Council Resolutions 1267 (sanctions on al-Qaeda and the Taliban, 1999), 1373 (2001, duties on all states; Counter-Terrorism Committee), 1540 (2004), 2178 (foreign fighters); the UN Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy (2006, four pillars); the Office of Counter-Terrorism (2017); FATF standards on financing.

**Other developments, 2025–26**
- **Syria:** the former jihadist group HTS took power (December 2024); its leader was removed from UN sanctions lists and received in Western capitals, raising the question of when a "terrorist" becomes a statesman.
- **Afghanistan:** the Taliban fight ISKP but shelter or tolerate the TTP and al-Qaeda, according to UN monitors; Pakistan has been at war with them since February 2026.
- **ISKP's reach:** attacks in Moscow (March 2024), Iran (January 2024), and Islamabad (February 2026).
- **Sahel:** juntas in Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger expelled French and US forces, left ECOWAS and formed the Alliance of Sahel States with Russian help; JNIM expanded, including a fuel blockade around Bamako in 2025 **[verify 2026 status]**.
- **Labels in dispute:** states brand opponents as terrorists (Israel on Hamas; India on Pakistan-based groups; Pakistan on the TTP and BLA; the US listing of cartels and of the BLA).

## 3. Causes

1. **Political:** occupation, repression, exclusion, state collapse, civil war.
2. **Ideological:** extremist readings of religion; ethnic separatism; far-right identity politics.
3. **Socio-economic:** youth unemployment, inequality, lack of education. These enable recruitment; they do not alone cause terrorism.
4. **Geopolitical:** foreign interventions; proxy sponsorship; ungoverned borderlands.
5. **Technological:** online propaganda, encrypted finance, cheap drones, 3D-printed weapons, AI-made content.
6. **Criminal links:** drugs, smuggling, kidnapping, illegal mining.

## 4. Counter-terrorism: approaches and lessons

| Approach | Example | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Military | US wars; drone strikes; Pakistan's Zarb-e-Azb | Clears territory; does not end ideology; civilian harm fuels recruitment |
| Law enforcement and intelligence | European and South-East Asian models | Most effective against cells; needs rule of law |
| Financial | FATF; sanctions lists | Raises costs; pushes groups to cash and crypto |
| Political settlement | Northern Ireland (1998); Colombia–FARC (2016); Aceh | Insurgencies usually end at the table |
| Preventing violent extremism | Education, deradicalisation (Saudi, Indonesian programmes), community policing | Slow; hard to measure; essential |
| International cooperation | UN, SCO-RATS, Interpol, intelligence-sharing | Blocked by rivalry and by selective definitions |

**Twenty-five years of the "war on terror" (analysis):** it destroyed al-Qaeda's core and ISIS's territory, at a cost of several trillion dollars and hundreds of thousands of lives; it left failed interventions, Islamophobia, eroded civil liberties and a jihadist movement that is more dispersed.

## 5. Pakistan in the global picture

- Over 80,000 lives lost and more than $150bn in economic cost since 2001 (official estimates) **[verify]**.
- First on the 2026 index; faces the TTP, Baloch separatists and ISKP at once.
- Completed two FATF action plans (2022).
- Seeks international action against sanctuaries in Afghanistan and against what it calls state-sponsored terrorism; faces Indian charges about Lashkar-e-Taiba and Jaish-e-Mohammad.

## 6. Way forward

1. Agree a legal definition that covers all perpetrators and protects lawful self-determination.
2. Address conflicts and occupation that feed grievance (Palestine, Kashmir, the Sahel).
3. Hold Kabul to its Doha commitments through regional pressure and incentives.
4. Cooperation on online radicalisation, crypto finance and drones.
5. Rule-of-law-based counter-terrorism: fair trials, no torture, no collective punishment.
6. Invest in prevention: education, jobs, local governance.
7. Do not politicise listings at the UN.
