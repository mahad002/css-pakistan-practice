## 1. Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Refugee** | A person outside their country with a well-founded fear of persecution (1951 Refugee Convention and 1967 Protocol; 149 parties). Pakistan, India and Bangladesh are not parties. |
| **Non-refoulement** | No return to a place of serious harm; regarded as customary law binding on all states |
| **Asylum-seeker** | Someone whose claim is not yet decided |
| **Internally displaced person (IDP)** | Displaced inside their own country |
| **Migrant** | Anyone moving for work, study or family; about 304 million international migrants (UN, 2024) **[verify]** |
| **Stateless person** | Not recognised as a national by any state |
| **Climate displacement** | Not covered by the 1951 Convention |

Global Compacts on Refugees and on Migration (2018) are non-binding. Agencies: UNHCR, IOM, UNRWA (for Palestinians).

Source: [UNHCR Global Trends](https://unhcr.org/globaltrends); [report PDF](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/global-trends-report-2025.pdf).

**Why the total fell:** large returns to Syria after Assad's fall and to Afghanistan from Iran and Pakistan (many forced), not an outbreak of peace. New displacement continued in Sudan, Gaza, Lebanon, DR Congo, Myanmar and, in 2026, Iran and Afghanistan.

**Main origins:** Syria, Afghanistan, Ukraine, Venezuela, Sudan, Palestine (about 6 million under UNRWA). **Main hosts:** Iran, Türkiye, Colombia, Germany, Uganda, Pakistan. About 70% of refugees live in low- and middle-income countries, usually next door to their own **[verify shares]**.

**Policy climate in 2025–26**
- **United States:** mass deportation drive; refugee admissions cut to a historic low; travel bans; asylum closed at the southern border **[verify details]**.
- **Europe:** the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum applies from June 2026; offshore processing and "return hubs"; far-right gains.
- **Aid cuts:** UNHCR and WFP shed staff and rations after US and European funding fell in 2025.
- **Mediterranean and Channel** crossings continue; thousands die each year. A boat disaster off Greece (June 2023) killed hundreds, many of them Pakistani.
- **Rohingya:** about one million in Bangladesh; no return to Myanmar.

## 3. Causes and effects

**Causes:** war and persecution; state failure; poverty and inequality; climate disasters; demographic imbalance (ageing North, young South); smuggling networks.

**Effects on hosts:** pressure on services and wages at the bottom; fiscal gains from young workers; cultural and political backlash. **On origin countries:** remittances (about $900bn worldwide; exceed aid and FDI to poor countries **[verify]**), brain drain, labour shortages. **On politics:** migration is the main driver of right-wing populism in the West.

## 4. Pakistan

- **Host:** over four decades of Afghan refugees; 2.66 million returns since September 2023 (domestic file `16`).
- **Source:** over 10 million Pakistanis abroad; record remittances of $41.6bn in FY26; irregular migration to Europe through Libya and Türkiye; brain drain (domestic file `23`).
- **Climate displacement:** about 8 million displaced by the 2022 floods.
- **Law:** no refugee statute; anti-smuggling laws of 2018.

## 5. Way forward

1. Share responsibility: fund host countries; expand resettlement and legal pathways.
2. Respect non-refoulement; returns must be voluntary, safe and dignified.
3. Tackle causes: conflict resolution, climate adaptation, development.
4. Legal labour migration matched to ageing economies' needs; recognition of skills.
5. Protect climate-displaced people through new instruments.
6. Prosecute smugglers; protect victims.
7. For Pakistan: a refugee law; safe, skilled labour export; diaspora engagement.
