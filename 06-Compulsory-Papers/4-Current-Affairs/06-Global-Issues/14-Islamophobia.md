# Islamophobia

## 1. Background

- **Definition.** Fear of, prejudice against or hatred of Muslims that leads to hostility, discrimination or violence. The UN Special Rapporteur on freedom of religion (2021) described it as having reached "epidemic proportions". The term was popularised by the Runnymede Trust report (UK, 1997).
- **Intellectual roots:** Orientalism (Edward Said, 1978): the West's image of the Muslim "other"; Huntington's "clash of civilizations" (1993), which predicted conflict along a "bloody" Islamic frontier.
- **Triggers:** the Iranian revolution (1979); the Rushdie affair (1989); 9/11 and the "war on terror"; ISIS attacks in Europe (2015–17); refugee flows (2015); populist politics.

## 2. Forms and examples

| Form | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Violence** | Christchurch mosque attacks, New Zealand (15 March 2019, 51 killed); Quebec City (2017); London, Ontario (2021); anti-Muslim riots in England (August 2024) |
| **Law and policy** | France's ban on headscarves in schools (2004), face veils (2010) and abayas (2023); Swiss minaret ban (2009); the US "Muslim ban" (2017) and new travel bans (2025) **[verify]**; surveillance programmes |
| **Desecration** | Qur'an burnings in Sweden and Denmark (2023); Denmark then banned such acts; cartoons controversies (2005, 2015, 2020) |
| **State-linked majoritarianism** | India: Citizenship Amendment Act (2019), lynchings, "bulldozer" demolitions, hijab bans, the Waqf Amendment Act (2025) **[verify]**; China: mass detention of Uyghurs in Xinjiang (the UN human rights office reported possible crimes against humanity, 2022); Myanmar: the Rohingya expulsions (2017) |
| **Media and online** | Stereotyping; hate speech amplified by algorithms; a surge after 7 October 2023, alongside a rise in antisemitism |
| **Politics** | Electoral gains for anti-immigrant parties in Europe; "great replacement" conspiracy theory |

## 3. International response

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| 15 March 2022 | The UN General Assembly, on a resolution introduced by Pakistan for the OIC, proclaimed **15 March the International Day to Combat Islamophobia**. |
| 15 March 2024 | A second resolution (115 in favour, none against, 44 abstentions), also led by Pakistan, called for a **UN Special Envoy to combat Islamophobia**. |
| 2025 | Miguel Ángel Moratinos, head of the UN Alliance of Civilizations, was given the envoy role **[verify date and title]**. |
| Ongoing | OIC Islamophobia Observatory reports; Human Rights Council resolution 16/18 (2011) on combating intolerance; a 2023 Council resolution on religious hatred after the Qur'an burnings. |

**The legal debate:** Muslim states seek limits on insult to religion; Western states defend free speech and reject "defamation of religions" as a legal concept. International law (ICCPR Article 20) already prohibits advocacy of religious hatred that incites discrimination, hostility or violence.

## 4. Causes (analysis)

1. Securitisation of Muslims after 9/11.
2. Economic anxiety and migration, exploited by populists.
3. Ignorance and media framing.
4. Acts of violent extremists, which are wrongly attributed to a whole faith.
5. Geopolitics: wars in Muslim lands create refugees and resentment in both directions.
6. Weak, divided response from Muslim states, some of which stay silent on Xinjiang or India for economic reasons.

## 5. Effects

- Discrimination in jobs, housing and policing; attacks on mosques and women in hijab.
- Alienation of young Muslims in the West, which extremists exploit.
- Damage to relations between the Muslim world and the West; obstacles to refugee protection.
- Double standards weaken the credibility of human-rights advocacy.

## 6. Pakistan's role and limits

- Led both UN resolutions; raises the issue at the OIC and the UN.
- Credibility requires consistency: protecting minorities at home and preventing misuse of blasphemy laws (domestic file `20`); speaking on all persecuted Muslims.

## 7. Way forward

1. Implement the UN envoy's mandate with data collection and annual reports.
2. Laws against hate crime and incitement, applied equally to all faiths.
3. Platform accountability for hate speech.
4. Education and interfaith dialogue (Alliance of Civilizations; the Document on Human Fraternity, 2019).
5. Muslim states: a common, principled position; investment in media, scholarship and cultural diplomacy.
6. Muslim communities in the West: civic participation and coalitions with other minorities.
7. Address root conflicts (Palestine above all).

## 8. Likely exam questions

3. Is the "clash of civilizations" thesis being realised? 

## 9. Model answer outline (Question 1)

- **Intro:** definition; the 15 March day. Thesis: Islamophobia is a political phenomenon produced by securitisation and populism; it needs law, education and consistent diplomacy.
- **Roots; forms** (table); **causes; effects.**
- **Diagram:** cause-and-effect cycle: extremist act → media generalisation → discrimination → alienation → recruitment by extremists.
- **Response so far; gaps; way forward.**
- **Conclusion:** the answer to prejudice is equal citizenship everywhere, including in Muslim-majority states.
