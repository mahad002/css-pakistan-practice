## 1. Background

- **Founded** 24 October 1945 by 51 states; now 193. Purposes (Charter, Article 1): peace and security, friendly relations, cooperation on economic, social and humanitarian problems, human rights.
- **Record.** *Successes:* decolonisation, peacekeeping (over 70 missions since 1948), eradication of smallpox, the law of the sea, human-rights treaties, humanitarian relief, the SDGs. *Failures:* Rwanda (1994), Srebrenica (1995), Iraq (2003), Syria, Ukraine, Gaza, Sudan.
- **Earlier reforms:** the Council grew from 11 to 15 members in 1965; the Human Rights Council and Peacebuilding Commission (2005–06); "Responsibility to Protect" (2005).

## 2. Why reform is demanded

1. **The Security Council reflects 1945.** Five permanent members (P5) with vetoes; no permanent seat for Africa (54 states), Latin America, or the Muslim world.
2. **The veto blocks action** when a permanent member or its ally is involved: Russia on Ukraine and Syria; the US on Gaza (several vetoes of ceasefire resolutions in 2023–25); nothing on the 2026 Iran war.
3. **Selective enforcement** of international law.
4. **Money.** A liquidity crisis: the US, the largest contributor (22% of the regular budget), withheld payments and left several bodies in 2025–26; China is the second-largest payer. Budget and staff cuts of 15–20% followed **[verify]**.
5. **Sprawl and duplication** across agencies.
6. **Competition** from ad hoc groups: G20, BRICS, the Board of Peace for Gaza.

## 3. Security Council reform: the camps

| Group | Members | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **G4** | India, Japan, Germany, Brazil | New permanent seats for themselves and for Africa |
| **Uniting for Consensus** ("Coffee Club") | Italy, **Pakistan**, South Korea, Argentina, Mexico, Türkiye, Spain, Canada and others | No new permanent members; more elected seats, some longer-term and renewable |
| **African Union** (Ezulwini Consensus, 2005) | 55 states | Two permanent seats with veto and five non-permanent seats for Africa |
| **L.69, Arab Group, small islands** | – | Wider representation for the South |
| **P5** | – | Say they are open in principle; none will give up the veto. The US backed two African permanent seats without veto (2024). |

**The legal bar:** amending the Charter needs two-thirds of the General Assembly and ratification by two-thirds of members **including all five permanent members** (Article 108).

**Veto restraint ideas:** the France–Mexico initiative and the ACT code of conduct (no veto in mass-atrocity cases); the **"veto initiative"** (General Assembly resolution 76/262, April 2022) requires a debate each time a veto is cast; "Uniting for Peace" (1950) lets the Assembly act when the Council is blocked.

## 5. Assessment (analysis)

- **Realist view:** the UN mirrors power. The veto keeps great powers inside the system; removing it would not make them obey.
- **Liberal view:** imperfect institutions still lower the cost of cooperation; reform is slow but possible (1965).
- **Likely path:** no Charter amendment soon. Change will come through practice: veto explanation, a stronger General Assembly, regional organisations, better peacekeeping finance, and a new Secretary-General's management reforms.

## 6. Pakistan's position and reasoning

1. Opposes new permanent seats: they would add privilege, not representation, and would seat India permanently on a body that handles Kashmir.
2. Supports more elected seats, with fair rotation and regional balance, and an OIC presence.
3. Wants limits on the veto and a stronger General Assembly.
4. Values peacekeeping, where it is a leading contributor.

## 7. Way forward

1. Enlarge the Council to about 25–26 with longer-term elected seats as a compromise.
2. A code of conduct on the veto in atrocity cases.
3. Predictable finance: assessed contributions paid on time; a broader donor base.
4. Rationalise agencies; move functions closer to the field.
5. A transparent process for choosing the Secretary-General.
6. Partnership with regional bodies (AU, OIC, ASEAN).
7. Update rules for new domains: AI, cyber, outer space.
