## 1. Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **World order** | The pattern of power, rules and institutions that governs relations among states |
| **Polarity** | The number of great powers: unipolar (1991–about 2008), bipolar (1947–91), multipolar |
| **Liberal international order** | The post-1945 system led by the US: UN, Bretton Woods institutions, open trade, alliances, and (after 1991) promotion of democracy |
| **Multipolarity vs multilateralism** | Many centres of power vs cooperation through shared rules. A world can be multipolar and not multilateral. |
| **Multiplex world** (Amitav Acharya) | Many actors and many "screens": no single hegemon, overlapping regional orders |
| **Non-polarity** (Richard Haass); **G-Zero** (Ian Bremmer) | Power so diffused that nobody leads |

## 2. How the order changed

1. **1945–91:** bipolar Cold War; the UN frozen by vetoes; decolonisation; the Non-Aligned Movement.
2. **1991–2008:** the "unipolar moment" (Charles Krauthammer); Fukuyama's "end of history"; globalisation; NATO enlargement; interventions in Kosovo, Afghanistan and Iraq.
3. **2008–2022:** the financial crisis damaged the Western model; China's rise; Russia's return (Georgia 2008, Crimea 2014, Syria 2015); Brexit and Trump (2016); Covid-19 exposed fragile supply chains and weak cooperation.
4. **2022–26:** the Ukraine war; Gaza; US–China technology war; tariffs; the 2026 Iran war; expansion of BRICS; middle powers acting independently.

## 5. Is multipolarity good for small and middle states? (analysis)

**Gains:** more partners and more bargaining power; alternative finance; room for mediation (Pakistan in 2026); less risk of one power imposing its will.

**Dangers:** weaker rules protect the weak less; more wars and arms races; economic fragmentation (the IMF warns of costs from "geoeconomic fragmentation"); collective problems (climate, pandemics, AI) go unsolved; pressure to choose sides.

**Balanced judgment:** the world is moving from a unipolar order with rules applied unevenly to a **multipolar order with fewer rules**. The task is to rebuild multilateral rules that reflect the new distribution of power.

## 6. Pakistan in the new order

1. **Assets:** location, nuclear status, a large army, links to China, the US and the Gulf at once, standing in the Muslim world.
2. **Weaknesses:** a small, indebted economy; political instability; two hostile borders.
3. **Strategy:** multi-alignment (no blocs); economic strength as the base of foreign policy; active multilateralism (UN, SCO chair, OIC); coalitions of middle powers; consistent support for international law, which protects weaker states (Kashmir, the Indus Waters Treaty, Palestine).
