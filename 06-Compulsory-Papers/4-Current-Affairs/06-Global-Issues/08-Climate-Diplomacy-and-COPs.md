## 1. Background: the regime in one table

| Year | Milestone | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| 1988 | IPCC created | Scientific assessments (the 6th was completed in 2023) |
| 1992 | **UNFCCC** (Rio Earth Summit) | Framework treaty; "common but differentiated responsibilities" (CBDR); 198 parties |
| 1997 | **Kyoto Protocol** (COP3; in force 2005) | Binding cuts for developed countries only; the US never ratified; carbon markets (CDM) |
| 2009 | **Copenhagen Accord** (COP15) | No binding deal; the 2°C goal; a promise of $100bn a year by 2020 |
| 2015 | **Paris Agreement** (COP21) | All countries submit Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs); goal "well below 2°C", aiming for 1.5°C; five-yearly ratchet |
| 2021 | Glasgow (COP26) | "Phase down" of coal; doubling adaptation finance |
| 2022 | Sharm el-Sheikh (COP27) | **Loss and damage fund** agreed; Pakistan chaired the G77 and led the push after its floods |
| 2023 | Dubai (COP28) | First global stocktake; "transitioning away from fossil fuels"; tripling renewables by 2030; fund set up (hosted by the World Bank) |
| 2024 | Baku (COP29) | New finance goal (NCQG): **$300bn a year by 2035** from developed countries, within a wider aim of $1.3 trillion; carbon-market rules (Article 6) |
| 2025 | **Belém (COP30)** | See below |
| 2026 | **Antalya (COP31)** | Ended without a cover decision |

## 2. COP30, Belém, Brazil (November 2025)

- **Agreed:** a call to **triple adaptation finance by 2035**; the "Baku to Belém Roadmap" towards $1.3 trillion a year; a Global Implementation Accelerator; indicators for the adaptation goal; a just-transition mechanism; Brazil's Tropical Forests Forever Facility launched alongside **[verify details]**.
- **Not agreed:** a roadmap to move away from fossil fuels, backed by over 80 countries, was blocked by oil producers. Brazil's presidency promised its own voluntary roadmaps on fossil fuels and deforestation.
- **Context:** the US sent no federal delegation after leaving the Paris Agreement; many new NDCs for 2035 were late or weak; the UN said 1.5°C will be overshot at least temporarily **[verify wording]**.

Sources: [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop30-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-belem/); [WRI](https://www.wri.org/insights/cop30-outcomes-next-steps); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [IISD](https://iisd.org/articles/insight/cop-30-outcome-what-it-means-and-whats-next).

## 3. COP31, Antalya, Türkiye (November 2026)

- **Arrangement:** Türkiye hosted and held the presidency (Murat Kurum); Australia's Chris Bowen led the negotiations, a compromise between the two bidders.
- **Outcome:** the talks ran into overtime, the longest COP on record, and **ended on 22 November 2026 without an overarching deal, the first such failure since Copenhagen in 2009**.
- **Why:** an early draft called for an "immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems"; it was removed after objections reported from Saudi Arabia, Russia and other producers; the EU and others refused a text that did not mention fossil fuels. Finance and carbon capture were also disputed.
- **What survived:** decisions on how workers and communities shape the just-transition mechanism, and next steps for reviewing climate finance. The 2023 Dubai language still stands.
- **Next:** COP32 in Addis Ababa inherits the dispute.

Sources: [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/22/nx-s1-6046195/cop31-climate-talks-close-disappointment); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/22/climate/cop31-summit-collapses-in-fossil-fuel-failure); [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/climate-conference-ends-signature-agreement-countries-clash-fossil/story?id=138359179); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/cop31-ends-failure-countries-reject-climate-deal-over-fossil-fuels). These are first-day press reports; check the UNFCCC site for the final texts.

## 4. Have the COPs achieved anything? (the 2026 question)

| Achievements | Failures |
|---|---|
| A universal legal framework (Paris) and a cycle of pledges and review | Emissions are still at record levels; pledges put the world on about 2.5–2.8°C of warming **[verify latest UNEP Emissions Gap figure]** |
| Expected warming cut from about 4°C (pre-Paris projections) to under 3°C | The $100bn promise was met two years late; much finance is loans |
| Renewable energy made mainstream; solar now the cheapest new power in most places | No binding enforcement; NDCs are voluntary |
| Loss and damage recognised and a fund created | The fund holds well under $1bn against needs of hundreds of billions **[verify]** |
| Carbon-market rules; adaptation goal; just-transition work | Consensus rule lets a few producers block; the US has twice left Paris; COP31 collapsed |

## 6. Pakistan

- Under 1% of emissions; ranked first in the Climate Risk Index for 2022 and 15th for 1995–2024 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1954718)).
- Role: champion of loss and damage (2022); member of the fund's board; pushes for grant-based finance and debt-for-climate swaps.
- Needs: about $348bn by 2030 (World Bank, 2022).
- Domestic follow-through is the weak point (domestic file `11`).

## 7. Way forward

1. Decision-making reform: majority voting as a last resort; shorter, implementation-focused COPs.
2. Finance: deliver the $300bn with a grant floor; capitalise the loss and damage fund; reform development banks; levies on shipping, aviation and fossil profits.
3. A fossil-fuel transition roadmap by a coalition of the willing if consensus fails.
4. Hold large emitters to 2035 NDCs; link trade measures to support, not penalty.
5. For vulnerable states: common negotiating blocs, bankable project pipelines, regional early-warning systems.
