# US–China Rivalry

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Global issues – international security; international political economy; cooperation and competition in the Pacific. **Past papers:** 2016, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2022 (trade war as second Cold War), 2026 (note on China and world order). **Very likely in 2028.**

## 1. Background

- **From engagement to competition.** After Nixon's 1972 visit and China's WTO entry (2001), the US bet that trade would make China a "responsible stakeholder". By the 2010s China had become the second-largest economy (the largest by purchasing power), the top trader for over 120 countries and a military peer in East Asia.
- **Turning points:** Obama's "pivot to Asia" (2011); Xi Jinping's Belt and Road Initiative (2013) and "Made in China 2025"; Trump's first trade war (2018); the US 2017 National Security Strategy naming China a "strategic competitor"; Biden's export controls on advanced chips (October 2022) and alliances (AUKUS, QUAD, Camp David trilateral).
- **Theories:** Thucydides Trap (Allison); power transition (Organski); offensive realism (Mearsheimer: the US will try to prevent a regional hegemon in Asia); liberal interdependence (mutual dependence restrains conflict).

**Reading (analysis).** The relationship in late 2026 is a **managed rivalry**: leaders meet often and truces are extended, while the technology contest hardens. Analysts cited by Reuters judged that Xi strengthened his hand: China showed it can retaliate (rare earths), and the US was distracted by the Iran war and domestic politics. After the November 2026 midterms a Democratic Congress may push a harder line on technology and Taiwan.

## 4. Is it a new Cold War?

| Similar | Different |
|---|---|
| Two leading powers with rival systems | Deep economic interdependence (trade of about half a trillion dollars) |
| Arms racing, including nuclear | No two closed blocs; most states hedge |
| Competition for the Global South | China is inside the same institutions (WTO, UN) |
| Technology denial regimes | Shared problems need both (climate, pandemics, AI safety) |
| Flashpoints (Taiwan in place of Berlin) | Russia is a third pole; under Trump the US also quarrels with its allies |

## 5. Implications for Pakistan

1. **Opportunity:** both powers courted Pakistan in 2025–26 (CPEC II; the US trade and minerals opening).
2. **Risk of forced choices:** technology standards (Huawei, AI chips), ports and minerals, votes on Taiwan, Xinjiang or the South China Sea.
3. **Arms:** reliance on Chinese systems limits US defence sales and invites sanctions on missile entities.
4. **India factor:** the US builds India as a counterweight to China, which tilts the South Asian balance, though US–India ties were strained by tariffs in 2025.
5. **Economy:** tariff diversion may shift orders to Pakistan; a global slowdown would cut exports and remittances.
6. **Model:** the 1971 bridge role; multi-alignment, as practised by Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Türkiye and Indonesia.

## 6. Way forward (for the world and for Pakistan)

1. Guardrails: military hotlines, AI-in-nuclear-command understandings, crisis rules on Taiwan and at sea.
2. Keep trade disputes inside rules; reform the WTO.
3. Cooperation where interests meet: climate, health, debt relief for poor countries.
4. For Pakistan: no bloc politics; transparent rules for foreign investment in ports, minerals and telecoms; build economic value to both; coordinate with other middle powers.
