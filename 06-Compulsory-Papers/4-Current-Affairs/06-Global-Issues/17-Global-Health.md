## 1. Background

- **World Health Organization (WHO):** founded 7 April 1948 (World Health Day); Geneva; 194 member states before recent withdrawals; Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus (second term to August 2027). Its tools: the International Health Regulations (IHR, 2005), declarations of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, technical guidance.
- **Other actors:** Gavi (vaccines), the Global Fund (AIDS, TB, malaria), CEPI, UNICEF, the World Bank, the Gates Foundation, Africa CDC.
- **Covid-19 (2020–23):** about 7 million reported deaths; WHO estimates about 15 million excess deaths in 2020–21 alone; world output fell about 3% in 2020; vaccines were developed in under a year, but "vaccine nationalism" left poor countries waiting (the COVAX scheme fell short); trust in science and institutions was damaged by misinformation.

## 3. Structural issues

1. **Equity:** the North–South divide over intellectual property, technology transfer and manufacturing capacity (the TRIPS waiver debate; mRNA hubs in Africa).
2. **Finance:** WHO depends on voluntary, earmarked funds; members agreed to raise assessed contributions by 20% (2025). The Pandemic Fund at the World Bank is small beside needs.
3. **Sovereignty versus cooperation:** states resist inspection and data-sharing; misinformation portrays treaties as loss of sovereignty.
4. **Antimicrobial resistance:** linked to over a million deaths a year; a 2024 UN political declaration set a 10% reduction target by 2030.
5. **Non-communicable diseases and mental health:** now most of the burden in developing countries.
6. **Climate and health:** heat, floods, vector-borne disease, air pollution.
7. **Universal health coverage** (SDG 3.8): about half the world lacks full coverage of essential services **[verify]**.
8. **Health security and geopolitics:** "vaccine diplomacy" by China, India and Russia in 2021; biosecurity fears; AI-enabled biology.

## 4. Covid-19 and the world order (the 2021 question, updated)

- It accelerated existing trends more than it created new ones: US–China mistrust, supply-chain "reshoring", digitalisation, state intervention, debt.
- It exposed weak global leadership (no G20 plan comparable to 2008) and inequality within and between states.
- It left lasting effects: learning losses, public debt, distrust, and a treaty process still unfinished six years later.

## 5. Pakistan

- Covid response through the NCOC was rated effective; vaccination reached most adults with Chinese and COVAX doses.
- Polio: endemic; campaigns disrupted by insecurity, mass Afghan returns and the 2026 conflict.
- Exposure to aid cuts: US-funded health and nutrition programmes ended in 2025.
- Local manufacturing: a large generic-drug industry; no WHO-prequalified vaccine plant **[verify]**.
- Spending under 1% of GDP (domestic file `14`).

## 6. Way forward

1. Conclude the PABS annex with a guaranteed share of products for developing countries; ratify the Agreement.
2. Sustainable WHO financing from assessed contributions; a broader donor base as the US steps back.
3. Regional manufacturing of vaccines and diagnostics.
4. One Health surveillance linking human, animal and environmental data.
5. Protect health care in conflict (UNSC Resolution 2286).
6. Invest in primary health care as the first line of pandemic defence.
7. For Pakistan: finish polio; build a national public-health institute and vaccine production; raise domestic funding.
