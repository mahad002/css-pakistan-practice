# The Indo-Pacific, QUAD and AUKUS

## 1. Background

- **The term.** "Indo-Pacific" joins the Indian and Pacific Oceans into one strategic space. Japan's Shinzo Abe promoted it (2007, "confluence of the two seas"); the US adopted it in 2017 and renamed its Pacific Command the Indo-Pacific Command (2018). China sees the term as a containment frame and prefers "Asia-Pacific".
- **Why the region matters:** about 60% of world GDP and population; most seaborne trade and oil pass through its chokepoints: Malacca, Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Lombok, the Taiwan Strait.
- **China's "Malacca dilemma":** about 80% of its oil imports pass through one strait. Its answers: ports and access points across the Indian Ocean (Gwadar, Hambantota, Kyaukpyu, Djibouti base), which Indian writers call the "string of pearls", and overland corridors such as CPEC.

## 3. Flashpoints

1. **Taiwan:** China claims it; the US follows "strategic ambiguity" under the Taiwan Relations Act. Taiwan makes most of the world's advanced chips.
2. **South China Sea:** China's "nine-dash line" overlaps claims by the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan. An arbitral tribunal ruled for the Philippines in 2016; China rejected it. Clashes near Second Thomas Shoal and Scarborough Shoal.
3. **East China Sea:** Senkaku/Diaoyu islands (Japan–China).
4. **Korean peninsula:** North Korea's nuclear and missile programme; its alliance with Russia (2024).
5. **India–China border** (Ladakh clash 2020; partial disengagement 2024).
6. **Indian Ocean:** naval competition; Diego Garcia; piracy; the Houthi campaign in the Red Sea.

## 5. Implications for Pakistan

1. **India's naval build-up** (carriers, nuclear submarines, P-8 aircraft, US logistics access) shifts the balance in the Arabian Sea.
2. **Gwadar** is viewed through the US–China lens; Pakistan insists it is commercial.
3. **Pakistan Navy modernisation:** Hangor submarines and Type 054A/P frigates from China, MILGEM corvettes from Türkiye; the AMAN multinational exercise (biennial since 2007) as naval diplomacy.
4. **Blue economy:** a 1,000 km coast, an extended continental shelf (2015), fisheries and shipping are under-used.
5. **Exclusion risk:** Pakistan is outside the Indo-Pacific groupings and outside IMEC.
6. **Sea-based nuclear weapons** in the region affect strategic stability (external file `11`).

## 6. Way forward

1. Promote an inclusive Indian Ocean order: no blocs, freedom of navigation under UNCLOS, a code of conduct at sea.
2. Maritime CBMs with India: an incidents-at-sea agreement; fishermen's release.
3. Keep Gwadar open to all investors.
4. Engage ASEAN (seek full dialogue partnership), Japan, South Korea and Australia economically.
5. Build the blue economy: shipping, shipbuilding, coastal tourism, offshore energy.
6. Cooperate on non-traditional threats: piracy, trafficking, disaster relief, climate and sea-level rise.
