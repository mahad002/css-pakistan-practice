## 1. The regime in one table

| Instrument | Year | Substance | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NPT** | 1968 (in force 1970) | Three pillars: non-proliferation, disarmament (Article VI), peaceful use. Five recognised weapon states (US, Russia, UK, France, China). 191 parties. | India, Pakistan, Israel never joined; North Korea withdrew (2003). Review Conferences failed in 2015, 2022 and 2026. |
| **IAEA** | 1957 | Safeguards and inspections; Additional Protocol | Access in Iran sharply reduced since 2025 **[verify]** |
| **CTBT** | 1996 | Bans all nuclear explosions | Not in force: needs ratification by the US, China, Iran, Israel, Egypt; signature by India, Pakistan, North Korea. Russia withdrew its ratification in 2023. |
| **FMCT** | proposed | Ban on producing fissile material for weapons | Blocked at the Conference on Disarmament |
| **NSG** | 1975 | 48 suppliers controlling nuclear exports | India received a waiver in 2008; neither India nor Pakistan is a member |
| **MTCR**, Wassenaar, Australia Group | – | Export-control clubs | India joined the MTCR in 2016 |
| **TPNW** | 2017 (in force 2021) | Bans nuclear weapons outright | No nuclear-armed state has joined |
| **Nuclear-weapon-free zones** | – | Latin America, South Pacific, South-East Asia, Africa, Central Asia | Proposals for the Middle East and South Asia failed |
| **US–Russia treaties** | – | ABM (1972; US left 2002), INF (1987; ended 2019), Open Skies (US left 2020, Russia 2021), **New START** (2010; 1,550 deployed strategic warheads each) | All gone |

## 3. Why the regime is in crisis (analysis)

1. **The "grand bargain" is broken:** weapon states have not disarmed (Article VI) while demanding restraint from others.
2. **Double standards:** Israel's undeclared arsenal; the India–US deal and NSG waiver; AUKUS submarines using weapons-grade fuel.
3. **The lesson of recent wars:** Ukraine gave up weapons and was invaded; Libya gave up its programme and its regime fell; Iran stayed below the threshold and was bombed; North Korea has weapons and is not attacked. This rewards proliferation.
4. **Great-power rivalry** has become three-sided (US, Russia, China), which bilateral treaties cannot manage.
5. **Technology** shortens decision time and blurs conventional and nuclear lines.
6. **Institutional paralysis:** consensus rules at the Conference on Disarmament and NPT reviews.
7. **Nuclear threats in war:** Russia's signalling over Ukraine; crises between nuclear-armed India and Pakistan (May 2025).

## 4. Nuclear security and peaceful use

- Nuclear Security Summits (2010–16); Convention on the Physical Protection of Nuclear Material; UN Security Council Resolution 1540 (2004) on non-state actors.
- Risks: theft of material, attacks on plants (Zaporizhzhia; strikes on Iran's safeguarded sites), cyber-attack.
- Nuclear power is returning as a low-carbon source: about 440 reactors; small modular reactors; a COP28 pledge to triple capacity by 2050.

## 5. Pakistan's position

- Outside the NPT as a nuclear-armed state; says it will join as a recognised weapon state or alongside India.
- Supports a criteria-based approach to NSG membership; opposes an FMCT that ignores existing stocks; observes a unilateral test moratorium since 1998.
- Proposed to India: a strategic restraint regime, a bilateral test ban, a nuclear-weapon-free zone.
- Strong export controls since 2004; IAEA safeguards on civilian reactors.
- Concern: discriminatory waivers for India; missile-related US sanctions; the erosion of arms control raising Chinese and then Indian numbers.

## 6. Way forward

1. A US–Russia interim arrangement on limits and transparency; bring China into risk-reduction talks.
2. A P5 joint pledge against testing; push the CTBT into force.
3. Negative security assurances: a binding promise not to use nuclear weapons against non-nuclear states.
4. No-first-use or sole-purpose declarations.
5. Treat non-NPT states by criteria, not favour.
6. A diplomatic settlement with Iran with IAEA verification.
7. Keep humans in control of nuclear decisions.
