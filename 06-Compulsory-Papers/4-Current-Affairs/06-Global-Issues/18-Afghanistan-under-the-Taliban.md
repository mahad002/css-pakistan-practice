## 1. Background

- **Return to power:** 15 August 2021, after the US–Taliban Doha Agreement (29 February 2020) and the collapse of the Republic. The US completed its withdrawal on 30 August 2021.
- **The regime:** the "Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan". Supreme leader Hibatullah Akhundzada rules from Kandahar by decree; an all-male, overwhelmingly Pashtun and Taliban cabinet sits in Kabul (acting prime minister Mullah Hasan Akhund; interior minister Sirajuddin Haqqani; foreign minister Amir Khan Muttaqi). No constitution, no elections, no political parties.
- **Doha commitments:** not to let Afghan soil be used against others; intra-Afghan dialogue. Neither has been met in the view of the UN and neighbours.

## 2. Current situation (November 2026)

| Area | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Women and girls** | Girls barred from school beyond grade six (since 2021–22) and from universities (December 2022); women barred from most work with NGOs and the UN, from parks and gyms; a 2024 "virtue and vice" law restricts women's voices and movement; medical training for women halted (December 2024). The UN calls this "gender apartheid"; the ICC issued arrest warrants for Akhundzada and the chief justice for persecution on gender grounds (July 2025) **[verify]**. |
| **Recognition** | **Russia** became the first state to recognise the government (July 2025). China, Pakistan, the UAE, Uzbekistan and others host Taliban ambassadors without formal recognition. India upgraded its mission in Kabul in 2025 **[verify]**. The Taliban do not hold Afghanistan's UN seat. |
| **Security** | Fighting ended across most of the country in 2021. ISKP attacks continue. UN monitors report that the TTP, al-Qaeda and others operate from Afghan soil; the Taliban deny it. |
| **War with Pakistan** | Border war in October 2025; "open war" from late February 2026 with Pakistani air strikes on Kabul, Kandahar and the east; ceasefires at Eid; China-hosted talks in Urumqi (April 2026); strikes again in June, September and October. UNAMA counted at least 358 civilian deaths; about 115,000 displaced ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan_war); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/29/why-pakistans-afghan-air-strikes-arent-stopping-armed-attacks)). |
| **Returnees** | Several million Afghans returned from Iran and Pakistan in 2023–26, most under pressure; about one million from Pakistan in January–October 2026 alone (UNHCR). |
| **Economy** | The economy shrank by about a quarter after 2021 and has stabilised at a low level. About $7bn of central-bank reserves remain frozen abroad (half in a Swiss-based fund). Aid has fallen steeply, especially after US cuts in 2025. A ban on opium poppy (2022) cut cultivation by about 95% and removed farmers' income. Projects: the Qosh Tepa canal on the Amu Darya (worrying Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan), mining deals with Chinese firms, TAPI and rail links. Earthquakes (Herat 2023; Kunar 2025) and drought add to need. Over 20 million people need humanitarian aid **[verify latest]**. |
| **Internal splits** | Reported tension between the Kandahar leadership and more pragmatic figures in Kabul over girls' education and outside engagement; a nationwide internet shutdown in 2025 **[verify]**. |

## 3. International approaches

| Actor | Approach |
|---|---|
| **UN** | UNAMA's mandate renewed annually; the "Doha process" meetings with special envoys (the Taliban attended the third round in 2024, without Afghan women at the table); an independent assessment (2023) proposed a roadmap linking normalisation to rights and inclusion. |
| **United States** | No recognition; limited contact on detainees and counter-terrorism; aid stopped in 2025; Trump demanded the return of Bagram air base (2025) **[verify]**. |
| **China** | First to accredit a Taliban ambassador (2024); investment in oil and copper (Mes Aynak); wants action against Uyghur militants; hosts the trilateral with Pakistan. |
| **Russia** | Removed the Taliban from its terrorist list and recognised the government (2025); the Moscow Format. |
| **Iran** | Trade and transit; disputes over Helmand water; mass deportation of Afghans. |
| **Central Asia** | Pragmatic trade, electricity and rail; Uzbekistan most active. |
| **India** | Returned through aid and diplomacy; Kabul uses the link as leverage against Pakistan. |
| **Qatar, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, UAE** | Mediation (Doha and Istanbul talks, October 2025); airport operations; aid. |
| **OIC** | Envoys and scholars' delegations on girls' education, without result. |

**The dilemma (analysis):** isolation has not changed Taliban policy and hurts ordinary Afghans; engagement without conditions rewards repression. Most states now practise "engagement without recognition".

## 4. Implications

1. **Regional security:** safe havens for the TTP, ISKP, ETIM and others; the Pakistan war.
2. **Refugees and returns:** a country unable to absorb millions.
3. **Narcotics:** the opium ban holds, but methamphetamine production grows.
4. **Rights:** the most severe restrictions on women in the world.
5. **Connectivity:** Central–South Asia projects wait on stability.
6. **Great-power competition:** China and Russia fill the space the West left.

## 5. Way forward

1. A regional consensus (Pakistan, Iran, China, Russia, Central Asia, India) with common asks: verifiable counter-terrorism action, inclusive governance, girls' education.
2. Step-by-step normalisation tied to those steps: sanctions relief, unfrozen reserves, the UN seat.
3. Humanitarian and basic-services aid kept separate from politics.
4. Economic integration (trade, transit, energy) as an incentive.
5. Support for Afghan women's education through online and community schools and scholarships abroad.
6. A UN-backed monitoring mechanism for Pakistan–Afghanistan border incidents.
