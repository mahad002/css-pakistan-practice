## 3. Structural themes

1. **Slowbalisation and fragmentation:** trade is growing more slowly than output; "friend-shoring"; export controls; rival technology standards.
2. **The trading system:** the Doha Round (2001) never concluded; the Bali Package (2013) gave the Trade Facilitation Agreement; the WTO's Appellate Body has been idle since 2019; regional pacts (RCEP, CPTPP, AfCFTA) and bilateral deals fill the gap.
3. **Debt and development finance:** slow restructurings under the G20 Common Framework (Zambia, Ghana, Ethiopia); calls to reform the IMF and World Bank (quotas, SDR rechannelling, the Bridgetown Initiative); the Fourth Financing for Development Conference (Seville, 2025).
4. **Aid retreat:** US, UK and European cuts in 2025 reduced official aid sharply.
5. **Inequality:** within countries and between them; the top 1% hold over 40% of global wealth **[verify]**.
6. **SDGs:** adopted 2015 for 2030 (17 goals, 169 targets) as successors to the 8 MDGs (2000–15). Only about one-sixth of targets are on track; hunger and extreme poverty reduction have stalled since 2020 **[verify latest SDG Report]**.
7. **Technology:** AI may raise productivity and displace routine work; the IMF estimates about 40% of jobs worldwide are exposed.
8. **Energy transition** (file `16`) and climate costs.
9. **Demography:** ageing rich countries and China; young Africa and South Asia.
10. **The Global South's rise:** emerging economies produce most global growth; BRICS exceeds the G7 at purchasing power parity.

## 4. Globalisation: the debate

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Lifted over a billion people out of poverty (China, India, Vietnam, Bangladesh) | Deindustrialised regions in rich countries, feeding populism |
| Cheaper goods, technology diffusion | Inequality; race to the bottom on tax and labour |
| Interdependence raises the cost of war | Interdependence can be weaponised (sanctions, chips, rare earths, Hormuz) |
| Global problems need global markets and rules | Shocks spread fast (2008, Covid, 2026 oil) |

The phrase for the present is "re-globalisation with security filters": trade continues but is routed by politics.

## 5. Implications for Pakistan

1. Oil above $90 widens the import bill and inflation (CPI near 10% in late 2026); the policy rate went back up to 12%.
2. Export demand depends on the US and EU; tariff changes matter (19% in the US; GSP+ in the EU).
3. Remittances ($41.6bn) depend on Gulf stability.
4. Debt: roll-overs and IMF reviews shape policy; global rates set borrowing costs.
5. Opportunity: supply-chain shifts out of China; IT services; minerals; a young workforce.

## 6. Way forward (global)

1. Restore a working WTO dispute system; plurilateral deals on digital trade and green goods.
2. A faster sovereign-debt workout mechanism; more concessional finance; debt-for-climate swaps.
3. Voice reform at the IMF and World Bank.
4. Global minimum tax and cooperation against illicit flows.
5. Social protection and skills to manage AI disruption.
