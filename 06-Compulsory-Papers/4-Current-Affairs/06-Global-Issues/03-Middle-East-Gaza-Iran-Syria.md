## A. Palestine and Gaza

### Background
- **Roots:** Balfour Declaration (1917); UN partition plan (Resolution 181, 1947); creation of Israel and the Nakba (1948); the 1967 war (occupation of the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem; Resolution 242); Oslo Accords (1993); Hamas rule in Gaza (2007) and the blockade; Abraham Accords (2020).
- **7 October 2023:** Hamas-led attack killed about 1,200 people in Israel; about 250 were taken hostage. Israel's war in Gaza killed more than 67,000 Palestinians by October 2025 according to Gaza's health ministry **[verify latest total]**; most of the 2.1 million population was displaced; famine was declared in Gaza City in August 2025 (IPC).
- **Law:** ICJ provisional measures in *South Africa v. Israel* (January 2024); ICJ advisory opinion that the occupation is unlawful (July 2024); ICC arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant (November 2024); a UN commission of inquiry found genocide (September 2025) **[verify]**. Israel rejects all of these.
- **Recognition:** the UK, France, Canada, Australia and others recognised Palestine in September 2025; over 150 UN members now do **[verify count]**.

## B. The Iran wars

### Background
- The 2015 nuclear deal (JCPOA); US withdrawal (2018); "maximum pressure"; Iran enriching to 60%.
- **June 2025:** a 12-day Israel–Iran war; US strikes on Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan (22 June); ceasefire on 24 June **[verify]**. UN "snapback" sanctions were restored in September 2025 **[verify]**.
- January 2026: large protests in Iran were suppressed; the US built up forces.

**Costs:** thousands of casualties; a strike on a school in Minab reportedly killed over 150; 23 US military deaths; US cost of at least $48.3bn; at least 25 seafarers killed; Iran estimates $270bn of damage; the IEA called it the greatest energy-security challenge in history (figures from the Wikipedia summary; treat as provisional).

**Legal debate:** critics call the strikes a breach of Article 2(4) of the UN Charter, since no armed attack was imminent; the US and Israel cite self-defence against a nuclear and missile threat. Congress did not authorise the war.

**Reactions:** the SCO (September 2026) condemned the attacks; BRICS called for restraint without assigning blame; most states stayed neutral; opinion polls showed wide public opposition.

## C. Syria (brief)

- Assad fell on 8 December 2024 to an offensive led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham. Ahmed al-Sharaa became transitional president (2025) under a constitutional declaration with a five-year transition.
- The US lifted most sanctions in 2025; al-Sharaa met Trump. A deal to integrate the Kurdish-led SDF (March 2025) was implemented unevenly; a ceasefire was declared in January 2026 and Kurdish language and citizenship rights were recognised. Clashes and cabinet reshuffles continued in 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_transitional_government)).
- Sectarian violence (the coast in March 2025; Sweida in July 2025) and Israeli strikes and occupation in the south are continuing problems **[verify 2026 status]**.

## D. The regional picture (analysis)

1. **Iran's "axis" is weakened** (Hamas, Hezbollah, Assad gone) but Iran retains the power to close Hormuz and strike the Gulf.
2. **Israel is militarily dominant but diplomatically isolated** and politically divided.
3. **Gulf states were attacked directly** and are diversifying security: the Saudi–Pakistan pact (2025) and the Makkah alliance with Türkiye (2026).
4. **The US is the decisive outside actor** but cannot convert force into settlement.
5. **China and Russia gain influence by not fighting;** China's 2023 Saudi–Iran mediation did not prevent war.
6. **The Arab League, OIC and GCC** issue statements; action comes from small coalitions.
7. **Palestine remains the central grievance.** No normalisation is stable without a state.

## E. Pakistan's position and role

- Supports a two-state solution with pre-1967 borders and East Jerusalem as capital; does not recognise Israel.
- Voted for Resolution 2803; joined the Board of Peace; conditional on any troop role being peacekeeping only. A February 2026 survey found most Pakistanis support sending troops ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/9/survey-finds-strong-pakistani-support-for-gaza-force-but-questions-linger)).
- Mediated in the Iran war; has defence commitments to Saudi Arabia.
- Options: humanitarian and reconstruction aid; peacekeepers under a clear UN mandate with Palestinian consent; diplomacy through the OIC and UN; refusal to take part in disarmament by force.

## F. Way forward

1. Gaza: deploy the ISF with a UN-anchored mandate; full Israeli withdrawal on a timetable; Palestinian governance leading to unified, elected leadership; reconstruction (estimated at over $50–70bn **[verify]**).
2. A time-bound path to statehood; a halt to settlements and annexation.
3. Iran: a ceasefire-for-reopening deal, then a nuclear agreement with verification and sanctions relief; a regional security dialogue including Iran and the Gulf.
4. Lebanon and Syria: Israeli withdrawal; support for state institutions.
5. Accountability through the ICJ and ICC.
