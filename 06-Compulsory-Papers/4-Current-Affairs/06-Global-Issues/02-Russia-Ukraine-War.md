## 1. Background

- **Roots:** the Soviet collapse (1991); Ukraine gave up Soviet nuclear weapons under the Budapest Memorandum (1994) in return for assurances; NATO's eastward enlargement and its 2008 Bucharest statement that Ukraine and Georgia "will become members"; Ukraine's 2014 Maidan revolution; Russia's annexation of Crimea and the war in Donbas (2014); the failed Minsk agreements (2014–15).
- **Invasion:** 24 February 2022. Russia failed to take Kyiv, then annexed four regions it only partly controls (Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson) in September 2022.
- **Course:** Ukrainian counter-offensives (late 2022; 2023, which failed); a grinding Russian advance in Donetsk from 2024; Ukraine's incursion into Russia's Kursk region (August 2024); mass drone and missile strikes on cities and energy systems by both sides.

**Two explanations**
| Realist (Mearsheimer) | Liberal / Ukrainian and Western view |
|---|---|
| NATO expansion threatened Russia's core security; a great power reacted as great powers do. | An imperial war of choice against a sovereign democracy; Putin denies Ukrainian nationhood; NATO is a defensive alliance that states join freely. |

## 3. The sticking points

1. **Territory:** Russia wants all of Donetsk and Luhansk and recognition of its gains; Ukraine will not cede land it holds.
2. **Security guarantees:** who protects Ukraine after a deal, and how.
3. **NATO:** Russia demands permanent neutrality.
4. **Ukraine's army size and Western troops.**
5. **Sanctions and about $300bn of frozen Russian assets,** mostly in Europe.
6. **Accountability:** the ICC warrant for Putin (2023); reparations.
7. **Elections and the Russian language and church in Ukraine.**

## 4. Global effects

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| **European security** | Finland (2023) and Sweden (2024) joined NATO, now 32 members; NATO agreed in 2025 to raise defence spending towards 5% of GDP by 2035 **[verify]**; Germany rearmed. |
| **Energy** | Europe cut Russian pipeline gas from about 40% of imports to a small fraction, turning to US and Qatari LNG; Russia redirected oil to India and China at a discount; the Nord Stream pipelines were sabotaged (2022). The 2026 Gulf war added a second shock: Europe's gas price tripled briefly in October 2026 ([Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)). |
| **Food and fertiliser** | Price spikes in 2022; the Black Sea Grain Initiative (2022–23). |
| **World order** | Russia–China "no limits" partnership; North Korean troops and Iranian drones for Russia; the Global South largely refused to take sides; sanctions pushed de-dollarisation. |
| **Warfare** | Cheap drones, electronic warfare and long-range strikes; lessons studied by every army, including in South Asia. |
| **Law** | The ban on conquest (UN Charter Article 2(4)) is weakened if borders change by force. |

## 5. Pakistan

- **Position:** neutrality; abstained on General Assembly resolutions; calls for dialogue; good relations with both.
- **Effects:** the 2022 oil, wheat and fertiliser shock deepened Pakistan's payments crisis; discounted Russian crude since 2023; Western attention and aid moved to Ukraine.
- **Lessons often drawn:** security assurances on paper (Budapest) do not replace deterrence; small states near great powers must keep strong defence and balanced diplomacy; energy diversification matters.

## 6. Way forward

1. A ceasefire along the current line with international monitoring.
2. Security guarantees for Ukraine that are credible without NATO membership (bilateral treaties, armed neutrality, European reassurance forces).
3. Territory: no legal recognition of annexation; status deferred, as with other frozen conflicts.
4. Phased sanctions relief tied to compliance; frozen assets used for reconstruction.
5. A new European security framework including arms control.
6. Humanitarian steps first: prisoners, deported children, nuclear-plant safety (Zaporizhzhia).
