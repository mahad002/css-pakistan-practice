# Artificial Intelligence and Technology Governance

## 1. Background

- **What changed.** "Generative" AI (large language models such as ChatGPT, released November 2022) made AI a general-purpose technology used by hundreds of millions. Progress depends on three inputs: advanced chips (dominated by Nvidia's designs and Taiwan's TSMC), data and energy.
- **Why it is a governance problem:** it crosses borders; a few firms in two countries (the US and China) lead; risks range from bias and job loss to weapons and loss of control; law moves more slowly than the technology.

## 2. Governance efforts

|---|---|
| **OECD AI Principles** (2019) and **UNESCO Recommendation on AI Ethics** (2021) | Non-binding principles |
| **AI safety summits** | Bletchley Park, UK (2023): declaration on frontier risks; Seoul (2024); Paris "AI Action Summit" (February 2025), where the US and UK did not sign the declaration; **New Delhi "AI Impact Summit" (February 2026)**, the first in the Global South, adopted a declaration ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/india-ai-impact-summit-2026-adopts-new-delhi-declaration)) **[verify contents]** |

| **European Union AI Act** (2024) | The first comprehensive law: risk tiers (unacceptable, high, limited, minimal); bans on social scoring; duties for general-purpose models; phased application 2025–27; simplification and delays proposed in 2025 **[verify status]** |
| **Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI** (2024) | First binding treaty, focused on human rights |
| **United States** | Biden's 2023 executive order was revoked in January 2025; the Trump administration's 2025 AI Action Plan favours deregulation, exports and energy for data centres; chip export controls on China continue (a new rule took effect on 19 November 2026) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/apec-summit-trump-xi-china-trade-taiwan.html)) |

| **United Nations** | Global Digital Compact (September 2024). | **BRICS** | Leaders' statement on AI governance (2025), reaffirmed at New Delhi in September 2026 |

**Three models:** rights-based regulation (EU), market-led innovation (US), state-led control (China). Most developing countries are rule-takers.

## 3. Key issues

1. **Military AI:** autonomous weapons ("killer robots"), AI-assisted targeting (reported in Gaza and Ukraine), drone swarms, AI in command and control. Talks on lethal autonomous weapons under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons have produced no treaty; a General Assembly process began in 2024–25 **[verify]**. Biden and Xi agreed in November 2024 that humans, not AI, should control nuclear launch decisions.
2. **Jobs and inequality:** the IMF estimates about 40% of jobs are exposed (60% in advanced economies); outsourcing work in countries like Pakistan and the Philippines is vulnerable.
3. **Information integrity:** deepfakes in elections and conflicts (seen in the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict and the 2026 wars).
4. **Bias, privacy and surveillance.**
5. **Concentration:** compute and talent in a handful of firms; the "AI divide" between North and South.
6. **Energy and water** demand of data centres.
7. **Safety of frontier systems:** loss of control, misuse for bioweapons or cyber-attack.
8. **Geopolitics:** the US–China chip war; Taiwan's centrality; Gulf states buying compute at scale; "sovereign AI" plans.
9. **The economy:** the IMF's October 2026 outlook credits AI investment for holding up growth and warns of debt-financed over-investment ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/10/13/world-economic-outlook-october-2026)).

## 4. Other technology governance (brief)

- **Cyber:** the UN Convention against Cybercrime (adopted December 2024; opened for signature in Hanoi, 2025); norms of state behaviour (UN OEWG).
- **Data:** the EU's GDPR as a global template; data-localisation laws.
- **Platforms:** the EU Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act; Australia's ban on social media for under-16s (from December 2025).
- **Space:** militarisation and debris; the Outer Space Treaty (1967) is outdated.
- **Biotechnology:** gene editing; dual-use research.

## 5. Pakistan

- National AI Policy 2025; Digital Nation Pakistan Act 2025; no data-protection law yet (domestic file `18`).
- Interests: access to compute and models; skills; a voice in UN processes; rules on military AI given India's capabilities; protection against disinformation.
- Positions Pakistan has supported: a legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons; equitable access for developing countries.

## 6. Way forward

1. A layered regime: UN scientific panel for shared evidence; binding rules on the highest risks (autonomous weapons, AI in nuclear command, bioweapon misuse); national laws for the rest.
2. Capacity-building: shared compute, open models, training for the Global South (a "CERN for AI" idea).
3. Transparency and audit duties for frontier developers; incident reporting.
4. Labelling of synthetic media; election safeguards.
5. Social policy: reskilling, safety nets.
6. Confidence-building between the US, China and other military powers on AI.
