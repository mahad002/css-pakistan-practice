> **As of:** 23 November 2026.
> **Source of the syllabus:** FPSC, "Syllabus for CE-2016 and onwards" ([official PDF](https://fpsc.gov.pk/assets/media/2024-10-22-01-45-26-Syllabus_for_CE-2016_and_onwards.pdf)). The FPSC site refused my connection (certificate error), so I confirmed the three sections and the 20/40/40 marks from public copies ([Knowt transcription](https://knowt.com/note/9aec29c3-33b8-47ec-8748-16f944476035/Comprehensive-Scheme-and-Syllabi-for-CSS), [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/610730145/Current-Affairs-CSS-Syllabus)). The individual bullet points below are paraphrased from the commonly circulated text of that syllabus. **[verify]** the exact wording against the official PDF once.

The syllabus opens by saying that candidates should show enough general knowledge of history, politics and international affairs to interpret current events. That sentence matters: the paper tests interpretation, not recall.

**Frequency key:** number of the 11 regular papers from 2016 to 2026 in which the item was the main subject of at least one question (my count from `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`). Tick the box when you have read the notes, updated them and written one outline.

## What the map tells you

1. **Climate and environment is the single most repeated theme** (7 of 11 papers). Prepare it in three forms: Pakistan's vulnerability, the COP process and climate finance.
2. **China/CPEC and the US–China contest** appear almost every year in some form.
3. **The 20/40/40 split is not followed.** Domestic governance and economy questions have taken two to four slots since 2019. Do not under-prepare domestic topics because the syllabus gives them 20 marks.
4. **Three syllabus items have never produced a full question** (Indonesia, Doha/Bali trade rounds, MDGs as such). Know one page on each. Spend your time elsewhere.
5. **Every year at least one question follows the biggest event of the previous 12 months** (Paris 2016, Qatar crisis 2018, Kashmir 2020, Afghanistan 2022, Ukraine 2022–24, Gaza 2024 and 2026, COP30 2026). For 2028 that points to the 2026 Iran war, the Afghanistan conflict, the Indus Waters award and the Makkah defence pact.
