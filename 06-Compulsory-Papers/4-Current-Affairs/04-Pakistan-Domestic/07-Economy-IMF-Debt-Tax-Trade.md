## 1. Background: the boom–bust cycle

Pakistan grows by consuming and importing. When growth passes about 4–5%, imports rise faster than exports, reserves fall, the rupee drops and the country returns to the IMF. This has happened roughly every four to five years. The roots are low savings and investment, a narrow tax base, low exports and an expensive energy system.

**2022–23 crisis.** Floods, the global commodity shock and political turmoil pushed reserves below $4bn in early 2023 and inflation to 38% in May 2023. A nine-month IMF Stand-By Arrangement (July 2023) averted default.

## 3. Structural problems (analysis)

1. **Tax.** Tax-to-GDP of roughly 10–11% **[verify FY26]**. Retail, real estate and agriculture are lightly taxed; salaried workers and manufacturing carry the load; heavy reliance on indirect taxes.
2. **Debt.** Interest takes more than the federal government's net revenue. New borrowing pays old debt.
3. **Exports.** Stuck near $30–32bn in goods; 60% textiles; few markets. Vietnam exports more than ten times as much.
4. **Investment.** About 13–14% of GDP, half the South Asian average.
5. **Energy.** High tariffs and circular debt make industry uncompetitive (file `08`).
6. **State-owned enterprises.** Losses of hundreds of billions of rupees a year.
7. **Human capital.** Low spending on health and education (files `13`, `14`).
8. **Remittance dependence.** $41.6bn hides the export failure and is exposed to Gulf instability.
9. **Elite capture.** Tax exemptions and subsidies for powerful sectors.
10. **Policy instability.** Frequent changes of rules deter investors.

**Stabilisation is real:** reserves have risen about five-fold from the 2023 low, inflation fell from 38%, and primary surpluses have been recorded. **But growth is weak,** poverty has risen and external shocks reverse gains quickly. This is "stabilisation without transformation".

## 4. Way forward

1. **Broaden the tax base:** enforce agricultural income tax, tax retail and property at market value, cut exemptions, digitise FBR (track and trace, e-invoicing).
2. **Export-led growth:** rationalise tariffs (National Tariff Policy 2025–30), competitive energy for exporters, diversify into IT, engineering, agri-processing, minerals.
3. **Debt management:** lengthen maturities, swap expensive debt, debt-for-nature and climate swaps, limit new guarantees.
4. **Finish energy reform** (file `08`).
5. **Privatise or restructure** loss-making enterprises transparently.
6. **Raise savings and investment:** stable rules, faster contract enforcement, one-window facilitation that is civilian-led and lawful.
7. **Invest in people:** education and health at 4% and 3% of GDP over time; expand BISP with graduation programmes.
8. **Plan the exit from the IMF** (2027) with a home-grown, parliament-approved reform plan so that the next boom does not end in the next bailout.
