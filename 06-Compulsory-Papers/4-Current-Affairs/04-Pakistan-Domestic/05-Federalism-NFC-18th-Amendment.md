# Federalism, the NFC Award and the 18th Amendment

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – political and economic. **Past papers:** 2020 (inter-provincial harmony and water), 2021 and 2024 (new provinces), 2025 (ethnicity). Read with files `03`, `06` and `22`.

---

## 1. Background

- **Federal design.** Four provinces, Islamabad Capital Territory, plus Gilgit-Baltistan and AJK with special status. Federal institutions: Senate (equal seats per province), Council of Common Interests (CCI, Article 153), National Economic Council (Article 156), National Finance Commission (NFC, Article 160).
- **History.** Centralisation under One Unit (1955–70) contributed to the loss of East Pakistan. The 1973 Constitution created a federal bargain. Military governments re-centralised.
- **18th Amendment (April 2010).** Abolished the Concurrent Legislative List; moved 17 ministries (including health, education, environment, labour) to provinces; strengthened the CCI; gave provinces joint ownership of oil and gas (Article 172(3)); renamed NWFP as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; added Article 25A (right to education) and Article 140A (local government); protected provincial NFC shares from reduction (Article 160(3A)).
- **7th NFC Award (2009, effective 2010).** Raised the provinces' share of the divisible pool to 57.5%. For the first time it used several criteria: population 82%, poverty 10.3%, revenue 5%, inverse population density 2.7%. Shares: Punjab 51.74%, Sindh 24.55%, KP 14.62%, Balochistan 9.09%. An extra 1% of the pool goes to KP for the costs of terrorism.

## 3. The debate

| Centre's case | Provinces' case |
|---|---|
| After transfers, the centre cannot pay for debt service and defence without borrowing. | The centre's problem is low tax collection (FBR) and loss-making enterprises, not the NFC. |
| Provinces under-tax agriculture, property and services and run cash surpluses. | Provinces took on health, education and policing costs; surpluses are an IMF requirement. |
| Devolved subjects (curriculum, drug regulation, population) need national standards. | The CCI exists for coordination; it should meet every 90 days as the Constitution requires. |
| The 2010 formula is outdated; the 2023 census changed population shares. | Reopening Article 160(3A) breaks the federal bargain and feeds ethnic grievance. |

## 4. Challenges

1. **Vertical imbalance:** the centre collects about 90% of taxes; provinces spend about a third or more of the total.
2. **Horizontal dispute:** the 82% weight on population rewards population growth and angers smaller provinces.
3. **Incomplete devolution:** power stopped at provincial capitals and did not reach districts (file `06`).
4. **Weak coordination forums:** irregular CCI meetings.
5. **Resource ownership disputes:** gas (Balochistan, Sindh), hydel profits (KP), minerals (the 2025 mines and minerals bills), water (Sindh–Punjab).
6. **Asymmetric units:** Gilgit-Baltistan and AJK lack representation in parliament and in the NFC.

## 5. Way forward

1. Conclude the 11th NFC by consensus. Lower the population weight; add criteria for forest cover, human development gaps, tax effort and fertility reduction.
2. Keep the 57.5% floor and solve the centre's gap through a **national fiscal pact**: provinces fund devolved functions fully and share BISP and higher education; the centre closes duplicate ministries.
3. Raise provincial revenue: agricultural income tax enforcement, urban property tax at market values, a harmonised sales tax on services.
4. Create **Provincial Finance Commissions** with binding awards to districts.
5. Convene the CCI on schedule and give it a permanent secretariat (Article 154 requires one).
6. Settle resource disputes by formula: net hydel profits, gas royalties, the 1991 Water Accord with telemetry.
7. Give Gilgit-Baltistan and AJK a defined fiscal share.

## 7. Model answer outline (Question 1)

- **Intro:** the 18th Amendment was the largest transfer of power since 1973. Thesis: it reduced centre–province political conflict but its fiscal and local-government halves were left unfinished.
- **Background:** One Unit; 1971; 1973; 2010.
- **Gains:** provincial ownership; end of presidential dissolution; reduced separatist appeal in Sindh and KP; provincial policy innovation (health cards, Sindh's services tax authority).
- **Shortfalls:** fiscal squeeze at the centre; uneven provincial capacity; standards fragmentation; no devolution to districts.
- **Diagram:** flow of the divisible pool: FBR taxes → 57.5% provinces (four shares) / 42.5% federation → debt, defence, the rest.
- **Way forward:** points above.
- **Conclusion:** the answer to an incomplete devolution is to complete it.
