# Water Crisis and the Indus Waters Treaty

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Treaty status:** India has held the treaty "in abeyance" since 23 April 2025. On 31 August 2026 the Court of Arbitration ruled that this is not permitted and the treaty remains in force. India rejects the ruling.
> **Syllabus:** Domestic – economic/social; external – India; Kashmir. **Past papers:** 2019 (Pakistan–Afghanistan water), 2020 (climate, water and inter-provincial harmony). **Very likely in 2028.**

---

## 1. Background

### The domestic water problem
- Pakistan depends on one river system. The Indus basin irrigation network is the largest contiguous one in the world.
- Per capita water availability fell from about 5,260 cubic metres in 1951 to under 1,000 today, the threshold of "water scarcity" **[verify latest figure; commonly cited about 900]**.
- Agriculture uses over 90% of withdrawals and produces under a quarter of GDP.
- Storage covers about 30 days of flow; the usual benchmark is 120 days or more **[verify]**. Tarbela and Mangla are losing capacity to silt.
- The 1991 Water Apportionment Accord divides 114.35 million acre-feet (MAF): Punjab 55.94, Sindh 48.76, KP 5.78 (plus 3.00 for civil canals), Balochistan 3.87. IRSA administers it.

### The Indus Waters Treaty (IWT), 1960
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Signed | 19 September 1960, Karachi, by Nehru and Ayub Khan; the World Bank is a signatory for certain purposes |
| Division | Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India. Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan, with India allowed limited uses: domestic, non-consumptive, some agriculture, and run-of-river hydropower within design limits |
| Institutions | Permanent Indus Commission (one commissioner each) |
| Disputes | Three steps: Commission ("questions"), Neutral Expert ("differences"), Court of Arbitration ("disputes") |
| Exit clause | None. Article XII allows change only by a new treaty agreed by both |

**Earlier cases:** Baglihar (Neutral Expert, 2007), Kishenganga (Court of Arbitration, 2013). Current cases concern the design of India's Kishenganga and Ratle plants. Pakistan went to the Court of Arbitration; India preferred the Neutral Expert; the World Bank started both in 2022. India sent notices seeking to "modify" the treaty in January 2023 and August 2024.

**What India can and cannot do (analysis).** India lacks the storage to stop the western rivers in the near term. It can alter the *timing* of flows (flushing, filling), withhold flood data and, over years, build storage and diversion works. For Pakistan the danger is uncertainty at sowing time and floods without warning, then a slow loss of water security.

**Domestic side in 2025–26.** The plan for new canals from the Indus for Cholistan caused protests in Sindh and was shelved through the CCI in 2025 **[verify]**. The 2025 floods showed that Pakistan suffers from too much water in some months and too little in others (file `11`).

## 3. Challenges

1. **External:** treaty suspension; upstream projects; climate-driven glacier melt; no treaty with Afghanistan on the Kabul River (asked in 2019).
2. **Governance:** flood irrigation, unlined canals (losses of 40% or more), near-free canal water (abiana covers a fraction of costs), unregulated tubewells (over a million).
3. **Storage and siltation:** Diamer-Bhasha (4,500 MW, about 6.4 MAF live storage) and Mohmand (800 MW) are delayed.
4. **Crops:** water-hungry rice and sugarcane in a water-scarce country.
5. **Inter-provincial mistrust:** Sindh's fears as the lower riparian; Kalabagh; sea intrusion in the Indus delta.
6. **Quality:** most wastewater is untreated; arsenic in groundwater.
7. **Urban water:** Karachi and Quetta face chronic shortages.

## 4. Way forward

**External**
1. Keep the legal track: enforce the 2026 award diplomatically at the World Bank, the UN and with major powers; publish hydrological data to show violations.
2. Offer technical talks on data-sharing and climate adaptation inside the treaty framework.
3. Negotiate a Kabul River arrangement when relations with Afghanistan allow.

**Domestic**
4. Implement the National Water Policy (2018): 10% of the development budget for water.
5. Conservation first: drip and sprinkler irrigation, laser levelling, canal lining, crop zoning.
6. Price water and regulate groundwater by licence.
7. Build storage of all sizes: complete Mohmand and Diamer-Bhasha; small dams; recharge aquifers with floodwater.
8. Telemetry on all barrages to rebuild trust in the 1991 Accord.
9. Wastewater treatment and reuse in cities.
