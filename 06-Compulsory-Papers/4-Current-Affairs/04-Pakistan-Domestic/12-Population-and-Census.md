## 1. Background

- **Census 2023** (the 7th, and the first digital one): **241.49 million** people; growth rate **2.55%** a year for 2017–23; about 39% urban. Provinces: Punjab about 127.7m, Sindh 55.7m, KP 40.9m, Balochistan 14.9m, Islamabad 2.4m. Pakistan is the fifth most populous country.
- **Growth history:** about 34 million in 1951. The population has grown roughly seven-fold in 75 years.
- **Why growth stays high:** total fertility rate of about 3.5 births per woman **[verify latest PDHS]**; contraceptive use of about one-third of couples; early marriage; low female education and employment; son preference; weak family-planning services; religious and social resistance; no political ownership.
- **Comparison:** Bangladesh (once East Pakistan, more populous than West Pakistan in 1971) now has about 175 million people and a fertility rate near 2. Iran cut fertility from over 6 to about 2 in two decades through clinics, female education and clerical support.

### World trends (for "global issues" questions)
- World population passed 8 billion on 15 November 2022 (UN). The UN's 2024 projections expect a peak of about 10.3 billion in the 2080s.
- India overtook China as the most populous country in 2023. China's population has been falling since 2022.
- **Two worlds:** fast growth in sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia; **depopulation** and ageing in East Asia and Europe (South Korea's fertility rate is below 1; Japan, Italy and China are shrinking). More than half of all countries are below the replacement rate of 2.1.
- Policy responses: pro-natal incentives (Hungary, South Korea, China's three-child policy), immigration, later retirement, automation.

## 3. Consequences for Pakistan

1. **Economy:** growth of 3–4% barely exceeds population growth; per capita income stagnates.
2. **Jobs:** a "youth bulge" becomes a dividend only with skills and employment; otherwise it feeds unrest and emigration.
3. **Services:** schools, hospitals and housing cannot keep up; over 20 million children out of school.
4. **Food and water:** per capita water under 1,000 cubic metres; wheat import needs.
5. **Cities:** unplanned urbanisation, slums, congestion.
6. **Environment:** deforestation, groundwater depletion, pollution.
7. **Women's health:** high maternal mortality and anaemia from closely spaced births.
8. **Federal politics:** seats, jobs and funds follow population counts, so every census is disputed (Karachi; Balochistan).

## 4. Way forward

1. Political ownership at the top; a national narrative on "balance" (*tawazun*) between population and resources, with support from religious scholars (the Iranian, Indonesian and Bangladeshi models).
2. Girls' secondary education and women's employment: the most effective long-run measures.
3. Family-planning services at every health facility; expand Lady Health Workers; involve the private sector and social marketing.
4. Enforce a minimum marriage age of 18 nationwide (Sindh 2013; Islamabad 2025 **[verify]**).
5. Change the NFC formula to reduce the population weight and reward fertility decline.
6. Skills and jobs for youth: technical training, IT, managed labour migration.
7. Regular, credible digital censuses linked to NADRA data.
