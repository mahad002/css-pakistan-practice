## 1. Background

- **Constitution.** Fundamental rights are in Articles 8–28: life and liberty (9), safeguards on arrest (10), fair trial (10A), dignity and ban on torture (14), movement, assembly, association (15–17), speech (19), religion (20), equality (25), education (25A), minorities (36, a principle of policy).
- **Treaties.** Pakistan has ratified seven of the nine core UN human rights treaties, including the ICCPR, ICESCR, CEDAW, CRC and the Convention against Torture. Compliance with 27 conventions is a condition of the EU's GSP+ trade preference.
- **Institutions.** National Commission for Human Rights (2012 Act), National Commission on the Status of Women, National Commission on the Rights of the Child, a National Commission for Minorities (law passed 2025 **[verify]**), provincial ombudspersons, the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (an NGO).

### Civil and political rights
- **Enforced disappearances,** especially in Balochistan and KP; a commission of inquiry has existed since 2011; criminalisation is incomplete.
- **Military trials of civilians** after 9 May 2023; lifetime immunity clauses in the 27th Amendment (file `03`).
- **Speech and assembly:** PECA 2025; bans on groups (the Pashtun Tahaffuz Movement in October 2024; JAAC in AJK in June 2026); long sentences for activists and lawyers in 2026, as reported.
- **Minorities and blasphemy:** misuse of blasphemy laws, mob violence (Jaranwala 2023, Sargodha and Swat 2024), forced conversion of Hindu and Christian girls in Sindh and Punjab, hostility to Ahmadis.
- **Sectarian violence:** the Islamabad Shia mosque bombing (6 February 2026); Kurram clashes (2024–25).
- **Press freedom:** in January 2026 an anti-terrorism court gave life terms to journalists over the 2023 protests, as reported **[verify]**. Pakistan ranks in the bottom quarter of the RSF World Press Freedom Index **[verify 2026 rank]**.
- **International comment:** the UN human rights chief urged Imran Khan's release and de-escalation in November 2026 ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641245/un-rights-chief-calls-for-imran-khans-release-urges-political-de-escalation)).

### Civic space and NGOs (the 2026 exam theme)
- International NGOs must register with the Interior Ministry (2015 policy); many were asked to leave in 2018. Local NGOs receiving foreign funds need an agreement with the Economic Affairs Division.
- Reasons states give: security (the 2011 fake vaccination campaign linked to the hunt for Bin Laden damaged trust), FATF rules on terror finance, foreign agendas.
- Global pattern: CIVICUS rates civic space as "repressed" or "closed" in a majority of countries; aid cuts in 2025 (USAID's dismantling) weakened NGOs everywhere.

## 3. Challenges

1. A gap between good laws and weak enforcement (police, prosecution, courts).
2. Patriarchal norms; low female mobility, property ownership and digital access.
3. Security-first governance that treats dissent as a threat.
4. Impunity for powerful actors.
5. Poverty: bonded labour in brick kilns and farms; child labour.
6. Weak, under-funded commissions.
7. Politicisation: rights are defended selectively by whoever is in opposition.

## 4. Way forward

1. Enforce existing laws: gender desks and women officers in police stations, special courts for gender-based violence, legal aid.
2. Economic inclusion: girls' stipends, safe transport, childcare, credit, inheritance enforcement, quotas actually filled.
3. Minimum marriage age of 18 everywhere.
4. Criminalise enforced disappearance and torture fully; ratify the disappearance convention.
5. Procedural safeguards against misuse of blasphemy laws (senior-officer inquiry before registration; penalties for false accusation).
6. Independent, funded human-rights commissions with power to inspect places of detention.
7. A clear, light-touch NGO law: transparency of funds without discretionary bans.
8. Human-rights education in police and civil-service training.
9. Use GSP+ and UN reviews as a reform calendar, not as a threat.
