## 1. Background

- **Weight in the economy.** Agriculture gives about 23–24% of GDP and employs about 37% of the labour force **[verify FY26 shares]**. Livestock is more than 60% of agricultural value. Major crops: wheat, rice, cotton, sugarcane, maize.
- **Food security has four parts** (FAO): availability, access, utilisation, stability. Pakistan usually produces enough calories. Its problem is access (poverty and prices), utilisation (nutrition, clean water) and stability (floods, policy shocks).
- **History.** The Green Revolution of the 1960s raised wheat and rice yields. Since the 1990s productivity growth has slowed; yields are far below potential and below India's Punjab or China.

## 3. Challenges

1. **Low yields:** poor seed (certified seed covers a minority of area), low mechanisation, weak extension services.
2. **Water:** flood irrigation; shortages at sowing; the Indus Waters Treaty dispute (file `10`).
3. **Climate:** heat at grain-filling, erratic monsoon, floods (2022 destroyed about 4 million acres of crops), pests (cotton's pink bollworm and whitefly).
4. **Land:** small, fragmented holdings beside large estates; tenancy insecurity; farmland lost to housing schemes.
5. **Markets:** middlemen (*arthi*), poor storage (post-harvest losses of 15–40% for perishables), price volatility, sudden export bans and import decisions.
6. **Policy distortions:** sugarcane protected though it is water-hungry; sugar and wheat "crises" tied to cartel behaviour.
7. **Credit and insurance:** small farmers depend on informal lenders.
8. **Research:** spending under 0.2% of agricultural GDP **[verify]**.
9. **Cotton decline:** output has roughly halved from its 2014–15 peak, forcing textile mills to import.
10. **Malnutrition:** diets heavy in wheat and sugar, poor in protein and micronutrients.

## 4. The GMO question (asked as a short note in 2026)

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Higher yields and pest resistance (Bt cotton cut pesticide use at first) | Pest resistance evolves; Pakistan's Bt cotton spread through unapproved, poor-quality seed |
| Drought-, heat- and salt-tolerant varieties for a changing climate | Dependence on multinational seed companies; cost of seed; intellectual-property disputes |
| Nutrition gains (for example, vitamin-enriched "golden rice") | Biosafety and biodiversity concerns; gene flow to local varieties |
| Lower import bills (edible oil, soybean feed) | Export risk: basmati rice buyers in the EU reject GM traces |

**Position in Pakistan:** Bt cotton is grown widely. GM food crops are not approved for cultivation; GM soybean imports for feed were disputed in 2022–23 and later allowed under rules **[verify]**. A balanced answer supports case-by-case approval under a strong biosafety regulator, labelling, and public research so that technology is not monopolised.

## 5. Way forward

1. Seed reform: enforce the Seed Act and Plant Breeders' Rights; public–private breeding; climate-resilient varieties.
2. Water productivity: drip, laser levelling, crop zoning, pricing.
3. Mechanisation and services for small farmers through rental centres and cooperatives.
4. Market reform: warehouse receipts, cold chains, electronic markets, predictable trade policy.
5. Diversify to oilseeds, pulses, horticulture and livestock to cut the edible-oil import bill (over $4bn a year **[verify]**).
6. Crop insurance and digital credit (Kissan cards).
7. Corporate and cooperative farming with safeguards for local land rights (the Green Pakistan Initiative under SIFC is contested on water and land grounds).
8. Nutrition: fortification, school meals, BISP *Nashonuma* for mothers and infants.
9. Strategic reserves and an early-warning system for food prices.
