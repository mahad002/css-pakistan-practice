# Education

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – social. **Past papers:** not asked alone; needed for 2026-style questions on human development, brain drain and SDGs (SDG 4).

---

## 1. Background

- **Constitution.** Article 25A (2010): free and compulsory education for all children aged 5 to 16. Article 37(b): remove illiteracy. Education has been a provincial subject since the 18th Amendment; the federation keeps higher-education standards (HEC) and coordinates through the Inter-Provincial Education Ministers' Conference.
- **Three parallel systems:** public schools, private schools (from low-fee to elite English-medium) and madrassas (over 30,000 **[verify]**). They produce different skills and world-views.
- **Policies:** National Education Policy 2009; Single National Curriculum (2021–22, now the "National Curriculum of Pakistan"); a national "education emergency" was declared in May 2024.

## 3. Challenges

1. **Access:** poverty, distance, missing facilities (boundary walls, toilets, water), child labour.
2. **Gender and regional gaps:** rural girls in Balochistan, ex-FATA and south Punjab are the most excluded.
3. **Quality:** rote learning, weak teacher training, multi-grade classrooms, "ghost" schools and teacher absenteeism.
4. **Finance:** low spending, most of it on salaries; development budgets are under-used.
5. **Governance:** political postings, weak school management, poor data.
6. **Fragmentation:** three systems, language-of-instruction confusion.
7. **Relevance:** graduates lack marketable skills; technical and vocational training reaches few.
8. **Shocks:** floods (thousands of schools damaged in 2022 and 2025), insecurity in KP and Balochistan, closures during the 2026 fuel crisis.
9. **Higher education:** quantity over quality; low research output; campus politics; brain drain of graduates.

## 4. Way forward

1. Raise spending towards 4% of GDP in steps, with a floor in provincial budgets.
2. Enrol the out-of-school through stipends for girls (BISP Taleemi Wazaif), second-shift schools, non-formal centres and public–private partnerships (the Punjab and Sindh Education Foundations).
3. Teachers: merit hiring, licensing, continuous training, attendance by biometric monitoring.
4. Foundational literacy and numeracy as the first target in every province.
5. Bring madrassas into the mainstream by registration and teaching of contemporary subjects.
6. Technical and vocational training linked to industry and overseas labour markets; digital skills.
7. School councils and elected local governments to manage schools.
8. Climate-resilient school buildings.
9. Universities: funding tied to quality, industry linkages, academic freedom.

- **Evidence:** literacy, spending, learning poverty.
- **Causes:** headings above.
- **Diagram:** an "education pipeline" funnel: 100 children enter class 1 → those who reach class 5 → class 10 → university, with drop-out causes at each stage **[fill with latest transition rates]**.
