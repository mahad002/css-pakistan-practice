# The 26th and 27th Amendments, and the Proposed 28th

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Status check:** 26th passed (October 2024). 27th passed (13 November 2025). **28th not tabled or passed** as of late October 2026.
> **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – political. **Past papers:** not yet asked. High probability for 2028.

---

## 1. Background: how the Constitution is amended

- Article 239: a bill must pass each House separately by two-thirds of total membership (224 in the National Assembly, 64 in the Senate), then receive presidential assent. A change to provincial boundaries also needs two-thirds of that province's assembly.
- Landmark amendments: 8th (1985, presidential power to dissolve assemblies), 13th (1997, removed it), 17th (2003, restored it), **18th (2010, parliamentary system restored, provincial autonomy)**, 21st (2015, military courts), 25th (2018, FATA merged with KP).

## 2. The 26th Amendment (passed 20–21 October 2024)

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| Chief Justice of Pakistan | No longer the senior-most judge automatically. A Special Parliamentary Committee picks one of the three senior-most judges. Fixed three-year term. |
| Constitutional benches | Created inside the Supreme Court and high courts to hear constitutional cases. Judges are nominated by the Judicial Commission. |
| Judicial Commission of Pakistan | Reconstituted to include members of parliament; it also evaluates high court judges' performance. |
| Suo motu power | Article 184(3) limited: the Court cannot pass orders beyond the petition before it. |
| Other | Article 9A added (right to a clean and healthy environment). Riba to be eliminated by 1 January 2028. |

First effect: Justice Yahya Afridi, third in seniority, became Chief Justice on 26 October 2024.

(These points are from standard reporting at the time. I did not re-search them in November 2026. **[verify]** against the text on na.gov.pk.)

## 3. The 27th Amendment (signed 13 November 2025)

**Passage.** Cabinet approval and introduction on 8 November 2025; National Assembly on 12 November; Senate on 13 November by 64 votes to none after the opposition walked out; presidential assent the same day ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2622483); [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2576886/27th-amend-rushed-through-senate-amid-opp-boycott); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan)).

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| **Federal Constitutional Court (FCC)** | A new court takes over constitutional interpretation, fundamental-rights cases and federation–province disputes from the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court becomes the final court for civil and criminal appeals. First FCC Chief Justice: Justice Aminuddin Khan **[verify]**. |
| **Judges' transfer** | High court judges can be transferred on the Judicial Commission's recommendation; refusal has consequences **[verify wording]**. |
| **Article 243** | The Army chief becomes Chief of Defence Forces. The post of Chairman Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee ended on 27 November 2025. Five-star officers keep rank, privileges and uniform for life. |
| **Immunity** | Lifetime immunity from criminal proceedings for the President and for five-star officers, as reported by international media. |
| **Provinces** | Cabinet-size limit for smaller provinces raised from 11% to 13% of assembly strength. Proposals to revisit the NFC share and to move education and population planning to the centre were dropped or deferred after PPP objections. |

**Reactions**

| Actor | Position |
|---|---|
| Government (PML-N, PPP, allies) | A constitutional court was promised in the 2006 Charter of Democracy; it will cut the Supreme Court's backlog; a unified military command modernises defence; parliament is sovereign. |
| PTI and the TTAP alliance | "A strike at the foundation of the constitutional structure". Announced protests. |
| Judges | Supreme Court Justices Mansoor Ali Shah and Athar Minallah resigned on 13 November 2025. A Lahore High Court judge also resigned. |
| International Commission of Jurists | Called it a "full-frontal assault on the rule of law". |
| JUI-F | Objected that clauses dropped from the 26th were brought back. |
| Business groups | Warned that abrupt change without consultation unsettles investors. |

## 4. The proposed 28th Amendment (not passed)

Reported contents, all unconfirmed drafts ([Dawn situationer](https://www.dawn.com/news/2001028); [The Nation, 19 May 2026](https://www.nation.com.pk/19-May-2026/what-s-controversy-surrounding-28th-amendment); [Friday Times, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/22-Oct-2026/28th-amendment-two-thirds-majority-without-ppp)):

| Proposal | Issue |
|---|---|
| **NFC** | Make the provinces' guaranteed share (not less than 57.5% of the divisible pool, protected by Article 160(3A)) flexible, or have provinces share costs such as defence, debt service, BISP and higher education. PPP opposes. |
| **Local government** | Strengthen Article 140A: fixed tenure, mandatory elections, direct transfers. MQM-P's key demand. |
| **New provinces** | An easier route to create provinces. The Sindh Assembly passed a resolution against dividing the province in September 2026. |
| **Federal role** | Education curriculum and population planning returned to the federation. |
| **Election Commission** | Changes to appointment. |

**Arithmetic.** Passing it without the PPP is difficult. The Law Minister said in May 2026 that no draft existed.

## 5. Assessment (analysis)

**Arguments for the changes**
1. Specialised constitutional courts exist in Germany, South Africa, Italy and Türkiye.
2. Pakistan's Supreme Court had over 50,000 pending cases **[verify]**, and suo motu powers were used erratically (for example, Steel Mills, Reko Diq).
3. Parliamentary involvement in judicial appointments is normal in many democracies.

**Arguments against**
1. **Process.** Both amendments passed in days, with late-night sittings and little debate. The 18th Amendment took a cross-party committee nine months.
2. **Independence.** The executive and legislature now influence who heads the courts, which bench hears constitutional cases and where judges serve.
3. **Timing.** The changes came while cases on the 2024 election and military trials were pending.
4. **Immunity for life** breaks the principle of equality before law (Article 25).
5. **Federal balance.** Each new round reopens the 18th Amendment settlement.

## 6. Way forward

1. Let the FCC and Supreme Court settle jurisdiction lines quickly through rules and practice directions.
2. Protect security of tenure: no transfer without consent or a reasoned, reviewable order.
3. Make Judicial Commission proceedings transparent, with published criteria for appointments.
4. Any 28th Amendment should go through a special all-party committee, the Council of Common Interests and provincial assemblies' views.
5. Solve the centre's fiscal problem first through provincial taxes (agricultural income, property, services) and expenditure-sharing agreements before touching Article 160(3A).
6. Give local governments constitutional protection as a stand-alone, consensus item.

## 8. Model answer outline (Question 1)

- **Intro:** two amendments in 13 months changed how judges are chosen, who hears constitutional cases and how the forces are commanded. Thesis: the goals (specialisation, efficiency) are defensible; the method and some clauses weaken checks and balances.
- **Background:** judicial activism 2007–2023; Charter of Democracy; post-2024 disputes.
- **Body:** contents of each amendment (use a two-column table); rationale; criticisms (process, independence, immunity, federal effects); comparison with Germany or South Africa, where constitutional courts were created with broad consensus and strong tenure guarantees.
