# CPEC and Its Second Phase

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic – economic; external – China. **Past papers:** 2016, 2017, 2018, 2023 (CPEC); 2017 and 2019 (BRI). Read with `05-Pakistan-External/03`.

---

## 1. Background

- **What it is.** The China–Pakistan Economic Corridor is the flagship of China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI, 2013). It links Kashgar in Xinjiang to Gwadar on the Arabian Sea through roads, rail, pipelines, power plants and industrial zones.
- **Launch.** Agreed in 2013; formally launched during President Xi's visit in April 2015 with a $46bn package, later described as $62bn. Actual investment to date is roughly $25–30bn **[verify]**.
- **Governance.** The Joint Cooperation Committee (JCC), co-chaired by Pakistan's planning minister and China's NDRC, with joint working groups.
- **Phase I (2015–about 2022): "early harvest".**
  - *Energy:* about 13–17 projects and roughly 8,000 MW (Sahiwal, Port Qasim and Hub coal; Thar coal; Karot and Suki Kinari hydro; Matiari–Lahore HVDC line).
  - *Transport:* Multan–Sukkur motorway, Havelian–Thakot (KKH phase II), Lahore Orange Line, Eastern Bay Expressway.
  - *Gwadar:* port operated by a Chinese company; New Gwadar International Airport began operations in January 2025 **[verify]**.
- **Why China wants it:** a shorter route to the Gulf that avoids the Strait of Malacca; development of Xinjiang; a stable partner on India's flank.
- **Why Pakistan wants it:** infrastructure, power, industrial relocation, and a role as a regional trade hub.

## 4. Assessment (analysis)

**Gains:** load-shedding ended; motorways cut travel time; Thar coal opened; thousands of jobs; strategic assurance from China.

**Shortfalls:**
1. Industrial phase is late: SEZs have few tenants.
2. Energy projects raised capacity payments and circular debt (file `08`).
3. Exports to China did not surge; the trade deficit widened.
4. Gwadar handles little cargo; local people complain about water, power, fishing rights and checkpoints (the *Haq Do Tehreek*).
5. Transparency: contract terms were not published, which fed the "debt trap" charge. Most scholars find the charge overstated for Pakistan, whose debt problem is broader than China.
6. Security costs and a special protection division.
7. Provincial complaints (the western route).
8. US–China rivalry: Washington is wary; Pakistan's 2025–26 opening to the US on minerals and ports must be balanced against Chinese sensitivities.

## 5. Way forward

1. Make SEZs work: one-window services, utilities at the gate, joint ventures and technology transfer.
2. Close ML-1 financing with a mix of Chinese and multilateral lenders; phase it.
3. Export focus: use the China–Pakistan FTA phase II; target agriculture, seafood, textiles, IT services.
4. Publish project terms; involve parliament's CPEC committee.
5. Share benefits locally: Gwadar water, power, vocational training, fishermen's access; provincial quotas in jobs.
6. Security through policing and intelligence plus political engagement in Balochistan.
7. Re-profile energy debt by negotiation.
8. Keep CPEC open to third parties (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Central Asia) and insulate it from great-power rivalry.

- **Intro:** scale and promise; thesis: CPEC delivered infrastructure and power but not yet industrial or export transformation; Phase II is the test.
- **Background:** BRI; 2015 launch; phases.
- **Achievements,** then **shortfalls** (headings above), with three figures.
