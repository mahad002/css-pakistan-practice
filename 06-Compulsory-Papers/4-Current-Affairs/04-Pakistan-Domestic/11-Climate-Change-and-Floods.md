## 1. Background

- **Low emitter, high victim.** Pakistan produces under 1% of global greenhouse gases and is among the countries most damaged by climate-related disasters.
- **Why it is exposed:** more than 7,000 glaciers in the north (the largest ice mass outside the poles); monsoon dependence; a long river plain; arid zones; a 1,000 km coast; poverty and weak planning.
- **Record:**
  - 2010 "super floods": about 20 million affected.
  - 2022: record monsoon; one-third of the country under water at the peak; about 33 million affected, over 1,700 deaths, damage and losses above $30bn and reconstruction needs of $16.3bn (Post-Disaster Needs Assessment). Pledges of about $10–11bn at Geneva (January 2023) were mostly loans and arrived slowly.
  - Heatwaves above 50°C (Jacobabad); glacial lake outburst floods; drought in Thar and Balochistan; winter smog in Punjab.
- **Policy framework:** National Climate Change Policy (2012, updated 2021); National Adaptation Plan (2023); updated NDC; the 26th Amendment added Article 9A (right to a clean and healthy environment).

## 4. Challenges

1. **Reactive disaster management:** NDMA and provincial bodies respond after the event; district capacity is weak; no elected local governments in much of the country.
2. **Building on riverbeds and floodplains;** poor enforcement of zoning; encroached drains.
3. **Deforestation and land degradation** in the north increase flash floods.
4. **Finance gap:** adaptation needs far exceed funds; most support is loans.
5. **Weak early warning** in the last mile; India's withheld data.
6. **Agriculture exposure:** cotton, wheat and rice losses; food inflation.
7. **Health:** heat stress, dengue and malaria after floods, smog.
8. **Institutional fragmentation** after devolution: climate is federal in talks, provincial in action.
9. **Environmental degradation** more broadly: air and water pollution, solid waste, loss of mangroves and wetlands (asked in 2025).

## 5. Way forward

1. **Resilient reconstruction:** rebuild homes, roads and schools to flood-proof standards (the Sindh housing programme after 2022 is a model).
2. **Floodplain management:** legal ban on riverbed construction; restore wetlands; "living Indus" nature-based solutions; recharge aquifers.
3. **Early warning for all:** radar, telemetry and mobile alerts down to village level.
4. **Local governments** with disaster budgets (file `06`).
5. **Climate-smart agriculture:** heat- and flood-tolerant seed, crop insurance, water-saving irrigation.
6. **Finance:** bankable projects for the Green Climate Fund and the loss and damage fund; debt-for-climate swaps; green sukuk; carbon markets; a national disaster risk financing strategy.
7. **Mitigation with co-benefits:** solar, wind, electric transport, clean brick kilns, an end to crop burning.
8. **Climate diplomacy:** lead the vulnerable-country coalition (Pakistan chaired the G77 in 2022 when the fund was agreed); press for grants, not loans.
9. **Regional cooperation:** flood data-sharing with India and Afghanistan; a Hindu Kush–Himalaya glacier monitoring network; joint action on smog.
