# Local Government

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – political. **Past papers:** not asked directly; needed for governance (2019, 2022), new provinces (2021, 2024) and the 2026 resilience question.

## 1. Background

- **Constitution.** Article 32 (principle of policy: encourage local government). Article 140A (added 2010): each province shall establish local government and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility to elected representatives. Elections are run by the Election Commission.
- **History: a paradox.** Local bodies were strongest under military rulers, who used them to bypass national parties: Ayub Khan's Basic Democracies (1959), Zia's Local Government Ordinances (1979), Musharraf's Devolution Plan (2001) with elected nazims and district governments. Elected provincial governments have usually weakened or delayed them because MPAs want control of development funds.
- **Supreme Court.** In 2022 (MQM v. Pakistan) the Court held that Article 140A requires real devolution and that provinces cannot take back municipal functions **[verify citation]**.

## 4. Why local government matters

1. **Service delivery.** Water, sanitation, waste, primary schools and clinics work best when run close to users.
2. **Disaster response.** Floods in 2022 and 2025 showed the cost of having no elected local tier to warn, evacuate and rebuild.
3. **Democracy.** It is a training ground for politicians and gives women, minorities, peasants and workers reserved seats.
4. **Alternative to new provinces.** Strong districts answer much of the "administrative distance" argument (file `22`).
5. **Urban management.** Karachi and Lahore need metropolitan governments with control over land, transport and water.

## 5. Challenges

1. **No continuity.** Each provincial government rewrites the law and dissolves its predecessor's councils.
2. **Provincial capture.** Development authorities, water boards and waste companies stay under provincial control.
3. **No money.** Provincial Finance Commissions are irregular or absent; local taxes (property tax) are collected by provinces.
4. **Bureaucratic control.** Deputy commissioners hold the real authority.
5. **Elite and biradari capture**, and party interference in non-party polls.
6. **Election delays** through delimitation changes and litigation.

## 6. Way forward

1. Amend Article 140A to guarantee a fixed term, elections within 90–120 days of expiry, and a list of minimum local functions.
2. Mandatory Provincial Finance Commission awards; a fixed share (for example, 30%) of provincial revenue to local governments by formula.
3. Transfer municipal agencies and urban property tax to city governments.
4. Directly elected mayors for metropolitan cities.
5. Keep reserved seats (33% for women) and add youth.
6. Independent local government commissions and audit to control corruption.
7. Capacity: a trained local government service and e-governance.

## 8. Model answer outline (Question 1)

- **Intro:** Article 140A's promise against the record (Punjab without elected councils since 2019). Thesis: without a third tier, the 18th Amendment only moved centralisation from Islamabad to four capitals.
- **Background:** 1959, 1979, 2001, 2010.
- **Evidence:** table of provinces above.
- **Causes:** MPAs' interest in funds; bureaucratic resistance; weak constitutional wording; party fears of rivals' local bases.
- **Diagram:** three tiers with arrows for functions and funds, showing the blocked arrow from province to district.
- **Consequences:** services, disaster response, urban decay, pressure for new provinces.
- **Way forward:** seven points.
- **Conclusion:** local government is the cheapest governance reform available.
