# National Integration, Ethnicity and New Provinces

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – political. **Past papers:** 2021 (should new provinces be created?), 2024 (would more provinces help or harm?), 2025 (is ethnicity a threat to integration?). The new-provinces question is due again on its three-year cycle and is part of the 28th Amendment debate.

---

## 1. Background

- **Diversity.** Main language groups by the 2023 census (approximate shares): Punjabi 37%, Pashto 18%, Sindhi 14%, Saraiki 12%, Urdu 9%, Balochi 3%, plus Hindko, Brahui, Kashmiri, Shina, Balti and others **[verify exact shares]**.
- **History of ethnic politics.**
  - Bengali language movement (1948–52) and the denial of the 1970 mandate led to 1971.
  - Baloch insurgencies: 1948, 1958, 1962, 1973–77, and since 2004.
  - Pashtun nationalism: the Pakhtunistan issue; today the Pashtun Tahaffuz Movement (banned October 2024).
  - Sindhi nationalism (G. M. Syed) and Mohajir politics (MQM) in urban Sindh.
  - Saraiki and Hazara province movements.
- **Theories you can cite.** *Primordialism* (identity is ancient and fixed), *instrumentalism* (elites use identity to compete for resources), *constructivism* (identity is shaped by state policy). Most scholars of Pakistan (Tariq Rahman, Feroz Ahmed, Adeel Khan, Katharine Adeney) explain ethnic conflict by centralisation and unequal distribution, not by diversity itself.

## 3. Is ethnicity a threat to integration? (analysis)

**It becomes one when:**
1. Power and resources are concentrated: Punjab has over half the population and dominates the army and bureaucracy.
2. Mandates are not respected (1970; disputed provincial elections).
3. The state answers political demands with force (Balochistan).
4. Resource-rich regions stay poor (Sui gas; Reko Diq; Gwadar).
5. Languages and cultures are denied recognition.
6. External actors exploit grievances.

**It need not be one:** diversity is managed peacefully where federalism works (India's linguistic states, Switzerland, post-2010 Sindh and KP nationalism moved into parliamentary politics). The 18th Amendment and the 7th NFC Award reduced separatist sentiment in Sindh and KP.

## 4. New provinces: the arguments

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Punjab (about 128 million) is larger than most countries; smaller units bring government closer. | New provinces mean new assemblies, secretariats and high courts: cost without better service. |
| South Punjab, Hazara and others have real grievances about distance and neglect. | Ethnic or linguistic lines may harden identities and create new minorities. |
| More provinces would dilute one province's dominance in the federation and the Senate. | Dividing Sindh or KP against their assemblies' will would inflame conflict. |
| Comparable states have many units: India 28 states, Nigeria 36, Türkiye 81 provinces. | Local governments can deliver proximity at lower cost. |
| Both PML-N and PPP have at times promised a South Punjab province. | NFC shares, water, and Senate seats would all need renegotiation. |

**Procedure (Article 239(4)):** a bill altering a province's limits needs two-thirds of both Houses **and** two-thirds of that province's assembly.

## 5. Way forward

1. **Complete federalism:** regular CCI, a fair NFC, provincial ownership of resources honoured in practice (Article 172(3)), Senate with more financial powers.
2. **Local government first** as the quickest route to administrative proximity.
3. **If provinces are created, do it by consensus and on administrative criteria** (population, area, viability), through a national commission, starting where local consent exists (South Punjab).
4. **Balochistan:** political dialogue, an end to enforced disappearances, jobs and gas for locals, a real share in Reko Diq and Gwadar, credible elections.
5. **Merged districts and GB:** deliver promised funds and representation.
6. **Cultural recognition:** promote regional languages in schools and media.
7. **Equitable representation** in federal services (review and fill quotas) and in the armed forces.
8. **National cohesion through shared services:** motorways, universities, sports, a common civic curriculum that includes all regions' histories.

- **Intro:** four provinces for over 240 million people; thesis: new provinces are justified on administrative grounds and by consensus, but they are not a substitute for local government and fair resource sharing.
- **Background:** One Unit; 1970 restoration of provinces; Article 239(4); earlier attempts (2012 commission on South Punjab).
- **Arguments for; arguments against** (table).
