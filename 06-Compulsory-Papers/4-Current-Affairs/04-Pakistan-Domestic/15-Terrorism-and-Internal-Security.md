## 1. Background

- **Phases.** (1) After 2001, militants displaced from Afghanistan settled in the tribal areas. (2) The TTP was formed in December 2007 under Baitullah Mehsud; violence peaked in 2009–13. (3) After the Army Public School attack (16 December 2014), the National Action Plan (NAP, 20 points) and Operation Zarb-e-Azb (2014) and Radd-ul-Fasaad (2017) cut deaths by over 80% by 2019. (4) Violence has risen every year since the Afghan Taliban returned to Kabul in August 2021.
- **Main groups.**

| Group | Aim and area | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)** | Overthrow the state and impose its version of sharia; KP and former FATA | Led by Noor Wali Mehsud since 2018. Absorbed several factions in 2020. A UN estimate in 2024 put it at 6,000–6,500 fighters, based in Afghanistan. The state calls it *Fitna al-Khawarij*. |
| **Baloch separatists (BLA, BLF, BRAS)** | Independence for Balochistan; attack security forces, Punjabi settlers and Chinese interests | BLA's Majeed Brigade uses suicide attacks; the US listed the BLA as a foreign terrorist organisation in 2025 **[verify date]**. The state calls it *Fitna al-Hindustan*. |
| **Islamic State Khorasan (ISKP)** | Sectarian and mass-casualty attacks | Claimed the Islamabad Shia mosque bombing of 6 February 2026. |
| **Hafiz Gul Bahadur group, Lashkar-e-Islam, Jamaat-ul-Ahrar** | North Waziristan, Khyber | Jamaat-ul-Ahrar attacked the Rangers headquarters in Karachi on 27 June 2026. |

- **Failed talks.** A ceasefire with the TTP brokered by the Afghan Taliban in 2021–22 collapsed; thousands of fighters had returned to KP under it.

- Operation Azm-e-Istehkam (announced June 2024) and intelligence-based operations; a federal constabulary; drone use.

- **Balochistan:** bans and prosecutions of rights activists (Mahrang Baloch was sentenced to life in June 2026, as reported); internet shutdowns; a mines and minerals push.

## 3. Why terrorism returned (analysis)

1. **Sanctuary in Afghanistan** and US weapons left behind in 2021.
2. **Unfinished NAP:** madrassa reform, criminal-justice reform, choking terror finance, NACTA's coordinating role and countering extremism were never completed.
3. **The 2021–22 resettlement** of TTP fighters.
4. **Merged districts neglected:** promised funds (3% of the NFC pool, Rs100bn a year) and civil institutions did not arrive after the 2018 merger.
5. **Centre–KP discord** on strategy.
6. **Balochistan's political grievance:** enforced disappearances, disputed elections, resource control, poverty beside mega-projects. A security-only approach widens it.
7. **Policing gaps:** police in KP are out-gunned; civilian counter-terrorism departments are weak.
8. **External sponsorship:** Pakistan accuses India of funding Baloch groups and the TTP (India denies it).
9. **Economy and youth unemployment.**

## 4. Way forward

1. **A revised NAP agreed by all parties and provinces,** with NACTA as the hub and quarterly reports to parliament.
2. **Police first:** equip and expand counter-terrorism departments; hand cleared areas to civilian administration.
3. **Fix prosecution:** forensic labs, witness protection, special prosecutors, so that military courts are not needed.
4. **Afghan policy with two tracks:** verifiable action against the TTP through China-, Qatar- and Saudi-backed mechanisms; calibrated force; open trade routes so that ordinary Afghans are not alienated.
5. **Merged districts:** deliver the development package; local government; jobs; land settlement.
6. **Balochistan political track:** dialogue with moderate nationalists, end enforced disappearances through law, a real provincial share in Reko Diq and Gwadar, credible local elections.
7. **Counter violent extremism:** curriculum, regulated madrassas, a counter-narrative (the *Paigham-e-Pakistan* fatwa of 2018), monitoring of online radicalisation with safeguards for speech.
8. **Financial tracking** to keep FATF gains.
9. **Border management:** fencing is complete on most of the 2,640 km; add legal crossings, biometric entry, and trade.
