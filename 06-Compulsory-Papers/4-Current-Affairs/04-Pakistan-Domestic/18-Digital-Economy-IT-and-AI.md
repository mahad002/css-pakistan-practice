# Digital Economy, IT and AI Policy

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** Domestic affairs – economic. **Past papers:** 2021 (military use of AI). Likely in 2028 as AI governance or as part of an economy or youth question. Read with file `19` and `06-Global-Issues/10`.

---

## 1. Background

- **Assets.** A young population; about 190–200 million mobile subscriptions and about 140–150 million broadband users **[verify PTA figures]**; one of the world's largest freelance workforces; NADRA's digital identity system; the Raast instant payment system (State Bank, 2021).
- **Weaknesses.** Low fixed broadband and fibre; spectrum shortage; a large digital gender gap; frequent internet shutdowns and slowdowns; low digital literacy; little venture capital after the 2021–22 boom.

## 3. Opportunities

1. **Exports:** IT and IT-enabled services are the fastest-growing export and need little imported input.
2. **Jobs:** freelancing and remote work for youth and for women at home.
3. **Tax and documentation:** digital payments (Raast), e-invoicing and FBR data integration can widen the tax base.
4. **Service delivery:** e-governance, digital land records, telemedicine, BISP payments.
5. **AI in agriculture, health and disaster warning.**
6. **Regional role:** data centres using surplus electricity; Gulf investment through SIFC.

## 4. Challenges

1. **Reliability of the internet:** shutdowns, throttling and the X ban of 2024–25 damaged freelancers and investor confidence. Industry bodies estimated large losses **[verify figures]**.
2. **Infrastructure:** fibre reaches few towers; spectrum is expensive; power outages.
3. **Skills:** about 75,000 IT graduates a year **[verify]**, many not job-ready; almost no AI research capacity.
4. **Law:** no data-protection Act; vague cyber-crime provisions; unclear rules for AI liability.
5. **Finance and payments:** limits on foreign-currency accounts and payment gateways (no PayPal); weak venture funding.
6. **Digital divide:** women, rural areas, Balochistan and the merged districts.
7. **AI risks:** job losses in business-process outsourcing, deepfakes and disinformation, bias, surveillance, dependence on foreign models and chips.
8. **Crypto risks:** volatility, money laundering, energy use; the IMF has questioned subsidised power for mining **[verify]**.
9. **Policy on paper:** the 2018 Digital Pakistan Policy and the 2021 cloud-first policy were weakly implemented.

## 5. Way forward

1. A guarantee of connectivity: a legal bar on blanket shutdowns; narrow, court-supervised blocking.
2. Pass a data-protection law that meets international standards (it is a condition for EU and US clients).
3. Infrastructure: fibre roll-out with right-of-way reform, affordable spectrum, rural coverage through the Universal Service Fund.
4. Skills at scale: AI and coding in schools and TVET; industry-run boot camps; scholarships for women.
5. Finance: easier inflows for freelancers, payment gateways, tax stability for IT exporters.
6. AI governance: a risk-based framework, public-sector pilots, a national compute facility shared by universities, an ethics and safety body.
7. Regulate virtual assets cautiously under PVARA with FATF compliance.
8. Digital public infrastructure: build on NADRA and Raast for a consent-based data exchange (the "India Stack" model).
9. Cybersecurity: a funded national CERT and critical-infrastructure standards.

## 6. Likely exam questions

1. Can the digital economy rescue Pakistan from its balance-of-payments trap?
2. Evaluate Pakistan's National AI Policy 2025. What is missing?
3. Artificial intelligence: opportunity or threat for developing countries such as Pakistan?

## 7. Model answer outline (Question 2)

- **Intro:** the policy's promise (one million trained by 2030); thesis: sound aims, but success depends on connectivity, data law, compute and trust, which the policy does not secure.
- **Contents:** six pillars.
- **Strengths; gaps** (funding, governance body, data protection, compute, link to industry, rights safeguards).
- **Diagram:** an "AI stack" pyramid: power and connectivity → data and law → compute → skills → applications; mark which layers Pakistan has.
- **Comparison:** India's AI mission and compute subsidy; UAE and Saudi investments; the EU's risk-based law.
- **Way forward:** points 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8.
- **Conclusion:** AI policy is first an education, energy and rule-of-law policy.
