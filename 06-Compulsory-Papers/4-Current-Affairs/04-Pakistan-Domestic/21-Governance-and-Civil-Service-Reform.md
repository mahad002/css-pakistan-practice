## 1. Background

- **Good governance** (World Bank, UNDP): participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus, equity, effectiveness, accountability.
- **The civil service.** Inherited from the Indian Civil Service. The 1973 reforms (Bhutto) removed constitutional protection of tenure, unified pay scales and created occupational groups. Recruitment to 12 groups is through the CSS examination (FPSC). The Pakistan Administrative Service dominates top posts.
- **Reform reports:** more than 20 commissions since 1947, including the National Commission for Government Reforms under Dr Ishrat Husain (2008) and his 2018–21 task force. Most recommendations were not implemented.
- **Accountability bodies:** NAB (1999), FIA, provincial anti-corruption establishments, the Auditor General, Public Accounts Committees, ombudsmen. NAB law was narrowed in 2022 and the Supreme Court finally upheld those changes in 2024 **[verify]**.

## 3. Diagnosis: why governance is weak

1. **Politicisation:** postings and transfers used as reward and punishment; average tenure of a secretary or district officer is often under a year.
2. **Generalist structure:** officers move between unrelated fields; specialists are sidelined.
3. **Incentives:** pay is low and compressed for skilled staff, with perks in kind; promotion by seniority; annual reports that rate nearly everyone "very good".
4. **Fear of accountability:** NAB cases against officers produced "pen-down" caution.
5. **Outdated rules:** Rules of Business 1973; file-based procedures; weak use of data.
6. **Over-centralisation:** provinces hold what districts should do; secretariats hold what field offices should do.
7. **Corruption and elite capture:** discretion in land, taxation, contracts, policing.
8. **Weak parliament and local government,** so the bureaucracy answers upward, not to citizens.
9. **Parallel structures:** authorities, companies and task forces created to bypass departments.
10. **Military presence in civilian roles** reduces civilian ownership and learning.

## 4. Way forward

**Civil service**
1. Recruitment: keep merit and the federal character; test aptitude and domain knowledge; allow lateral entry of specialists at mid and senior levels.
2. Tenure protection (the Supreme Court's *Anita Turab* judgment, 2012) written into law; an independent Civil Service Board for senior postings.
3. Performance management: measurable objectives, 360-degree review, forced distribution of ratings.
4. Pay: monetise perks, widen the scale for technical cadres.
5. Training throughout a career, including digital and public-finance skills.

**Systems**
6. E-governance end to end: e-office, e-procurement (EPADS), open data, citizen dashboards.
7. Right to Information laws enforced; proactive disclosure.
8. Accountability that is independent and even-handed: one law, fixed tenure for heads, protection for good-faith decisions, whistle-blower and conflict-of-interest laws.
9. Devolve to elected local governments with their own staff.
10. Regulatory guillotine: remove redundant licences and approvals.

**Politics**
11. A cross-party agreement to keep administration out of partisan contest.
12. Strong parliamentary committees and Public Accounts Committees chaired by the opposition.
