## A. Brain drain and the SDGs

### Background
- **Brain drain:** emigration of skilled people (doctors, engineers, IT workers, academics) that reduces a country's human capital.
- **Scale.** Bureau of Emigration figures show roughly 700,000–860,000 Pakistanis registering for overseas work each year in 2022–24, mostly to the Gulf; a rising share is "highly qualified" or "highly skilled" **[verify 2025 and 2026]**. Over 10 million Pakistanis live abroad.
- **Push factors:** low pay, unemployment and under-employment of graduates, political instability, insecurity, inflation, weak research funding, lack of merit.
- **Pull factors:** wages, skilled-visa schemes (UK, Germany's opportunity card, Canada, Australia), Gulf demand, universities abroad.

### Effects
| Loss | Gain |
|---|---|
| Shortage of doctors, nurses and engineers at home; public money spent on training is lost | Remittances: a record $41.6bn in FY26 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2014313)) |
| Fewer innovators, teachers and taxpayers | Skills, networks and investment if migrants return or engage ("brain circulation") |
| Weaker institutions as capable people exit | Lower unemployment pressure |

### Link to the SDGs (2030)
- Directly hits SDG 3 (health workers), SDG 4 (teachers, researchers), SDG 8 (decent work), SDG 9 (innovation). Indirectly weakens SDG 16 (institutions).
- Pakistan adopted the SDGs as national goals in 2016. It ranks in the lower third of the SDG Index (about 137th–140th of 167) **[verify latest]**. Globally only about a sixth of SDG targets are on track (UN SDG Report) **[verify]**.

### Way forward
1. Jobs and merit at home: research grants, competitive pay for scarce skills, start-up finance.
2. Stable politics and security.
3. Turn drain into circulation: diaspora chairs at universities, tax-free return schemes, dual-posting for doctors, virtual mentoring.
4. Managed migration: skills certified to destination standards; bilateral agreements that include training investment by host countries.
5. Use remittances productively: diaspora bonds, Roshan Digital Accounts, matching grants.
6. Train more than the country needs in nursing and IT, as the Philippines and India do.

## B. Natural resources: development or conflict?

### Concepts
- **Resource curse / paradox of plenty** (Richard Auty; Sachs and Warner): resource-rich countries often grow more slowly because of corruption, conflict and neglect of other sectors.
- **Dutch disease:** resource exports raise the currency and hurt manufacturing.
- **Rentier state:** government lives on rents, not taxes, and is less accountable.
- **Greed versus grievance** (Paul Collier): resources fund rebellion and motivate it.

### Examples
| Curse | Blessing |
|---|---|
| DR Congo (cobalt, coltan), Nigeria's delta, Sudan and South Sudan (oil, gold), Venezuela, Libya, Iraq | Norway (sovereign wealth fund), Botswana (diamonds with strong institutions), Chile (copper stabilisation fund), UAE (diversification) |

Current resource contests: critical minerals and rare earths in US–China rivalry; Greenland; Ukraine's minerals deal with the US (2025); the Strait of Hormuz and oil in the 2026 war.

### Pakistan
- **Assets:** Reko Diq copper-gold (Barrick; first production targeted for about 2028 **[verify]**), Saindak, Thar coal (about 175 billion tonnes), gas at Sui, salt, gemstones, rare-earth prospects; a claimed mineral endowment of trillions of dollars that is unproven.
- **2025–26:** the Pakistan Minerals Investment Forum (April 2025); provincial mines and minerals bills that drew objections over provincial rights; a $500m memorandum with US Strategic Metals (September 2025). Analysts at CSIS note the gap between memoranda and bankable projects ([CSIS, April 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)).
- **Conflict link:** Balochistan's insurgency draws on the sense that gas and minerals leave the province while it stays poorest; attacks on mining sites (Chagai, April 2026) and on Chinese workers.

### Lessons and way forward
1. Institutions before extraction: transparent contracts (join and apply the EITI standard), parliamentary and provincial oversight.
2. Honour Article 172(3) and the provincial share; direct community benefit (jobs, royalties to districts, water, schools).
3. A resource fund to save windfalls and stabilise budgets.
4. Value addition at home (smelting, refining) and skills transfer.
5. Environmental and social safeguards.
6. Do not let great-power competition over minerals choose Pakistan's partners; diversify buyers.

## C. NGOs and shrinking civic space

- **"Force multiplier":** NGOs extend the state's reach in health, education, relief and rights (Edhi, Aga Khan network, The Citizens Foundation, flood relief in 2022 and 2025).
- **Why trust fell:** foreign funding and suspicion of foreign agendas; the 2011 fake vaccination campaign; poor transparency in some NGOs; counter-terror finance rules (FATF); governments' intolerance of rights advocacy; populist politics; competition from state programmes; aid cuts in 2025.
- **Pakistan:** INGO registration policy (2015), expulsions (2018), bank-account and EAD requirements, PECA and assembly restrictions (files `19`, `20`).
- **Way forward:** one clear charity law; risk-based (not blanket) financial scrutiny; NGO self-regulation and public audits; partnership contracts for service delivery; protection for peaceful advocacy.

## D. The "resilient state" question (2026, Q2)

A compound question on climate, population, governance, security and fiscal stress. Build the answer as a chain:

**Population growth (2.55%)** → pressure on water, food, jobs → **climate shocks** (2022, 2025) destroy assets → **fiscal stress** (interest above net federal revenue) leaves no money for adaptation or services → weak **governance** cannot plan or deliver → grievance and under-policed spaces feed **insecurity** → insecurity and instability deter investment → low growth returns the cycle to the start.

**Policy package:** (1) tax and debt reform to create fiscal space; (2) elected local governments for services and disaster response; (3) population stabilisation through girls' education and health services; (4) climate-resilient infrastructure and risk finance; (5) rule-of-law-based security with political settlements in KP and Balochistan; (6) a cross-party minimum agenda so that policy survives elections.
