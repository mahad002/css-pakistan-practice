# The Judiciary

## 1. Background

- **Structure before November 2025.** Supreme Court (apex), five high courts (Lahore, Sindh, Peshawar, Balochistan, Islamabad), Federal Shariat Court, district courts, and special courts and tribunals.
- **History in four phases.**
  1. *Validation* (1955–2000): courts upheld takeovers under the doctrine of necessity (Tamizuddin 1955, Dosso 1958, Nusrat Bhutto 1977, Zafar Ali Shah 2000).
  2. *Assertion* (2007–2013): the lawyers' movement restored Chief Justice Iftikhar Chaudhry; heavy use of suo motu powers under Article 184(3).
  3. *Activism and controversy* (2013–2023): two prime ministers removed by court orders (Gilani 2012, Nawaz Sharif 2017); commercial rulings such as Reko Diq (2013) led to large international awards against Pakistan.
  4. *Restructuring* (2024–2026): the 26th and 27th Amendments.
- **Performance problem.** More than two million cases are pending across all courts **[verify latest Law and Justice Commission figure]**. Pakistan ranked 129th of 142 on the World Justice Project Rule of Law Index 2024 **[verify 2026 rank]**.

## 4. Challenges

1. **Independence.** Executive and legislative weight in appointments, bench formation and transfers.
2. **Delay.** Too few judges (roughly 4,000 for over 240 million people **[verify]**), adjournment culture, weak case management.
3. **Access and cost.** Litigation is slow and expensive; the poor rely on informal jirgas and panchayats.
4. **Lower courts.** Where most citizens meet the law; under-resourced and exposed to local pressure.
5. **Criminal justice chain.** Poor investigation and prosecution give low conviction rates in terrorism cases, which is then used to justify military courts.
6. **Credibility.** Perceptions of selective accountability, by the old activist court and by the new arrangement.
7. **Jurisdiction confusion.** Supreme Court versus FCC versus constitutional benches of high courts.

## 5. Way forward

1. Clear, published rules dividing work between the Supreme Court and the FCC.
2. Transparent, merit-based appointments with recorded reasons.
3. Case-management reform: limits on adjournments, e-filing, video hearings, performance data published by court.
4. More judges and courts, with priority to district level; fill vacancies on time.
5. Alternative dispute resolution centres under the ADR Act 2017; commercial courts for investors.
6. Reform the whole criminal justice chain: forensic capacity, witness protection, an independent prosecution service.
7. End military trials of civilians by fixing anti-terrorism courts.
8. A working Supreme Judicial Council for judicial accountability.

## 7. Model answer outline (Question 2)

- **Intro:** scale of pendency; thesis: delay is a management and capacity failure more than a legal one.
- **Causes:** judge-to-population ratio; adjournments; weak investigation; frivolous litigation by government departments (the state is the largest litigant); outdated procedure codes (1898, 1908).
- **Effects:** economy (contract enforcement), security (low convictions), rights (under-trial prisoners).
- **Diagram:** funnel from "cases filed" to "decided" with the blockages labelled.
- **Reforms:** eight points above; cite the Model Criminal Trial Courts experiment and e-courts.
- **Conclusion:** structural redesign at the top will not help citizens unless the district courts work.
