## 2. How answers are marked (what examiners say)

FPSC publishes examiner reports. The comments on this paper repeat year after year:

| Examiner complaint | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Answers were "descriptive and general" (CE-2018 report) | Do not narrate history. Answer the exact question asked. |
| Candidates wrote long text instead of headings or points and could not build a clear argument (CE-2018) | Use headings that carry your argument. A marker should follow your case by reading the headings alone. |
| Candidates reproduced memorised facts (CE-2018) | Facts must support a point. Add "so what?" after each fact. |
| Historical analysis was missing (CE-2018) | Give a short, relevant background, then link it to today. |
| Candidates who drew maps and diagrams properly scored well (CE-2014 report, as quoted on public copies) | Draw one simple map or diagram per answer where it fits. |
| Reliance on outdated guidebooks (Express Tribune commentary, 2026) | Use this year's data. Date your facts. |

Sources: [Examiner Reports CE-2018 (public copy)](https://cssplatformbytha.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Examiner-Reports-CE2018.pdf); [CE-2014 report (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/383442270/Examiners-Report-2014-pdf); [Tribune, "Why CSS candidates fail the exam"](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2584842/why-css-candidates-fail-the-exam). I could not open the official FPSC PDF copies directly (the FPSC site returned a certificate error), so treat the wording as paraphrase and check the originals on fpsc.gov.pk.

**My reading of the marking (analysis, not an FPSC statement):** A 20-mark answer that is accurate but descriptive usually earns 8–10. Answers that reach 12–14 do four things: they answer every part of the question, they argue a position, they use current data with sources, and they end with workable recommendations. Very few scripts cross 14.

## 3. The answer template that scores

Use this for every 20-mark question.

| Part | Length | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 80–120 words | Define the issue in one line. State the current position (one dated fact). Give your thesis: your direct answer to the question. |
| **Background** | 80–120 words | Only the history needed to understand today. Three to five dated points. |
| **Main body: analysis under headings** | 600–800 words | Four to seven headings. Each heading is a claim, not a label. Under each: point, evidence (fact, figure, date, source), then the implication. If the question has two parts, give each part its own section. |
| **Map, diagram, table or flowchart** | Half a page | One visual. Label it, number it ("Fig. 1") and refer to it in your text. |
| **Critical analysis / counter-view** | 100–150 words | Give the other side. Say why your position still holds. |
| **Way forward** | 120–180 words | Five to seven specific, numbered recommendations: who should do what. |
| **Conclusion** | 50–80 words | Return to the question. Restate your answer. No new facts. |

**Total length:** about 1,000–1,400 words, which is 7–9 handwritten pages for most people. Short notes (10 marks): 450–600 words, 3–4 pages, with the same mini-structure.

**Heading style.** Write "Capacity payments, not fuel, now drive circular debt" rather than "Causes". A heading that states the claim shows analysis before the marker reads the paragraph.

**Data style.** Give the figure, the year and the source in brackets: "Remittances reached a record $41.6bn in FY26 (SBP)". Three to six well-chosen figures per answer are enough.

**Balance.** On political topics (civil–military relations, the 26th and 27th Amendments, the PTI–government conflict) present the main positions fairly, then give a reasoned view based on the Constitution and institutions. Do not attack or praise a party or person. The marker may hold any political view.

## 4. Time plan for the 3 hours

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | MCQs. Fill the OMR sheet carefully. Do not leave blanks unless negative marking is announced on the paper. |
| 0:25–0:35 | Read all seven questions. Choose four. Jot a 6-line outline for each on the last page of the answer book. |
| 0:35–1:10 | Answer 1 (your strongest) |
| 1:10–1:45 | Answer 2 |
| 1:45–2:20 | Answer 3 |
| 2:20–2:55 | Answer 4 |
| 2:55–3:00 | Underline headings, check question numbers, label diagrams |

That is **35 minutes per answer**. The most common way to lose marks is to spend 50 minutes on the first answer and leave the fourth half-written. Four average answers beat two excellent ones plus two fragments.

**Choosing questions.** Prefer questions where you can (a) give current data, (b) draw a map or diagram and (c) write a concrete way forward. Avoid a question if you cannot answer one of its parts.

## 5. Common mistakes

1. **Answering the topic, not the question.** If the question says "critically evaluate", you must judge. If it says "with reference to Pakistan", half the answer must be about Pakistan.
2. **Missing a part.** Many questions have two or three demands. Underline each one and give each a heading.
3. **Old facts.** Quoting 2019 figures in 2028 signals a guidebook answer.
4. **No dates or sources.** "Recently" is weak. "On 31 August 2026 the Court of Arbitration ruled…" is strong.
5. **One-sided emotion.** Avoid slogans on India, the US, Israel or domestic politics. State the facts, then the analysis.
6. **No way forward.** Every Pakistan-related answer needs recommendations.
7. **Untidy script.** Leave a line between sections. Use a blue or black pen and one marker for headings. Start each answer on a new page.
8. **Over-long introductions.** Reach your first analytical heading by the end of the first page.
9. **Invented quotations and statistics.** Markers notice. If you are unsure, give a range or drop the figure.
10. **Ignoring MCQs.** The 20 MCQ marks decide many pass/fail outcomes. Practise them weekly (`11-MCQ-Bank.md`).

## 6. How to study from this folder (15 hours a week, about 3 of them for this paper)

| Phase | When | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundations | Feb–Mar 2027 | Read `03-Foundations/` twice. Learn the glossary. Start the daily routine in `10-Keeping-It-Current.md`. |
| 2. Pakistan | Apr–Jul 2027 | One backgrounder from `04-` or `05-` every three days. For each: read, update with new events, write the outline from memory. |
| 3. Global | Aug–Oct 2027 | Same method for `06-Global-Issues/`. Pause for the MPT screening test as needed. |
| 4. Writing | Nov 2027–Jan 2028 | One full timed answer every other day (35 minutes, by hand). Compare with `07-Model-Answers.md`. Practise the 15 maps. |
| 5. Final month | Jan–Feb 2028 | Re-read all updated backgrounders, the facts sheet and your own update log. Do 50 MCQs a day. |

**Use the backgrounders actively.** Each one ends with likely questions and an outline. Close the file and rebuild the outline on paper. That is the skill being tested.

**Keep the pack alive.** Much of this folder will be 15 months old by exam day. The Iran war, the Afghanistan conflict, the 28th Amendment, the IMF programme and Gaza are all moving. `10-Keeping-It-Current.md` tells you how to update in 20 minutes a day.
