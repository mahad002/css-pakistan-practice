## 2. United Nations

**Structure (six principal organs):** General Assembly (193, one vote each), Security Council (15), Economic and Social Council (54), Trusteeship Council (inactive), International Court of Justice (The Hague, 15 judges), Secretariat (Secretary-General).

**Security Council:** Five permanent members with a veto (US, UK, France, Russia, China) and ten elected for two years. A decision needs nine votes and no veto. Only the Council can pass binding resolutions under Chapter VII.

**Secretary-General:** António Guterres (Portugal), second term ends 31 December 2026. The Council recommends and the Assembly appoints. As of 20 November 2026 no successor had been recommended. Rebeca Grynspan (Costa Rica) led the straw polls ([Wikipedia summary of polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection); [Al Jazeera, 15 Nov 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/15/secret-votes-and-vetoes-how-will-the-next-un-chief-be-picked)).

**Pakistan's role**
- Elected to the Council eight times: 1952–53, 1968–69, 1976–77, 1983–84, 1993–94, 2003–04, 2012–13, **2025–26**. The current term ends 31 December 2026.
- Held the presidency in July 2025. The Council adopted Resolution 2788 on peaceful settlement of disputes, sponsored by Pakistan ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2557277/un-adopts-pakistan-sponsored-resolution-on-peaceful-dispute-settlement); [MOFA](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/pakistan-assumes-presidency-of-the-united-nations-security-council-for-the-month-of-july-2025)).
- Voted for Resolution 2803 (17 November 2025) endorsing the US plan for Gaza (13 for, Russia and China abstaining) ([UN press](https://press.un.org/en/2025/sc16225.doc.htm)).
- One of the largest contributors to UN peacekeeping since 1960, with more than 230,000 personnel over time **[verify latest count]**.
- On reform, Pakistan belongs to the **Uniting for Consensus** group (with Italy and others). It opposes new permanent seats, which the G4 (India, Japan, Germany, Brazil) want.
- UNMOGIP has monitored the Kashmir ceasefire line since 1949. India says its mandate lapsed after 1972.

## 3. IMF and World Bank

**IMF: how it works.** Members pay quotas, which set voting power (US about 16.5%, an effective veto on major decisions needing 85%). The IMF lends to countries with balance-of-payments problems, on conditions. Main windows: Stand-By Arrangement (short), Extended Fund Facility (EFF, structural, 3–4 years), Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF, climate). Its unit of account is the SDR. Managing Director: Kristalina Georgieva (second term from October 2024).

**Pakistan and the IMF.** Over 20 programmes since 1958 (commonly counted as 24 or 25 **[verify]**). Current: a 37-month EFF of about $7bn approved in September 2024, plus an RSF of about $1.4bn approved in 2025. The fourth EFF review reached staff-level agreement around 8 October 2026 for about $1.21bn; Board approval was pending at the time of writing ([ProPakistani, 8 Oct 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/08/imf-reaches-staff-level-agreement-with-pakistan-on-4th-eff-review-unlocking-1-2-billion-loan/)). Details: `04-Pakistan-Domestic/07`.

**World Bank.** Lends for development. Five arms: IBRD, IDA (concessional), IFC (private sector), MIGA, ICSID. President: Ajay Banga. It brokered the Indus Waters Treaty and appoints the Neutral Expert and the chair of the Court of Arbitration. A 10-year Country Partnership Framework for Pakistan (2026–35, about $20bn) was announced in January 2025 **[verify figure]**.

## 4. WTO

**How it works.** Sets trade rules by consensus. Core principles: most-favoured-nation treatment and national treatment. Disputes go to panels and then the Appellate Body, which has been unable to hear appeals since December 2019 because the US blocks appointments. Director-General: Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Nigeria).

**Syllabus items.** The **Doha Development Round** began in 2001 to help developing countries (agriculture, subsidies). It stalled over farm subsidies. The **Bali Package** (9th Ministerial, 2013) produced the Trade Facilitation Agreement, the first multilateral deal in WTO history, in force since 2017.

**Today.** The system is under strain from unilateral tariffs. The US Supreme Court struck down the emergency-powers tariffs on 20 February 2026 and the administration replaced them with a 10% surcharge under Section 122 of the Trade Act ([Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/from-ieepa-to-section-122)). See `06-Global-Issues/07`.

**Pakistan.** Founding member. India withdrew Pakistan's MFN status in 2019. Pakistan benefits from the EU's GSP+ scheme (since 2014), which depends on 27 conventions on rights and governance.

## 5. FATF

**How it works.** Sets 40 Recommendations against money laundering and terrorist financing. Countries are assessed by peers. Weak ones go on the "grey list" (increased monitoring) or "black list" (call for action: Iran, North Korea, Myanmar).

**Pakistan.** Grey-listed 2008–10, 2012–15 and June 2018–October 2022. The last episode required two action plans with 34 items. Exit improved access to finance. Asked in the 2020 paper. Status after 2022: not listed **[verify 2026 status]**.

## 6. Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

**Origin and members.** Grew out of the "Shanghai Five" (1996). Members: China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, India, Pakistan (both 2017), Iran (2023), Belarus (2024). Focus: the "three evils" of terrorism, separatism and extremism; later trade, energy and connectivity. Bodies: Council of Heads of State (CHS), Council of Heads of Government (CHG), Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS).

**Recent.** Islamabad hosted the CHG in October 2024. The 2025 summit was in Tianjin. The 26th CHS met in Bishkek on 31 August–1 September 2026, adopted the Bishkek Declaration on the 25th anniversary and criticised the US–Israel attacks on Iran. **Pakistan took the chair and will host the 2027 summit** ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2626913/1); [Al Jazeera, 1 Sep 2026](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/1/sco-slams-attacks-sanctions-on-iran-key-takeaways-from-bishkek-summit)).

**Assessment (analysis).** Useful to Pakistan for access to Central Asia, a platform shared with China and Russia, and a room where India must sit with it. Limits: decisions by consensus, India–Pakistan and India–China friction, little economic integration.

## 7. Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC)

**Origin.** Founded after the arson attack on Al-Aqsa Mosque (21 August 1969). Bodies: Islamic Summit, Council of Foreign Ministers (CFM), General Secretariat. Affiliated: Islamic Development Bank (Jeddah), ISESCO/ICESCO (Rabat), COMSTECH (Islamabad).

**Pakistan.** Hosted the 2nd Summit (Lahore, 1974), an extraordinary summit (Islamabad, 1997) and CFM sessions (latest: 48th, Islamabad, March 2022). Leads the OIC Contact Group on Jammu and Kashmir. Sponsored the UN General Assembly resolution that made 15 March the International Day to Combat Islamophobia (2022).

**Assessment (analysis).** Strong on declarations (Palestine, Kashmir, Islamophobia), weak on action. Causes: Saudi–Iran rivalry, differing ties with Israel and the West, no enforcement tools. The 2026 Iran war, in which Iran struck Gulf states, deepened the split.

## 8. SAARC

**Members.** Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka. Charter rule: decisions by unanimity; bilateral disputes excluded. SAFTA came into force in 2006.

**Status.** Dormant. The last summit was the 18th (Kathmandu, 2014). The 19th, due in Islamabad in November 2016, was postponed after India withdrew. Intra-regional trade is about 5% of total trade, against roughly 25% in ASEAN. India prefers BIMSTEC. After Bangladesh's change of government (2024) and the BNP's election win (February 2026), Dhaka and Islamabad have spoken of reviving SAARC.

## 9. Economic Cooperation Organization (ECO)

**Members.** Founders Iran, Pakistan, Türkiye; joined in 1992 by Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan. Charter: Treaty of Izmir (1977, revised). ECO Trade and Development Bank in Istanbul.

**Pakistan.** Hosted the 13th Summit (Islamabad, 2017). The 17th Summit was held in Khankendi, Azerbaijan (July 2025) **[verify]**. Projects: Islamabad–Tehran–Istanbul rail and road corridor; ECO Trade Agreement (ECOTA), not fully implemented. Asked in the 2024 paper.

**Assessment.** Large potential (about half a billion people, energy and transit), low delivery: intra-ECO trade is under 10% of members' trade; sanctions on Iran, instability in Afghanistan and similar export baskets hold it back.

## 10. BRICS

**Members.** Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa; from 2024 Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE; Indonesia from January 2025; Saudi Arabia is usually counted as the eleventh. "Partner countries" form an outer circle. Institutions: New Development Bank (Shanghai, 2014) and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement.

**Recent.** 17th summit Rio de Janeiro (July 2025). 18th summit New Delhi, 12–13 September 2026: the New Delhi Declaration opposed unilateral sanctions, backed local-currency settlement and UN reform, and avoided blaming either side in the Iran war ([Al Jazeera, 13 Sep 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/13/brics-summit-2026-what-are-the-key-takeaways)).

**Pakistan.** Applied in 2023. Membership needs consensus; India's position is the obstacle. Pakistan has sought to join the New Development Bank **[verify status]**.

## 11. G20 and G7

**G20.** 19 countries plus the EU and the African Union (admitted 2023). About 85% of world GDP. Rotating presidency: Brazil 2024, South Africa 2025 (Johannesburg; the US stayed away), **United States 2026 (leaders due in Miami in December 2026)** ([China Briefing on APEC and G20 timing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/apec-2026-in-shenzhen-what-the-leaders-meeting-and-trump-xi-talks-mean-for-business/)). Pakistan is not a member. It is affected through debt-relief frameworks and climate finance.

**G7.** US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Canada. 2026 presidency: France (Evian) **[verify]**.

## 12. GCC

**Members.** Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman. Over four million Pakistanis work in GCC states and send more than half of all remittances **[verify share]**. A Pakistan–GCC free trade agreement was initialled in September 2023 **[verify whether signed and ratified]**. The 2026 Iran war hit Gulf states directly and tested the Council's unity.
