# Pakistan's Foreign Policy Since 1947 — Phases and Timeline

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. Events before 2025 are settled history. Events of 2025–26 come from news sources checked in November 2026 (see the external-affairs files for links).
> **Why you need this:** Examiners complain that "historical analysis is missing". Two or three dated points from this timeline fix that in any external-affairs answer.

---

## 1. The constants

| Constant | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Security against India** | The central driver since 1947: Kashmir, four wars or major conflicts (1947–48, 1965, 1971, 1999) and the May 2025 conflict. |
| **Search for external balancers** | The US (1950s, 1980s, 2000s), China (since the 1960s), the Gulf (since the 1970s). |
| **Geography** | A junction of South Asia, Central Asia, China and the Gulf. An asset (transit, CPEC) and a burden (Afghan wars). |
| **Economic dependence** | Aid, loans and remittances have shaped choices: 20-plus IMF programmes; reliance on Saudi, UAE and Chinese deposits. |
| **Islamic identity** | Support for Palestine and Kashmir; the OIC; no recognition of Israel. |
| **Nuclear deterrence** | Since 1998, the base of security policy. |

**Guiding text:** Jinnah's February 1948 broadcast: friendship with all, aggressive designs against none. Article 40 of the Constitution calls for bonds with Muslim countries and support for international peace.

## 2. Phases

### Phase 1 (1947–1953): Survival and searching
- 1947–48: First Kashmir war. UN resolutions of 1948–49 call for a plebiscite. Ceasefire line from 1 January 1949.
- 1947: Joined the UN (30 September). Afghanistan cast the only vote against.
- 1950: Liaquat Ali Khan visited the US, not the USSR. Pakistan recognised the People's Republic of China (1950); relations began in May 1951.

### Phase 2 (1954–1962): Alignment with the West
- 1954: Mutual Defence Assistance Agreement with the US; joined SEATO.
- 1955: Joined the Baghdad Pact (CENTO from 1959).
- 1959: Bilateral defence agreement with the US.
- 1960: Indus Waters Treaty (19 September, Karachi; World Bank as broker). U-2 spy plane flown from Peshawar shot down over the USSR.
- **Lesson:** Alliances brought arms and aid but not support on Kashmir.

### Phase 3 (1962–1971): Bilateralism and the China opening
- 1962: After the Sino-Indian war the US armed India; Pakistan felt let down.
- 1963: Pakistan–China boundary agreement (2 March).
- 1965: Second war with India; US arms embargo on both. 1966: Tashkent Declaration (Soviet mediation).
- 1971: Pakistan helped arrange Kissinger's secret trip to Beijing (July). Civil war, Indian intervention, loss of East Pakistan (16 December).
- **Lesson:** Do not depend on one patron.

### Phase 4 (1972–1979): Bhutto's independent line
- 1972: Simla Agreement (2 July): disputes to be settled bilaterally; ceasefire line becomes the Line of Control. Left the Commonwealth (rejoined 1989) and SEATO.
- 1974: Lahore Islamic Summit (OIC); recognition of Bangladesh. India's first nuclear test (May) spurred Pakistan's programme.
- 1976–79: US pressure and sanctions over the nuclear programme (Symington and Glenn amendments). 1979: Left CENTO; joined the Non-Aligned Movement.

### Phase 5 (1979–1989): Frontline state against the USSR
- December 1979: Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. Pakistan became the channel for US and Saudi support to the mujahideen. Over three million Afghan refugees arrived.
- 1981: $3.2bn US aid package. 1985: SAARC founded; ECO re-launched.
- 1988: Geneva Accords; Soviet withdrawal completed February 1989.
- **Costs:** weapons, drugs, militancy and sectarianism at home.

### Phase 6 (1990–2001): Sanctions and isolation
- 1990: Pressler Amendment sanctions cut US aid.
- 1989 onwards: Uprising in Indian-held Kashmir.
- 1996–2001: Pakistan was one of three states to recognise the Taliban government.
- 1998: India tested (11 and 13 May); Pakistan replied (28 and 30 May, Chagai). Sanctions followed.
- 1999: Lahore Declaration (February), Kargil conflict (May–July), military coup (12 October).

### Phase 7 (2001–2011): Ally in the "war on terror"
- 2001: After 9/11 Pakistan joined the US-led campaign. 2004: named a Major Non-NATO Ally.
- 2001–02: Military standoff with India after the attack on the Indian parliament.
- 2004–08: Composite dialogue and back-channel with India; ended by the Mumbai attacks (November 2008).
- 2007: TTP formed; terrorism inside Pakistan surged.
- 2009: Kerry–Lugar–Berman Act ($7.5bn civilian aid).
- 2011: Raymond Davis affair (January), Abbottabad raid (2 May), Salala attack (26 November). Trust with the US collapsed.

### Phase 8 (2013–2021): China, CPEC and "geo-economics"
- 2013–15: CPEC launched (Xi Jinping's visit, April 2015; first package about $46bn).
- 2014: APS Peshawar attack (16 December); National Action Plan.
- 2016: Uri attack; SAARC summit in Islamabad postponed.
- 2017: Full member of the SCO (June).
- 2018–22: FATF grey list.
- 2019: Pulwama–Balakot crisis (February). India revoked Article 370 (5 August); Pakistan downgraded ties and stopped trade. Kartarpur corridor opened (November).
- 2020: US–Taliban Doha Agreement (29 February).
- 2021: LoC ceasefire reaffirmed (February). Taliban returned to Kabul (15 August).

### Phase 9 (2022–2024): Crisis management
- January 2022: First National Security Policy, centred on economic security.
- February 2022: Prime Minister Imran Khan was in Moscow on the day Russia invaded Ukraine. April 2022: removed by a vote of no confidence; he alleged a US conspiracy, which Washington denied.
- 2022: Catastrophic floods; exit from the FATF grey list (October).
- 2023: Near-default; IMF Stand-By Arrangement; SIFC created to draw Gulf investment; deportation drive against undocumented Afghans began (October).
- January 2024: Iran and Pakistan exchanged air strikes, then quickly restored ties.
- September 2024: 37-month IMF Extended Fund Facility of about $7bn. October 2024: Islamabad hosted the SCO heads-of-government meeting; India's foreign minister attended.

### Phase 10 (2025–2026): High-risk activism
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Jan 2025 | Pakistan began its eighth term on the UN Security Council (2025–26). |
| 22 Apr 2025 | Pahalgam attack. India put the Indus Waters Treaty "in abeyance" (23 April). |
| 7–10 May 2025 | India–Pakistan military conflict; ceasefire on 10 May after US mediation. |
| Jun–Sep 2025 | Rapid thaw with the US: Field Marshal Munir's White House lunch (June), a trade deal with a 19% tariff (31 July), Sharif and Munir at the White House (25 September). |
| 17 Sep 2025 | Saudi–Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement. |
| Oct 2025 | Border war with the Afghan Taliban; Doha ceasefire (19 October). |
| Nov 2025 | Pakistan voted for UNSC Resolution 2803 on Gaza; later joined the Board of Peace (January 2026). |
| Feb 2026 | "Open war" with Afghanistan (from 27 February). US–Israel war on Iran (from 28 February). |
| Mar–Jun 2026 | Pakistan mediated between Washington and Tehran: ceasefire of 8 April, Islamabad Talks (11–12 April), Islamabad Memorandum (17 June), which collapsed in July. |
| 7 Aug 2026 | Makkah Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Türkiye. |
| 31 Aug 2026 | Court of Arbitration ruled India cannot hold the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance. |
| 1 Sep 2026 | Pakistan took the SCO chair for 2026–27. |

**Reading of this phase (analysis):** Pakistan moved from isolation to visibility. It used three assets: military performance in May 2025, personal access to the Trump White House, and trust in both Riyadh and Tehran. The risks are over-extension (two hostile borders plus Gulf commitments), dependence on one US president, and a weak economy that cannot fund a large role.

## 3. Five debates to know

1. **Geo-strategy or geo-economics?** The 2022 National Security Policy promised an economic pivot. Events since 2025 pulled policy back to security.
2. **Balancing the US and China.** Can Pakistan keep CPEC and a US minerals and trade relationship at once?
3. **Saudi Arabia and Iran.** Neutrality (parliament's 2015 vote on Yemen) versus treaty commitments (2025, 2026).
4. **Afghanistan.** "Strategic depth" turned into a liability: the Taliban government shelters, or fails to restrain, the TTP.
5. **India.** Dialogue is frozen. Options range from managed hostility to back-channel talks. Water has joined Kashmir as a core dispute.
