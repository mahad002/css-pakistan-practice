# Key Terms Glossary

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. Definitions are in my own words. Each has an example you can reuse in an answer.
> **How to use:** Learn ten a week. In the exam, define a term the first time you use it, in one line.

---

## A. Power and order

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Balance of power** | States prevent any one state from dominating, by arming (internal balancing) or allying (external balancing). | Pakistan's partnership with China offsets India's size. |
| **Bandwagoning** | Joining the stronger side instead of balancing against it. | Small states aligning with the US after 1991. |
| **Hedging** | Keeping good ties with rival powers to avoid choosing. | Gulf states with the US and China; Pakistan with Washington and Beijing in 2025–26. |
| **Hegemony** | Leadership by one state that sets the rules, by force and consent. | The US after 1945 and after 1991. |
| **Unipolarity / bipolarity / multipolarity** | One, two, or several great powers. | Bipolar Cold War; unipolar 1991–2008; a contested multipolar world now. |
| **Multilateralism** | Three or more states working through common rules. | UN, WTO, Paris Agreement. |
| **Minilateralism** | Small, issue-based groups. | QUAD, AUKUS, I2U2, the Makkah defence alliance. |
| **Rules-based international order** | The post-1945 system of institutions and law built mainly by the US and allies. | Critics say it is applied selectively (Iraq 2003, Gaza). |
| **Global South** | Developing countries of Asia, Africa and Latin America as a political grouping. | G77, BRICS outreach. |
| **Middle power** | A state that is not a great power but shapes outcomes in its region or on an issue. | Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia; Pakistan's mediation in 2026. |
| **Sphere of influence** | A region where a great power claims special rights. | Russia's view of Ukraine; the US "Monroe Doctrine" revived over Venezuela in 2026. |
| **Thucydides Trap** | Danger of war when a rising power threatens a ruling one (Graham Allison). | China and the US. |
| **Strategic autonomy** | Freedom to choose partners issue by issue. | India buying Russian oil while in the QUAD. |
| **Non-alignment / multi-alignment** | Staying out of blocs / engaging all blocs at once. | NAM (1961); India today. |

## B. Security and war

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Deterrence** | Preventing an attack by threatening unacceptable cost. It needs capability, credibility and communication. | Nuclear deterrence between India and Pakistan. |
| **Compellence** | Using threats or limited force to make an opponent change behaviour. | India's strikes in May 2025 aimed to compel Pakistan on militancy. |
| **Minimum credible deterrence** | Keeping the smallest nuclear force that still deters. | Pakistan's declared posture since 1998. |
| **Full spectrum deterrence** | Pakistan's doctrine of deterring threats at strategic, operational and tactical levels. | Linked to India's "Cold Start" idea; includes short-range Nasr. |
| **Second-strike capability** | Ability to retaliate after absorbing a nuclear attack. | Submarine-launched missiles (India's Arihant class; Pakistan's Babur-3 tests). |
| **Mutual assured destruction (MAD)** | Both sides can destroy each other, so neither starts. | US–USSR. |
| **Stability–instability paradox** | Nuclear stability at the top can encourage smaller conflicts below. | Kargil 1999; Balakot 2019; May 2025. |
| **Escalation ladder / escalation dominance** | Steps from crisis to all-out war / being able to win at every step. | Each side claimed dominance in May 2025. |
| **Strategic stability** | A situation where no side has an incentive to strike first or race for arms. | Weakened by missile defence, MIRVs, drones and short decision times. |
| **Hybrid warfare** | Mixing military, economic, cyber, information and proxy tools below the level of open war. | Disinformation networks (EU DisinfoLab's 2020 "Indian Chronicles" report), FATF pressure, proxies. Asked in 2019. |
| **Fifth-generation warfare (5GW)** | A loose term for information and perception warfare. Popular in Pakistani debate. Use carefully. | Social media campaigns. |
| **Grey-zone conflict** | Coercion that stays under the threshold of war. | China's coast guard in the South China Sea. |
| **Proxy war** | Fighting through third parties. | Iran-backed groups; great-power proxy wars in Syria and Sudan. |
| **Asymmetric warfare** | A weak actor uses unconventional methods against a strong one. | TTP, Houthis, drones against tanks. |
| **Pre-emptive vs preventive strike** | Pre-emptive: against an imminent attack. Preventive: against a future threat. The first is debated in law; the second is generally unlawful. | Israel–US strikes on Iran (2025, 2026) are widely described as preventive. |
| **Collective defence** | An attack on one is an attack on all. | NATO Article 5; Article 2 of the 2026 Makkah agreement. |
| **Collective security** | All states act together against any aggressor. | The UN Charter's design, blocked by the veto. |
| **Non-traditional security** | Threats other than armies: climate, pandemics, water, food, cyber. | Pakistan's 2022 and 2025 floods. |
| **Human security** | Security of people (freedom from fear and want), not only of the state. | Pakistan's National Security Policy 2022 uses the idea. |
| **Counter-terrorism vs counter-insurgency** | CT targets networks. COIN wins over the population as well. | Zarb-e-Azb (2014) and Azm-e-Istehkam (2024). |
| **Lawfare** | Using law as a weapon. | Pakistan's Indus Waters arbitration; South Africa v. Israel at the ICJ. |

## C. Economy

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Geo-economics** | Using economic tools for strategic ends, or putting economic goals at the centre of foreign policy. | Pakistan's 2021–22 "pivot to geo-economics"; sanctions; BRI. |
| **Geopolitics** | How geography shapes power. | Gwadar, Hormuz, Malacca. |
| **Economic statecraft / weaponised interdependence** | Using trade, finance or technology links to coerce. | US chip export controls; China's rare-earth controls; closing Hormuz. |
| **Decoupling / de-risking** | Cutting / reducing dependence on a rival's economy. | US and EU policy towards China. |
| **Friend-shoring** | Moving supply chains to allied countries. | Chip plants moving to the US, India, Vietnam. |
| **De-dollarisation** | Reducing use of the US dollar in trade and reserves. | BRICS local-currency settlement. |
| **Balance of payments** | A country's record of all transactions with the world. The current account is trade plus income plus remittances. | Pakistan's FY26 current account: about −$0.14bn (SBP). |
| **Fiscal deficit / primary balance** | Spending minus revenue / the same but excluding interest. | The IMF target for Pakistan is a primary surplus. |
| **Twin deficits** | Fiscal and current-account deficits together. | Pakistan's recurring boom–bust cause. |
| **Circular debt** | A chain of unpaid bills in the power sector. | About Rs1.6–1.7 trillion in mid-2026. |
| **Conditionality** | Policy conditions attached to loans. | IMF Extended Fund Facility. |
| **Debt trap** | Debt so heavy that new loans mainly repay old ones. "Debt-trap diplomacy" is a contested charge against China. | Hambantota is the usual, disputed example. |
| **Middle-income trap** | Growth stalls after a country leaves low-income status. | A risk for many Asian economies. |
| **Elite capture** | Powerful groups bend policy and subsidies to their benefit. | UNDP's 2021 Pakistan report estimated elite privileges at about $17.4bn. |
| **Rentier state** | A state living on external rents (oil, aid, location) and not on taxing citizens. | Gulf monarchies; some describe Pakistan's "strategic rents". |
| **Demographic dividend** | A growth boost when the working-age share is high, if jobs and skills exist. | About two-thirds of Pakistanis are under 30. |

## D. Governance and law

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Federalism** | Power shared between centre and provinces by the constitution. | 18th Amendment (2010). |
| **Devolution / decentralisation** | Moving power down to provinces and local governments. | Article 140A. |
| **Separation of powers / judicial review** | Legislature, executive and judiciary check each other / courts can strike down laws. | Debate over the 26th and 27th Amendments. |
| **Basic structure doctrine** | Some features of a constitution cannot be amended away. | Accepted in India (Kesavananda, 1973); contested in Pakistan. |
| **Hybrid regime** | A system mixing elected institutions with strong unelected influence. | The Economist Intelligence Unit has placed Pakistan in "hybrid" or "authoritarian" bands in recent years **[verify latest]**. |
| **Praetorianism** | Military dominance of politics. | A standard term in writing on Pakistan. |
| **Good governance** | Participation, rule of law, transparency, accountability, effectiveness. | World Bank indicators. |
| **Civic space** | Room for citizens, media and NGOs to organise and speak. | Asked in 2026. |
| **Populism** | Politics framed as "the pure people" against "the corrupt elite". | Trump, Modi, Erdoğan, Imran Khan are all described this way by scholars. |
| **Securitisation (Copenhagen School)** | Presenting an issue as an existential threat to justify extraordinary measures. | Migration in Europe; dissent in many states. |
| **Responsibility to Protect (R2P)** | States must protect people from mass atrocities. If they fail, the international community may act (UN, 2005). | Libya 2011; its absence over Gaza and Sudan. |
| **Self-determination** | A people's right to decide its political status. | Kashmir; Palestine. |
| **Universal jurisdiction, ICC, ICJ** | Courts for individuals (ICC) and for states (ICJ). | ICC warrants for Netanyahu (2024) and Putin (2023). |

## E. Climate and development

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitigation / adaptation** | Cutting emissions / coping with impacts. | Solar power / flood defences. |
| **Loss and damage** | Harm beyond what adaptation can prevent. | Fund agreed at COP27 (2022), set up at COP28. |
| **NDC** | Nationally Determined Contribution: each country's climate pledge under Paris. | Pakistan's updated NDC **[verify the 2025 figures]**. |
| **CBDR** | Common but differentiated responsibilities: all must act, rich countries more. | Core of the Global South's case. |
| **Climate justice** | Those who polluted least suffer most and deserve support. | Pakistan emits under 1% of global greenhouse gases. |
| **NCQG** | New Collective Quantified Goal: $300bn a year by 2035, agreed at COP29 in Baku. | Developing countries wanted $1.3 trillion. |
| **Just transition** | Moving away from fossil fuels fairly for workers and poor countries. | COP31 work programme. |
| **SDGs** | 17 Sustainable Development Goals for 2030 (adopted 2015). They replaced the 8 MDGs (2000–2015). | Asked in 2026. |
| **HDI / MPI** | Human Development Index (health, education, income) / Multidimensional Poverty Index. | Pakistan's HDI 0.544, rank 168 (HDR 2025). |
