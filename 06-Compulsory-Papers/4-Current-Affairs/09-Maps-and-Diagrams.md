# 15 Maps and Diagrams Worth Learning to Draw

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. Geography does not change; the labels for current events do, so update them.
> **Why:** Examiner reports say candidates who drew maps and diagrams properly scored well. A figure shows command of the subject in thirty seconds of the marker's time.

## Rules for exam drawing

1. **Two minutes, no more.** Practise each figure until you can draw it from memory in under two minutes.
2. **Box it.** Draw a rectangle about one-third of a page. Title above ("Fig. 1: Strait of Hormuz"), a north arrow for maps, and a one-line caption below.
3. **Shapes, not art.** Use straight lines and simple curves. A country can be a rough polygon. Accuracy of relative position matters; coastline detail does not.
4. **Label in capitals,** horizontally. Six to ten labels are enough.
5. **Use two tools:** a blue or black pen for outlines, a pencil for shading. Hatch with diagonal lines instead of colouring.
6. **Refer to it** in your text: "as Fig. 1 shows, …".
7. **One figure per answer.** Place it after the background or in the middle of the analysis.
8. **Practise on plain paper** with a timer. Trace each map from an atlas five times, then draw it blind.

## 1. CPEC routes (Khunjerab to Gwadar)

**Use for:** CPEC, China, Balochistan, Gwadar, connectivity.

**Steps**
1. Draw Pakistan as a tilted rectangle: narrow at the top right (north), wide at the bottom left (south-west coast).
2. Draw the coastline along the bottom as a gentle line running west to east. Mark **Gwadar** near the western end and **Karachi** two-thirds along.
3. At the top right mark **Khunjerab Pass** and write "CHINA (Kashgar)" above it.
4. Draw the **Karakoram Highway** as a line from Khunjerab down through **Gilgit** to **Islamabad**.
5. From Islamabad draw three lines to Gwadar:
   - **Western route:** through **D.I. Khan – Zhob – Quetta – Gwadar** (left side).
   - **Central route:** through **D.I. Khan – D.G. Khan – Khuzdar – Gwadar**.
   - **Eastern route:** through **Lahore – Multan – Sukkur – Karachi – coastal highway – Gwadar** (right side, along the Indus).
6. Draw a dashed line Karachi–Lahore–Peshawar and label it **ML-1 railway**.
7. Put small squares for SEZs at **Rashakai**, **Faisalabad** and **Dhabeji**. Label neighbours: Afghanistan (west), Iran (south-west), India (east), Arabian Sea (south).

**Caption:** "CPEC: about 3,000 km linking Xinjiang to the Arabian Sea; Phase II launched September 2025."

## 2. Strait of Hormuz

**Use for:** Iran war, energy security, Gulf, Pakistan's mediation.

**Steps**
1. Draw a wide body of water entering from the left: the **Persian Gulf**. Draw a second body on the right: the **Gulf of Oman**, opening to the **Arabian Sea**.
2. Join them with a narrow, upside-down V-shaped channel. This bend is the strait.
3. Above the channel write **IRAN**. Mark **Bandar Abbas** on the Iranian coast at the top of the bend and draw a long thin island just below it: **Qeshm**.
4. Below the channel draw a thumb of land pointing north into the bend: the **Musandam peninsula (OMAN)**. South of it write **UAE**, with **Fujairah** on the outer (east) coast and **Dubai** on the inner coast.
5. Further left along the southern shore label **Qatar**, **Saudi Arabia**, **Kuwait** at the head of the Gulf.
6. Draw two thin parallel arrows through the channel for inbound and outbound shipping lanes.
7. On the far right edge mark **Gwadar (Pakistan)**.

**Caption:** "About one-fifth of world oil and LNG passes through the strait (narrowest point about 33 km); closed since March 2026."

## 3. Indo-Pacific: chokepoints and the first island chain

**Use for:** US–China rivalry, QUAD, AUKUS, South China Sea, Taiwan.

**Steps**
1. Draw the coast of Asia as a line running from top right (Korea) down and left to the Malay peninsula, then west to India (a triangle pointing down) and on to Arabia.
2. East of the coast, draw from top to bottom: **Japan** (a curved strip), **Taiwan** (a small oval off China's coast), the **Philippines** (a cluster), and **Indonesia** (a long horizontal chain) with **Australia** below.
3. Connect Japan – Taiwan – Philippines – Borneo with a dashed line: the **first island chain**.
4. Shade the sea between Vietnam, the Philippines and Borneo: **South China Sea**. Draw a dashed U-shape inside it: **nine-dash line**.
5. Mark chokepoints with X: **Malacca** (between Malaya and Sumatra), **Taiwan Strait**, **Hormuz**, **Bab el-Mandeb**.
6. Mark **Gwadar**, **Chabahar**, **Hambantota**, **Djibouti** and **Diego Garcia** (a dot in the central Indian Ocean).
7. Write QUAD beside the US (arrow from the east), Japan, India, Australia.

**Caption:** "The Indo-Pacific: sea lanes, chokepoints and competing networks."

## 4. Kashmir and the Line of Control

**Use for:** India, Kashmir, May 2025, Siachen.

**Steps**
1. Draw a rough square for the former state of Jammu and Kashmir, tilted so the top leans right.
2. Draw a wavy line from the lower left (near **Jammu–Sialkot**) up and to the right, ending at point **NJ9842**. Label it **LoC (about 740 km)**.
3. West and north of the line write **AZAD KASHMIR** (thin strip, with **Muzaffarabad**) and **GILGIT-BALTISTAN** (large area on top, with **Gilgit**, **Skardu**).
4. East and south of the line write **Indian-administered J&K** with **Srinagar** in the valley and **Jammu** in the south; to the east **LADAKH** (**Leh**, **Kargil**).
5. From NJ9842 draw a short line north: **Siachen Glacier / AGPL**.
6. In the far north-east shade **Aksai Chin (China)** and draw the **LAC**.
7. Mark **Pahalgam** south-east of Srinagar. Below Jammu, draw the **Working Boundary** continuing to the international border. Label Pakistan (west), China (north-east), India (south).

**Caption:** "Jammu and Kashmir: divided by the LoC since 1949/1972; three nuclear powers meet here."

## 5. Durand Line and Pakistan's western border

**Use for:** Afghanistan, TTP, refugees, internal security, Balochistan.

**Steps**
1. Draw Pakistan's western edge as a line running from the top (north) down and left to the coast.
2. At the very top draw a thin finger of Afghan land pointing east to China: the **Wakhan corridor**, with Tajikistan above it.
3. Label the long border **Durand Line (1893), 2,640 km**.
4. Mark crossings: **Torkham** (level with Peshawar–Kabul), **Kharlachi**, **Ghulam Khan**, **Angoor Adda**, **Chaman** (level with Quetta–Kandahar).
5. On the Pakistani side shade a strip along the upper half: **former FATA / merged districts** (Bajaur, Khyber, Kurram, North and South Waziristan). Mark **Peshawar**, **Bannu**, **D.I. Khan**, **Quetta**.
6. On the Afghan side mark **Kabul**, **Jalalabad (Nangarhar)**, **Khost**, **Paktika**, **Kandahar**.
7. At the bottom mark the **Iran border (909 km)** and **Gwadar**.

**Caption:** "The Durand Line: unrecognised by Kabul, fenced by Pakistan, crossed by militants and traders."

## 6. Indus basin and the Indus Waters Treaty

**Use for:** water, IWT, India, inter-provincial harmony, floods.

**Steps**
1. Draw the **Indus** as one long line from the top right (Tibet/Ladakh), looping west through Gilgit-Baltistan, then south through the whole country to the **Arabian Sea** near Karachi.
2. From the right, draw five tributaries joining it in Punjab, top to bottom: **Jhelum**, **Chenab**, **Ravi**, **Beas** (joins the Sutlej inside India), **Sutlej**. They merge at **Panjnad** and meet the Indus at **Mithankot**.
3. From the left, draw the **Kabul River** joining near Attock.
4. Bracket the Indus, Jhelum and Chenab: **"Western rivers: Pakistan"**. Bracket Ravi, Beas, Sutlej: **"Eastern rivers: India"**.
5. Draw the LoC/border as a dashed line crossing the upper tributaries.
6. Mark dams: **Tarbela** (Indus), **Mangla** (Jhelum), **Diamer-Bhasha** (upper Indus); Indian projects **Kishenganga** (Jhelum tributary), **Baglihar** and **Ratle** (Chenab).
7. Mark barrages **Sukkur** and **Kotri** in Sindh.

**Caption:** "Indus Waters Treaty 1960: held 'in abeyance' by India since April 2025; tribunal ruled it in force, August 2026."

## 7. Israel and the Palestinian territories

**Use for:** Palestine, Gaza, the Trump plan, Middle East.

**Steps**
1. Draw a vertical coastline on the left: the **Mediterranean Sea**.
2. Draw Israel as a tall, narrow shape tapering to a point at the bottom (**Eilat / Gulf of Aqaba**).
3. On the right side, in the middle, draw a kidney-shaped bulge cut out of Israel: the **West Bank**, with **Jerusalem** on its western edge, **Ramallah** above it and **Hebron** below.
4. On the coast in the south-west draw a small rectangle: the **Gaza Strip** (about 41 km long), with **Gaza City** in the north and **Rafah** on the Egyptian border.
5. Along the right edge draw the **Jordan River** from the **Sea of Galilee** to the **Dead Sea**.
6. Label neighbours: **Lebanon** (north), **Syria / Golan Heights** (north-east), **Jordan** (east), **Egypt / Sinai** (south-west).
7. Inside Gaza draw a dashed line parallel to the eastern edge and label it "yellow line (2025)".

**Caption:** "Territories occupied since 1967: West Bank, East Jerusalem, Gaza (and the Golan)."

## 8. Ukraine and the front line

**Use for:** Russia–Ukraine war, European security, energy.

**Steps**
1. Draw Ukraine as a wide, flat oval. Below it draw the **Black Sea** with the diamond-shaped **Crimea** hanging from the southern coast, and the small **Sea of Azov** to its east.
2. Label **Russia** to the east and north-east, **Belarus** to the north, **Poland** to the west, **Romania** and **Moldova** to the south-west.
3. Draw the **Dnipro River** from the top centre curving down to the Black Sea. Mark **Kyiv** on it in the north.
4. In the east, mark four regions from top to bottom: **Luhansk**, **Donetsk**, **Zaporizhzhia**, **Kherson**. Shade the parts east and south of a dashed line that runs roughly from near **Kharkiv** down past **Donetsk city** to the Dnipro near **Kherson city**. Label the dashed line "front line (approx.)".
5. Shade Crimea ("annexed 2014").
6. Mark **Odesa** on the south-west coast.

**Caption:** "Russia claims four regions and Crimea; about a fifth of Ukraine is occupied [verify current share]."

## 9. South Asia

**Use for:** SAARC, Bangladesh, regional integration, India–Pakistan.

**Steps**
1. Draw India as a large triangle pointing down, with a bulge at the top right.
2. Attach **Pakistan** to the north-west side, **Nepal** and **Bhutan** as two small rectangles along the northern edge, and **Bangladesh** as a small shape tucked into the north-east, opening south to the **Bay of Bengal**.
3. Place **Sri Lanka** as a teardrop below the tip and the **Maldives** as dots to the south-west.
4. Add **Afghanistan** west of Pakistan and **China** across the top; **Myanmar** east of Bangladesh.
5. Mark capitals: Islamabad, New Delhi, Dhaka, Kathmandu, Thimphu, Colombo, Malé, Kabul.
6. Draw a dashed sea route **Karachi – Colombo – Chittagong**.
7. Mark the narrow **Siliguri corridor** between Nepal and Bangladesh.

**Caption:** "SAARC's eight members; intra-regional trade about 5%."

## 10. Central Asia and regional connectivity

**Use for:** Central Asia, ECO, Türkiye, UAP railway, TAPI, CASA-1000.

**Steps**
1. Draw a large shape at the top: **Kazakhstan**. Below it, from left to right: **Turkmenistan** (beside the **Caspian Sea** on the far left), **Uzbekistan** (centre), **Kyrgyzstan** and **Tajikistan** (right, small).
2. Below those draw **Iran** (left), **Afghanistan** (centre) and **Pakistan** (right and down to the sea). **China** on the far right.
3. Draw the **UAP railway** as a line: **Tashkent – Termez – Mazar-i-Sharif – Kabul – Kharlachi – Peshawar**, then on to **Karachi/Gwadar**.
4. Draw **TAPI** as a dotted line: Turkmenistan – Herat – Kandahar – Quetta – Multan – India.
5. Draw **CASA-1000** as a zigzag line: Kyrgyzstan – Tajikistan – Kabul – Peshawar.
6. Draw the **KKH alternative**: Kashgar – Khunjerab – Islamabad.
7. On the left add a long arrow **Islamabad – Tehran – Istanbul** (ECO rail corridor) and mark **Chabahar** and **Bandar Abbas**.

**Caption:** "Routes from landlocked Central Asia to the sea; all southern routes cross Afghanistan or Iran."

## 11. SCO members on an outline of Eurasia

**Use for:** SCO, BRICS, multipolarity.

**Steps**
1. Draw a very wide rectangle across the top: **Russia**.
2. Below it on the right draw **China**. Between them, in the centre, the Central Asian block: **Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan**.
3. Below China's western half draw **Pakistan** and **India**; to the west of Pakistan draw **Iran**.
4. To the west of Russia add a small box: **Belarus**.
5. Hatch all ten members. Leave **Afghanistan** and **Mongolia** unhatched and label them "observers" **[verify current status]**.
6. Mark **Beijing** (secretariat), **Tashkent** (RATS), **Bishkek** (2026 summit) and **Islamabad** (2027 summit).

**Caption:** "SCO: 10 members, about 40% of world population; Pakistan chairs 2026–27."

## 12. Escalation ladder (diagram)

**Use for:** nuclear deterrence, India–Pakistan crises, strategic stability.

**Steps**
1. Draw a ladder with eight rungs.
2. Label from the bottom: (1) terrorist attack / trigger; (2) diplomatic and economic measures; (3) firing across the LoC; (4) limited air strike; (5) missile and drone strikes on mainland bases; (6) full conventional war; (7) tactical nuclear use; (8) strategic nuclear exchange.
3. Draw a bracket beside rung 4: "Balakot 2019". Beside rung 5: "May 2025".
4. Draw a horizontal dashed line between rungs 6 and 7: "nuclear threshold".
5. Add an arrow on the side pointing up: "less time, more risk".

**Caption:** "Each crisis has stopped one rung higher."

## 13. Circular debt chain (flow diagram)

**Use for:** energy, economy, governance.

**Steps**
1. Draw five boxes in a row: **Consumers → DISCOs → CPPA-G (central buyer) → Power producers (IPPs, GENCOs) → Fuel suppliers (PSO, gas companies)**.
2. Draw arrows left to right labelled "electricity bills / payments".
3. Under the DISCO box write the leaks: "line losses, theft, low recovery, delayed tariff notification, unpaid subsidies".
4. Under the IPP box write "capacity payments (about 60% of cost), dollar-indexed".
5. Draw a curved arrow from the last box back to the government: "arrears → budget, bank borrowing".
6. Write the stock in a circle: "about Rs1.6–1.7 trillion (mid-2026)".

**Caption:** "Cash does not flow through the chain, so each link owes the next."

## 14. Court structure before and after the 26th and 27th Amendments

**Use for:** judiciary, constitutional amendments, separation of powers.

**Steps**
1. Left side, title "Before Oct 2024": a pyramid of three levels: **Supreme Court** (constitutional + appellate; CJP = senior-most judge), **High Courts (5)**, **District courts**. Beside it a small box "Federal Shariat Court".
2. Right side, title "After Nov 2025": at the top, two boxes side by side: **Federal Constitutional Court** (constitution, fundamental rights, federation–province disputes) and **Supreme Court** (civil and criminal appeals).
3. Below them **High Courts** with a note "constitutional benches; judges transferable".
4. At the side draw two boxes with arrows to the top: **Judicial Commission** (judges + parliamentarians) and **Special Parliamentary Committee** (selects CJP from three senior-most; three-year term).
5. Draw **District courts** at the base on both sides.

**Caption:** "Constitutional jurisdiction moved from the Supreme Court to a new court."

## 15. Pakistan: provinces and proposed provinces

**Use for:** new provinces, federalism, NFC, ethnicity, census.

**Steps**
1. Draw the outline of Pakistan (as in map 1).
2. Divide it: **Balochistan** (large, south-west), **Sindh** (south-east, on the coast), **Punjab** (east-centre), **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** (north-west strip), **Islamabad** (dot), **Gilgit-Baltistan** and **Azad Kashmir** (north and north-east).
3. Mark capitals: Quetta, Karachi, Lahore, Peshawar, Gilgit, Muzaffarabad.
4. Hatch the southern third of Punjab (**Multan, Bahawalpur, D.G. Khan**): "proposed South Punjab".
5. Hatch the area east of the Indus in KP (**Abbottabad, Mansehra**): "proposed Hazara".
6. Write the 2023 census populations beside each province: Punjab 127.7m, Sindh 55.7m, KP 40.9m, Balochistan 14.9m.
7. Add NFC shares in brackets if the question is fiscal: 51.74%, 24.55%, 14.62%, 9.09%.

**Caption:** "Four provinces for over 240 million people; Article 239(4) requires the affected province's two-thirds consent to change boundaries."

## Quick generic diagrams (learn the shapes)

| Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Vicious cycle** (boxes in a circle) | Boom–bust economy; poverty–population; instability; Islamophobia |
| **Three pillars under a roof** | NPT; sustainable development (economic, social, environmental) |
| **Triangle** | Pakistan–US–China; risk = hazard × exposure × vulnerability |
| **Timeline arrow** | COPs; foreign policy phases; Pakistan–US relations |
| **Pyramid** | AI risk tiers; health-care levels; court hierarchy |
| **Two-column table** | For and against; before and after; positions of two sides |
| **Balance scale** | Security versus liberty; speech versus order |
| **Funnel** | Education pipeline; case backlog; tax base |
| **Bar chart (hand-drawn)** | Warheads by country; tariffs; budget shares |
