# Pakistan–Türkiye Relations

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Syllabus:** External – Muslim world (Turkey). **Past papers:** 2023 (ties beyond economics and their geopolitical spillover).

---

## 1. Background

- **Roots:** the Khilafat Movement (1919–24) in support of the Ottoman caliphate; Muslim League aid to Turkey in its war of independence.
- **Cold War:** both in the Baghdad Pact/CENTO (1955) and the Regional Cooperation for Development (1964, with Iran), which became ECO in 1985.
- **Mutual support:** Türkiye backs Pakistan on Kashmir (Erdoğan has raised it at the UN General Assembly); Pakistan backs Türkiye on Northern Cyprus and against the Gülen network and the PKK. Both support Azerbaijan; Pakistan does not recognise Armenia.
- **Institutions:** High-Level Strategic Cooperation Council (since 2009); Strategic Economic Framework (2020); Preferential Trade Agreement in goods (signed 2022, in force 2023).

## 3. Geopolitical spillover (the 2023 question)

| Region | Effect of the partnership |
|---|---|
| **South Asia / Himalayas** | Turkish support on Kashmir angers India, which has drawn closer to Greece, Cyprus and Armenia in reply. |
| **South Caucasus** | Pakistan and Türkiye backed Azerbaijan in the 2020 Karabakh war and after; a three-way defence relationship has formed. |
| **Arabian Peninsula** | Once a source of friction (the 2019 Kuala Lumpur summit, the Qatar blockade), now aligned through the 2026 Makkah pact as Saudi–Turkish relations improved. |

## 4. Challenges

1. **Economics lags politics:** small trade, distance, similar export baskets (textiles), no direct shipping advantage, currency crises in both.
2. **Third-party limits:** US sanctions and export controls on Turkish defence products; Türkiye's NATO obligations.
3. **Different depth of ties with India, Iran and the Gulf.**
4. **Over-reliance on leaders' chemistry.**
5. **Alliance ambiguity:** what the Makkah pact obliges in practice.

## 5. Way forward

1. Turn the PTA into a full free trade agreement including services and investment.
2. Operationalise the Islamabad–Tehran–Istanbul rail and road corridor and a shipping link.
3. Joint defence production: drones, naval vessels, aircraft components, with Saudi finance.
4. Turkish investment in construction, energy, dairy, tourism and the Karachi economic zone.
5. Education and culture: scholarships, Turkish-language centres, television co-production, tourism.
6. Coordinate in the OIC, ECO, D-8 and the UN on Palestine, Kashmir and Islamophobia.
7. Clarify alliance commitments through the new committee and keep them defensive.
