## 1. Background

- **1947–71:** East Pakistan held the majority of Pakistan's population. Grievances over language (1952), economic disparity and the denial of the Awami League's 1970 election victory led to the military operation of March 1971, civil war, Indian intervention and the surrender at Dhaka on 16 December 1971.
- **Recognition:** Pakistan recognised Bangladesh in February 1974 at the Lahore Islamic Summit. A tripartite agreement (April 1974) with India settled prisoners of war.
- **Unresolved issues from 1971:** Bangladesh's demand for a formal apology; division of pre-1971 assets (Dhaka has claimed about $4.3–4.5bn); repatriation of "stranded Pakistanis" (Biharis).
- **1975–2008:** warmer ties under Ziaur Rahman, Ershad and the BNP; both were founding members of SAARC (1985), which was Bangladesh's idea.
- **2009–2024 (Sheikh Hasina):** relations at their lowest. War-crimes trials and executions of Jamaat-e-Islami leaders (2013–16) drew Pakistani protests; visas and trade were restricted; Dhaka aligned closely with India and joined the boycott of the 2016 SAARC summit in Islamabad.

## 4. Opportunities and limits (analysis)

**Opportunities**
1. Trade complementarity: Pakistani cotton and yarn for Bangladesh's garment industry; rice, onions, cement, pharmaceuticals; Bangladeshi jute and tea.
2. Revival of SAARC or a smaller regional platform; China–Pakistan–Bangladesh trilateral contacts began in 2025 **[verify]**.
3. Defence cooperation: training, JF-17 interest **[verify]**.
4. Learning from Bangladesh: population policy, women's employment, microfinance, garment exports.

**Limits**
1. 1971 remains a live moral and political issue for Bangladeshis; any government in Dhaka must respect it.
2. India surrounds Bangladesh on three sides and controls its rivers and power supply; Dhaka will rebalance, not switch sides.
3. Bangladeshi politics is volatile; Pakistan should avoid being identified with one party.
4. Distance and thin transport links.

## 5. Way forward

1. Address 1971 with grace: build on the 1974 agreement's words of regret and on Musharraf's 2002 statement; a joint historians' commission.
2. A free trade or preferential agreement; direct shipping and flights; business visas on arrival.
3. Settle the stranded-Pakistanis issue humanely.
4. Cooperation on climate (both are delta and flood states), disaster management and textiles.
5. Work together to revive SAARC: propose the postponed 19th summit or a ministerial meeting.
6. Keep the relationship off the India–Pakistan axis: frame it as bilateral and economic.
7. University, media and cricket exchanges for the generation born after 1971.
