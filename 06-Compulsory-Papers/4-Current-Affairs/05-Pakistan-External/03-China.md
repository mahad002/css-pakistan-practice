## 1. Background

**Why the relationship lasts:** a shared concern about India, no bilateral dispute, China's need for access to the Arabian Sea and stability in Xinjiang, and Pakistan's need for arms, capital and diplomatic cover (for example China's holds at the UN on listings and its position on Kashmir).

## 2. Current situation (November 2026)

### Defence
- China supplies about 80% of Pakistan's major arms imports (SIPRI, 2020–24) **[verify latest]**. Key systems: JF-17 (co-produced), J-10C fighters, PL-15 missiles, HQ-9 air defence, VT-4 tanks, Type 054A/P frigates and eight Hangor-class submarines under delivery.
- **May 2025:** Pakistan's use of J-10C and PL-15 against Indian aircraft was the first combat test of these systems. Indian officers said China gave Pakistan live intelligence; China and Pakistan deny direct involvement **[verify statements]**. Purchase of the J-35 stealth fighter has been reported but not confirmed **[verify]**.

### Economy
- CPEC phase II launched at the 14th JCC (Beijing, 26 September 2025): five corridors; priorities ML-1, KKH realignment, Gwadar, SEZs, agriculture, IT, minerals ([CPECinfo](https://cpecinfo.com/cpec-phase-ii-officially-launched-at-14th-jcc-meeting/)).
- A May 2026 joint statement around the 75th anniversary restated these but set no date for the 15th JCC and gave no ML-1 financing ([summary](https://pakera.pk/cpec-2-0-china-pakistan-joint-statement-may-2026-explained/)).
- China is Pakistan's largest trading partner (about $23bn, with a large deficit for Pakistan) and largest bilateral creditor (roughly a quarter or more of external public debt). It regularly rolls over deposits and commercial loans (about $4bn in deposits plus loans) **[verify figures]**.

### Diplomacy
- **Afghanistan:** China hosted the Pakistan–Afghanistan talks in Urumqi (1–7 April 2026) and promotes a China–Pakistan–Afghanistan trilateral ([Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/2/pakistan-afghanistan-hold-talks-in-china-to-end-months-of-conflict)).
- **Iran war:** Pakistan and China issued a joint five-point initiative on 31 March 2026 calling for a ceasefire and normal navigation in the Strait of Hormuz ([Wikipedia: Pakistan in the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_in_the_2026_Iran_war)).
- **SCO:** Pakistan took the chair in September 2026 with Chinese support.
- A Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission meeting was reported in Islamabad on 16–17 September 2026 **[verify]**.
- **Space and technology:** Chinese launches of Pakistani satellites; a Pakistani astronaut is due to fly to China's space station **[verify date]**.

### Irritants
1. **Security of Chinese nationals:** attacks at Dasu (2021), Karachi (2022, 2024) and Besham (2024). Beijing has pressed for stronger protection and, reportedly, a role for its own security personnel, which Pakistan resists.
2. **Unpaid dues** to Chinese power producers and slow approvals.
3. **Pakistan's opening to the US** (minerals, crypto, ports, a 19% tariff deal). Chinese analysts watch for anything that affects Gwadar or sensitive technology. Pakistan says ties with one are not at the cost of the other.
4. **Slow phase II:** few factories in the SEZs; ML-1 unfunded.
5. **Trade imbalance.**

## 3. Assessment (analysis)

- **Realist reading:** a balancing alignment against India that deepened after May 2025.
- **Dependence risk:** arms, debt and trade are concentrated in one partner.
- **The new triangle:** in 2025–26 Pakistan is closer to both Washington and Beijing than at any time since the 1970s. That is an opportunity (a bridge role, as in 1971) and a risk if US–China rivalry sharpens over minerals, ports or technology.
- **China's caution:** Beijing gives strong support on India and core security, but it lends on commercial terms and avoids military entanglement in Pakistan's western wars.

## 4. Way forward

1. Deliver security for Chinese workers through capable policing and community consent.
2. Clear power-sector dues and re-profile debt by agreement.
3. Move from loans to equity and joint ventures; relocate Chinese industry into working SEZs.
4. Export more to China: agriculture, seafood, minerals with processing, IT.
5. Finance ML-1 through a consortium to avoid sole-creditor risk.
6. Transparency towards parliament and provinces.
7. A declared policy of "no camp politics": no bases for anyone, no technology leakage, open ports for commerce.
8. Use China's weight for regional peace: Afghanistan, Iran, and crisis communication with India.
9. Deepen people-to-people ties: language, scholarships, tourism through Khunjerab.
