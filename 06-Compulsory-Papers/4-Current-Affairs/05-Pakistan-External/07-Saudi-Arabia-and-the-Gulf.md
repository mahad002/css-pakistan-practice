# Pakistan–Saudi Arabia and the Gulf (with a note on Indonesia)

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. **Defence pacts verified:** (1) Saudi–Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement, 17 September 2025. (2) Makkah Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Türkiye, signed 7 August 2026, in force 14 November 2026.
> **Syllabus:** External – Muslim world (Saudi Arabia, Indonesia); organisations (GCC). **Past papers:** 2016 and 2024 (Saudi–Iran balance), 2018 (Islamic Military Alliance; Qatar crisis), 2020 (GCC). **Likely in 2028.**

## 1. Background

- **Foundations:** religious affinity (the two Holy Mosques), labour and remittances, oil on credit, and military cooperation since the 1960s. Pakistani troops were stationed in the Kingdom in the 1980s. A 1982 protocol covers training and deputation.
- **Financial support:** oil on deferred payment after the 1998 nuclear tests; deposits and oil facilities in 2018, 2021 and 2023 that helped Pakistan meet IMF conditions.
- **Strains:** Pakistan's parliament declined to join the Yemen war (April 2015); Riyadh's displeasure over the 2019 Kuala Lumpur summit and over criticism of the OIC on Kashmir (2020).
- **Institutions:** Saudi–Pakistan Supreme Coordination Council (2019); the Islamic Military Counter Terrorism Coalition (2015), first commanded by General Raheel Sharif.
- **People:** about 2.5–2.7 million Pakistanis work in Saudi Arabia and about 1.6–1.8 million in the UAE; the Gulf supplies more than half of remittances **[verify latest]**.

## 2. The defence pacts

### Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement (SMDA), 17 September 2025
- Signed in Riyadh by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif.
- Core clause: aggression against either is aggression against both. It does not require automatic military intervention.
- Context: Israel's strike on Hamas leaders in Doha (9 September 2025) shook Gulf confidence in US protection.
- The "nuclear umbrella" question: officials were ambiguous; Pakistan's doctrine remains India-specific. Treat any claim of extended deterrence as unconfirmed.

### Makkah Joint Defence Agreement / "Mecca Defence Alliance", 7 August 2026
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Parties | Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Türkiye (signed in Makkah by MBS, Sharif and Erdoğan) |
| Article 2 | An external armed attack on any party is considered an attack on all; assistance follows an assessment and Article 51 of the UN Charter |
| Structure | Secretariat in Saudi Arabia; first secretary-general a Pakistani; a Strategic Political and Defence Committee meeting twice a year (Istanbul 31 August, Riyadh 5 October 2026) |
| Membership | Open to others by consensus; Syria, Bangladesh and Egypt were discussed |
| Entry into force | 14 November 2026 after three ratifications (Pakistan ratified 23 October) |
| Invocation | Reported on 5 October 2026 after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia; Türkiye limited itself to defensive and technical support; Pakistan had said in September that it was not considering an immediate military response |

Sources: [IISS, Aug 2026](https://www.iiss.org/online-analysis/online-analysis/2026/08/pakistan-saudi-arabia-and-turkiye-a-new-defence-pact/); [Al Jazeera, 7 Aug 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/07/middleeast/saudi-arabia-turkey-pakistan-pact-intl); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca_Joint_Defence_Agreement).

**Deployments in 2026.** Pakistani fighter aircraft arrived at King Abdulaziz Air Base on 11 April 2026 after an Iranian strike on a Saudi petrochemical site. One account says Pakistan later deployed about 8,000 troops, 16 aircraft and two drone squadrons after Riyadh invoked the pact **[verify: single source]** ([Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)).

**Not confirmed.** A "Badr" defence pact among Indonesia, Pakistan, Türkiye and Qatar (listed for 21 October 2026 on one chronology page) and a Pakistan–Kuwait defence accord (28 August 2026) could not be confirmed by a search. **[verify]** before using.

## 3. Assessment (analysis)

| Gains for Pakistan | Risks |
|---|---|
| Status as a security provider; leverage for investment, oil and deposits | Entanglement in Yemen or a Saudi–Iran clash, against the 2015 neutrality precedent |
| Deterrent value against India through powerful friends | Damage to the mediator role and to ties with Iran |
| Defence-industrial cooperation with Türkiye and Saudi finance | Sectarian strain at home |
| A hedge as US guarantees look less reliable | Over-stretch: two hot borders plus Gulf deployments |
| A first step towards a wider Muslim security framework | Unclear commitments: analysts call the pact "largely symbolic" or a "framework, not a NATO"; no integrated command as of late August 2026 |

India said it expects Riyadh to respect its "interests and sensitivities". Iran's foreign minister welcomed the pact in public while others in Tehran criticised it.

## 4. Economic ties (November 2026)

- **Saudi Arabia:** deposits at the State Bank (about $5bn) and a deferred-oil facility, rolled over annually **[verify]**; investment pledges of $5bn (2024) with slow delivery; interest in Reko Diq and a refinery.
- **UAE:** deposits of about $2–3bn; port and logistics investments (AD Ports at Karachi); the largest source of FDI in some years **[verify]**.
- **Qatar:** long-term LNG contracts; a $3bn investment pledge (2022); the Ras Laffan complex was hit by Iranian strikes in 2026.
- **Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain:** labour, fuel credit, defence training.
- **GCC free trade agreement:** initialled September 2023 **[verify completion]**.
- **SIFC** was created to channel Gulf investment.
- **The 2026 war** hit Gulf cities and airports, raising questions about jobs and remittances, though FY26 remittances still set a record ($41.6bn).

## 5. Indonesia (the syllabus names it)

- The largest Muslim-majority country; partners in the OIC, D-8 and, since 2025, alongside BRICS (Indonesia is a member; Pakistan is not).
- Preferential Trade Agreement (2012); Pakistan imports palm oil and exports rice, citrus and textiles; trade about $4bn, heavily in Indonesia's favour **[verify]**.
- President Prabowo visited Pakistan in December 2025 **[verify]**; defence talks, including interest in JF-17 aircraft, were reported **[verify]**.
- Indonesia offered troops for the Gaza stabilisation force.

## 6. Way forward

1. Define the pacts publicly as **defensive**; seek parliamentary debate and oversight.
2. Reassure Iran through dialogue; propose regional security talks that include Tehran.
3. Convert goodwill into investment: bankable projects, dispute resolution, refinery and mining deals.
4. Upgrade labour exports: skills certification for Vision 2030 sectors; protect workers' rights.
5. Finish the GCC free trade agreement; raise non-traditional exports (food, IT, construction services).
6. Defence industry: joint production with Saudi finance and Turkish technology.
7. With Indonesia: move from a PTA to a comprehensive agreement; cooperation on palm-oil pricing, halal industry and defence.
