## 1. Background

- **Kashmir dispute.** At partition the Hindu ruler of Muslim-majority Jammu and Kashmir acceded to India (October 1947). War followed. UN Security Council Resolution 47 (1948) and UNCIP resolutions called for a plebiscite. A ceasefire line (1949) became the Line of Control (LoC, about 740 km) under the Simla Agreement (1972).
- **Wars and crises:** 1947–48, 1965, 1971, Siachen (1984), Kargil (1999), 2001–02 standoff, Mumbai (2008), Uri (2016), Pulwama–Balakot (2019).
- **5 August 2019.** India revoked Article 370 and 35A and split the state into two union territories. Pakistan downgraded relations and suspended trade. India's Supreme Court upheld the move in December 2023. An elected assembly returned in October 2024 but statehood has not been restored **[verify]**.
- **Positions.** Pakistan: Kashmir is an international dispute to be settled under UN resolutions and Kashmiri wishes. India: it is an integral part of India; the only issue is "cross-border terrorism" and Pakistani-held territory; all matters are bilateral (Simla).

## 2. The May 2025 conflict

| Date (2025) | Event |
|---|---|
| 22 April | Gunmen killed 26 civilians, mostly tourists, at Pahalgam. India blamed Pakistan-based Lashkar-e-Taiba through "The Resistance Front". Pakistan denied involvement and offered a neutral inquiry. |
| 23–24 April | India held the Indus Waters Treaty "in abeyance", closed the Attari crossing and cut diplomatic staff. Pakistan closed its airspace to Indian airlines, suspended trade and said it could hold the Simla Agreement in abeyance. |
| 7 May | India's "Operation Sindoor": missile and air strikes on nine sites in Punjab and AJK that it called terrorist infrastructure. Pakistan reported civilian deaths and said it shot down several Indian aircraft, including Rafales. India later accepted it lost aircraft without giving numbers **[verify wording]**. |
| 8–9 May | Drone and artillery exchanges along the LoC and border. |
| 10 May | India struck Pakistani air bases; Pakistan launched "Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos" against Indian bases. A ceasefire took effect that afternoon. President Trump announced it and claimed mediation; India says it was agreed directly between the two military operations chiefs. |

Sources: [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/what-led-recent-crisis-between-india-and-pakistan); [USIP](https://www.usip.org/india-pakistan/); [Dawn timeline](https://www.dawn.com/news/1910509); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_conflict).

**Why it mattered (analysis).**
1. The first conflict between nuclear states fought mainly with stand-off missiles, drones and beyond-visual-range air combat, across the international border, not only the LoC.
2. India declared a "new normal": a major terror attack will be treated as an act of war, with no distinction between militants and their state sponsors.
3. Pakistan drew confidence from its air performance with Chinese-made J-10C fighters and PL-15 missiles. General Asim Munir was made Field Marshal.
4. The US re-entered as crisis manager, which India resents and Pakistan welcomes.
5. Water became a front (file `10` of the domestic folder).

## 4. Challenges

1. No crisis-management channel beyond the military hotline; any new attack could trigger faster, larger strikes.
2. Shrinking decision time: drones, cruise and hypersonic missiles, strikes near nuclear command sites.
3. Water insecurity and the legal stalemate.
4. Kashmir: demographic and legal changes since 2019; no political process.
5. Domestic politics on both sides rewards hard lines; media jingoism.
6. Proxy accusations: India on Lashkar and Jaish; Pakistan on Indian support for the BLA and TTP.
7. Zero trade (potential estimated by the World Bank at $37bn) and no people-to-people contact.
8. Third parties: US mediation that India rejects; China's backing of Pakistan; India's closer defence ties with the US, France and Israel.

## 5. Way forward

1. **Crisis stability first:** restore high commissioners; a dedicated NSA or political hotline; reaffirm the 2021 LoC ceasefire; extend the 1988 non-attack agreement on nuclear facilities to cover command sites; pre-notification of cruise missile tests and drone rules.
2. **Water:** use the award to seek technical talks on data-sharing; involve the World Bank.
3. **Terrorism:** verifiable action against proscribed groups under Pakistan's own law and FATF commitments; a joint or neutral investigation mechanism for attacks.
4. **Kashmir:** keep the legal position; press for restoration of statehood and rights; revive the 2004–07 back-channel ideas (soft borders, self-governance, demilitarisation) when conditions allow.
5. **Low-politics openings:** Kartarpur, pilgrim visas, prisoners and fishermen, cricket at neutral venues, smog and flood cooperation.
6. **Trade by stages,** starting with medicines and farm goods.
7. **Use multilateral rooms** (SCO chair in 2027) without expecting them to solve bilateral issues.
