## 1. Background

- **The border.** The Durand Line (1893, 2,640 km) was drawn between British India and Amir Abdur Rahman. No Afghan government, including the Taliban, has formally accepted it. Pakistan regards it as a settled international border and has fenced most of it.
- **History.** Afghanistan opposed Pakistan's UN entry (1947) and backed "Pakhtunistan". Pakistan hosted the anti-Soviet jihad (1979–89), recognised the first Taliban government (1996–2001), then joined the US-led war (2001) while being accused of sheltering the Taliban. The US–Taliban Doha Agreement (29 February 2020) led to the US exit and the Taliban's return on 15 August 2021.
- **The reversal.** Islamabad expected a friendly Kabul. Instead TTP attacks from Afghan soil rose sharply. The Afghan Taliban and the TTP share ideology, history and tribal ties, and the TTP has pledged allegiance to the Taliban's leader. Kabul says the TTP is Pakistan's internal problem and denies giving sanctuary.
- **Recognition.** Russia became the first state to recognise the Taliban government (July 2025). Pakistan raised its mission to ambassador level in 2025 without formal recognition **[verify]**.

## 4. Why air strikes have not ended attacks (analysis)

1. The Taliban cannot or will not act against ideological kin; doing so risks defections to ISKP.
2. The TTP is dispersed, mobile and partly based inside Pakistan.
3. Civilian casualties harden Afghan opinion and help recruitment.
4. Closing the border hurts Pashtun traders on both sides and Pakistan's own exports (Afghanistan was a market of over $1bn and the route to Central Asia).
5. Kabul has options: India reopened its embassy in Kabul in 2025 **[verify]**; Russia recognised it; China, Iran and Central Asia trade with it.
6. Terrorism in Pakistan in 2026 stayed at decade-high levels despite the campaign (domestic file `15`).

## 5. Other issues

- **Trade and transit:** the Afghanistan–Pakistan Transit Trade Agreement (2010); Afghan trade shifting to Iran's Chabahar and Central Asian routes.
- **Refugees:** over 2.6 million returns since September 2023 (domestic file `16`).
- **Water:** no treaty on the Kabul and Kunar rivers; the Taliban announced dam plans on the Kunar in 2025 **[verify]**.
- **Connectivity:** Uzbekistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan railway (feasibility agreed 17 July 2025), CASA-1000, TAPI, CPEC extension: all stalled by the conflict.

## 6. Way forward

1. A verified security mechanism under third-party guarantee (China, Qatar, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia): joint monitoring, relocation of TTP fighters away from the border, intelligence-sharing.
2. Calibrated use of force that avoids cities and civilians; no open-ended campaign.
3. Keep trade corridors open and separate them from security disputes.
4. Regional pressure through the SCO (Pakistan chairs in 2026–27), the Moscow Format and neighbours' meetings; a common position on terrorism in exchange for engagement and aid.
5. Fix the home front: policing, the merged districts, political consensus with KP (domestic file `15`).
6. Humane, phased refugee policy.
7. Long term: a border-management treaty in practice even without formal recognition of the Line; people-to-people and tribal links.
