## 1. Background

- **The five republics:** Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan. Independent since 1991. Landlocked (Uzbekistan is doubly landlocked). Rich in gas (Turkmenistan), oil and uranium (Kazakhstan), hydropower (Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan), cotton and gold (Uzbekistan).
- **Pakistan's offer:** the shortest route to warm water. Karachi and Gwadar are about 2,700 km from Tashkent, closer than Bandar Abbas by some routes and far closer than Black Sea or Baltic ports.
- **Pakistan's interest:** energy imports, export markets of about 80 million people, transit income, and strategic depth in the economic sense.
- **Policy:** "Vision Central Asia" (2021) with five strands: political, trade and investment, energy and connectivity, security and defence, people-to-people.
- **The barrier:** every direct route crosses Afghanistan. The Wakhan corridor separates Pakistan from Tajikistan by as little as 16 km.

## 3. Current situation (November 2026)

- **The Afghanistan war is the main setback.** Closures at Torkham and Chaman from October 2025 and the fighting from February 2026 stopped most transit. Central Asian states are shifting cargo to Iran (Chabahar, Bandar Abbas) and the Caspian "Middle Corridor".
- **Kazakhstan** offered to finance a connecting railway in January 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan%E2%80%93Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan_Railway_Project)).
- **SCO:** the Bishkek summit (31 August–1 September 2026) handed the chair to Pakistan, which hosts the 2027 summit ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2626913/1)).
- **Great-power competition:** Russia's hold has loosened since 2022; China is the largest trader and builder (China–Kyrgyzstan–Uzbekistan railway under construction); the US held a C5+1 leaders' meeting in Washington in November 2025 with a focus on critical minerals **[verify]**; Türkiye works through the Organisation of Turkic States; the EU through its Global Gateway.
- **Azerbaijan** (not Central Asian but linked): investment pledges of $2bn and defence purchases (JF-17) **[verify]**.

## 4. Challenges

1. Insecurity and politics in Afghanistan; no recognised government to sign financing with.
2. Finance: who lends billions for a railway through a war zone?
3. Technical: gauge breaks, mountains (Salang), customs procedures, visas and banking.
4. Pakistan's own weaknesses: slow ports and railways, security in KP and Balochistan.
5. Competing corridors: Chabahar/INSTC, the Middle Corridor, China–Central Asia rail.
6. Small trade base and similar exports (cotton, textiles).

## 5. Way forward

1. Peace with Kabul is the precondition (file `02`); keep transit separate from security disputes where possible.
2. Use alternatives now: the Karakoram route through China; sea–land routes through Iran; air cargo.
3. Finish CASA-1000 and negotiate winter back-flow of Pakistan's surplus power.
4. Phase the UAP railway; seek a consortium (Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, China, Qatar, multilateral banks).
5. Trade facilitation: banking channels, visa-on-arrival for business, TIR and digital customs, Gwadar and Karachi dry-port links.
6. Use the SCO chair (2026–27) and ECO for a connectivity agenda.
7. Soft power: scholarships, medical tourism, direct flights, religious tourism.
