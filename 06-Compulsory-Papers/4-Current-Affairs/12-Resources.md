# Resources — The Few Worth Your Time

> **As of:** 23 November 2026. You study about 15 hours a week across all papers, alone and outside Pakistan. This list is deliberately short. Everything on it is available online or as an e-book.
> **Principle:** One good source read actively beats five skimmed. For this paper, a daily newspaper plus this pack plus two or three books is enough.
> Book details are from memory of standard editions. Check the latest edition before buying.

---

## 1. The official documents (read once, free)

| Document | Where | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| **FPSC syllabus for CE-2016 onwards** | [fpsc.gov.pk](https://fpsc.gov.pk/assets/media/2024-10-22-01-45-26-Syllabus_for_CE-2016_and_onwards.pdf) | Read the Current Affairs page and the recommended reading list. Tick off `01-Syllabus-Map.md` against it. |
| **FPSC past papers** | fpsc.gov.pk → Past Papers; mirror: [cssprep.com.pk](https://cssprep.com.pk/css-current-affairs-past-papers-desc/) | Read the real wording of every question from 2016. Note the command words (critically evaluate, discuss, analyse). |
| **FPSC examiner reports** | fpsc.gov.pk → Examiners' Reports (annual) | Read the General Knowledge-II paragraphs for the last five years. They tell you what loses marks. |
| **Pakistan Economic Survey** (each June) | finance.gov.pk | Read the Overview and Executive Summary (about 20 pages). Take numbers for `08`. |
| **Budget in Brief** (each June) | finance.gov.pk | Two pages of tables: outlay, revenue, debt service, defence, development. |
| **Constitution of Pakistan** | na.gov.pk | Keep the PDF. Read the Articles cited in this pack, and the text of the 26th and 27th Amendments. |

## 2. Daily and weekly reading

| Source | Why | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Dawn** (dawn.com; e-paper) | The standard of record for CSS. Balanced editorials. | 20 minutes a day as in `10-Keeping-It-Current.md`. Editorial + one op-ed. Monday's Business & Finance section for the economy. |
| **The Express Tribune / The News / Business Recorder** | Second opinion; Business Recorder for economic data | Only when you need more on a story. |
| **Al Jazeera English** | Clear explainers on the Muslim world, South Asia and the 2026 wars | Read the explainer on each major event. |
| **BBC / Reuters** | Neutral reporting and timelines | For checking facts and dates. |
| **The Economist** (weekly; "The world this week") | Two-page global summary; strong on economics | Weekly hour. You do not need the whole magazine. |
| **The Diplomat** (thediplomat.com) | South and Central Asia analysis | One article a week on a pack topic. |

## 3. Books: the core five

Read these in this order during February–August 2027. One chapter a week is enough.

| # | Book | Why this one | How to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Joshua Goldstein and Jon Pevehouse, *International Relations*** (on the FPSC list) or **John Baylis, Steve Smith and Patricia Owens, *The Globalization of World Politics*** | A beginner's textbook for someone with no IR background. Either is fine; do not read both. | Read the chapters on theories, security, international organisations, political economy and environment. Skip the rest. Make a one-page summary per chapter. |
| 2 | **Abdul Sattar, *Pakistan's Foreign Policy 1947–2019: A Concise History*** (latest edition; on the FPSC list) | The standard short history by a former foreign minister. | Read fully once. It supplies the "historical analysis" examiners miss. Compare with `03-Foundations/03`. |
| 3 | **Ishrat Husain, *Governing the Ungovernable* (2018)** | The clearest account of why Pakistan's institutions under-perform, with reform proposals. | Read the chapters on the economy, civil service, judiciary, federalism and local government. Borrow its recommendations for "way forward" sections. |
| 4 | **Tim Marshall, *Prisoners of Geography* (2015)** | Short, readable geopolitics with maps. It was the first MCQ in the 2026 paper. | Read the chapters on China, the Middle East, India and Pakistan, Russia and the Arctic. Practise the maps. |
| 5 | **Maleeha Lodhi (ed.), *Pakistan: Search for Stability* (2024)** (or her earlier *Pakistan: Beyond the Crisis State*, 2011) | Essays by Pakistani experts on governance, economy, security, foreign policy and education. | One essay per topic alongside the matching backgrounder. Note two arguments and one quotation in your own words from each. |

## 5. Journals and think tanks (one piece a month)

| Source | What it offers | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Institute of Strategic Studies Islamabad (ISSI)**, issi.org.pk | Short "Issue Briefs" on current foreign-policy events; the journal *Strategic Studies* | Search the site for your topic before writing a practice answer. |
| **Islamabad Policy Research Institute (IPRI)**; **Institute of Regional Studies** | Policy briefs on the region | As above. |
| **Tabadlab**; **PIDE**; **SDPI** | Economy, governance, climate, energy | Data and reform ideas. |
| **PICSS / CRSS / PIPS** | Monthly and annual security statistics | Numbers for terrorism answers. |
| **International Crisis Group** (crisisgroup.org) | Balanced reports on Pakistan, Afghanistan, Kashmir, the Middle East | Read executive summaries and recommendations. |
| **Carnegie, Brookings, CSIS, Chatham House, IISS, Stimson** | Western policy analysis; Stimson's South Asia programme is strong on nuclear issues | One article on a pack topic; note the argument and one fact. |
| ***Foreign Affairs* / *Foreign Policy*** | Long-form debate on world order | One article a month. A few free articles are available monthly. |
| **SIPRI, IMF, World Bank, UNDP, UNHCR, IEA, Germanwatch, IEP** | The annual indices | Summaries only; take the Pakistan line and the global headline. |

## 6. Monthly CSS magazines (use with care)

| Magazine | Good for | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| *Jahangir's World Times* (JWT) | Month-by-month timelines, MCQs, sample essays | Quality varies; essays are often descriptive. Use for dates and MCQs, not as model answers. |
| *CSS Digest*, *Contemporary Affairs* (Imtiaz Shahid) | Compilations of op-eds by topic | Many pieces are reprinted and dated. Check every figure. |

The Express Tribune noted in 2026 that candidates rely too much on outdated guidebooks. Treat any guidebook as a list of topics, never as a source of facts.

## 8. Audio and video (for commutes)

- **Podcasts:** BBC *Global News Podcast* (daily, 30 minutes); The Economist *The Intelligence*; *The Pakistan Experience* and *Uncensored* style long interviews for domestic debates (listen critically); Dawn News English bulletins.
- **Explainer channels:** Al Jazeera *Start Here*; Vox; CaspianReport (geopolitics with maps); DW News.
- Use them to build background and vocabulary. Do not count them as study hours for writing.

## 9. How to combine everything (per topic)

1. Read the backgrounder in this pack (15 minutes).
2. Read one outside piece: an ISSI brief, a Dawn op-ed or a book chapter (20 minutes).
3. Add two updates and one new argument to the backgrounder (10 minutes).
4. Close everything and write the outline from memory (10 minutes).
5. Every third topic, write the full answer by hand in 35 minutes and compare it with `07-Model-Answers.md`.

## 10. What not to do

- Do not buy a shelf of guidebooks.
- Do not read three newspapers a day.
- Do not memorise other people's essays.
- Do not rely on social media for facts.
- Do not cite a statistic without a year and a source.
