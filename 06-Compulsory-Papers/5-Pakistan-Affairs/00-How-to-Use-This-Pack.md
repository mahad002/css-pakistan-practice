### What the paper really tests

The syllabus begins with ideology and history, but the papers have moved. From 2016 to 2026 only about 15% of questions were on pre-1947 history. About 85% were on post-1947 politics, the constitution, the economy, society, security and foreign relations. See `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`.

This means:

1. You cannot pass on history alone. Most years have only one pre-1947 question.
2. You can pass comfortably with **one history question + three contemporary questions**, if the contemporary ones are written with facts and structure.
3. This paper overlaps heavily with Current Affairs and the Essay. Work done here pays three times.

## 2. How answers are marked

FPSC does not publish a marking scheme. What follows comes from examiner comments quoted in the press, FPSC annual report extracts, and the consistent advice of people who scored well. Treat it as strong guidance, not rules.

Examiners have repeatedly said that candidates:

- did not answer the question asked, and wrote a prepared answer on the topic instead;
- showed little conceptual grasp and relied on guidebooks ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2584842/why-css-candidates-fail-the-exam); FPSC 2014 report as quoted in the press);
- wrote without logical order or evidence;
- made basic language and spelling errors.

Marks in this paper are tight. A 20-mark answer that gets 11–12 is a good answer. 13–14 is excellent. Four answers at 11 plus 14/20 in MCQs gives 58. That is a strong score. In 2018 about 89% of candidates failed this paper ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2021/09/10/89-of-css-candidates-failed-in-pakistan-affairs/)), so do not expect generous marking.

What earns marks, in order of weight:

| What the examiner looks for | How you show it |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Every heading answers a part of the question. Use the question's own words in your headings |
| Argument | A clear position in the introduction. Each section pushes it forward |
| Evidence | Dates, names, article numbers, figures, events. Two or three per section |
| Analysis | "Why" and "so what", not only "what happened" |
| Balance | For contested issues, give the main views, then your judgement |
| References | Two to four scholars, reports or documents, used accurately |
| Presentation | Outline at the start, headings, short paragraphs, a map or simple diagram where it helps |
| Conclusion | A judgement and, for current issues, a way forward |

## 3. The answer template that scores

Use this for every 20-mark question.

**Step 0 — Read the question twice (2 minutes).** Underline the command word (discuss, critically evaluate, analyse, compare) and each part. If a question has two parts, give both parts space. Jot a six-line outline in the margin.

**1. Outline (optional but useful, 5–6 lines).** A numbered list of your headings. It tells the examiner your answer is organised before they read it.

**2. Introduction (80–120 words).** Three moves:
- One sentence of context.
- One or two sentences that define the key term or frame the issue.
- **A thesis sentence** that directly answers the question.

**3. Background (100–150 words).** Only what is needed. Dates in order. Do not retell the whole history.

**4. Main body (600–750 words, 5–8 headings).** Each heading is a claim, not a label. Write "Congress rule proved the minority's fears" rather than "Congress ministries". Under each:
- the point in one sentence;
- evidence (date, name, figure, article);
- one sentence of analysis that ties it back to the question.

**5. Critical analysis / counter-view (100–150 words).** Where scholars disagree, say so. Example: on the Lahore Resolution, set the traditional view (I.H. Qureshi) beside Ayesha Jalal's argument that Jinnah used the demand as a bargaining position.

**6. Way forward (for contemporary questions, 80–120 words).** Four to six specific measures. Name the institution that would act (CCI, NFC, Parliament, NACTA, NEPRA).

**7. Conclusion (60–80 words).** Restate your judgement in fresh words. End on a forward-looking line. No new facts.

### Length and time

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| MCQs | 30 min (fixed) |
| Choose four questions and outline them | 8–10 min |
| Each answer | 33–35 min |
| Final check | 5 min |

In 33–35 minutes most people can hand-write **900–1,100 words**, about 6–8 pages. The model answers in `05-Model-Answers.md` run to about 1,100–1,200 words so that you see the full material. In the exam, aim for about 1,000 words. **Four complete answers beat three long ones and one rushed one.** Stop each answer on time.

### Using references honestly

- Use a quotation only when you are sure of the wording and the author. A wrong quotation costs more than no quotation.
- Paraphrase with the name: "Ayesha Jalal argues that…", "According to the Economic Survey 2025–26…".
- Primary documents count as references: the Lahore Resolution, the Objectives Resolution, Article 153, UNSC Resolution 47, the Indus Waters Treaty.
- `07-Personalities-and-Quotes.md` lists what is safe to use.

### Diagrams and maps

A rough map earns goodwill if it is correct and relevant: the Indus river system for water questions, the Kashmir map with the LoC, the CPEC routes, a flowchart of how a bill becomes a constitutional amendment. Keep it small. Label it. Do not draw one if it adds nothing.

## 4. Common mistakes

1. **Writing the topic, not the question.** "Critically evaluate the 18th Amendment's effect on health and education" is not "write all you know about the 18th Amendment".
2. **All narrative, no analysis.** Three pages of dates with no "therefore".
3. **Out-of-date facts.** Citing last year's Economic Survey, or writing about the 26th Amendment as the latest when the 27th has passed.
4. **One-sided writing on contested events** (1971, martial laws, civil–military relations, Kashmir). Be factual and fair. Criticise policies, not institutions or people.
5. **Invented quotations and statistics.** Examiners know the standard ones.
6. **No headings**, or headings that are single words.
7. **Unequal answers.** Ten pages on the first answer, three on the fourth.
8. **Ignoring the second half of a two-part question.**
9. **Skipping MCQs preparation.** Twenty marks are the easiest marks in the paper. Aim for 14+.
10. **Choosing "popular" questions only.** A less popular question you know well is often marked more kindly because fewer weak scripts sit beside yours.

## 5. How to study from this folder

You start in February 2027 with about 15 hours a week across all subjects. Give this paper about 3 hours a week for ten months, then more in the last eight weeks.

| Phase | When | What to do with this pack |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | Feb–Apr 2027 | Read `03-Notes-History/` in order, one file a week. After each file, do the matching MCQs in `08-MCQ-Bank.md` and write one 20-minute outline from "likely questions" |
| 2. State and constitution | May–Jun 2027 | History files 09–12, then contemporary files 04, 09, 19, 21. Learn the amendments table by heart |
| 3. Contemporary | Jul–Sep 2027 | The rest of `04-Notes-Contemporary/`, two a week. Update each "current position" section yourself from Dawn |
| 4. MPT (screening) | Sep–Oct 2027 | `06-Timeline-and-Dates.md`, `07-Personalities-and-Quotes.md`, full `08-MCQ-Bank.md`. Pakistan Affairs is about 20% of the MPT |
| 5. Writing | Nov 2027–Jan 2028 | One timed answer a week at first, then two. Mark it against the template above. Read the model answers after you write, not before |
| 6. Final month | Jan–Feb 2028 | `09-Quick-Revision.md` daily. Refresh figures from the Economic Survey 2026–27 and Budget 2027–28. Check what Parliament and the courts did in 2027 |

### Keeping it current

This pack is frozen at 23 Nov 2026. The written exam is in February 2028. Before the exam you must update:

- the latest Economic Survey (June 2027) and budget;
- any constitutional amendment after the 27th (a 28th was being discussed in Nov 2026 but had not been tabled);
- Pakistan–Afghanistan, Pakistan–India, the Iran war and Gaza;
- the political situation (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa governor's rule, PTI cases, elections).

A simple habit: every Sunday, read the Dawn editorial page and add two or three lines to the "current position" section of the relevant file.
