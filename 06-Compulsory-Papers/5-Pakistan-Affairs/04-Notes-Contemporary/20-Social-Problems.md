## A. Poverty

### Facts

- **National poverty rate: 28.9%** in the Economic Survey 2025–26 — a rise, and the first new official estimate in several years ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/06/12/pakistan-economic-survey-2025-26-gdp-at-four-year-high-poverty-climbs-to-289percent)).
- By the World Bank's higher international lines the share is far larger (over two-fifths).
- **Multidimensional poverty** (health, education, living standards) is higher than income poverty, and far higher in rural areas, Balochistan, the merged districts and rural Sindh.
- **Unemployment 7.1%**, the highest in about two decades; much higher for young graduates and women.
- Inequality: wide gaps between provinces, districts, and urban and rural areas.

### Causes

1. Slow growth that barely beats population growth; stabilisation without jobs.
2. The inflation shock of 2022–23 (food prices), which wiped out real wages.
3. Floods (2022, 2025) that destroyed homes, crops and livestock.
4. Low human capital: illiteracy, poor health, large families.
5. Unequal land and assets; informal, insecure work.
6. Regressive taxes and high energy prices.
7. Weak local government and services.
8. Conflict and displacement in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan.
9. Low female participation in paid work.

### Strategies

- **Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP, 2008):** unconditional cash to about ten million poor families, paid to women; conditional transfers for schooling (*Taleemi Wazaif*) and nutrition (*Nashonuma*). Its budget has grown each year under the IMF programme.
- **Ehsaas (2019–22)** umbrella; **Pakistan Poverty Alleviation Fund**; microfinance (Akhuwat, Kashf, Khushhali); provincial programmes; zakat and Bait-ul-Mal; the Prime Minister's youth schemes; minimum wage laws.
- **What is missing:** jobs. Cash transfers cushion poverty; only growth in labour-using sectors (agriculture, construction, small manufacturing, IT), skills and women's work reduce it.

## B. Education

See `13-Land-and-People.md` section 7 for facts and the HEC. Summary for a social-problems answer:

- Literacy about 61–63%; women about 53%; Balochistan far lower.
- **About 25–26 million children out of school.**
- **Spending about 0.8% of GDP** — a record low ([Geo](https://www.geo.tv/latest/668356-pakistans-poverty-rises-education-spending-drops-to-record-low-survey)).
- Learning poverty: about three in four ten-year-olds cannot read and understand a simple text (World Bank).
- Three parallel systems; weak technical training.

**Strategies:** Art. 25A laws in every province; stipends for girls; public–private partnerships (education foundations); school meals; teacher recruitment on merit and monitoring by data; a declared "education emergency" (May 2024); second-shift and non-formal schools; technology for teaching. **Needed:** spend 4% of GDP; put every out-of-school child's nearest school in working order; foundational literacy; skills.

## C. Health

### Facts

- Public spending about **0.8–1% of GDP**; most health spending is out of pocket.
- About **40% of children under five are stunted**; widespread anaemia among women.
- Infant and maternal death rates among the highest in South Asia.
- **Polio:** Pakistan and Afghanistan are the last two endemic countries; cases jumped in 2024 and fell again in 2025 **[verify 2026 count]**.
- High burdens of hepatitis C, tuberculosis and diabetes; dengue and malaria outbreaks after floods; a growing mental-health load.
- Shortages of nurses and of doctors in rural areas, while trained doctors emigrate.
- Unsafe water causes a large share of illness.

### Causes

Low spending; neglect of primary and preventive care in favour of big hospitals; population growth; poverty and malnutrition; weak regulation of private care and medicines; fragmented responsibilities after devolution; attacks on and refusal of vaccinators.

### Strategies

- The **Lady Health Worker** programme (1994): about 100,000 workers at the doorstep.
- Expanded Programme on Immunisation; the polio emergency operations centres.
- **Sehat Sahulat / health cards:** state-paid hospital insurance, universal in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; revised in Punjab.
- Basic Health Units and Rural Health Centres; public–private management in Sindh and Punjab.
- The Drug Regulatory Authority (2012); hepatitis elimination programme (2024).
- **Needed:** double spending; primary care and nutrition first; family planning as a health service; clean water; a national health data system; fair pay to keep staff.

## D. Sanitation and water

- Open defecation has fallen sharply over two decades but persists in rural Sindh, Balochistan and south Punjab.
- Only a minority of households have *safely managed* sanitation: most sewage is untreated and flows into rivers, canals and the sea.
- A large share of drinking-water sources is contaminated (bacteria, arsenic).
- Solid waste: big cities collect little more than half; open dumping and burning.
- Consequences: diarrhoea (a leading killer of children), stunting, typhoid (including drug-resistant strains), hepatitis A and E; girls dropping out of schools without toilets.

**Strategies:** the National Sanitation Policy (2006) and Drinking Water Policy (2009); "Clean Green Pakistan"; the Pakistan Approach to Total Sanitation (community-led); provincial WASH plans; waste-management companies; filtration plants. **Needed:** empowered local governments with funds (sanitation is a municipal job); sewage treatment in every city; water testing; behaviour change through schools and mosques.

## E. Climate change and global warming (2023 special, 2024)

### Exposure

- Pakistan emits **under 1%** of global greenhouse gases and is consistently ranked among the countries most harmed by climate disasters.
- **Floods:** 2010 (20 million affected); **2022** (about 1,700 dead, 33 million affected, about $30 billion in damage and losses); **2025** (around a thousand dead; damage about Rs 822 billion; severe in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Punjab).
- **Heat:** temperatures near or above 50°C in Sindh and south Punjab; lost working hours and crop yields.
- **Glaciers:** over 7,000 in the north; thousands of glacial lakes, dozens judged dangerous; outburst floods.
- **Water and food:** shifting monsoons; falling yields of wheat and cotton; sea intrusion in the Indus delta.
- **Air:** winter smog in Lahore and central Punjab among the world's worst.

### Effects on Pakistan

Economy (repeated reconstruction; lost growth), agriculture and food prices, health, displacement and urban migration, energy (hydropower variability, cooling demand), inter-provincial water disputes, and security.

### Policy so far

- National Climate Change Policy (2012, updated 2021); **Climate Change Act (2017)**; National Adaptation Plan (2023).
- Pledge (2021): cut projected emissions by 50% by 2030 (15% unconditionally, the rest with finance); 60% renewable power and 30% electric vehicles by 2030.
- Ten Billion Tree programme; Living Indus; Recharge Pakistan; protected areas.
- Diplomacy: as chair of the G-77 in 2022 Pakistan led the push for the **Loss and Damage Fund** agreed at COP27.
- Finance: Geneva pledges of about $9–10 billion after the 2022 floods (mostly loans, slowly disbursed); the IMF's **RSF** (2025); green bonds.
- **Art. 9A** (2024): right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment.

### A development model that protects the environment (2024)

1. **Adaptation first:** flood-plain zoning, early warning, resilient housing and roads, restored wetlands and mangroves.
2. **Water:** storage, groundwater recharge, efficient irrigation.
3. **Climate-smart agriculture:** heat- and flood-tolerant seed, crop insurance, changed crop calendars.
4. **Clean energy:** solar, wind, hydro, with a grid that can use them; phase down imported coal and oil.
5. **Cities:** public transport, electric two- and three-wheelers, building codes, waste management, urban forests.
6. **Industry:** efficiency and compliance with export markets' carbon rules (the EU's border carbon levy).
7. **Finance:** carbon markets, green bonds, debt-for-nature swaps, and a firm claim on international climate funds.
8. **Institutions:** provincial and district climate plans; empowered local government; a functioning Climate Change Authority.
9. **Public awareness** and climate education.

## F. The media (2025)

### Growth

State television alone until 2002; then PEMRA licensed private channels — now around a hundred; hundreds of FM stations; over a hundred million internet users and tens of millions on social media.

### Asset

- Informs and educates; gives voice to citizens and regions.
- Holds power to account: the Lawyers' Movement, exposure of corruption, coverage of disasters (earthquake 2005, floods).
- Supports elections and debate; promotes culture and languages.
- Mobilises relief and national solidarity (May 2025).

### Liability

- Sensationalism and the ratings race; trivial "breaking news".
- Partisanship: channels aligned with parties and business interests.
- Disinformation and hate speech, multiplied by social media and now by AI-generated content.
- Trial by media; invasion of privacy; neglect of rural, women's and development issues.
- Pressure and censorship: direct and indirect controls, advertising leverage, violence against journalists; Pakistan ranks low on press-freedom indices.
- The 2025 PECA amendments and internet shutdowns narrowed online space.

### Effect on politics and opinion in this century

Shaped the fall of Musharraf; built and broke political reputations; turned politics into a daily contest of narratives; lately moved the main arena from television to phones, where parties, institutions and anonymous networks compete without editors.

### Way forward

Self-regulation with an independent complaints body; transparent ownership and advertising rules; safety and fair pay for journalists; media literacy in schools; fact-checking; regulation of platforms that protects against incitement without criminalising criticism; a strong public broadcaster.

## G. Other social problems (one line each)

Gender inequality (Pakistan has ranked at or near the bottom of the Global Gender Gap Index); child labour and street children; drug abuse; violence against women and children; sectarian and mob violence; unplanned urbanisation and housing shortage; youth unemployment and emigration of skills.

## H. Common roots and a common strategy

**Roots:** low public spending on people; population growth; weak local government; elite-centred policy; instability; climate shocks.

**Strategy:** treat human development as national security (National Security Policy 2022); finance it through a reformed NFC with conditional grants; empower local governments; use BISP's database to target services; partner with the private and non-profit sectors; measure results openly.
