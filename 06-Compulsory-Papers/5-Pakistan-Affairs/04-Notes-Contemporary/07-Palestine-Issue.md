# 7. The Palestine Issue

Syllabus item X. **Not asked since the 2016 syllabus began.** Pakistan's direct involvement since 2025 (the Security Council seat, the Board of Peace, the stabilisation-force debate) makes it a likely question for 2028. It is also a Current Affairs and Essay topic.

By the October 2025 ceasefire Gaza's health authorities had recorded more than 67,000 Palestinians killed. Most of Gaza's housing and infrastructure was destroyed and nearly the whole population displaced.

## 2. The core issues

1. **Borders and statehood:** a Palestinian state on the 1967 lines.
2. **Jerusalem (Al-Quds):** claimed by both as capital; Al-Aqsa.
3. **Refugees:** about 6 million registered with UNRWA; right of return.
4. **Settlements:** some 700,000 Israeli settlers in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, illegal under international law (UNSC Resolution 2334, 2016).
5. **Security:** Israel's demands; Palestinian demands for an end to occupation and blockade.
6. **Gaza's governance and reconstruction;** disarmament of Hamas.
7. **Palestinian division** between Fatah (West Bank) and Hamas (Gaza).

## 3. Pakistan's position

### History

- **Before 1947:** the Muslim League opposed the Balfour Declaration and passed resolutions for Palestine, including at the **Lahore session of March 1940**. Jinnah wrote to British and American leaders against partition of Palestine. Iqbal spoke for the Palestinian cause.
- **1947:** Sir **Zafarullah Khan** led the case against partition at the UN.
- Pakistan has **never recognised Israel**; its passports are marked not valid for Israel.
- Pakistani pilots flew for Arab air forces in 1967 and 1973.
- **1974:** the Lahore Islamic Summit recognised the PLO as the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinians. Pakistan recognised the State of Palestine in 1988.
- Consistent votes for Palestine at the UN and OIC; humanitarian aid; scholarships for Palestinian students.

### Stated policy

A sovereign, viable and contiguous State of Palestine on the **pre-June 1967 borders with Al-Quds Al-Sharif as its capital**, in line with UN and OIC resolutions; no recognition of Israel before that.

### Why Pakistan holds this line

- **Ideology and public opinion:** solidarity with the Muslim ummah; overwhelming public feeling.
- **Principle:** self-determination under UN resolutions — the same principle Pakistan invokes on **Kashmir**. Abandoning it in one case weakens it in the other.
- **Founders' legacy.**
- **Relations with the Arab and wider Muslim world.**
- **Strategic concern** about Israel–India defence cooperation.

### The recognition debate

- *For engagement (a minority view):* Arab states have normalised; possible economic, technological and diplomatic gains; influence in Washington.
- *Against:* principle and the Kashmir parallel; public opposition; no gain for Palestinians; Israel's conduct in Gaza.
- After 2023 the debate is effectively closed. Saudi Arabia, the key state for Pakistan, ties normalisation to a credible path to Palestinian statehood.

## 4. Current position (to 23 Nov 2026)

**In Gaza**

- The ceasefire of 10 October 2025 holds on paper. On 18 November 2026 the **Board of Peace reported to the Security Council that the ceasefire faced imminent collapse**: Israel and Hamas had both failed to meet commitments, and Israeli forces had begun a major operation ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/18/board-of-peace-warns-gaza-ceasefire-faces-imminent-collapse-without-action)).
- Analysts estimate Israel controls well over half of Gaza's territory; withdrawal is tied by Israel to Hamas's disarmament.
- The **International Stabilisation Force** has deployed only a small fraction of its planned 20,000 troops.
- Palestinian elections, due in late 2026, have been postponed to 2027.
- Reconstruction has barely begun; humanitarian access remains restricted.

**Pakistan's role**

- **Security Council member (2025–26):** voted for Resolution 2803 while stating reservations that it lacked a clear path to statehood; held the Council presidency in July 2025.
- One of eight Arab and Muslim states (with Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, the UAE, Qatar, Turkey and Indonesia) that worked with Washington on the September 2025 plan.
- **Joined the Board of Peace** at Davos in January 2026, describing it as a body for reconstruction and diplomacy.
- **Stabilisation force:** Pakistan has said it would consider a peacekeeping and humanitarian role only — **not disarming Hamas** — and that Parliament would decide. At the February 2026 Washington meeting it was not among the first five countries to commit troops. No deployment decision is confirmed as of November 2026 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1974390); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2633670/pakistan)).
- A Gallup Pakistan survey early in 2026 found most Pakistanis supported sending peacekeepers ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/9/survey-finds-strong-pakistani-support-for-gaza-force-but-questions-linger)). Religious parties and the opposition warn against serving an arrangement that sidelines Palestinian self-rule.
- Aid: many relief consignments through Egypt and Jordan.

## 5. Analysis

1. **The two-state solution is endorsed by almost everyone and implemented by no one.** Settlements, annexation talk and Gaza's destruction have made it physically harder. Over 150 UN members now recognise Palestine, including the UK and France among the permanent members of the Security Council — but recognition has not changed facts on the ground.
2. **The UN's weakness:** the US veto has blocked binding action for decades. General Assembly votes and ICJ opinions carry moral weight without enforcement. (The same structural point applies to Kashmir — the 2026 paper asked exactly that about Kashmir.)
3. **The Trump plan:** stopped the large-scale war and freed hostages and prisoners. But it places Gaza under an external board, postpones statehood, and depends on Hamas disarming and Israel withdrawing — neither of which has happened.
4. **Muslim-world disunity:** the OIC issues statements; states pursue separate interests.
5. **Pakistan's dilemma:** stay out and lose influence over the outcome, or take part and risk being seen as policing Palestinians for others. Its conditions (UN mandate, peacekeeping only, Palestinian consent, parliamentary approval) are the right tests.
6. **Link to Pakistan's wider position:** the issue touches relations with the USA and the Gulf, the Iran war, domestic religious politics, and the Kashmir case.

## 6. Way forward

- Hold to the principled position: statehood on 1967 lines; Al-Quds as capital.
- Work through the OIC, the Arab League, the General Assembly and the ICJ/ICC processes for accountability and a time-bound path to statehood.
- Support Palestinian unity and reform of the Palestinian Authority.
- Any troop role only under a clear UN mandate, limited to protection and relief, with Palestinian agreement and Parliament's vote.
- Sustained humanitarian and reconstruction help; training for Palestinian doctors, engineers and officials.
- Use the Board of Peace seat to press for Israeli withdrawal, open crossings and Palestinian governance.
