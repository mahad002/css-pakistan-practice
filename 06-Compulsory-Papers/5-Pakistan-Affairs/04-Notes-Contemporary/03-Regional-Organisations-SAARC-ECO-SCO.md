# 3. Regional Economic Cooperation: SAARC, ECO, SCO and Pakistan's Role

Syllabus item V. SCO asked in 2017 and 2020. SAARC and ECO have not been asked since 2016. Pakistan holds the SCO chair for 2026–27, so an SCO question is likely again.

| | SAARC | ECO | SCO |

## A. SAARC

### Background

- Proposed by Bangladesh's President Ziaur Rahman (1980). Charter signed at the first summit in Dhaka, 1985.
- Charter principles: sovereign equality, non-interference, **unanimous decisions**, and **no discussion of bilateral or contentious issues**.
- Instruments: **SAPTA** (1995); **SAFTA** (signed at the Islamabad summit, January 2004; in force 2006); SAARC Development Fund; South Asian University (New Delhi); regional centres; a food bank; visa exemption scheme.

### Record

- South Asia is one of the least integrated regions in the world: trade within the region is about **5% of its total trade** (ASEAN: about a quarter).
- Summits were meant to be yearly; only 18 have been held in forty years. The **18th was at Kathmandu in November 2014**.
- The **19th summit, due in Islamabad in November 2016**, was called off when India withdrew after the Uri attack and others followed. None has been held since.

### Why SAARC has failed

1. **India–Pakistan hostility** — the core reason.
2. **Asymmetry:** India has about three-quarters of the region's population and economy and borders almost every member; smaller states fear dominance.
3. **The charter** excludes the very disputes that block cooperation, and unanimity gives every member a veto.
4. **Similar economies** competing in the same exports; long "sensitive lists" under SAFTA; non-tariff barriers.
5. **Poor connectivity:** Pakistan has not given India transit to Afghanistan; India has restricted trade with Pakistan since 2019.
6. **India's alternatives:** BIMSTEC and sub-regional groups that leave Pakistan out.
7. **Weak secretariat and funding.**
8. **Afghanistan:** no agreed representation since the Taliban returned.

### Pakistan's role

- Founder member; hosted summits in 1988 and 2004 (SAFTA); proposed bringing China in as a member (China is an observer).
- Supports revival and offers to host the pending summit.
- Since 2025 Pakistan, China and Bangladesh have explored a three-way cooperation track; commentators have discussed it as a possible alternative to SAARC **[verify status]**.

### Current position

Frozen. After the May 2025 conflict there is no prospect of a summit. Some cooperation continues at official level (the secretariat, the Development Fund). The trade potential — the World Bank once estimated India–Pakistan trade could be many times its actual level — remains unused.

### Way forward

Separate economic work from political disputes; start with low-politics areas (climate and disasters, health, energy grids, river data); allow sub-groups of willing members; amend the charter to permit majority decisions on technical matters; reopen the Wagah–Attari trade route when politics allow.

## B. ECO

### Background

- **Regional Cooperation for Development (RCD)**, 1964: Iran, Pakistan, Turkey — all then US allies.
- Revived as ECO in 1985 under the **Treaty of Izmir** (1977, amended). Expanded in **1992** to take in Afghanistan and six ex-Soviet Muslim republics.
- Covers about 8 million sq km and over 500 million people; rich in oil, gas and minerals; most members landlocked.

### Instruments and projects

- **ECO Trade Agreement (ECOTA)**, signed at Islamabad in 2003 — still not fully operational.
- ECO Trade and Development Bank (Istanbul); ECO Vision 2025.
- Transport: the **Islamabad–Tehran–Istanbul (ITI) freight train**; the Kazakhstan–Turkmenistan–Iran railway; the TIR road-transit system.
- Energy projects linked to the region: **TAPI** gas pipeline, **CASA-1000** power line, the **Iran–Pakistan** gas pipeline (stalled by US sanctions).

### Pakistan's role

- Founder; hosted the ministerial meeting that produced the Quetta Plan of Action (1993) and summits in Islamabad in 1995 and 2017 (the 13th); offers Karachi and Gwadar as the region's ports; links ECO to CPEC.
- Recent summits: Tashkent (November 2023) and **Khankendi, Azerbaijan (July 2025)**.
- An ECO ministerial meeting was held in Islamabad in late 2026, from which Afghanistan was reported excluded amid the Pakistan–Kabul conflict **[verify]**.

### Why ECO under-performs

1. Intra-regional trade is only about **8%** of members' trade.
2. **Afghanistan's instability** blocks the land routes between South and Central Asia.
3. **Sanctions on Iran**, and since 2025–26 war on Iran, block banking, pipelines and transit.
4. Members sell the same things (energy, cotton) and look to outside markets and to Russia and China.
5. Rival frameworks: SCO, the Eurasian Economic Union, the Organisation of Turkic States.
6. Weak finance, slow ratification, poor border infrastructure.

### Way forward

Operationalise ECOTA; finish missing rail and road links; build barter and local-currency trade to work around sanctions where lawful; tie ECO corridors to CPEC and Gwadar; cooperate on energy and tourism; a joint approach to stabilising Afghanistan.

## C. SCO

### Background

- **Shanghai Five (1996):** China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan — to settle borders and build military confidence. Uzbekistan joined in 2001 and the SCO was formed.
- Charter 2002. **Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS)** at Tashkent.
- **Pakistan:** observer 2005; **full member 9 June 2017** at Astana, together with India.
- The SCO covers about 40% of the world's people and a quarter or more of world output; four nuclear powers; major energy producers and consumers.

### Why the SCO matters to Pakistan (2017, 2020)

| Field | Benefit |
|---|---|
| **Security** | Cooperation and intelligence-sharing through RATS; joint exercises ("Peace Mission"); a common stand against terrorism and separatism |
| **Energy** | Access to Central Asian and Russian oil, gas and electricity (TAPI, CASA-1000, Russian LNG and crude) |
| **Connectivity and trade** | Fits with CPEC and the Belt and Road; routes to Central Asia; the Uzbekistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan railway plan |
| **Diplomacy** | A seat with China and Russia; a channel to Moscow (ties have warmed since 2014: gas pipeline project, drills, oil purchases); a rare forum where Pakistani and Indian leaders meet |
| **Afghanistan** | The SCO–Afghanistan Contact Group; all Afghanistan's neighbours are members or partners |
| **Balance** | Reduces dependence on the West; supports a multipolar order |

### Limits

- **India–Pakistan rivalry** inside the organisation. India does not endorse Belt and Road language because CPEC passes through Gilgit-Baltistan. In 2025–26 the two traded accusations on terrorism at SCO meetings.
- The charter bars bilateral disputes.
- China–Russia and China–India differences; India's ties with the USA.
- It is not a military alliance and has little money of its own; plans for an SCO development bank moved slowly.
- Economic results so far are modest.

### Current position (to Nov 2026)

- **October 2024:** Pakistan hosted the SCO Council of Heads of Government in Islamabad; India's foreign minister attended — the first such visit in almost a decade.
- **Tianjin summit, 31 August – 1 September 2025.**
- **Bishkek summit, 31 August – 1 September 2026:** the Bishkek Declaration condemned the US strikes on Iran, backed a "just multipolar order", and agreed a security coordination centre. India again withheld support for Belt and Road wording. **Pakistan took over the chair** and will host the next summit in Islamabad; **Lahore** was named SCO tourism and cultural capital for 2026–27 ([Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/1/sco-slams-attacks-sanctions-on-iran-key-takeaways-from-bishkek-summit); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2008079)).
- A Pakistani minister attended an SCO forum in New Delhi in November 2026; no bilateral talks took place.

### What Pakistan should do with the chair

1. Set a practical agenda: connectivity, energy, counter-terrorism, climate and disaster response.
2. Use the forum for quiet contact with India.
3. Push an SCO approach to Afghanistan: recognition steps tied to action against the TTP and other groups.
4. Promote trade in local currencies and progress on an SCO bank.
5. Host a well-run summit that shows stability.

## D. Overall analysis

- Regionalism around Pakistan fails where **politics blocks economics** (SAARC), stalls where **instability and sanctions block routes** (ECO), and works best where **great powers supply the drive** (SCO).
- Pakistan trades little with any neighbour except China. Its regional trade share is among the lowest in the world for a country of its size.
- The common requirement for all three bodies is peace on Pakistan's eastern and western borders.
