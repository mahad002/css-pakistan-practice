# 15. Pakistan's Relations with Neighbours (excluding India)

Syllabus item XXI: China, Afghanistan, Iran. CPEC has its own file (22) and the Afghan war has its own (17). This file gives the bilateral picture for each, plus short notes on the "extended neighbourhood" that examiners sometimes fold in (Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Central Asia, Russia, Bangladesh).

## A. China

### Milestones

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1950 | Pakistan recognised the People's Republic (among the first Muslim countries to do so); diplomatic relations 21 May 1951 |
| 1955 | Bandung: Zhou Enlai and Muhammad Ali Bogra met |
| **1963** | **Boundary Agreement (2 March)**; air services agreement |
| 1965, 1971 | Chinese diplomatic and material support in the wars with India |
| 1971 | Pakistan arranged Kissinger's secret visit to Beijing; backed China's UN seat |
| 1978 | **Karakoram Highway** opened |
| 1980s–90s | Defence and nuclear cooperation; Chashma nuclear plants; Heavy Mechanical Complex, Taxila |
| 2002–07 | Gwadar port phase I; Free Trade Agreement (2006) |
| 2005 | Treaty of Friendship, Cooperation and Good-Neighbourly Relations |
| **2013–15** | Belt and Road announced; **CPEC** launched with Xi Jinping's visit (April 2015) |
| 2019–26 | Support on Kashmir at the UN and on FATF; CPEC Phase II; JF-17, J-10C, HQ-9 and other systems; cooperation in the May 2025 conflict; mediation between Islamabad and Kabul |

### Why the relationship lasts

- A shared interest in balancing India.
- No history of conflict; a settled border.
- China's need for a route to the Arabian Sea and stability in Xinjiang's neighbourhood.
- Pakistan's need for arms, investment and diplomatic support without political conditions.

### Areas

- **Defence:** China is Pakistan's largest arms supplier by far (about four-fifths of imports in recent years, by SIPRI's count); joint production of the JF-17; naval frigates and submarines.
- **Economy:** China is Pakistan's largest trading partner and a major creditor (roughly a quarter to a third of external debt, including deposits and commercial loans). Trade is heavily in China's favour.
- **Nuclear energy:** Chashma and Karachi reactors.
- **Diplomacy:** mutual support on Kashmir, Taiwan, Xinjiang, Tibet, the South China Sea.

### Irritants

- **Security of Chinese nationals:** attacks at Dasu (2021), Karachi University (2022), Besham (March 2024) and Karachi airport (October 2024).
- **Unpaid dues** to Chinese power producers; slow special economic zones.
- **Trade imbalance;** limited technology transfer; opacity of contracts.
- **Pakistan's opening to the USA** (minerals, ports) watched carefully in Beijing.
- **ML-1 railway:** after years of delay it was reported in 2026 to have moved to ADB-led financing.

### Present position

Strong. China backed Pakistan diplomatically and with equipment in 2025, hosted the Pakistan–Afghanistan talks at Urumqi (April 2026) and remains the indispensable partner. The task for Pakistan is to keep this partnership while widening its options.

## B. Afghanistan

Full treatment in file 17. In brief:

- **Structural problems since 1947:** the **Durand Line** (1893), which no Afghan government has formally recognised; the Pakhtunistan issue; Afghanistan's vote against Pakistan's UN membership.
- **Interdependence:** a landlocked neighbour dependent on Karachi and Gwadar; the Afghanistan–Pakistan Transit Trade Agreement (1965; revised 2010); shared tribes; millions of refugees since 1979.
- **Since August 2021:** Pakistan expected a friendly government. Instead the TTP found sanctuary; attacks in Pakistan soared; Pakistan began expelling undocumented Afghans (from October 2023); border closures hit trade.
- **2025–26:** border fighting (October 2025), a Qatar- and Turkey-brokered ceasefire (19 October 2025) that broke down; Pakistani air strikes on Kabul, Kandahar and Paktia and a declared "open war" (27 February 2026); a truce for Eid (March 2026); the China-hosted **Urumqi talks (1–7 April 2026)** where Pakistan demanded that Kabul declare the TTP a terrorist group, dismantle it and give verifiable proof; renewed clashes from late October 2026.
- **Pakistan's interests:** no sanctuary for the TTP or Baloch groups; a recognised and quiet border; transit to Central Asia; orderly return of refugees; no hostile third-country presence.
- **Kabul's position:** the TTP is Pakistan's internal problem; Pakistan violates Afghan airspace; trade should not be used as a weapon. In November 2026 it accused Pakistan of backing anti-Taliban groups.

## C. Iran

### Milestones

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1947 | Iran was the first country to recognise Pakistan; the Shah was the first head of state to visit (1950) |
| 1955–79 | Allies in the Baghdad Pact/CENTO and RCD (1964); Iranian support in 1965 and 1971 and against the Baloch insurgency of the 1970s |
| 1979 | Islamic Revolution: Pakistan recognised the new government quickly, but ties cooled as Pakistan stayed close to the USA and Saudi Arabia |
| 1980s–90s | Sectarian violence in Pakistan with Saudi–Iranian overtones; rivalry in Afghanistan (Iran backed the Northern Alliance, Pakistan the Taliban); the killing of Iranian diplomats at Mazar-i-Sharif (1998) |
| 2009–13 | **Iran–Pakistan gas pipeline** agreement; Iran built its section; Pakistan's is unbuilt because of US sanctions |
| 2016 | Arrest of Kulbhushan Jadhav, who Pakistan said had operated from Chabahar |
| **January 2024** | Iran struck alleged Jaish al-Adl bases in Panjgur; Pakistan struck alleged Baloch militant hideouts in Sistan-Baluchestan two days later; ties were restored within days |
| April 2024 | President Raisi's visit; target of $10 billion trade |
| June 2025 | Israel–Iran war: Pakistan condemned the strikes on Iran and supported its right of self-defence |
| **2026** | **US–Israeli war on Iran from 28 February.** Pakistan condemned the attack, kept its border open for relief, and became the main mediator; **direct US–Iran talks in Islamabad (11–12 April)**. By November the conflict was "frozen", with a US blockade and Hormuz largely shut; Iran's new Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, had ordered an end to the "no war, no peace" situation **[verify details]** |

### Interests and common ground

- A 900-km border; security in the two Balochistans.
- Energy: Iranian gas and electricity (Iran already supplies power to Makran).
- Trade: border markets; barter arrangements; rail link (Quetta–Zahedan; the Islamabad–Tehran–Istanbul train).
- ECO and SCO membership.
- Shared positions on Palestine and Afghanistan's stability.

### Irritants

- **Cross-border militancy:** Jaish al-Adl attacks in Iran; Baloch separatists sheltering on the Iranian side.
- **US sanctions,** which block banking and the pipeline; Iran has warned of arbitration and a multi-billion-dollar penalty.
- **The Saudi factor:** Pakistan's defence agreement with Riyadh (2025).
- **India's presence at Chabahar.**
- **Smuggling** of fuel and goods.
- **Sectarian sensitivities** at home.

### Present position

Closer than at any time since 1979, because of Pakistan's stance and mediation in the war. Risks: refugees and instability on the border if Iran weakens further; Pakistan caught between Tehran, Riyadh and Washington; fuel and remittance shocks from a closed Hormuz ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/no-war-no-peace-can-iran-break-the-deadlock-with-the-us)).

## D. The extended neighbourhood (short notes)

| Country | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Saudi Arabia** | Home to over two million Pakistani workers and the largest source of remittances; oil on deferred payment and central-bank deposits; Pakistani troops have served there for decades; **Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement (17 September 2025)** — aggression against either is aggression against both |
| **UAE, Qatar** | Remittances, investment, LNG (Qatar); deposits |
| **Turkey and Azerbaijan** | Defence co-production (MILGEM corvettes, drones); support on Kashmir; trilateral cooperation |
| **Central Asian republics** | Energy and transit hopes (CASA-1000, TAPI, the Uzbekistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan railway); all depend on Afghan stability |
| **Russia** | Defence cooperation agreement (2014); joint drills; energy talks; SCO |
| **Bangladesh** | Rapid thaw since August 2024; direct trade and flights |
| **Sri Lanka, Nepal, Maldives** | Defence training and trade; partners in a dormant SAARC |

## E. Overall analysis

1. Pakistan has **one fully friendly border** (China). Peace on the western borders would transform its economy and security.
2. Relations are **security-heavy and trade-light:** trade with Iran and Afghanistan is a small fraction of potential; even with China it is one-sided.
3. **Third parties shape each relationship:** the USA (Iran, China), India (Afghanistan, Iran), Saudi Arabia (Iran).
4. **Border management** — fencing, crossings, refugees, smuggling — is now a core part of neighbourhood policy.

## F. Way forward

- **China:** secure Chinese personnel; clear arrears; make zones work; seek industrial relocation and agricultural technology, not only loans.
- **Afghanistan:** a verifiable deal on the TTP through China, Qatar, Turkey and the SCO; separate humanitarian and trade matters from security where possible; lawful and humane refugee policy.
- **Iran:** joint border security mechanisms; barter and local-currency trade within the law; a negotiated way out of the pipeline deadlock; continued mediation.
- **General:** a "neighbours first" economic policy; visa and transit reforms; use the SCO and ECO.
