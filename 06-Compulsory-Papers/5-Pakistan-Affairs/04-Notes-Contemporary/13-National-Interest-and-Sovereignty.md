# 13. Pakistan's National Interest; Challenges to Sovereignty

## A. National interest

### 1. Concept

- **National interest:** the goals a state must secure for its survival, security and well-being, and which guide its foreign and domestic policy.
- Hans Morgenthau (*Politics Among Nations*): interest "defined in terms of power" is the main signpost of international politics. Realists treat survival as the first interest.
- Liberal and constructivist writers add that interests are shaped by values, institutions and identity — relevant to a state founded on an idea.

### 2. Levels of interest (a classification often attributed to Donald Nuechterlein)

| Level | Meaning | Pakistan's examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival / core** | The existence of the state | Territorial integrity; the nuclear deterrent; national unity |
| **Vital** | Serious harm if not protected | Security against India; water from the Indus system; internal security against terrorism; economic solvency; the Kashmir cause |
| **Major** | Important but negotiable | Trade access; energy supply; CPEC; relations with the USA, China and the Gulf; the diaspora's welfare |
| **Peripheral** | Desirable | Prestige; cultural influence |

### 3. Pakistan's national interests

1. **Sovereignty and territorial integrity.**
2. **Security:** credible deterrence; a peaceful neighbourhood.
3. **Economic development:** growth, exports, investment, energy and food security.
4. **National cohesion** and constitutional order.
5. **Ideological identity:** an Islamic republic with a democratic constitution.
6. **Just settlement of Kashmir** under UN resolutions.
7. **Water security.**
8. **Solidarity with the Muslim world;** Palestine.
9. **A stable Afghanistan** that does not host anti-Pakistan groups.
10. **A respected place in the world:** UN, OIC, SCO; peacekeeping; mediation.

The **National Security Policy 2022–26** put it this way: citizens' security and dignity at the centre, and economic security as the base of all other security.

### 4. Who defines it?

- In the constitution: the federal cabinet answerable to Parliament; the National Security Committee.
- In practice: the security establishment has had the decisive voice on India, Afghanistan, the USA and nuclear policy.
- **Problem:** a narrowly defined interest (military security first) crowded out economic and human security. Examples: the cost of the Afghan policy; trade with India forgone.

### 5. Has policy served the national interest? (see file 10, section 4)

- Served: the China partnership; the nuclear deterrent; staying out of Yemen (2015); balanced handling of the 2026 Iran war.
- Not served: dependence on single patrons; the use of proxies; neglect of exports and human development; unsettled relations with all neighbours but one.

### 6. What a sound definition requires

Debate in Parliament; economic strength as the first priority; peace with neighbours as a means to it; consistency across governments; a professional diplomatic service.

## B. Challenges to sovereignty

### 1. Concept

- **Sovereignty:** the supreme authority of a state within its territory (internal) and its independence from outside control (external). The basis of the modern state system since the Peace of Westphalia (1648); protected by Art. 2(1) and 2(4) of the UN Charter.
- In Pakistan's constitution sovereignty belongs to Allah and is exercised by the people through their chosen representatives (Preamble, Art. 2A).
- No state has absolute sovereignty today. Treaties, markets, technology and international law limit all states. The question is how much freedom of decision a state keeps.

### 2. External challenges

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Military violation of territory** | US **drone strikes** (2004–18); the **Abbottabad raid** (2 May 2011); the **Salala attack** (26 November 2011); Indian strikes at Balakot (2019) and in **May 2025**; Iranian strikes in Balochistan (January 2024); cross-border attacks from Afghanistan |
| **Economic conditionality** | IMF programmes dictating taxes, tariffs and budgets; the FATF grey list (2018–22); dependence on deposits and rollovers from friendly states; debt to China |
| **Water** | India's suspension of the Indus Waters Treaty: a neighbour claiming a hand on the country's lifeline |
| **Sponsored militancy and proxies** | Alleged Indian support for Baloch groups and the TTP; sanctuaries in Afghanistan |
| **Great-power pressure** | "With us or against us" (2001); pressure over China, Russia and Iran ties; sanctions on missile entities (2024) |
| **Information and cyber** | Disinformation networks; data held abroad; spyware |
| **Alliance obligations** | Commitments that may bind future choices: the Saudi defence agreement; any role in Gaza |

### 3. Internal challenges

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Loss of the state's writ** | Militant control of parts of Swat (2007–09) and the tribal areas; no-go areas; attacks on highways and railways in Balochistan; riverine gangs |
| **Insurgency and separatism** | Balochistan |
| **Extremism** | Groups that reject the constitution; mob violence |
| **Institutional imbalance** | Where unelected institutions override elected ones, popular sovereignty is diluted |
| **Weak governance and corruption** | A state that cannot tax, police or deliver loses authority |
| **Economic weakness** | The root: a state that cannot pay its way cannot say no |
| **Political polarisation** | Invites outside mediation and manipulation |
| **Ungoverned economies** | Smuggling, the informal sector, illegal trade across the western borders |

### 4. Analysis

1. **Sovereignty is earned through capacity.** A strong economy, legitimate government and social cohesion are its real guarantees. The nuclear deterrent protects against invasion, not against dependence.
2. **Many breaches were invited or tolerated.** Drone strikes took place with unstated consent at times; aid was accepted with strings. The lesson is transparency: terms debated in Parliament.
3. **External and internal challenges feed each other.** Outsiders exploit internal divisions; internal failures are blamed on outsiders.
4. **Interdependence is not surrender.** Joining the IMF, FATF standards or a defence pact is itself a sovereign act. The test is whether the choice was free, informed and beneficial.
5. **The 2025 conflict** showed both sides of the coin: Pakistan defended its airspace effectively, yet its water treaty was suspended by a neighbour without cost.

### 5. Way forward

1. **Economic self-reliance:** raise revenue and exports; end the cycle of bail-outs.
2. **Supremacy of the constitution and Parliament** in defining and overseeing foreign commitments.
3. **Restore the state's writ** through law: police, courts, local government, development in neglected areas.
4. **Political settlement** in Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
5. **Credible deterrence and modern air and missile defence.**
6. **Active diplomacy and international law:** the UN, the ICJ and arbitration (as on water).
7. **Balanced external relations;** no bases or blank cheques.
8. **Data and cyber sovereignty:** domestic capacity, legal safeguards.
9. **National consensus** on core interests.
