# 1. Pakistan and the Changing Regional Apparatus; Pakistan's Role in the Region

Syllabus items III and IX. Asked as "geostrategic importance" (2023, 2023 special) and as Pakistan's role in South Asian stability (2024). The events of 2025–26 make a direct question likely.

"Regional apparatus" means the arrangement of power, alliances and institutions around Pakistan. The question is always: what has changed, what does it mean for Pakistan, and what should Pakistan do?

## 1. Background: three earlier orders

| Period | Regional order | Pakistan's place |
|---|---|---|
| 1947–89, Cold War | US and Soviet blocs; India non-aligned but close to Moscow | US ally (SEATO, CENTO); front-line state against the Soviets in Afghanistan |
| 1990–2001, unipolar decade | US dominance; sanctions on Pakistan; Taliban in Kabul; India's economic opening | Isolated; nuclear tests (1998); Kargil |
| 2001–21, war on terror | US and NATO in Afghanistan; rise of China; US–India partnership | Non-NATO ally and suspect at once; CPEC from 2015 |

## 2. What has changed since 2021

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **US exit from Afghanistan** (August 2021) | Taliban rule; the TTP revived; Pakistan lost its value to Washington as a supply route, then regained relevance in 2025–26 for other reasons |
| **US–China rivalry** | Trade and technology war; pressure on states to choose. Pakistan depends on China for arms and investment and on the West for export markets and IMF support |
| **India's rise and its limits** | Fifth-largest economy; Quad member; defence deals with the USA. But US–India ties were strained in 2025 by tariffs and by Washington's renewed warmth towards Pakistan |
| **May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict** | Four days of air, missile and drone strikes (7–10 May) after the Pahalgam attack; ceasefire announced by President Trump; the Indus Waters Treaty still held "in abeyance" by India. A new, lower threshold for conventional strikes between nuclear states |
| **Pakistan–Afghan Taliban rupture** | Border fighting in October 2025; "open war" from late February 2026; an uneasy pause after China-hosted talks; clashes again in November 2026 |
| **Middle East wars** | Gaza war from October 2023 and the October 2025 ceasefire; Israel–Iran war of June 2025; **US–Israeli war on Iran from 28 February 2026**, a fragile ceasefire in April, and by November 2026 a "frozen" conflict with the Strait of Hormuz largely shut |
| **Gulf realignment** | Saudi–Iran rapprochement (2023, via China); **Saudi–Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement (17 September 2025)**: aggression against one is treated as aggression against both |
| **China's westward links** | CPEC Phase II; China as mediator between Kabul and Islamabad (Urumqi process, 2026) |
| **Russia's turn to Asia** | After 2022; energy sales to Asia; recognised the Taliban government (July 2025); cooperation with Pakistan through the SCO |
| **Central Asia** | Demand for routes to the sea: the Uzbekistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan railway framework (2025), CASA-1000, TAPI — all hostage to Afghan stability |
| **Bangladesh** | Fall of Sheikh Hasina (August 2024); warmer ties with Pakistan; cooler with India |
| **Multilateral shifts** | SCO and BRICS expanding; SAARC inactive since 2014 |

## 3. Current position (to 23 Nov 2026)

- **Mediator.** Pakistan became the main channel between Washington and Tehran in the 2026 war. With Turkey and Egypt it helped secure a pause in US strikes in March; the Prime Minister helped secure a two-week ceasefire in early April; **Islamabad hosted direct US–Iran talks on 11–12 April 2026**, which ended without agreement. Mediation continued through the year; Iran still calls Pakistan "the mediator" ([Al Jazeera, April 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/4/8/how-pakistan-managed-to-get-the-us-and-iran-to-a-ceasefire); [Al Jazeera, Nov 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/17/is-the-us-israel-war-on-iran-turning-into-a-frozen-conflict)).
- **United States.** A marked improvement since mid-2025: tariff on Pakistani goods set at 19%; a critical-minerals MoU (September 2025); US Export-Import Bank financing for Reko Diq; cooperation on counter-terrorism. A full trade agreement is still under negotiation ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/minerals-diplomacy-meets-market-reality-case-pakistan)).
- **China.** Remains the main strategic partner and arms supplier. CPEC Phase II agreed in 2025; the ML-1 railway was reported in mid-2026 to have moved to ADB-led financing ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2616236/ml-1-removed-from-cpec-framework-1)).
- **Saudi Arabia and the Gulf.** The 2025 defence agreement; a further three-way "Makkah Joint Defence Alliance" with Saudi Arabia and Turkey was reported in August 2026 **[verify]**.
- **SCO.** Pakistan took the chair of the Council of Heads of State after the Bishkek summit (31 August – 1 September 2026) and will host the next summit in Islamabad ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2008079)).
- **UN.** Non-permanent member of the Security Council for 2025–26 (its eighth term).
- **Gaza.** Member of the Board of Peace (January 2026); has not committed troops to the stabilisation force.
- **India.** No dialogue; treaty in abeyance; back-channel and Track II contacts only.
- **Afghanistan.** Hostile; diplomatic contact minimal; China is the go-between.

## 4. Pakistan's role in the region: assets and limits

| Assets | Limits |
|---|---|
| Location between China, Central Asia, the Gulf and South Asia | Weak economy; dependence on the IMF and on rollovers from friends |
| Nuclear deterrent and a large, experienced military | Terrorism at home: ranked the country most affected by terrorism in 2025 (Global Terrorism Index 2026) |
| Working relations with Washington, Beijing, Riyadh, Tehran and Ankara at once | Hostile borders east and west at the same time |
| A record in UN peacekeeping (among the largest contributors over six decades) | Political instability; governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; Balochistan insurgency |
| Voice in the OIC and the Muslim world | Low trade with neighbours; SAARC paralysed |
| Large diaspora and workforce in the Gulf | Exposure to oil shocks and to any closure of Hormuz |

## 5. Analysis

1. **From geostrategy to geo-economics — still unfinished.** The National Security Policy (2022) said economic security was the core. In practice Pakistan's regional weight in 2025–26 came from military performance and diplomacy, not trade.
2. **Multi-alignment works when the powers need you.** Pakistan gained from being useful to the USA (Iran channel, minerals), China (corridor), and Saudi Arabia (security) at the same time. It becomes dangerous if they demand exclusive loyalty.
3. **Mediation raises standing but carries risk.** A mediator is blamed by both sides when talks fail, and a neighbour's war (Iran) spills over through refugees, fuel prices, sectarian tension and the Balochistan border.
4. **The Saudi pact is a commitment as well as a gain.** It brings investment and status. It could also pull Pakistan into Gulf conflicts and complicates ties with Iran.
5. **Two-front pressure is the central constraint.** While India and Afghanistan are both hostile, resources go to security and the regional connectivity role cannot be realised.
6. **Internal strength decides external role.** A stable polity and a growing economy are the base of influence.

## 6. Way forward

1. Keep balanced relations with the USA and China; avoid bloc politics.
2. Seek a managed peace with India: restore the ceasefire mechanisms, reopen talk on water through the treaty's own procedures, and resume trade in stages.
3. A verifiable arrangement with Kabul on the TTP, through China, Qatar and Turkey, with trade and transit as incentives.
4. Use the SCO chair to push connectivity, counter-terrorism cooperation and Afghan stabilisation.
5. Define in Parliament the scope of the Saudi defence commitment and of any Gaza deployment.
6. Turn location into income: transit trade, energy corridors, ports, with security along the routes.
7. Put the economic house in order: exports, tax base, energy costs.
