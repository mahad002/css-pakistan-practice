# 22. The China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)

Falls under syllabus items XXI (neighbours), VII (economy) and III (regional change). Asked three times: as a "game changer" (2017), Phase II and jobs (2021), challenges and opportunities (2025). Also common in Current Affairs.

## 1. What it is

- A package of energy, transport, port and industrial projects linking **Kashgar in Xinjiang to Gwadar** on the Arabian Sea, about 3,000 km.
- The flagship of China's **Belt and Road Initiative** (announced 2013).
- Launched during President **Xi Jinping's visit in April 2015** with agreements worth about **$46 billion**; the notional total later rose to about $62 billion.
- Run by a **Joint Cooperation Committee (JCC)** co-chaired by Pakistan's planning minister and China's National Development and Reform Commission.
- Long-Term Plan: 2017–2030.

## 2. What has been built (Phase I, 2015–2022)

About **$25 billion** actually invested.

| Area | Projects |
|---|---|
| **Energy** | Over a dozen power projects adding roughly 8,000 MW: coal plants at **Sahiwal, Port Qasim and Hub** (1,320 MW each); **Thar** coal mines and plants; hydropower at **Karot** (720 MW) and **Suki Kinari** (884 MW); wind farms in Sindh; Quaid-i-Azam Solar Park; the **Matiari–Lahore HVDC** transmission line |
| **Roads** | Karakoram Highway Phase II (Havelian–Thakot); **Multan–Sukkur motorway (M-5)**; Hakla–D.I. Khan motorway (western route); roads in Balochistan |
| **Gwadar** | Port operations by a Chinese company; free zone; Eastbay Expressway; **New Gwadar International Airport** (opened 2024–25); desalination, hospital and vocational institute |
| **Urban transport** | Orange Line metro train, Lahore |
| **Digital** | Cross-border optical fibre cable (Khunjerab–Rawalpindi) |

**Results:** ended the acute power shortage; modern north–south roads; an estimated 200,000 or more jobs; a deep-water port ready for use.

## 3. Phase II

Aims: **industrialisation, agriculture, science and technology, socio-economic projects, and private business links** rather than government loans.

- **Special Economic Zones:** Rashakai (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa), Allama Iqbal Industrial City (Faisalabad), Dhabeji (Sindh), Bostan (Balochistan) and others. Progress has been slow: utilities, security and incentives lag.
- **ML-1:** upgrade of the Karachi–Peshawar railway (about 1,700 km; about $6.7–7 billion). Approved in principle years ago; financing terms never closed.
- **Karakoram Highway realignment** around the Dasu and Bhasha reservoirs: China agreed in 2025–26 to finance most of it.
- **Agriculture:** seeds, mechanisation, cold chains, exports of meat, cherries and other produce to China.
- **IT and green energy.**

### Current position (to Nov 2026)

- **14th JCC, Beijing, September 2025:** launched "CPEC 2.0" with five corridors — growth, livelihood, innovation, green development and regional connectivity — and an action plan for 2025–29. No breakthrough on unpaid dues to Chinese power producers. For ML-1, the two sides spoke of a consortium of bilateral and multilateral lenders ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1940653)).
- **Mid-2026:** the government told a parliamentary committee that **ML-1 had been taken out of the CPEC framework** and would seek Asian Development Bank and other financing, beginning with the Karachi–Rohri section ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2616236/ml-1-removed-from-cpec-framework-1)).
- **Security:** attacks on Chinese nationals in 2024 (Besham, Karachi) led Beijing to press for stronger protection. Baloch militants stepped up attacks on roads, railways and mining in 2025–26.
- **Extension to Afghanistan,** agreed in principle at trilateral meetings in 2025, is frozen by the Pakistan–Afghan conflict.
- **A new factor:** US interest in Pakistani minerals and ports since 2025. Pakistan says its ties with Washington are not at China's expense.

## 4. Opportunities (2025)

1. **Energy security** — achieved in volume; the task now is cost.
2. **Connectivity:** cheaper internal transport; links between backward regions and markets.
3. **Industrialisation:** relocation of Chinese labour-intensive industry to the zones; joint ventures; export growth (2021 question).
4. **Jobs and skills:** construction first, then factories and services; vocational training.
5. **Gwadar as a regional hub:** transshipment; a route for western China, Central Asia and Afghanistan.
6. **Agricultural modernisation** and access to China's food market.
7. **Development of Balochistan, Gilgit-Baltistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** — if benefits are shared.
8. **Regional role:** transit income; a stake for neighbours in Pakistan's stability.
9. **Strategic depth to the China partnership.**

## 5. Challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Security** | Attacks on Chinese personnel and projects; the cost of a special protection force; Balochistan's insurgency |
| **Debt and payments** | Loans and guaranteed returns in dollars; arrears to Chinese power producers of several hundred billion rupees; capacity payments (file 14). Note: Chinese debt is a large part, but not the majority, of Pakistan's external debt |
| **Slow second phase** | Zones without gas, power and roads; red tape; tax and policy changes; little industrial relocation so far |
| **Trade imbalance** | Imports from China several times exports; the free trade agreement favoured China until its 2019 revision |
| **Provincial grievances** | Complaints that the eastern route (Punjab, Sindh) was favoured over the western; Gwadar residents' protests over fishing, water and checkpoints (the *Haq Do Tehreek*) |
| **Transparency** | Contract terms not published; limited parliamentary oversight |
| **Capacity and governance** | Weak project preparation; frequent changes of government and of CPEC's managers |
| **Geopolitics** | India objects because the route crosses Gilgit-Baltistan; US criticism of "debt-trap" lending; rivalry between Gwadar and Iran's Chabahar |
| **IMF constraints** | Limits on new guarantees and tax breaks for zones |
| **Environment** | Coal power; fragile mountain ecology |

## 6. Is it a "game changer"? (2017)

**The case for:** the largest foreign investment in Pakistan's history, arriving when no one else was investing; it removed the power and transport bottlenecks; it tied China's interests to Pakistan's stability.

**The case for caution:** infrastructure alone does not create exports; the energy contracts created a fiscal burden; the industrial phase has under-delivered; benefits have been uneven.

**Judgement:** CPEC changed the game for infrastructure. Whether it changes the economy depends on Pakistan's own reforms — security, energy pricing, zones that work, skills and policy stability.

## 7. Way forward

1. Secure projects and people through better policing and by giving local communities a stake.
2. Settle the power-sector arrears and renegotiate terms cooperatively.
3. Make three or four zones fully operational before announcing more; one-window service; guaranteed utilities.
4. Negotiate better market access in China; build export capacity in agriculture and light manufacturing.
5. Local hiring and training quotas; technology transfer clauses.
6. Publish contracts; strengthen parliamentary oversight.
7. Complete the western route and Gwadar's water, power and fisheries projects.
8. Blend financing: China, the ADB, the Gulf and the private sector.
9. Keep CPEC open to third countries and insulated from domestic politics.
