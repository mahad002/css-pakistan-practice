# 11. Ethnic Issues and National Integration

Syllabus item XVI. Asked six times since 2016: national culture (2017), the integration crisis (2018), regional parties (2022), ethnic exclusion and 1971 (2023 special), resource distribution among ethnic regions (2024), diversity and unity (2025). One of the safest topics to prepare.

## 1. Concepts

- **National integration:** the process by which people of different regions, languages and groups come to share a sense of belonging to one political community and loyalty to its institutions.
- Two dimensions (Myron Weiner): **horizontal** — between regions and groups; **vertical** — between rulers and ruled.
- Two approaches: **assimilation** (one identity replaces others) and **pluralism** or "unity in diversity" (many identities under a shared civic one). Pakistan's experience shows that forced assimilation backfires.
- **Ethnicity:** group identity based on language, descent, culture and territory. It becomes political when linked to grievances over power and resources.
- Useful references: Rounaq Jahan, *Pakistan: Failure in National Integration*; Tahir Amin, *Ethno-National Movements of Pakistan*; Feroz Ahmed, *Ethnicity and Politics in Pakistan*; Katharine Adeney, *Federalism and Ethnic Conflict Regulation in India and Pakistan* (on the FPSC list); Farzana Shaikh, *Making Sense of Pakistan*.

## 2. The make-up of Pakistan

| Group (mother tongue, 2023 census) | Share | Home region |
|---|---|---|
| Punjabi | about 37% | Punjab |
| Pashto | about 18% | Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, northern Balochistan, Karachi |
| Sindhi | about 14% | Sindh |
| Saraiki | about 12% | Southern Punjab |
| Urdu | about 9% | Urban Sindh |
| Balochi (and Brahui) | about 4–5% | Balochistan |
| Others | — | Hindko, Kashmiri, Shina, Balti, Burushaski, Khowar, Hazaragi and more |

**The structural problem:** one province, Punjab, has just over half the population (about 53%), most of the army and bureaucracy, and the largest share of National Assembly seats. Balochistan has 44% of the land and about 6% of the people. No constitutional design fully solves this imbalance.

## 3. How the problem developed

| Period | Development |
|---|---|
| 1947–71 | Centralisation; Urdu as the sole national language; One Unit (1955); parity; military rule. Result: **East Pakistan's separation** |
| 1970s | The 1973 Constitution gave provinces a Senate and councils. But the Balochistan government was dismissed (1973) and an insurgency followed; language riots in Sindh (1972) |
| 1980s | Sindh's MRD uprising (1983); rise of the **MQM** (1984) among Urdu-speakers over quotas and jobs; Afghan refugees changed demographics; the Kalabagh Dam dispute |
| 1990s | Karachi violence and operations; "Pakhtunkhwa" demand |
| 2000s | Fifth Baloch insurgency after the killing of **Nawab Akbar Bugti (2006)**; military operations in the tribal areas |
| 2010s | **18th Amendment and 7th NFC Award (2009–10)**; renaming of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; demands for Saraiki and Hazara provinces; **FATA merger (2018)**; Pashtun Tahaffuz Movement (2018) |
| 2020s | Escalating Baloch militancy; Baloch Yakjehti Committee protests; canals dispute between Sindh and Punjab (2024–25); governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (2026); debate on new provinces |

## 4. The grievances, province by province

### Balochistan

- **History:** accession of Kalat (1948); insurgencies in 1948, 1958, 1962–63, 1973–77 and since 2004.
- **Grievances:** gas piped to the rest of the country since the 1950s while much of the province lacked it; low royalties; fear of becoming a minority through Gwadar and mega-projects; **enforced disappearances**; little say over Reko Diq, Saindak and CPEC; poverty and illiteracy the worst in the country; engineered provincial governments.
- **State view:** foreign-backed terrorism (the arrest of Indian officer Kulbhushan Jadhav in 2016 is cited); sardars who blocked development; large federal transfers since 2010.
- **Efforts:** Aghaz-i-Huqooq-i-Balochistan (2009); a higher NFC share (9.09%); gas arrears; quotas; the Southern Balochistan package; reconciliation offers.
- **Present:** insurgency at its most intense. The **Jaffar Express hijacking (March 2025)**, the coordinated attacks of **31 January 2026**, and a wave in April–May 2026 targeted railways, highways and mining. Barrick slowed work at Reko Diq. Leaders of the Baloch Yakjehti Committee were detained in 2025 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/2/how-balochistan-attacks-threaten-pakistans-promises-to-china-trump)).

### Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and the merged districts

- Two decades of war, displacement and military operations.
- The 2018 merger promised Rs 100 billion a year for ten years and 3% of the divisible pool; delivery fell far short.
- Disputes over **net hydel profit** and the provincial share.
- Opposition to new operations; the PTM's rights campaign (banned October 2024).
- Sectarian fighting in Kurram (2024–25).
- Political confrontation between the PTI provincial government and the centre, ending in **governor's rule (11 October 2026)**.

### Sindh

- **Rural Sindh:** water (the Indus downstream of Punjab; opposition to Kalabagh Dam and to the **Cholistan canals**, which the CCI shelved in April 2025 after province-wide protests); fear of demographic change; ownership of Karachi's revenues.
- **Urban Sindh:** Urdu-speakers' complaints over the quota system, local government powers and census counts.
- The PPP's firm opposition to any division of Sindh in the new-provinces debate.

### Punjab

- Resentment at being blamed as "big brother".
- **Southern Punjab (Saraiki belt):** poverty and under-representation; a long-standing demand for a separate province; a South Punjab secretariat exists.

### Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir

- Neither is a province under the constitution; demands for constitutional rights and representation in Parliament.
- Protest movements over wheat subsidy, electricity prices and land (the Awami Action Committees, 2023–25).

### Cross-cutting

- **Language:** regional languages feel neglected in schools and media.
- **Sect and religion:** sectarian violence; insecurity of minorities.
- **Class:** the poor in every province have more in common with each other than with their own elites.

## 5. Root causes of weak integration (2018)

1. **Over-centralisation** and the denial of provincial autonomy for most of the country's history.
2. **Military rule,** which shut the smaller provinces out of power.
3. **Punjab's weight** in every national institution.
4. **Unequal development** and disputes over resources: water, gas, minerals, the NFC.
5. **Language and cultural policy** that equated unity with uniformity.
6. **Use of force** for political problems (1971, Balochistan, Karachi).
7. **Weak national parties;** engineered politics in the provinces.
8. **External interference** exploiting grievances.
9. **Failure of the education system and media** to build a shared civic identity.
10. **Economic stagnation:** a shrinking pie sharpens every quarrel.
11. **Absence of local government.**

## 6. Forces of integration

- Islam and shared history; Urdu as a link language; the constitution and Parliament; national parties; an integrated economy and labour market (Pashtuns in Karachi, Punjabis in Balochistan, Sindhis and Baloch in the Gulf); motorways and telecoms; cricket and popular culture; the federal civil services with provincial quotas; response to common threats (floods, the May 2025 conflict).

## 7. Constitutional and fiscal tools (2024)

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Senate** | Equal seats for each province |
| **Council of Common Interests** (Arts. 153–154) | Joint federal–provincial decisions on Part II of the Federal List (water, power, gas, ports, census, regulators) |
| **NFC** (Art. 160) | Shares federal taxes. The **7th Award (2009)** gave provinces **57.5%** and replaced the population-only formula with **population 82%, poverty and backwardness 10.3%, revenue 5%, inverse population density 2.7%**. Horizontal shares: Punjab 51.74%, Sindh 24.55%, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa 14.62%, Balochistan 9.09% |
| **18th Amendment** | Abolished the Concurrent List; Art. 172(3) joint ownership of oil and gas; Art. 161 royalties and hydel profits; Art. 158 a province's first claim on its gas; Art. 160(3A) no cut in provincial share |
| **Art. 27** | Quotas in federal services |
| **Art. 38(g)** | Fair shares of provinces in federal services, including autonomous bodies |
| **Art. 140A** | Local government |
| **Art. 251** | Promotion of provincial languages |

### Fair distribution of resources among regions (2024)

- **Problems:** the NFC is still dominated by population, which rewards growth and penalises sparsely populated Balochistan; no award has been agreed since 2009 (the 7th has been extended every year; the 11th Commission was constituted in 2025 **[verify progress]**); provinces do not pass money down to districts; royalties are disputed; mega-projects bypass local people.
- **Remedies:** a new award with more weight for poverty, area, human development and revenue effort; **Provincial Finance Commissions** so that money reaches backward districts; a fixed share of royalties for the producing district; local hiring and training quotas in CPEC and mining; settlement of net hydel profit and gas arrears through the CCI; a transparent equalisation fund for the merged districts, Balochistan and Gilgit-Baltistan.

## 8. Does provincial autonomy strengthen integration? (2024)

**Yes:** autonomy answers the core grievance; the 18th Amendment calmed mainstream nationalist politics in Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; federations that share power (India, Switzerland, Canada) hold together better than centralised multi-ethnic states.

**But:** autonomy stopped at provincial capitals; local government was not empowered; provincial elites captured the gains; in Balochistan, where elected politics lacks credibility, autonomy on paper did not reduce alienation.

**Conclusion:** devolution is necessary but not sufficient. It must reach districts and be matched by credible politics.

## 9. The new-provinces debate (Nov 2026)

- **For:** smaller units are easier to govern; services come closer; Punjab's dominance is reduced; long-standing Saraiki and Hazara demands are met.
- **Against:** if imposed from the centre it reopens ethnic wounds (Sindh above all); higher administrative cost; may be a route to weaken provincial governments and the 18th Amendment; Art. 239(4) requires the consent of two-thirds of the affected provincial assembly for any change to its boundaries.
- **A sound test:** provinces on administrative grounds, by consensus, through the constitutional procedure.

## 10. Way forward

1. Implement the 18th Amendment in spirit; make the CCI meet on schedule.
2. A new NFC Award and Provincial Finance Commissions.
3. Empowered, elected local governments everywhere.
4. **Balochistan:** end enforced disappearances through law and courts; dialogue with nationalists who accept the federation; local ownership of mining and port projects; credible elections; jobs and education.
5. **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa:** restore elected government; honour merger commitments; consult locals on security operations.
6. Fair water sharing under the 1991 Accord with telemetry; no canal or dam without CCI consensus.
7. Respect languages and cultures; teach regional languages; a curriculum that celebrates diversity.
8. Broad recruitment into the army and civil services; visible presence of every region in national institutions.
9. National parties that organise in all provinces; accept regional parties as partners.
10. Media, sport, youth exchanges and inter-provincial scholarships.
11. Economic growth that reaches the periphery.
