# 8. Changing Security Dynamics; Challenges to National Security

Syllabus item XI. Touched in 2021 (hybrid warfare) and 2024 (security threats and civil–military relations). This file is the **framework** for any broad security question. Details sit in other files: nuclear (02), non-traditional threats (06), India and Kashmir (16), Afghanistan (17), proxy wars (18).

## 1. What "national security" means now

- **Old definition:** defence of borders by armed force.
- **Comprehensive definition** (National Security Policy 2022–26, Pakistan's first public policy of this kind, issued January 2022): the safety, dignity and prosperity of citizens. **Economic security is the core**; military security and human security depend on it.
- The policy's pillars: national cohesion, economic future, defence and territorial integrity, internal security, foreign policy, human security.

## 2. How the security environment has changed

| Then | Now |
|---|---|
| One main front (India) | **Two active fronts** — India (May 2025) and Afghanistan (2025–26) — plus a tense Iranian border during the 2026 war |
| War as armies and tanks | **Multi-domain war:** air power with long-range missiles, drones, electronic warfare, cyber, space, information |
| Deterrence assumed to rule out conventional strikes | A **lower threshold:** India struck inside Pakistan proper in 2019 and 2025 and has announced that major attacks will be treated as acts of war |
| Water guaranteed by treaty | **Water as leverage:** the Indus Waters Treaty held in abeyance by India since April 2025 |
| Terrorism declining (2015–20) | **Terrorism resurgent:** 2025 the worst year in a decade; Pakistan ranked first on the Global Terrorism Index 2026 |
| US as security patron | **Multi-alignment:** Chinese equipment, a Saudi defence pact, renewed US ties |
| Security as a military matter | **Economy, climate, cohesion** as security issues |

## 3. The challenges

### A. External

1. **India.** Unresolved Kashmir; no dialogue since 2019; the May 2025 exchange; India's military spending several times Pakistan's; India's stated doctrine of punishing attacks by striking across the border; the water threat; alleged support for militancy in Balochistan.
2. **Afghanistan.** TTP and other groups based there; Kabul's refusal to act; open fighting in 2025–26; the Durand Line dispute; refugees.
3. **Iran and the Gulf.** A long, porous border through Balochistan; exchange of strikes in January 2024; the 2026 war's spill-over (refugees, smuggling, fuel prices, sectarian tension); obligations under the Saudi defence agreement.
4. **Great-power rivalry.** Pressure to take sides between the USA and China; sanctions risk on missile and other programmes.
5. **Maritime.** Protection of sea lanes and Gwadar; India's naval growth; the closure of Hormuz in 2026.

### B. Internal

6. **Terrorism and insurgency** in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan.
7. **Political polarisation** and contested legitimacy since 2022; governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (October 2026).
8. **Economic fragility:** debt, low reserves relative to needs, IMF dependence — limiting defence modernisation and social spending alike.
9. **Ethnic and sectarian fault lines;** alienation in the periphery.
10. **Extremism** and mob violence.
11. **Governance:** weak police and courts; low trust in institutions.

### C. Emerging and non-traditional

12. **Climate and water:** floods, heat, glacier loss, storage shortage.
13. **Cyber and information:** attacks on infrastructure; disinformation; AI-generated content.
14. **Population and human development:** 2.55% growth; 25 million children out of school.
15. **Energy and food security.**
16. **Technology gap:** drones, AI, space and precision weapons change the balance quickly and cost money.

## 4. Current position (to Nov 2026)

- **After May 2025:** Pakistan's air force and air defences were widely judged to have performed well; both sides claimed success. The lessons drawn in Pakistan: integrate the services (hence the Chief of Defence Forces), invest in air defence, drones and long-range conventional rockets (hence the Army Rocket Force Command), and keep Chinese supply lines. The risk: both sides now plan for a faster, deeper next round.
- **Defence budget:** raised by about a fifth in 2025–26 and to about Rs 3 trillion in 2026–27.
- **Western border:** after the fighting of February–March 2026 and the Urumqi talks, clashes resumed from late October 2026; a TTP ambush on 11 November killed ten policemen ([Long War Journal](https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2026/11/taliban-pakistan-border-clashes-continue-ttp-attack-kills-10-pakistani-police.php)).
- **Balochistan:** coordinated attacks in January 2026 and a surge in April–May, with mining and transport projects targeted ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/25/train-bomb-in-pakistans-baloch-region-why-violence-is-on-the-rise)).
- **Gulf war:** Hormuz largely shut in November 2026; oil and remittance exposure; Pakistan as mediator.
- **Decision-making:** the National Security Committee, apex committees and the SIFC; the 27th Amendment's new command structure.

## 5. Analysis

1. **The centre of gravity is internal.** External enemies exploit internal weakness: a poor economy, political division and neglected provinces.
2. **Deterrence held, but the ladder is shorter.** Each crisis since 1999 has gone a rung higher. Crisis communication with India is thinner than at any time since the 1990s.
3. **A two-front posture is unaffordable for long.** Diplomacy must close at least one front.
4. **Kinetic success without political follow-through does not last** — the lesson of the tribal areas after 2014.
5. **Security and development are one problem** in Balochistan and the merged districts.
6. **Institutional balance matters for security.** Decisions made by a narrow circle lack the legitimacy that sustained national effort needs.

## 6. Way forward

1. **Economic revival** as the first security priority.
2. **Political reconciliation** at home; restore elected government in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; a political track in Balochistan.
3. **Crisis management with India:** hotlines, notification agreements, back-channel talks; reopen the treaty's dispute procedures.
4. **Afghanistan:** verifiable action on the TTP in return for trade and transit, through regional mediators.
5. **Counter-terrorism led by police and courts;** complete the National Action Plan.
6. **Modernise selectively:** air defence, drones, cyber, space, intelligence fusion — not an open-ended arms race.
7. **Human security:** schools, health, family planning, climate adaptation.
8. **Parliamentary ownership** of security policy; update the National Security Policy when it expires in 2026.
9. **Balanced diplomacy** among the great powers and in the Gulf.
