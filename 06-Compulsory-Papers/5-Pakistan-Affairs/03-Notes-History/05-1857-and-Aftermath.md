# 5. The War of Independence 1857 and its Aftermath

Not named in the syllabus, but it is the hinge between "downfall" and "renaissance". Useful for MCQs and as background in answers on Aligarh, Deoband and the evolution of Muslim identity.

## 1. What to call it

| Name | Used by | Implied view |
|---|---|---|
| Sepoy Mutiny | British writers of the time | An army revolt only |
| War of Independence | Pakistani and Indian nationalist writers (V.D. Savarkar used the phrase in 1909) | A national rising |
| Great Rebellion / Uprising of 1857 | Most modern historians | A wide but uneven revolt of soldiers, princes, landlords and peasants across north and central India |

A fair statement: it began as a mutiny, spread into a civil rebellion in large parts of north India, but was not all-India and had no single leadership or plan.

## 2. Causes

**Political**

- **Doctrine of Lapse** (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–56): states without a natural heir were annexed — Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854).
- **Annexation of Awadh (1856)** on the ground of misrule. Many Bengal Army sepoys came from Awadh.
- Disrespect to the Mughal emperor: Bahadur Shah Zafar was told his heirs would lose the title and leave the Red Fort.
- Pension of Nana Sahib (adopted son of the last Peshwa) stopped.

**Economic**

- Heavy land revenue; resumption of rent-free grants; ruin of old landholders (taluqdars of Awadh).
- Decline of Indian handicrafts under British imports; loss of court and army employment.

**Religious and social**

- Missionary activity and fear of forced conversion.
- Reforms seen as interference: abolition of sati (1829), Widow Remarriage Act (1856), the 1850 law protecting converts' inheritance.
- English education; racial arrogance of officials.

**Military**

- Indian soldiers far outnumbered Europeans (roughly 5 or 6 to 1) but had low pay and no promotion.
- General Service Enlistment Act (1856) required service overseas, against caste rules.
- Loss of allowances for service in newly annexed areas.

**Immediate cause**

- The new **Enfield rifle**. Its greased cartridges, rumoured to be coated with cow and pig fat, had to be bitten open. This offended both Hindus and Muslims.

## 3. Course of events

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 29 March 1857 | Mangal Pandey attacked officers at Barrackpore; hanged in April |
| **10 May 1857** | Sepoys at **Meerut** mutinied and marched to Delhi |
| 11 May 1857 | Delhi taken; **Bahadur Shah Zafar** proclaimed emperor of Hindustan |
| June–July 1857 | Risings at Kanpur (Nana Sahib, Tantia Tope), Lucknow (Begum Hazrat Mahal), Bareilly (Khan Bahadur Khan), Jhansi (Rani Lakshmi Bai), Bihar (Kunwar Singh), Faizabad (Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah) |
| September 1857 | British retook Delhi; General **Bakht Khan** had led the defence. Bahadur Shah captured at Humayun's tomb; his sons shot by Hodson |
| March 1858 | Lucknow retaken |
| June 1858 | Rani of Jhansi killed at Gwalior |
| 1858 | Bahadur Shah tried and exiled to **Rangoon**, where he died in 1862 |
| April 1859 | Tantia Tope executed; resistance ended |

## 4. Why it failed

1. No central leadership or common aim; each leader fought for a local cause.
2. Limited area: Punjab, Bengal, the south and most princes stayed quiet or helped the British. Sikh and Gurkha troops fought for the British.
3. Poor arms, supplies and communication; the British had the telegraph and sea reinforcements.
4. No modern-minded leadership; the educated classes stayed aloof.
5. Able British commanders (the Lawrences, Nicholson, Havelock, Campbell, Outram) and control of key lines.

## 5. Results

**Constitutional**

- **Government of India Act 1858:** the East India Company's rule ended. India passed to the Crown. A **Secretary of State for India** with a council in London; the Governor-General became **Viceroy** (Lord Canning was the first).
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation, 1 November 1858:** no more annexations, respect for treaties with princes, religious non-interference, equal access to office (in principle).
- **Indian Councils Act 1861:** a few nominated Indians added to the legislative council.

**Military**

- Ratio of European to Indian troops raised; artillery kept in European hands.
- Recruitment shifted to "martial races": Punjabis, Pathans, Sikhs, Gurkhas.

**Political and social**

- End of the Mughal dynasty.
- Princes and landlords became allies of the Raj.
- Wider gap between rulers and ruled.
- A deliberate policy of balancing communities.

## 6. Effects on Muslims

The British held Muslims mainly responsible, since the rising had been made in the name of the Mughal emperor and many of its leaders were Muslim.

| Area | What happened |
|---|---|
| Reprisals | Mass executions and confiscations in Delhi; Muslims were expelled from the city for a time; mosques occupied (the Jama Masjid was returned in 1862) |
| Jobs | Muslims lost ground in government service. Hunter's *The Indian Musalmans* (1871) documented how few posts they held in Bengal |
| Land | Estates of "rebels" confiscated |
| Education | Muslims kept away from English schools; Hindus moved ahead in the professions |
| Language | Urdu came under attack from the Hindi movement from 1867 |
| Politics | Leaderless and distrusted, Muslims withdrew from public life |

### Two responses

- **Sir Syed's response:** explain the causes of 1857, rebuild trust, take up modern education → Aligarh.
- **The ulema's response:** protect faith and learning away from the state → Deoband (1866).

Both began within a decade of the defeat. That is the "renaissance" the syllabus refers to.

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
