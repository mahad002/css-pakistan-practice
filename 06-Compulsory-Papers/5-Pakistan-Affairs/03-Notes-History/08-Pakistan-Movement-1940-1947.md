# 8. The Pakistan Movement, Part 3: 1940–1947

From the Lahore Resolution to independence. Asked: the 3 June Plan and its implementation (2018); Jinnah's leadership 1937–47 (2023).

**The story in one line:** the war gave the League leverage; the British made offers in 1940, 1942, 1945 and 1946; the 1945–46 elections proved the League's mandate; the Cabinet Mission Plan failed; and the 3 June Plan divided India.

---

## 1. Wartime offers, 1940–1942

### August Offer, 8 August 1940 (Viceroy Linlithgow)

- Dominion status after the war; a body to frame the constitution after the war.
- Expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council; a War Advisory Council.
- **A pledge to minorities:** Britain would not transfer power to any system of government whose authority was denied by large and powerful elements in India's national life.

Congress rejected it. The League welcomed the pledge but did not accept the offer, since Pakistan was not conceded.

### Cripps Mission, March–April 1942

**Why:** Japan had taken Singapore (February 1942) and Rangoon (March 1942). Britain needed Indian support. The USA and China were pressing London.

**Sir Stafford Cripps** reached Delhi on 22 March 1942. Proposals:

1. Dominion status after the war, with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
2. A constituent assembly elected by the provincial legislatures, with the princely states represented.
3. **Any province could stay out** of the new union and form its own (the non-accession clause).
4. Defence to stay under British control during the war.

| Party | Response |
|---|---|
| Congress | Rejected: it wanted immediate control of defence and a national government, and disliked the non-accession clause. The phrase "a post-dated cheque" is commonly attributed to Gandhi |
| Muslim League | Rejected: the right of Muslims to a separate state was not clearly accepted; the opt-out was for provinces, not for the Muslim nation. But Jinnah noted that the principle of partition had been recognised for the first time |

### Quit India, August 1942

- Congress passed the **Quit India resolution at Bombay on 8 August 1942**. Gandhi's call: "Do or die". The leaders were arrested the next morning. Violence followed and was suppressed.
- Jinnah called it an attempt to force the British to hand power to Congress and ignore Muslims. The League's reply was **"Divide and Quit"**.
- **Effect:** with Congress leaders in jail until 1944–45, the League spread. It formed or joined ministries in Bengal, Sindh, Assam and NWFP during 1942–43. The Sindh Assembly passed a resolution supporting Pakistan in March 1943 (moved by G.M. Syed).

---

## 2. Gandhi–Jinnah Talks, 9–27 September 1944

**Basis:** the **C.R. Formula** of C. Rajagopalachari: the League to back the demand for independence; after the war a commission would mark out the contiguous Muslim-majority districts in the north-west and east; a plebiscite of **all** inhabitants there would decide on separation; defence, commerce and communications to be run jointly.

The talks were held at Jinnah's house in Bombay.

| Issue | Gandhi | Jinnah |
|---|---|---|
| Nationhood | Muslims are not a separate nation; partition as between members of one family | Muslims are a nation by any definition |
| Timing | Independence first, then partition | Partition first, as part of the settlement |
| Who votes | All residents of the areas | Muslims only (self-determination of a nation) |
| Area | Muslim-majority districts only | All six provinces (Punjab, Sindh, NWFP, Balochistan, Bengal, Assam), with adjustments |
| Link after partition | A treaty for common subjects | Two fully sovereign states |
| Gandhi's status | Spoke as an individual | Wanted Congress to be bound |

Jinnah called the offer "a shadow and a husk, a maimed, mutilated and moth-eaten Pakistan".

**Result:** failure. **Gain for the League:** Gandhi had negotiated with Jinnah as an equal and had discussed partition. Jinnah's standing as the Muslim spokesman rose.

---

## 3. Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945

- **Wavell Plan, 14 June 1945:** a new Executive Council in which all members except the Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief would be Indian, with **equal numbers of Muslims and caste Hindus**.
- **Simla Conference, 25 June – 14 July 1945.** Congress leaders were released to attend.
- **Why it failed:** Jinnah insisted that the League alone should nominate all Muslim members. Congress, with Maulana Azad as its president, would not give up its right to nominate a Muslim. The Unionists also wanted a seat. Wavell declared failure.
- **Importance:** it showed that no settlement was possible without Jinnah. It set the question for the coming elections: who speaks for the Muslims?

In July 1945 Labour won the British election (Attlee). Japan surrendered in August. Elections in India were announced.

---

## 4. Elections of 1945–46

The League fought on one issue: Pakistan, and its claim to be the sole representative of Muslims.

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Central Legislative Assembly (December 1945) | League won **all 30 Muslim seats**, with about 87% of the Muslim vote. Congress won the great majority of general seats |
| Provincial assemblies (early 1946) | League won about **428 of 492 Muslim seats** (sources give 425–440) |
| Bengal | 113 of 119 Muslim seats → League ministry under H.S. Suhrawardy |
| Punjab | About 73–79 of 86 → largest party, but kept out by a Unionist–Congress–Akali coalition under Khizr Hayat Tiwana |
| Sindh | League ministry under Sir Ghulam Hussain Hidayatullah |
| NWFP | Congress ministry under Dr Khan Sahib; League won 17 Muslim seats |

**Why the turnaround since 1937:** the Lahore Resolution gave a clear aim; Congress rule was remembered; the League had built an organisation during the war; students (Aligarh, Islamia College Lahore, Islamia College Peshawar), ulema and pirs campaigned; Jinnah's leadership.

**Delhi Convention, 7–9 April 1946:** about 470 League legislators met. The resolution, moved by Suhrawardy, demanded **one sovereign independent state** of the north-east and north-west zones. Members took an oath to sacrifice for Pakistan.

---

## 5. Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

**Members:** Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State), Sir Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander. Arrived 24 March 1946. After talks at Simla failed, they announced their own plan on **16 May 1946**.

### The plan

1. **A sovereign Pakistan was rejected** (too many non-Muslims in a six-province Pakistan; a smaller one not viable in their view).
2. A **Union of India** with only three subjects: **foreign affairs, defence, communications**, and the money to pay for them.
3. All other subjects and residuary powers with the **provinces**.
4. Provinces placed in **three sections** to draft group constitutions:
   - **A:** Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa (Hindu majority)
   - **B:** Punjab, NWFP, Sindh (with Balochistan)
   - **C:** Bengal, Assam
5. A province could leave its group after the first election under the new constitution. Any province could ask for the constitution to be reviewed after **ten years**.
6. A **constituent assembly** elected by the provincial assemblies (389 seats including the states).
7. An **interim government** of Indian parties.

### What happened

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 6 June 1946 | League **accepted**: the groups contained "the basis and foundation of Pakistan" |
| 16 June | Mission's statement on the interim government: if a party refused, the Viceroy would go ahead with those willing |
| 25 June | Congress accepted the long-term plan with its own reading (grouping not compulsory) and refused the interim government |
| Late June | Wavell did not invite the League to form the government. Jinnah saw this as a breach of faith |
| **10 July** | **Nehru's press conference:** Congress would enter the assembly "completely unfettered by agreements" and might change the grouping |
| 27–29 July | League Council at Bombay **withdrew its acceptance** and called for **Direct Action** |
| **16 August** | **Direct Action Day.** Great Calcutta Killing: several thousand dead in four days. Riots spread to Noakhali, Bihar, UP (Garhmukteshwar) and later Punjab |
| 2 September | Interim government sworn in under Nehru |
| 26 October | League joined: **Liaquat Ali Khan** (Finance), I.I. Chundrigar (Commerce), Abdur Rab Nishtar (Communications), Ghazanfar Ali Khan (Health), **Jogendra Nath Mandal** (Law) |
| 9 December | Constituent Assembly met without the League |
| February 1947 | Liaquat's budget taxed big business; Congress ministers complained they could not spend without the Finance Member's consent. The coalition did not work |

### Who was responsible for the failure?

- **Maulana Azad** (*India Wins Freedom*) blamed Nehru's 10 July statement as the turning point.
- **H.M. Seervai** (*Partition of India: Legend and Reality*) and **Ayesha Jalal** argue that Congress preferred partition to sharing power in a weak centre, and that Jinnah's acceptance was genuine.
- **Congress view:** the compulsory groups would have forced Assam and NWFP into Muslim-dominated sections against their will; a three-subject centre would be too weak to govern.
- **British role:** the Mission's wording on groups was unclear, and London's ruling in December 1946 came too late.

**Why Jinnah accepted a plan that rejected Pakistan:** it gave Muslims control of all of Punjab and Bengal (undivided), parity-like weight in a weak centre, and a legal route to leave after ten years. This is the strongest evidence for the view that he would have settled for less than full partition if Muslim power was secured.

---

## 6. Attlee's statement and Mountbatten

- **20 February 1947:** Attlee announced that Britain would leave India by **June 1948**, and that **Lord Mountbatten** would replace Wavell.
- **2 March 1947:** Khizr Tiwana's ministry in Punjab resigned after a League civil-disobedience campaign. Riots in Rawalpindi, Multan, Lahore, Amritsar.
- Mountbatten arrived on 22 March and was sworn in on 24 March. After meeting leaders he concluded that partition was the only way. His first plan (to hand power to provinces) was dropped when Nehru objected. **V.P. Menon** drafted a new plan based on two dominions.
- Congress now demanded that if India was divided, **Punjab and Bengal must be divided too**.

---

## 7. The 3 June Plan, 1947

Announced by Mountbatten on 3 June. Nehru, Jinnah and Baldev Singh spoke on All-India Radio that evening.

### Terms

| Area | How its future would be decided |
|---|---|
| **Punjab and Bengal** | Each assembly to meet in two parts (Muslim-majority districts and the rest). If either part voted for partition, the province would be divided |
| **Sindh** | Its assembly to decide |
| **NWFP** | A **referendum** (because it had a Congress ministry but a Muslim population) |
| **Balochistan** | The Shahi Jirga and the non-official members of Quetta Municipality |
| **Sylhet** (Assam) | A referendum on joining East Bengal |
| **Boundaries** | A **Boundary Commission** if partition was chosen |
| **Princely states** | British paramountcy would lapse; states to join one or other dominion, taking geography into account |
| **Timing and status** | Power to pass to two dominions. The date was later fixed at **15 August 1947** — ten months earlier than Attlee's deadline |

### Acceptance

- League Council: 9–10 June 1947, accepted as a compromise.
- All-India Congress Committee: 14–15 June.
- The Sikhs accepted through Baldev Singh but were the most unhappy.

### Implementation (the 2018 question)

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| 20 June | Bengal Assembly: partition chosen. East Bengal for Pakistan |
| 23 June | Punjab Assembly: partition chosen. West Punjab for Pakistan |
| 26 June | Sindh Assembly voted to join Pakistan |
| End of June | Balochistan's Shahi Jirga and Quetta members voted for Pakistan |
| 6–7 July | **Sylhet referendum:** majority for East Bengal |
| 6–17 July | **NWFP referendum:** 289,244 for Pakistan, 2,874 for India. The Khudai Khidmatgars boycotted it because an independent "Pakhtunistan" was not on the ballot |
| June–August | **Partition Council** (first as Partition Committee) divided assets and staff. Civil and military officers chose which state to serve. The army was divided under a Joint Defence Council with Field Marshal Auchinleck as Supreme Commander |
| 18 July | Indian Independence Act received royal assent |
| 8 July – 13 August | Boundary Commissions worked under Radcliffe |
| 11 August | Pakistan's Constituent Assembly elected Jinnah its president |
| 14 August | Mountbatten transferred power at Karachi |
| 15 August | Jinnah sworn in as Governor-General by Chief Justice Sir Abdur Rashid; Liaquat Ali Khan as Prime Minister |
| 17 August | Radcliffe Award published |

### Assessment

- **Speed:** 72 days from plan to independence. Records, troops, canals and railways had to be divided in weeks. The haste made the violence and the refugee crisis worse.
- **For Pakistan:** it got a state, but a smaller one than claimed — Jinnah's "moth-eaten" Pakistan — without Calcutta, East Punjab or Assam (except Sylhet).
- **Left open:** the princely states, above all Kashmir; the boundaries; the division of assets and waters.

---

## 8. Indian Independence Act, 18 July 1947

1. Two independent dominions, India and Pakistan, from **15 August 1947**.
2. Each to have a **Governor-General** appointed by the King on the advice of the dominion's cabinet. (Mountbatten hoped to be Governor-General of both; the League chose Jinnah.)
3. The **constituent assemblies** would also act as legislatures, with full power to make any law and to repeal British acts.
4. Until new constitutions were made, the **Government of India Act 1935** would apply, with adaptations.
5. British **paramountcy over the princely states lapsed**; treaties with them and with the tribal areas ended.
6. The office of Secretary of State for India was abolished; the title "Emperor of India" was dropped.
7. Territories of Pakistan: East Bengal, West Punjab, Sindh, Balochistan, and NWFP and Sylhet after their referendums.

---

## 9. Radcliffe Award, 17 August 1947

**Sir Cyril Radcliffe**, a lawyer who had never been to India, chaired both commissions. He arrived on 8 July and had about five weeks.

| Commission | Muslim League nominees | Congress nominees |
|---|---|---|
| Punjab | Justice Din Muhammad, Justice Muhammad Munir | Justice Mehr Chand Mahajan, Justice Teja Singh |
| Bengal | Justice Abu Saleh Muhammad Akram, Justice S.A. Rahman | Justice C.C. Biswas, Justice B.K. Mukherjea |

The members split on community lines, so Radcliffe decided alone. The terms: mark the boundary on the basis of **contiguous majority areas of Muslims and non-Muslims**, taking into account **"other factors"**.

### Pakistan's grievances

| Area | Decision | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| **Gurdaspur** district (Muslim majority, about 51%) | Three of four tehsils (Gurdaspur, Batala, Pathankot) to India; only Shakargarh to Pakistan | Gave India its land route to Kashmir |
| **Ferozepur and Zira** tehsils (Muslim majority) | To India | Control of the Ferozepur headworks on the Sutlej |
| Parts of Lahore, Amritsar (Ajnala) and Jullundur districts | Muslim-majority tehsils to India | — |
| **Madhopur headworks** (Ravi) | To India | Canal water dispute of 1948 |
| **Calcutta** | To India | Bengal's port, industry and capital, though its hinterland in East Bengal grew the jute |
| **Murshidabad, Nadia** (Muslim majority) | To India | Khulna (non-Muslim majority) to Pakistan in exchange; Chittagong Hill Tracts (non-Muslim) also to Pakistan |

**Allegations of interference.** Pakistan has always held that Mountbatten influenced the Punjab line. In 1992 Christopher Beaumont, Radcliffe's private secretary, stated that the Ferozepur line was changed at a late stage under pressure. Radcliffe destroyed his papers. Indian writers reply that "other factors" (canals, railways, the Sikh shrines) justified the decisions.

Jinnah called the award unjust, incomprehensible and even perverse, but said that Pakistan, having agreed to abide by it, would do so.

## 10. Why did partition happen? Views to cite

| View | Held by |
|---|---|
| Muslims were a nation; partition was the logical end of their history | I.H. Qureshi; the official Pakistani account |
| British divide-and-rule created and fed the communal divide | Indian nationalist writers |
| Congress's refusal to share power (1937, 1946) made it unavoidable | Azad (in part), H.M. Seervai |
| Jinnah sought parity at the centre; Congress chose partition over a weak centre | Ayesha Jalal |
| British haste under Mountbatten turned a division into a catastrophe | Many historians, including Stanley Wolpert (*Shameful Flight*) |

A balanced conclusion: partition had long-term causes (identity, representative politics, economic competition) and short-term causes (1937–39, the war, the Cabinet Mission's failure, the violence of 1946–47). It was not inevitable in 1940. It was close to inevitable after August 1946.

## 11. Jinnah's leadership, 1937–1947 (the 2023 question)

| Quality | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Organiser | Rebuilt the League after 1937: mass membership, provincial branches, *Dawn* (weekly 1941, daily 1942), student and women's wings |
| Unifier | Brought the premiers of Punjab, Bengal and Assam into the League (1937); kept landlords, ulema, students and businessmen in one party |
| Strategist | Used the war: stayed out of Quit India, built ministries, insisted at Simla (1945) that the League alone spoke for Muslims |
| Negotiator | Gandhi–Jinnah talks; Cabinet Mission; accepted the 16 May plan to keep both Punjab and Bengal whole, then turned to Direct Action when it collapsed |
| Constitutionalist | Used law, argument and elections; mass action only once (1946) |
| Integrity | Opponents and British officials alike regarded him as incorruptible |
| Result | 109 Muslim seats in 1937 → about 428 in 1946 → a state in 1947 |

**Criticisms:** the demand was left vague for too long; the cost for Muslims who stayed in India; Direct Action opened the door to violence; the League had weak roots in the provinces that became Pakistan. See model answer 5.
