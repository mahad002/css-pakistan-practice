# Syllabus Map — Pakistan Affairs

The syllabus below is the FPSC "CE-2016 and onwards" syllabus for General Knowledge Paper III. It has **28 items**. I confirmed the list against two independent copies of the FPSC text ([eduinput.com](https://eduinput.com/?p=49913), [mkg.com.pk](https://mkg.com.pk/blog/css-pakistan-affairs-syllabus)). The official PDF is on fpsc.gov.pk under CSS → Syllabus; my tools could not open that site, so check the wording there once.

**Frequency** counts questions in the twelve papers from 2016 to 2026 (CSS 2016–2026 plus the 2023 special exam; 84 questions). A question can count under two items. See `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md` for the year-by-year list.

Tick the box when you have (a) read the notes, (b) done the MCQs, (c) written one timed answer.

## Reading the map

- **Highest return:** XXVII with federalism, XV/XII (democracy and political system), XVI (integration), VII/XXVI (economy), XIV/XIII (foreign policy and the US), XX (energy), XXIII/XXII (Kashmir and India), XXIV (Afghanistan).
- **History you cannot skip:** ideology with Iqbal and Jinnah; Sirhindi and Shah Waliullah; Aligarh; 1937–39; 1940–47.
- **In the syllabus but not asked since 2016:** nuclear programme (IV), Palestine (X), SAARC/ECO (V), challenges to sovereignty (XIX). These are due. Both the nuclear file and the Palestine file have a great deal of new material since 2025.

## FPSC suggested readings (for reference)

The syllabus lists these titles. You do not need to read them all. `10-Resources.md` says which are worth your time.

Adeney, *Federalism and Ethnic Conflict Regulation in India and Pakistan*; Candland, *Labor, Democratization and Development in India and Pakistan*; Chari et al., *Perception, Politics and Security in South Asia*; Cohen, *The Future of Pakistan*; Zahid Hussain, *Frontline Pakistan*; Bose and Jalal, *Modern South Asia*; Jalal, *Democracy and Authoritarianism in South Asia*; Lyon, *Conflict between India and Pakistan: An Encyclopedia*; Mass, *Back to Pakistan*; Newberg, *Judging the State*; Atiff Qureshi, *Pakistan: Manifest Destiny*; Riedel, *Deadly Embrace*; Schofield, *Kashmir in Conflict*; Niaz Shah, *Islamic Law and the Law of Armed Conflict*; Farzana Shaikh, *Making Sense of Pakistan*; Wynbrandt, *A Brief History of Pakistan*; Hathaway and Kugelman (eds.), *Powering Pakistan*; Alahdad, *Pakistan's Energy Sector: From Crisis to Crisis*.
