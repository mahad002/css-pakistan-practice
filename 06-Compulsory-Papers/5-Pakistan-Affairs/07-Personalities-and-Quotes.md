# Personalities, Scholars and Quotations

Three parts: (A) people to know, (B) scholars and what each argues, (C) quotations that are safe to use.

**Rule for the exam:** a correct paraphrase with the author's name earns the same credit as a quotation and carries no risk. Use a direct quotation only if it is in section C and you remember it exactly.

## B. Scholars and their arguments (paraphrase these)

### On the freedom movement and ideology

| Scholar and work | Argument in brief |
|---|---|
| **I.H. Qureshi**, *The Muslim Community of the Indo-Pakistan Subcontinent* (1962); *The Struggle for Pakistan* (1965) | Muslims formed a distinct community from the start of their history in India and resisted absorption; Pakistan was the natural outcome of that separate identity |
| **K.K. Aziz**, *The Making of Pakistan: A Study in Nationalism* (1967) | Muslim nationalism grew from historical, political, religious and cultural factors together. In *The Murder of History* (1993) he exposed factual errors and distortions in Pakistani textbooks |
| **Stanley Wolpert**, *Jinnah of Pakistan* (1984) | Jinnah's personal will and advocacy were decisive in creating the state. *Shameful Flight* (2006) blames Mountbatten's haste for the scale of partition's violence |
| **Ayesha Jalal**, *The Sole Spokesman* (1985) | Jinnah used the demand for Pakistan to win parity for Muslims at an all-India centre; partition in its final form was what Congress chose and Jinnah had tried to avoid. *The State of Martial Rule* (1990): early defence needs and alliances gave the bureaucracy and army dominance over parties |
| **Ian Talbot**, *Pakistan: A Modern History* (1998); *Provincial Politics and the Pakistan Movement* | The League's late and shallow roots in the Muslim-majority provinces, and its reliance on local elites, shaped Pakistan's later political weakness |
| **Khalid bin Sayeed**, *Pakistan: The Formative Phase* (1960) | The viceregal tradition and the League's organisation explain early Pakistani politics |
| **Sharif al Mujahid**, *Quaid-i-Azam Jinnah: Studies in Interpretation* | Jinnah's vision was consistently of a Muslim state informed by Islamic principles |
| **S.M. Ikram**, *Modern Muslim India and the Birth of Pakistan* | Traces the intellectual line from Shah Waliullah and Sir Syed to Iqbal and Jinnah |
| **Aziz Ahmad**, *Studies in Islamic Culture in the Indian Environment* | Two streams in Indo-Muslim history: synthesis with India and insistence on distinctness |
| **Yohanan Friedmann**, *Shaykh Ahmad Sirhindi* (1971) | Sirhindi's political influence has been overstated; he was first a Sufi thinker |
| **Francis Robinson**, *Separatism among Indian Muslims* (1974) | The interests of the UP Muslim elite and British policy shaped separatism |
| **H.M. Seervai**, *Partition of India: Legend and Reality* | Congress leaders bear major responsibility for partition |
| **Maulana Azad**, *India Wins Freedom* (1959; full text 1988) | Congress errors in 1937 and 1946 made partition possible |
| **R. Coupland**, *The Constitutional Problem in India* (1942–43) | Describes the working of the 1935 Act and the Congress ministries |
| **W.W. Hunter**, *The Indian Musalmans* (1871) | The ruin of Bengal's Muslims under British rule |
| **Farzana Shaikh**, *Making Sense of Pakistan* (2009) | Uncertainty about the role of Islam in national identity lies behind many of Pakistan's problems |
| **Venkat Dhulipala**, *Creating a New Medina* (2015) | Contrary to Jalal, Pakistan was widely debated and clearly imagined as a state among UP Muslims |

## C. Quotations that are safe to use

Wording checked against standard published texts. Minor variations exist between editions; the sense is not in doubt.

### Quaid-i-Azam

1. "The problem in India is not of an inter-communal character, but manifestly of an international one, and it must be treated as such." — Presidential address, Lahore, 22 March 1940.
2. "The Hindus and Muslims belong to two different religious philosophies, social customs, literatures. They neither intermarry nor interdine together and, indeed, they belong to two different civilisations which are based mainly on conflicting ideas and conceptions." — Lahore, 22 March 1940.
3. "Mussalmans are a nation according to any definition of a nation, and they must have their homelands, their territory and their state." — Lahore, 22 March 1940.
4. "You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other place of worship in this State of Pakistan. You may belong to any religion or caste or creed — that has nothing to do with the business of the State." — Constituent Assembly, 11 August 1947.
5. "Pakistan is not going to be a theocratic State — to be ruled by priests with a divine mission." — Broadcast to the people of the United States, February 1948.
6. On the Rajagopalachari formula, 1944: it offered "a shadow and a husk, a maimed, mutilated and moth-eaten Pakistan".
7. On the Radcliffe Award (broadcast, 31 August 1947): it was "an unjust, incomprehensible and even perverse award", but Pakistan had agreed to abide by it.

**Use with care (commonly attributed; wording varies or source uncertain):** Kashmir as the "jugular vein" of Pakistan; "Unity, Faith, Discipline" (the motto is authentic; the order of words has been debated); "Think a hundred times before you take a decision…"; "parting of the ways" (reported remark after the Calcutta Convention, 1928). Attribute them as "is reported to have said".

### Allama Iqbal

8. "I would like to see the Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sind and Baluchistan amalgamated into a single State. Self-government within the British Empire, or without the British Empire, the formation of a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim State appears to me to be the final destiny of the Muslims, at least of North-West India." — Allahabad, 29 December 1930.
9. From his letter to Jinnah, 21 June 1937 (paraphrase is safer): he asked why the Muslims of north-west India and Bengal should not be considered as nations entitled to self-determination, like other nations in India and outside it.

### Others

10. G.K. Gokhale on Jinnah (as quoted by Sarojini Naidu): "He has true stuff in him, and that freedom from all sectarian prejudice which will make him the best ambassador of Hindu–Muslim unity."
11. Stanley Wolpert, *Jinnah of Pakistan*: "Few individuals significantly alter the course of history. Fewer still modify the map of the world. Hardly anyone can be credited with creating a nation-state. Mohammad Ali Jinnah did all three."
12. Liaquat Ali Khan on the Objectives Resolution (1949): he considered it "the most important occasion in the life of this country, next in importance only to the achievement of independence".
13. Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (1965): if India built the bomb, "we will eat grass or leaves, even go hungry, but we will get one of our own".
14. Beverley Nichols, *Verdict on India* (1944), called Jinnah "the most important man in Asia".
15. UNDP, *Human Development Report 1994*, on human security: "freedom from fear and freedom from want".

### Documents to cite instead of people

The Lahore Resolution (1940); the Objectives Resolution (1949); the Constitution (cite articles); UN Security Council Resolution 47 (1948) on Kashmir; Resolutions 242 (1967) and 2803 (2025) on Palestine; the Indus Waters Treaty (1960), Art. IX and XII; the Simla Agreement (1972); the Water Apportionment Accord (1991); the 7th NFC Award (2009); the National Security Policy (2022); the Economic Survey 2025–26; the Hamoodur Rahman Commission Report.

### What not to do

- Do not invent a quotation and attach a famous name to it.
- Do not quote statistics to two decimal places unless they are from the latest Economic Survey or census.
- Do not use Urdu verses unless you can write them correctly and they fit the argument; one apt couplet of Iqbal in an ideology answer is enough.
