# Resources — The Few That Matter

You work full-time and have about three hours a week for this paper. The notes in this folder are meant to be your main text. The list below is short on purpose: what to add, why, and how much of each to read.

Prices and editions change; buy the latest edition. For someone outside Pakistan, most of these are available as e-books or can be ordered from Pakistani booksellers who ship abroad.

## 1. Core books (buy or borrow these three or four)

| Book | Use it for | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ian Talbot, *Pakistan: A Modern History*** | The best single-volume history from the freedom movement to the 2000s. Balanced and well sourced | Read once, a chapter a week, in Phase 1–2. Take one page of notes per chapter. This is your main "scholarly reference" |
| **Hamid Khan, *Constitutional and Political History of Pakistan*** | Constitutions, amendments, cases, each government's record | Do not read cover to cover (it is long). Use it as a reference for files 09–11 of the history notes and file 19 of the contemporary notes. Get the latest edition for coverage of the 26th and 27th Amendments |
| **Abdul Sattar, *Pakistan's Foreign Policy: A Concise History*** | Foreign policy from 1947, by a former foreign minister | Read the chapters on the pacts, China, 1971, Afghanistan, the nuclear issue and post-9/11. Skim the rest |
| **I.H. Qureshi, *The Struggle for Pakistan*** *or* **K.K. Aziz, *The Making of Pakistan*** | The traditional account of the freedom movement, which examiners know well | Pick one. Read in Phase 1 alongside history files 06–08 |

## 2. One step further (choose according to your weak areas)

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| Ayesha Jalal, *The Sole Spokesman* | The revisionist reading of Jinnah. Read the introduction and conclusion so that you can state her argument fairly |
| Stanley Wolpert, *Jinnah of Pakistan* | Readable biography; good for the 1937–47 question |
| S.M. Ikram, *Modern Muslim India and the Birth of Pakistan* | Reformers and educational movements in more depth |
| Ishrat Husain, *Governing the Ungovernable* | Institutions and the economy; excellent for essays too |
| Hasan-Askari Rizvi, *Military, State and Society in Pakistan* | Civil–military relations, written carefully |
| Farzana Shaikh, *Making Sense of Pakistan* (on the FPSC list) | Identity and ideology; strong for critical answers |
| Victoria Schofield, *Kashmir in Conflict* (on the FPSC list) | Kashmir from 1846 to the 2000s |
| Zahid Hussain, *No-Win War* | Pakistan, the USA and Afghanistan after 2001 |
| Anatol Lieven, *Pakistan: A Hard Country* | Society, provinces and the state; lively and useful for national integration |
| Katharine Adeney, *Federalism and Ethnic Conflict Regulation in India and Pakistan* (on the FPSC list) | Federalism and ethnicity; read the Pakistan chapters |

Most of the other titles on the FPSC "suggested readings" list are dated or marginal. You do not need them.

## 3. Guidebooks: a caution

Pakistani guidebooks for this paper (for example the compilations by Ikram Rabbani, M.R. Kazimi's *A Concise History of Pakistan*, or the Jahangir's World Times series) are widely used and are handy for MCQ facts and for seeing how local candidates frame answers. Examiners have complained for years that candidates reproduce guidebook answers without analysis. If you buy one, use it for facts and past-paper MCQs only. Do not copy its style.

## 4. Free official sources (essential for the contemporary half)

| Source | What to take from it | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Pakistan Economic Survey** — finance.gov.pk | The "Highlights" and "Overview" (about 20 pages). Note 15 figures: growth, sector shares, inflation, exports, remittances, reserves, debt, poverty, literacy, education and health spending, population, power capacity | Each June. The Survey of **June 2027** is the one the 2028 examiner will expect |
| **Budget Speech and Budget in Brief** — finance.gov.pk | Total outlay, tax target, interest, defence, development, deficit | Each June |
| **Constitution of Pakistan** — the National Assembly's site (na.gov.pk) publishes the updated text | Read the Preamble, Arts. 1–40, and the articles listed in history file 10. Check the text of the 26th and 27th Amendments and anything later | Once, then check for new amendments in late 2027 |
| **FPSC** — fpsc.gov.pk | Syllabus, past papers, examiners' reports in the annual reports, rules | Before applying, and to verify the format |
| **State Bank of Pakistan** — sbp.org.pk | Annual Report's executive summary; monthly data on reserves and remittances | Twice a year |
| **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics** — pbs.gov.pk | 2023 census results; labour force and household surveys | Once |
| **National Security Policy 2022–26** (summary) | Definitions for security answers | Once |
| **UN documents** — un.org | Text of Security Council resolutions on Kashmir (47) and Palestine (242, 2803) | When preparing those topics |
| **IMF country page for Pakistan** — imf.org | Press releases after each programme review: a one-page official summary of the economy | After each review |

## 5. Newspapers and analysis (free)

| Source | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **Dawn** — dawn.com | Editorials and the op-ed page, three or four times a week. Writers worth following for this paper: on foreign policy and security, Maleeha Lodhi, Zahid Hussain, Muhammad Amir Rana; on the economy, Khurram Husain and the Business pages; on law and the constitution, the legal columnists. Clip one article a week into the matching contemporary file |
| **The Express Tribune, The News, Business Recorder** | For news detail and budget coverage |
| **Institute of Strategic Studies Islamabad (issi.org.pk); Islamabad Policy Research Institute (ipripak.org); Institute of Policy Studies (ips.org.pk)** | Issue briefs on foreign policy, water, energy — written at exactly the level of a CSS answer |
| **PIDE (pide.org.pk)** | Short policy notes on the economy, energy, governance |
| **PILDAT (pildat.org)** | Reports on democracy, parliament and civil–military relations |
| **Pak Institute for Peace Studies (pakpips.com); CRSS; PICSS** | Annual security reports: the terrorism figures to quote |
| **International: Chatham House, Crisis Group, CSIS, Carnegie, Al Jazeera explainers, BBC** | For balanced outside views on Pakistan's politics, India–Pakistan and Afghanistan |
| **Story of Pakistan (storyofpakistan.com); the National Archives' Quaid-i-Azam pages** | Short entries on events and personalities of the freedom movement; texts of Jinnah's speeches |

## 6. Past papers

- **Official:** fpsc.gov.pk → CSS → Past Papers (PDFs by year).
- **Convenient lists with attempted solutions:** [cssprepforum.com](https://cssprepforum.com/css-2016-solved-pakistan-affairs-past-paper/) (one page per year, 2010–2025); [cssprep.com.pk](https://cssprep.com.pk/css-pakistan-affairs-past-papers-desc/) (2026 paper and a trend table). Treat the "solved" answers there as other candidates' attempts, not as models.
- **Discussion:** cssforum.com.pk has threads on each year's paper and on preparation strategy. Advice there varies in quality.
- **MCQs:** the FPSC past papers themselves are the best source. For the MPT, use the solved papers of earlier screening tests.

## 7. A weekly routine that fits three hours

| Slot | Time | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 min | Read one notes file (or half of a long one). Mark what you cannot recall |
| 2 | 30 min | MCQs for that topic from `08-MCQ-Bank.md`; add five of your own |
| 3 | 45 min | One timed answer or one detailed outline from "likely questions" |
| 4 | 30 min | Dawn op-eds; add two or three dated lines to the "current position" of a contemporary file |
| 5 | 15 min | Revise the previous week's card in `09-Quick-Revision.md` |

From November 2027 swap slot 1 for a second timed answer.

## 8. Getting your answers checked

You are studying alone. Marks in this paper depend heavily on structure and relevance, which are hard to judge in your own work. Options:

- Self-mark against the template in `00-How-to-Use-This-Pack.md` using a simple rubric: relevance (5), argument (4), evidence (4), analysis (4), structure and conclusion (3).
- Exchange scripts with one or two other candidates online.
- Paid evaluation by a CSS mentor for four to six answers near the end is worth considering; you do not need a full coaching course for this paper.
- Ask an AI assistant to mark an answer against the rubric and the relevant notes file, and to list missing facts. Check any fact it adds.
