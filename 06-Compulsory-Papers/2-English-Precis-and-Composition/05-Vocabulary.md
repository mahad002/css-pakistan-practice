# Vocabulary: Synonyms and Antonyms (Q1, 20 marks)

Part I of the paper is 20 MCQs on an OMR sheet, done in the first 30 minutes. Ten ask for the word **closest** in meaning to a word in capitals. Ten ask for the word **most opposite** in meaning. Four options each. One mark each. No negative marking is stated on the papers examined.

Contents:

1. How the MCQs work, and how to answer them
2. What the words have been like, year by year
3. Section A: every MCQ word found in the 2014 and 2016–2026 papers (224 words)
4. Section B: 355 further high-yield words, grouped by meaning
5. Roots, prefixes and suffixes
6. Study method: a spaced-repetition schedule

Total in this file: 579 words, each with meaning, synonym and antonym.

---

## 1. How the MCQs work

### 1.1 The format

```
Choose the word that is nearly most similar in meaning to the capitalized word.
1. EPHEMERAL    (a) eternal   (b) fleeting   (c) spiritual   (d) costly
...
Choose the word that is nearly most opposite in meaning to the capitalized word.
11. TACITURN    (a) reserved  (b) loquacious (c) sullen      (d) tactful
```

### 1.2 The traps

| Trap | Example | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| A synonym offered in the antonym half (and the reverse) | TACITURN, opposite: "reserved" is listed first | Before each half, write a large S or A on the question paper. Re-read the instruction at item 11 |
| A word that looks or sounds like the key word | TORTUOUS / torturous; VENAL / venial; INGENIOUS / ingenuous | Learn the confused pairs in `07-Pairs-of-Words.md` |
| A word with two meanings, tested on the rarer one | ESSAY = attempt; PALL = become dull; QUARRY = prey; PEAKED = pale | Check whether the options are verbs, nouns or adjectives. They tell you which sense is meant |
| Options that are all vaguely positive or negative | | Decide the charge of the key word (good, bad, neutral) and its strength, then match |
| A rare word nobody knows | ACCUBATION, CROMULENT, NUMMARY | Use roots and elimination. Guess. Do not spend more than 40 seconds |

### 1.3 A method for each item

1. Cover the options. Say a meaning of the key word to yourself.
2. Check the part of speech from the options.
3. Strike out options that are clearly wrong. Usually two go at once.
4. Between the last two, choose the one that could replace the key word in a sentence (synonym) or that makes the exact opposite sentence (antonym).
5. If you do not know the word: look for a root (section 5), a similar word you know, or the tone of the word. Then guess. A blank earns nothing.
6. Mark the OMR sheet carefully. Fill the bubble for the right item number.

### 1.4 What score to expect

| Kind of year | Examples | Realistic score with this file learned |
|---|---|---|
| Standard advanced vocabulary (GRE level) | 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2026 | 13–17 |
| Mixed, some rare words | 2016, 2017, 2018, 2022 | 10–14 |
| Mostly rare or literary words | 2021, 2025 | 6–10 |

You cannot prepare for a 2025-type paper by learning rarer and rarer words. The return is too low. Learn the 579 words here thoroughly. Your GRE preparation covers the same ground. Count on 11 or more, and make your margin in Part II.

**Source note.** The official FPSC papers could not be opened. The word lists and year labels come from third-party past-paper sites (cssprep.com.pk and cssprepforum.com). Their year labels are usually right but not guaranteed, and their answer keys contain errors. The meanings, synonyms and antonyms below are written from standard dictionary senses, not copied from those keys.

## 6. Study method: spaced repetition

### 6.1 The cards

Make one card per word (paper cards, or Anki on your phone, which is quick to set up).

| Side | Content |
|---|---|
| Front | The word only |
| Back | Meaning in five words or fewer; one synonym; one antonym; one short sentence of your own |

Writing your own sentence is the step that makes the word stick. Do not skip it.

### 6.2 The schedule

New words per day: 8. Time: 15 minutes a day, every day. At 8 a day, 579 words take about 73 days, so the list is complete in under three months and the rest of the year is review.

| Review | When | If you know it | If you do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same day, evening | Move to box 2 | Stays in box 1 |
| 2 | Next day | Move to box 3 | Back to box 1 |
| 3 | 3 days later | Move to box 4 | Back to box 1 |
| 4 | 7 days later | Move to box 5 | Back to box 1 |
| 5 | 16 days later | Move to box 6 | Back to box 1 |
| 6 | 35 days later | Move to box 7 | Back to box 1 |
| 7 | Every 2 months until the exam | Stays | Back to box 1 |

With paper cards this is the Leitner box system: seven envelopes. Anki does the same thing automatically.

### 6.3 Order of learning

| Weeks (from February 2027) | Words | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | 1–224 | Section A, past-paper words |
| 5–7 | 225–400 | Section B, groups B1 to B9 |
| 8–10 | 401–579 | Section B, groups B10 to B17 |
| 11 onwards | Reviews only, plus new words you meet in reading (limit: 3 a day) | Dawn editorials, your GRE list |

### 6.4 Weekly self-test

Every Sunday, 10 minutes:

1. Take 20 cards at random from boxes 3 and above.
2. Write a synonym and an antonym for each without looking.
3. Score out of 40. Record it. Below 30 means you are adding new words too fast. Slow down to 5 a day.

### 6.5 Turn the list into MCQs

In the last three months, test in exam form. For each word, cover the page and ask: "Which of these four is closest?" using the other words in the same group as wrong options. The five mock papers in `11-Mock-Papers/` each have 20 MCQs built from this file.

### 6.6 Reading

Vocabulary learned only from lists fades. Read one editorial a day from a quality newspaper (Dawn is free online) for ten minutes. Underline two words. Add them to the cards if they are not already there. This reading also feeds the précis, the comprehension and the essay paper.
