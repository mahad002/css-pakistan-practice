## 2. Direct and indirect narration

### 2.1 What the papers asked

In 2016 and 2018 the task was a short dialogue (two speakers, about ten lines) with the instruction to rewrite it in a paragraph in indirect speech, for 5 marks. Single sentences for conversion have not been set in this period, but reported-speech errors appear in Q4 almost every year.

### 2.2 The four changes

| Change | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Reporting verb | *said to* becomes *told* (statements), *asked* (questions), *ordered / requested / advised* (commands), *exclaimed / wished* (exclamations) | He said to me, "Wait." → He told me to wait. |
| 2. Tense | If the reporting verb is past, move each tense one step back | see table below |
| 3. Pronouns | First person follows the speaker; second person follows the listener; third person stays | He said to me, "I like your pen." → He told me that he liked my pen. |
| 4. Time and place words | Near becomes far | see table below |

**Tense shift (reporting verb in the past)**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| is / am / are | was / were |
| writes | wrote |
| is writing | was writing |
| has written | had written |
| has been writing | had been writing |
| wrote | had written |
| was writing | had been writing |
| had written | had written (no change) |
| will / shall | would |
| can / may | could / might |
| must | had to (or must, for a standing rule) |
| would, should, could, might, ought to | no change |

No tense change when: the reporting verb is present or future ("He says..."); the statement is a permanent truth or habit ("The teacher said that the earth is round"); the statement is an unreal condition ("He said, 'If I were rich, I would help'" stays).

**Time and place words**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the previous week |
| next month | the following month |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |
| come | go (when the place changes) |

### 2.3 Sentence types

| Type | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | She said, "I am tired." | She said that she was tired. |
| Yes/no question | He said to me, "Do you know her?" | He asked me whether I knew her. |
| Wh- question | She said, "Where do you live?" | She asked where I lived. |
| Command | The officer said, "Open the gate." | The officer ordered them to open the gate. |
| Negative command | Mother said, "Do not go out." | Mother forbade me to go out. / Mother told me not to go out. |
| Request | He said, "Please lend me your pen." | He requested me to lend him my pen. |
| Advice | The doctor said, "Take rest." | The doctor advised him to take rest. |
| Suggestion | He said, "Let us go for a walk." | He suggested that they should go for a walk. |
| Exclamation (joy) | He said, "Hurrah! We have won." | He exclaimed with joy that they had won. |
| Exclamation (sorrow) | She said, "Alas! I am ruined." | She exclaimed with sorrow that she was ruined. |
| Exclamation (wonder) | He said, "What a fine building!" | He exclaimed that it was a very fine building. |
| Wish | She said, "May you live long!" | She prayed that I might live long. |
| Greeting | He said, "Good morning, sir." | He respectfully wished him good morning. |
| Yes / No | He said, "Yes, I will." | He replied in the affirmative. / He said that he would. |
| Thanks | She said, "Thank you." | She thanked him. |

Remember: an indirect question has statement word order and no question mark.

### 2.4 Turning a dialogue into a paragraph

1. Identify the speakers. Use names or "the former / the latter" sparingly.
2. Report each turn with a suitable verb. Vary the verbs: *asked, replied, added, explained, pointed out, admitted, objected, suggested, agreed, inquired, remarked, observed, assured, warned*.
3. Join short turns: "Ali asked..., and Bilal replied that...".
4. Drop fillers (well, oh, you know) or express them with an adverb (hesitantly, with surprise).
5. Keep it one paragraph, past tense, third person, no quotation marks.

**Worked example**

> **Asad:** Hello, Hamza. Where are you going in such a hurry?
> **Hamza:** I am going to the library. My examination starts tomorrow.
> **Asad:** Why did you not prepare earlier?
> **Hamza:** I was ill last week. I could not study at all.
> **Asad:** Do you need my notes?
> **Hamza:** Yes, please. That would be a great help.
> **Asad:** Come to my house this evening and take them.
> **Hamza:** Thank you. I shall never forget your kindness.

*Paragraph:* Asad greeted Hamza and asked him where he was going in such a hurry. Hamza replied that he was going to the library because his examination started the next day. Asad asked why he had not prepared earlier, and Hamza explained that he had been ill the previous week and had not been able to study at all. Asad then asked whether he needed his notes. Hamza replied gratefully that he did and said that they would be a great help. Asad told him to come to his house that evening and take them. Hamza thanked him and said that he would never forget his kindness.

## 3. Active and passive voice

Not set as a separate question in 2016–2026, but passive forms are tested in sentence correction, and you need them for the précis and translation.

### 3.1 Forms

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | He writes a letter. | A letter is written by him. |
| Present continuous | He is writing a letter. | A letter is being written by him. |
| Present perfect | He has written a letter. | A letter has been written by him. |
| Past simple | He wrote a letter. | A letter was written by him. |
| Past continuous | He was writing a letter. | A letter was being written by him. |
| Past perfect | He had written a letter. | A letter had been written by him. |
| Future simple | He will write a letter. | A letter will be written by him. |
| Future perfect | He will have written a letter. | A letter will have been written by him. |
| Modal | He can write a letter. | A letter can be written by him. |
| Infinitive | I want you to write a letter. | I want a letter to be written by you. |

The perfect continuous and future continuous tenses are not normally put into the passive.

### 3.2 Special patterns

| Pattern | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Command | Shut the door. | Let the door be shut. / You are ordered to shut the door. |
| Negative command | Do not waste time. | Let time not be wasted. / You are advised not to waste time. |
| Request | Please help me. | You are requested to help me. |
| Question (do/does/did) | Did he break the glass? | Was the glass broken by him? |
| Wh- question | Who wrote this book? | By whom was this book written? |
| Two objects | He gave me a pen. | I was given a pen by him. / A pen was given to me by him. |
| Verb + preposition | They laughed at him. | He was laughed at by them. |
| "People say that..." | People say that he is rich. | It is said that he is rich. / He is said to be rich. |
| Verbs taking other prepositions than *by* | His conduct surprised me. | I was surprised **at** his conduct. |
| | The news pleased her. | She was pleased **with** the news. |
| | I know him. | He is known **to** me. |
| | This box contains books. | Books are contained **in** this box. |
| Intransitive verbs | He died. The sun rose. | No passive. |

## 4. Analogies

Set in 2016 and 2017: eight items, attempt five, four options each, 5 marks. The item gives a pair in capitals. You choose the option pair with the same relationship.

### 4.1 Method

1. Make a short sentence that states the relationship exactly: "A LION makes the sound called a ROAR."
2. Put each option into the same sentence, in the same order.
3. If two options fit, make your sentence more precise.
4. Watch the order. CLOT : BLOOD is not the same as BLOOD : CLOT.
5. Watch the part of speech. A noun : verb pair needs a noun : verb answer.

### 4.2 Relationship types

| Type | Example | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Creature : sound | LION : ROAR :: frog : croak | A lion roars. |
| Synonyms | EXPEDITE : HASTEN :: hinder : obstruct | To expedite is to hasten. |
| Antonyms | SHADOW : LIGHT :: silence : sound | Shadow is the absence of light. |
| Part : whole | PAGE : BOOK :: brick : wall | A page is part of a book. |
| Raw material : product | WOOD : FURNITURE :: flour : bread | Furniture is made of wood. |
| Worker : tool | SURGEON : SCALPEL :: carpenter : saw | A surgeon uses a scalpel. |
| Worker : quality | SURGEON : DEXTEROUS :: judge : impartial | A surgeon must be dexterous. |
| Person : ruling passion | LECHER : LUST :: miser : greed | A lecher is driven by lust. |
| Emotion : action | FEARFUL : COWER :: angry : rant | A fearful person cowers. |
| Mass : coagulated form | CLOT : BLOOD :: curd : milk | A clot is thickened blood. |
| Degree | WARM : HOT :: damp : soaked | Hot is a stronger degree of warm. |
| Cause : effect | VIRUS : DISEASE :: spark : fire | A virus causes disease. |
| Category : member | BIRD : SPARROW :: tree : oak | A sparrow is a kind of bird. |
| Thing : place | SHIP : HARBOUR :: aircraft : hangar | A ship is kept in a harbour. |
| Thing : measure | TEMPERATURE : THERMOMETER :: pressure : barometer | A thermometer measures temperature. |
| Study : subject | BOTANY : PLANTS :: zoology : animals | Botany is the study of plants. |
| Young : adult | CALF : COW :: cub : lion | A calf is a young cow. |
| Group : member | FLOCK : SHEEP :: swarm : bees | A flock is a group of sheep. |
| Doer : act | THIEF : STEAL :: judge : adjudicate | A thief steals. |
| Symbol : meaning | DOVE : PEACE :: scales : justice | A dove stands for peace. |

### 4.3 Practice

Choose the pair that best matches the relationship.

1. AUTHOR : NOVEL :: (a) painter : brush (b) composer : symphony (c) reader : book (d) actor : stage
2. DROUGHT : FAMINE :: (a) rain : cloud (b) flood : river (c) infection : fever (d) hunger : food
3. MISER : GENEROSITY :: (a) coward : courage (b) soldier : discipline (c) judge : law (d) scholar : books
4. WHISPER : SHOUT :: (a) sprint : jog (b) glance : stare (c) weep : laugh (d) walk : path
5. PETAL : FLOWER :: (a) tree : forest (b) ink : pen (c) spoke : wheel (d) water : river
6. CARPENTER : CHISEL :: (a) tailor : cloth (b) farmer : crop (c) sculptor : statue (d) mason : trowel
7. OBSTINATE : YIELD :: (a) timid : fear (b) taciturn : talk (c) greedy : want (d) loyal : serve
8. LIBRARY : BOOKS :: (a) granary : grain (b) school : teacher (c) road : car (d) bank : clerk
9. EPHEMERAL : LASTING :: (a) rapid : fast (b) frugal : thrifty (c) scarce : abundant (d) cold : icy
10. CARTOGRAPHER : MAPS :: (a) lexicographer : dictionaries (b) surgeon : patients (c) astronomer : stars (d) pilot : aircraft
11. PROLOGUE : PLAY :: (a) chapter : book (b) preface : book (c) epilogue : novel (d) index : book
12. HERD : CATTLE :: (a) pack : wolves (b) lion : pride (c) fish : school (d) bee : hive

**Answers**

1 (b) A composer creates a symphony as an author creates a novel.
2 (c) Drought causes famine; infection causes fever.
3 (a) A miser lacks generosity; a coward lacks courage.
4 (b) A shout is a much stronger form of a whisper; a stare is a much stronger form of a glance. (Option (a) is a degree pair too, but in the reverse order: strong to weak.)
5 (c) A petal is one of several like parts of a flower; a spoke is one of several like parts of a wheel.
6 (d) A mason works with a trowel as a carpenter works with a chisel. (Cloth and crop are materials or products, not tools.)
7 (b) An obstinate person does not yield; a taciturn person does not talk.
8 (a) A library stores books; a granary stores grain.
9 (c) Antonyms.
10 (a) A cartographer makes maps; a lexicographer makes dictionaries.
11 (b) A prologue comes before a play; a preface comes before a book.
12 (a) A herd is a group of cattle; a pack is a group of wolves. (The others are in the reverse order.)

## 5. Grouping of words

### 5.1 What the syllabus says

Twenty words of moderate difficulty are given at random. You sort them into ten pairs of words with **similar** meanings or **opposite** meanings, as the question directs. Ten marks, so one mark a pair.

No paper from 2016 to 2026 that could be checked has set this. If it appears, it is easy marks for anyone who has learned `05-Vocabulary.md`, because that file is arranged in synonym groups.

### 5.2 Method

1. Read the instruction: similar or opposite?
2. Mark each word with its part of speech (n, v, adj). Pairs share a part of speech.
3. Pair the certain ones first and cross them off.
4. The hard words pair with each other by elimination.
5. Write the answer as a clean list of ten pairs. Check that every word is used once.

### 5.3 Practice set 1 (similar meanings)

Group these twenty words into ten pairs of similar meaning:

> frugal, candid, obstinate, abundant, timid, thrifty, hostile, brief, frank, weary, stubborn, plentiful, concise, fearful, antagonistic, tired, praise, hinder, laud, obstruct

### 5.4 Practice set 2 (opposite meanings)

Group these twenty words into ten pairs of opposite meaning:

> transient, scarce, expand, humble, permanent, arrogant, reveal, contract, copious, conceal, generous, ancient, reckless, modern, miserly, cautious, praise, victory, censure, defeat

### 5.5 Practice set 3 (similar meanings, harder)

> ephemeral, loquacious, placate, indolent, fleeting, garrulous, appease, slothful, mendacious, lucid, untruthful, clear, zenith, wane, peak, decline, erudite, novice, learned, beginner

### 5.6 Practice set 4 (opposite meanings, harder)

> benevolent, verbose, acquit, opulent, malevolent, laconic, convict, destitute, exacerbate, rigid, alleviate, pliant, clandestine, ally, overt, adversary, zeal, mutable, apathy, constant

**Answers**

Set 1: frugal–thrifty; candid–frank; obstinate–stubborn; abundant–plentiful; timid–fearful; hostile–antagonistic; brief–concise; weary–tired; praise–laud; hinder–obstruct.

Set 2: transient–permanent; scarce–copious; expand–contract; humble–arrogant; reveal–conceal; generous–miserly; ancient–modern; reckless–cautious; praise–censure; victory–defeat.

Set 3: ephemeral–fleeting; loquacious–garrulous; placate–appease; indolent–slothful; mendacious–untruthful; lucid–clear; zenith–peak; wane–decline; erudite–learned; novice–beginner.

## 6. Sentence completion (mixed blanks)

In 2017 the paper gave eight sentences with a blank and four options each, and asked candidates to rewrite five with the blank filled. Four tested prepositions. The other four tested a word choice (*acknowledged* receipt), a short response (*neither do I*), a modal (*ought to*) and a tense (*was digging*).

### 6.1 What such blanks test

| Point | Example | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Collocation (words that go together) | The manager ___ receipt of my letter. | acknowledged |
| | He ___ a crime. / She ___ a mistake. | committed / made |
| | to ___ attention, to ___ a visit, to ___ a decision | pay, pay, take or make |
| Short agreement | I do not like coffee, and ___. | neither do I |
| | She can swim, and ___. | so can I |
| Modal meaning | We ___ respect our elders. (duty) | ought to / should |

### 6.2 Practice

Fill each blank with the best word or phrase.

1. He ___ a grudge against his former partner. (holds / bears / keeps / takes)
2. Hardly had the game begun ___ it started to rain. (than / when / then / that)
3. I have not seen her ___ she left for Dubai. (for / from / since / till)
4. The accused ___ the charge. (refused / denied / rejected / declined)
5. She speaks English fluently, and ___ her brother. (so does / so is / neither does / also)
6. If I ___ you, I would not sign it. (am / was / were / be)
7. The government must ___ steps to control prices. (do / make / take / give)
8. He ___ to smoke, but he gave it up last year. (was used / used / uses / is used)
9. Neither of the two candidates ___ suitable. (are / were / is / have been)

## 7. Vocabulary from the passage

In 2016 one comprehension item (5 marks) listed five meanings and asked for the words in the passage that matched them. The reverse form (give the meaning of underlined words) was common before 2016.

Method:

1. Note the part of speech of the given meaning. "To grow smaller" needs a verb.
2. Scan the passage for words of that kind that you would not use every day.
3. Test by substitution: put the meaning in place of the word. The sentence must still make sense.
4. Give the word exactly as it appears in the passage.
5. For "give the meaning" items, give the meaning that fits the passage, in the same grammatical form (a verb for a verb), in a word or short phrase.

Practice: for each practice passage in `02-Comprehension.md`, pick five uncommon words and write a meaning that fits the context. Then check a dictionary.
