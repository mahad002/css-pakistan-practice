# Grammar and Sentence Correction (Q4, 10 marks)

Eight sentences are given. You correct any five. Each is worth 2 marks.

The syllabus says each sentence has "a clear structural flaw in terms of grammar or punctuation", that you must make only the needed correction, and that 2–3 sentences will turn on punctuation.

Contents:

1. How to answer Q4
2. What past papers tested
3. The rules (17 families), each with examples
4. 150 practice sentences
5. Answers with the rule applied

---

## 1. How to answer Q4

1. Read all eight. Mark the five where you can name the error.
2. Rewrite the whole sentence. Do not write only the changed word.
3. Change only what is wrong. Do not improve style. Do not replace words that were correct.
4. Some sentences have two errors (2021–2026 papers often did). Read to the end before you write.
5. If two corrections are possible, write one clean version. Do not give alternatives with a slash.
6. Underlining the corrected word is optional. Naming the rule is not required.
7. Number your answers with the numbers on the question paper: (i), (iii), (iv)...

A fixed checking order for any sentence:

| Pass | Look at | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Subject and verb | Do they agree? Is something between them misleading me? |
| 2 | Verb tense | Does the time word (since, yesterday, by then, when) match the tense? |
| 3 | Pronouns | Right case (I/me, who/whom)? Clear reference? Agrees in number? |
| 4 | Prepositions | Right one after this verb or adjective? One too many? One missing? |
| 5 | Articles and nouns | Countable or uncountable? Missing "the" or "a"? |
| 6 | Opening phrase | If it starts with an -ing or -ed phrase, does the subject right after the comma do that action? |
| 7 | Pairs and lists | Not only/but also, either/or, lists: same grammatical form on both sides? |
| 8 | Connectors | Although...but, no sooner...than, hardly...when, so...that |
| 9 | Comparison | -er/-est doubled? "Other" missing? Like compared with like? |
| 10 | Punctuation | Comma splice, apostrophe, question mark in an indirect question |

## 3. The rules

### Rule 1. Subject–verb agreement

The verb agrees with its real subject, not with the nearest noun.

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| A phrase between subject and verb does not change the number | The list of items are on the desk. | The list of items **is** on the desk. |
| *as well as, along with, together with, with, in addition to, besides*: verb agrees with the first subject | The captain, with his players, were late. | The captain, with his players, **was** late. |
| *either... or, neither... nor, or*: verb agrees with the nearer subject | Neither the teacher nor the students was ready. | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** ready. |
| *each, every, either, neither, everyone, anybody, nobody, one of* take a singular verb | Each of the players have a kit. | Each of the players **has** a kit. |
| *a number of* + plural verb; *the number of* + singular verb | The number of cars are rising. | The number of cars **is** rising. |
| Two nouns for one thing or person: singular | The poet and philosopher are dead. (one man) | The poet and philosopher **is** dead. |
| Amounts, distances, sums as one unit: singular | Five thousand rupees are a fair price. | Five thousand rupees **is** a fair price. |
| Nouns plural in form, singular in sense: *news, mathematics, physics, politics, economics, measles* | The news are bad. | The news **is** bad. |
| Nouns singular in form, plural in sense: *cattle, people, police, poultry* | The police has arrived. | The police **have** arrived. |
| *many a* + singular noun + singular verb | Many a man have tried. | Many a man **has** tried. |
| Relative clause: verb agrees with the antecedent | He is one of those who works hard. | He is one of those who **work** hard. |
| *There is / there are* agrees with the noun that follows | There is many problems. | There **are** many problems. |
| Collective noun as a unit: singular verb and singular pronoun. Do not mix | The jury has given their verdict. | The jury has given **its** verdict. |

### Rule 2. Tense

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Finished time (yesterday, last year, in 2019, ago): simple past, not present perfect | I have met him last week. | I **met** him last week. |
| *since* (point of time) and *for* (period) with an action still continuing: present perfect or present perfect continuous | He works here since 2018. | He **has worked** here since 2018. |
| *since* and *for* are not interchangeable | I have lived here since five years. | I have lived here **for** five years. |
| Earlier of two past actions: past perfect | When we arrived, the film already started. | When we arrived, the film **had** already **started**. |
| Stative verbs (know, believe, belong, own, understand, have = possess, resemble, consist) are not used in continuous tenses | I am knowing the answer. | I **know** the answer. |
| No future tense in time and condition clauses (when, until, before, after, as soon as, if, unless) | I shall wait until he will come. | I shall wait until he **comes**. |
| *did* + base form | Did he went? | Did he **go**? |
| Future perfect for an action complete before a future point | By 2030 he will retire. | By 2030 he **will have retired**. |
| *This is the first time* + present perfect | This is the first time I visit Quetta. | This is the first time I **have visited** Quetta. |
| *Ever since* + past point, main clause in present perfect | Ever since he came, he is complaining. | Ever since he came, he **has been complaining**. |
| Sequence: past reporting verb, past tense after it (except permanent truths) | He said that he is ill. | He said that he **was** ill. |
| Irregular forms | He has went. / I have drank. / The sun has rose. | He has **gone**. / I have **drunk**. / The sun has **risen**. |

Often confused verbs:

| Verb | Past | Past participle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| lie | lay | lain | to rest (no object) |
| lay | laid | laid | to put something down (takes an object) |
| lie | lied | lied | to tell a lie |
| rise | rose | risen | to go up (no object) |
| raise | raised | raised | to lift something (takes an object) |
| hang | hung | hung | a picture, a coat |
| hang | hanged | hanged | a person (execution) |
| flow | flowed | flowed | a river |
| fly | flew | flown | a bird |

### Rule 3. Conditionals and unreal forms

| Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Real, future | If + present, will + base | If it **rains**, we **will** stay. |
| Unreal, present | If + past, would + base | If I **were** rich, I **would** travel. |
| Unreal, past | If + past perfect, would have + past participle | If he **had worked**, he **would have passed**. |
| Inverted | Had / Were / Should + subject | **Had** I **known**, I would have come. |

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| No *will* or *would* in the if-clause | If he will come, I will tell him. | If he **comes**, I will tell him. |
| *were* for all persons in unreal conditions | If I was you... | If I **were** you... |
| *wish* + past for the present; *wish* + past perfect for the past | I wish I am taller. / I wish I went there yesterday. | I wish I **were** taller. / I wish I **had gone** there yesterday. |
| *as if / as though* + past (unreal) | He behaves as if he is the owner. | He behaves as if he **were** the owner. |
| *It is (high) time* + past | It is time you go to bed. | It is time you **went** to bed. |
| *would rather* + subject + past | I would rather you stay. | I would rather you **stayed**. |
| *unless* = if not. Never add a second negative | Unless you do not hurry... | Unless you **hurry**... |
| *hope* for the possible, *wish* for the unreal | I wish you get well soon. | I **hope** you get well soon. |

### Rule 4. Articles

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| *a* before a consonant sound, *an* before a vowel sound | a hour, an European, an university, a honest man, a MA | **an** hour, **a** European, **a** university, **an** honest man, **an** MA |
| No article with uncountable or abstract nouns used generally | The honesty is the best policy. | Honesty is the best policy. |
| *the* with superlatives, ordinals, unique things | He is best student. / Sun is hot. | He is **the** best student. / **The** sun is hot. |
| *the* with rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, deserts, newspapers, holy books, plural or descriptive country names | Indus, Himalayas, Holy Quran, United States | **the** Indus, **the** Himalayas, **the** Holy Quran, **the** United States |
| No *the* with single mountains, lakes, most countries, cities, languages, meals, games | the Mount Everest, the English, the cricket | Mount Everest, English, cricket |
| Musical instruments take *the* | She plays violin. | She plays **the** violin. |
| *bed, school, college, hospital, prison, church, mosque*: no article when used for their main purpose | He goes to the school at eight. (as a pupil) | He goes to school at eight. |
| Uncountable nouns never take *a/an*: advice, information, news, weather, furniture, luggage, work, progress, scenery | a good news, an advice, a fine weather | good news, **a piece of** advice, fine weather |
| *a/an* with a singular countable noun for a job or a class | He is engineer. | He is **an** engineer. |
| *the* + adjective = a class (plural) | Rich should help the poors. | **The** rich should help **the poor**. |

### Rule 5. Prepositions

Three kinds of error: the wrong preposition, an unnecessary preposition, a missing preposition.

**Verbs that take no preposition** (the Urdu pattern adds one):

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| discuss about the plan | discuss the plan |
| enter into the room | enter the room (but: enter into an agreement) |
| reach at the station | reach the station |
| resemble with his father | resemble his father |
| order for tea | order tea |
| await for the result | await the result |
| attack on the enemy (verb) | attack the enemy |
| request for leave (verb) | request leave |
| marry with her | marry her |
| investigate into the case | investigate the case |
| comprise of five parts | comprise five parts |
| emphasise on the point | emphasise the point |
| stress on the need (verb) | stress the need |
| contact to him | contact him |
| tell to him, ask to him | tell him, ask him |
| regret at the delay | regret the delay |
| visit to England (verb) | visit England |
| sign on the form | sign the form |

**Verbs and adjectives with a fixed preposition:**

| Expression | Expression | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| accuse of | charge with | blame for |
| congratulate on | prevent from | insist on |
| succeed in | prefer to | superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior to |
| married to | angry with (a person), angry at (a thing) | good at, bad at |
| afraid of | fond of | proud of |
| interested in | deal in (goods), deal with (a matter) | die of (a disease), die from (a cause) |
| differ from (be unlike), differ with (disagree) | agree with (a person), agree to (a proposal), agree on (a point) | part from (a person), part with (a thing) |
| consist of | depend on | rely on |
| object to | apply to (a body) for (a thing) | reply to |
| listen to | look forward to + -ing | used to + -ing (accustomed) |
| aware of | capable of | confident of |
| responsible for | sorry for | famous for |
| similar to | different from | contrary to |
| comply with | cope with | part with |
| abide by | adhere to | attend to |
| absorbed in | addicted to | devoid of |
| contingent on | conducive to | impervious to |
| redolent of | reticent about | averse to |
| acquainted with | accustomed to | conscious of |
| deprived of | cure of | rid of |
| sit for / take (an examination) | preside over | prohibit from |

**Time and place:**

| Point | Example |
|---|---|
| *at* a point of time; *on* a day or date; *in* a month, year, season, part of day | at 5 p.m., on Monday, on 14 August, in May, in 2028, in the morning (but: at night, at noon) |
| No preposition before *next, last, this, every* | I shall come next Friday. (not: on next Friday) |
| *since* a point; *for* a period; *from... to* a range | since 2019; for six years; from 9 to 5 |
| *by* = not later than; *till/until* = up to | Submit it by Friday. Wait till Friday. |
| *in* = at the end of a period; *within* = before the end | He will return in a week. Finish within an hour. |
| *at* a small place or a point; *in* a large place or an enclosed space | at the bus stop, in Lahore |
| *in* = position; *into* = movement to the inside | He is in the room. He walked into the room. |
| *on* = position; *onto / upon* = movement | The cat jumped onto the wall. |
| *between* two; *among* more than two | between the two brothers; among the five heirs |
| *beside* = next to; *besides* = in addition to | Sit beside me. Besides Urdu, he speaks Pashto. |
| *with* an instrument; *by* an agent; *in* ink, pencil | cut with a knife; written by Faiz; write in ink |
| *on* a committee, *on* a bus or train, *in* a car, *on* foot, *by* bus | He is on the board. |

Section 5 of `09-Other-Question-Types.md` has the preposition drill for Q5(b).

### Rule 6. Pronouns

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Subject forms (I, he, she, we, they, who) for subjects; object forms (me, him, her, us, them, whom) after verbs and prepositions | Between you and I... / Him and me went. | Between you and **me**... / **He and I** went. |
| After *let*: object form | Let you and I go. | Let you and **me** go. |
| After *than* and *as* (formal): the form the full clause would need | He is taller than me. | He is taller than **I** (am). |
| Complement of *to be* (formal): subject form | It is me who is to blame. | It is **I** who **am** to blame. |
| *who* for a subject, *whom* for an object. Test: he = who, him = whom | Whom do you think will win? / The man who I met. | **Who** do you think will win? / The man **whom** I met. |
| *who* for persons; *which* for things; *that* after superlatives, *all, same, only, any, none* | The boy which won... / This is the same pen which I lost. | The boy **who** won... / This is the same pen **that** I lost. |
| Reflexive needed after *enjoy, avail, absent, pride, acquit, resign* (when there is no other object) | He enjoyed during the holidays. / He availed of the chance. | He enjoyed **himself** during the holidays. / He availed **himself** of the chance. |
| No reflexive after *keep, stop, turn, rest, qualify* used without an object | Keep yourself away from bad company. | Keep away from bad company. |
| *one... one's* (not *his*) | One should obey his parents. | One should obey **one's** parents. |
| No repeated subject | The man who came here he is my uncle. | The man who came here is my uncle. |
| Order of persons: second, third, first (polite) | I and my friend went. | **My friend and I** went. |
| Pronoun must agree with its antecedent | Each of the girls gave their opinion. | Each of the girls gave **her** opinion. |
| Consistent person | If some of you make a noise, they will be punished. | If some of you make a noise, **you** will be punished. |
| *its* = belonging to it; *it's* = it is | The dog wagged it's tail. | The dog wagged **its** tail. |

### Rule 7. Adjectives, adverbs and comparison

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| No double comparative or superlative | more better, most cleverest | better, cleverest |
| Comparative for two, superlative for three or more | the best of the two | the **better** of the two |
| Compare with *other* inside the same group | He is taller than any boy in the class. | He is taller than any **other** boy in the class. |
| Compare like with like | The climate of Quetta is cooler than Karachi. | ...cooler than **that of** Karachi. |
| *senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer* take *to*, not *than* | senior than me | senior **to** me |
| Absolute adjectives are not compared: unique, perfect, ideal, universal, round, chief, complete | most unique | unique |
| *elder, eldest* for family members and before a noun; *older* with *than* | He is elder than I. | He is **older** than I. |
| *fewer* for countable; *less* for uncountable | less mistakes | **fewer** mistakes |
| *few* = hardly any; *a few* = some; *little / a little* likewise for uncountable | He has little friends. | He has **few** friends. |
| Adverb modifies a verb; adjective follows *be, seem, look, feel, taste, smell, sound* | He speaks fluent. / The rose smells sweetly. | He speaks **fluently**. / The rose smells **sweet**. |
| *fast, hard, late* are already adverbs; *hardly* = scarcely; *lately* = recently | He works hardly. / He runs fastly. | He works **hard**. / He runs **fast**. |
| *too* = more than is acceptable; *very* = to a high degree | I am too happy to see you. | I am **very** happy to see you. |
| *enough* follows the adjective | enough tall | tall **enough** |
| *too... to*; *so... that*. Do not mix | He is too weak that he cannot walk. | He is **too weak to walk**. / He is **so weak that he cannot walk**. |
| *as... as* with the positive degree | as better as | as **good** as |
| *much* with comparatives and past participles; *very* with positives and present participles | very better, much interesting | **much** better, **very** interesting |

### Rule 8. Modifiers

A modifying phrase must stand next to the word it describes.

| Type | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Dangling participle: the subject after the comma must do the action | Walking in the garden, a snake bit him. | **While he was walking** in the garden, a snake bit him. |
| | Being a hot day, we stayed indoors. | **It being** a hot day, we stayed indoors. / **As it was** a hot day, we stayed indoors. |
| | Having eaten dinner, the dishes were washed. | Having eaten dinner, **we washed** the dishes. |
| Misplaced phrase | He ordered a meal from the restaurant that was high in protein. | He ordered a meal **that was high in protein** from the restaurant. |
| | They left the hotel where they had been staying in a car. | They left **in a car** the hotel where they had been staying. *or* In a car, they left the hotel where they had been staying. |
| Position of *only, even, nearly, almost, just, hardly*: directly before the word modified | He only works when he needs money. | He works **only when** he needs money. |
| | She almost spent all her salary. | She spent **almost all** her salary. |
| Squinting modifier | Students who study often pass. | Students who study **regularly** pass. / Students who study pass **in most cases**. |
| Fragment (no main verb) | A young girl in the corner, holding a balloon. | A young girl in the corner **is** holding a balloon. |

### Rule 9. Parallelism

Items joined by *and, or, but, than, as* or by paired connectors must have the same grammatical form.

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Lists | He likes hiking, to swim and cricket. | He likes hiking, **swimming and playing cricket**. |
| *not only... but also*, *either... or*, *neither... nor*, *both... and*: the same kind of word after each half | He not only lost his job but also his house. | He lost **not only** his job **but also** his house. |
| | She is either a doctor or she is a nurse. | She is **either** a doctor **or** a nurse. |
| Comparisons | Reading is better than to watch television. | Reading is better than **watching** television. |
| *rather than* | I was rather impressed by his manner than by his matter. | I was impressed **by his manner rather than by his matter**. |
| Shared auxiliary: each verb must be complete | I always have and always shall be your friend. | I always have **been** and always shall be your friend. |
| Shared number | One of my arms was broken and my legs bruised. | One of my arms was broken and my legs **were** bruised. |

### Rule 10. Conjunctions and negatives

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| *although / though* is not followed by *but* (use *yet* or nothing) | Although he is rich, but he is unhappy. | Although he is rich, he is unhappy. |
| *because / as / since* is not followed by *so* or *therefore* | As he was ill, so he stayed home. | As he was ill, he stayed home. |
| *no sooner... than* | No sooner did he arrive when it rained. | No sooner did he arrive **than** it rained. |
| *hardly / scarcely / barely... when* | Hardly had he gone than she came. | Hardly had he gone **when** she came. |
| *lest... should* (no *not*) | Work hard lest you should not fail. | Work hard lest you **should fail**. |
| *so... that*; *such... that* | He is so honest as everyone trusts him. | He is so honest **that** everyone trusts him. |
| *both... and* (not *as well as*) | Both Ali as well as Asad came. | Both Ali **and** Asad came. |
| *unless, until* already negative | Wait until I do not return. | Wait until I **return**. |
| *without* is a preposition, not a conjunction | He will not come without he is asked. | He will not come **unless** he is asked. |
| *like* (preposition) + noun; *as* (conjunction) + clause | Do like I do. | Do **as** I do. |
| *that* is not used before a question word in indirect speech | He asked that where I lived. | He asked where I lived. |
| *whether* for alternatives; *whether or not* only for "regardless" | Tell me whether or not he can come. (acceptable) | Tell me **whether** he can come. (tighter) |
| *when* for a point; *while* for a continuing action | I shivered when I was waiting. | I shivered **while** I was waiting. |
| Double negatives cancel | I cannot by no means allow it. / He could hardly do no better. | I **can** by no means allow it. / He could hardly do **any** better. |
| *supposing* and *if* are not used together | Supposing if he fails... | Supposing he fails... |
| *the reason... is that* (not *because*) | The reason is because he was late. | The reason is **that** he was late. |

### Rule 11. Verb patterns: gerund or infinitive

| Followed by -ing | Followed by to + verb | Followed by object + bare verb |
|---|---|---|
| enjoy, avoid, mind, finish, suggest, consider, deny, risk, practise, keep, miss, postpone, admit, dislike, resist | manage, decide, hope, want, agree, refuse, promise, afford, fail, plan, expect, offer, learn, tend, seem | make, let, help (bare verb or to), see, hear, watch, feel, bid |
| cannot help, it is no use, it is worth, look forward to, be used to, object to, with a view to, be accustomed to, succeed in, insist on, prevent from | be able to, be about to, would like to, used to (past habit) | had better, would rather, need not, dare not |

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| I managed finishing the painting. | I managed **to finish** the painting. |
| I could not help but laugh. (formal exam English) | I could not help **laughing**. |
| I am looking forward to see you. | I am looking forward to **seeing** you. |
| He made me to wait. | He made me **wait**. |
| He suggested me to go. | He suggested **that I should go**. |
| He is used to get up early. | He is used to **getting** up early. |
| He stopped to smoke. (if the meaning is "gave up") | He stopped **smoking**. |
| She succeeded to win. | She succeeded **in winning**. |
| You had better to go. | You had better **go**. |
| I would rather to stay. | I would rather **stay**. |

### Rule 12. Reported speech and indirect questions

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| *say* (to someone); *tell* someone | He said me... / He told that... | He **told me**... / He **said that**... |
| Indirect question: statement word order, no question mark, no *do/does/did* | He asked me what was my name. | He asked me what **my name was**. |
| | I do not know where does he live. | I do not know where **he lives**. |
| | Tell me what is the time. | Tell me what **the time is**. |
| Tense moves back after a past reporting verb | He said that he is playing chess. | He said that he **was** playing chess. |
| Permanent truths stay in the present | The teacher said that the earth moved round the sun. | ...that the earth **moves** round the sun. |
| Yes/no questions take *if* or *whether* | He asked me that was I ready. | He asked me **whether I was** ready. |

Full narration rules are in `09-Other-Question-Types.md`.

### Rule 13. Active and passive

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Passive = *be* + past participle | The work has done. / The letter was wrote. | The work has **been done**. / The letter was **written**. |
| Intransitive verbs have no passive: *die, happen, occur, arrive, belong, consist, exist, rise, appear, disappear* | He was died in 1990. / The accident was happened. | He **died** in 1990. / The accident **happened**. |
| Stative *belong, resemble, consist*: active, simple | This is belonged to me. | This **belongs** to me. |
| *I agree* (verb), not *I am agree* | I am agree with you. | I **agree** with you. |

### Rule 14. Nouns

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Uncountable, no plural: advice, information, furniture, luggage, baggage, scenery, poetry, machinery, equipment, stationery, knowledge, homework, traffic, bread, news, work (= labour), hair (collective), evidence, jewellery, luck | informations, furnitures, sceneries, poetries, hairs, works | information, furniture, scenery, poetry, hair, work |
| Always plural: trousers, scissors, spectacles, thanks, alms, goods, premises, surroundings, riches, proceeds | The scissor is blunt. | The scissors **are** blunt. |
| Plural in sense, no -s: cattle, people, police, deer, sheep, fish (usually), aircraft | cattles, sheeps | cattle, sheep |
| Number + unit before a noun is singular | a five-rupees note, a ten-years-old boy, a three-days visit | a five-**rupee** note, a ten-**year**-old boy, a three-**day** visit |
| *dozen, hundred, thousand, million* stay singular after a number | two dozens eggs, five thousands people | two **dozen** eggs, five **thousand** people |
| Compound plurals | brother-in-laws, passer-bys | brothers-in-law, passers-by |
| Possessive | boys's school, one of my friend's house | boys' school, the house of one of my friends |
| *one of* + plural noun | one of my friend | one of my **friends** |

### Rule 15. Word order, inversion and question tags

| Point | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Direct question: auxiliary before subject | What country he belongs to? | What country **does he belong** to? |
| Negative or limiting opener (never, seldom, hardly, rarely, not only, no sooner, under no circumstances, little): invert | Never I have seen such a mess. | Never **have I** seen such a mess. |
| *So / neither / nor* + auxiliary + subject | I do not like it, and neither he does. | ...and neither **does he**. |
| Question tag: same auxiliary, opposite polarity, pronoun subject | You have been working hard, isn't it? | You have been working hard, **haven't you**? |
| *What a* + countable; *What* + uncountable | What an awful weather! | What awful weather! |
| Adverbs of frequency: before the main verb, after *be* | He comes always late. | He **always comes** late. |
| *both, all* with a negative: use *neither, none, not all* | Both of them did not come. / All students did not pass. | **Neither** of them came. / **Not all** students passed. |

### Rule 17. Punctuation-based corrections

The syllabus promises two or three of these. Full rules are in `04-Punctuation.md`.

| Fault | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Comma splice | He was tired, he went to bed. | He was tired**, so** he went to bed. / He was tired**;** he went to bed. |
| Missing comma after an opening phrase or clause | After finishing the report he went home. | After finishing the report**,** he went home. |
| Non-essential clause needs two commas | My father who is a doctor lives in Multan. | My father**,** who is a doctor**,** lives in Multan. |
| *however, therefore, moreover* between two sentences | He tried hard, however he failed. | He tried hard**; however,** he failed. |
| Apostrophe | Its a pity the team lost it's captain. | **It's** a pity the team lost **its** captain. |
| Question mark in an indirect question | He asked where I lived? | He asked where I lived**.** |
| Quotation | He said I am tired. | He said**, "I am tired."** |
| Comma before a coordinating conjunction joining two full clauses | She worked hard but she failed. | She worked hard**,** but she failed. |
| Colon after a verb or preposition | The colours are: red and blue. | The colours are red and blue. |
