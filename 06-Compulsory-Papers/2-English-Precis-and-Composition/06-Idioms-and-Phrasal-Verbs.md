# Idioms and Phrasal Verbs

Idioms are not named in the CE-2016 syllabus as a separate question, but the paper has used them often:

| Year | Where idioms or phrasal verbs appeared |
|---|---|
| 2015 | Q5(a): use five of eight idioms in sentences |
| 2016 | Q6(b): use five of eight idioms in sentences (5 marks) |
| 2017 | Q6(b): the same (5 marks) |
| 2018 | Q6(b): the same (5 marks) |
| 2019 | Q6: use five of eight idioms in sentences (10 marks). No pairs of words that year |
| 2020 | Q6: the same (10 marks). No pairs of words that year |
| 2021–2026 | No idiom question. Q6 was pairs of words. Phrasal verbs still appear in the preposition blanks of Q5(b), and Urdu idioms appear in the translation |

So idioms are a real possibility for Q6 in 2028, worth up to 10 marks. Phrasal verbs are tested every year in Q5(b).

This file has **352 idioms** and **163 phrasal verbs**, each with a meaning and an example sentence. Past-paper items come first.

---

## 1. How to answer an idiom question

The instruction is: "Use ONLY FIVE of the following in sentences which illustrate their meaning."

1. Choose the five you know for certain.
2. Write one sentence for each. The sentence must **show the meaning**. A sentence in which any other phrase would fit earns nothing.
3. You may give the meaning in brackets first. It is not required, but it protects you if the sentence is unclear.
4. Use the idiom in its normal form. Change only the tense and the pronoun (*one's* becomes *his, her, my*).
5. Keep sentences 12–20 words long. Avoid first-person trivia.

| Idiom | Weak sentence (meaning not shown) | Good sentence |
|---|---|---|
| A wild goose chase | He went on a wild goose chase. | The false address sent the police on a wild goose chase across the city. |
| Sit on the fence | My brother sits on the fence. | When the vote was called, he sat on the fence and supported neither side. |
| Nip in the bud | The problem was nipped in the bud. | The principal nipped the cheating in the bud by changing the papers on the first day. |

Layout:

```
(i) Nip in the bud (to stop at an early stage)
    The principal nipped the cheating in the bud by changing the papers on the first day.
```
