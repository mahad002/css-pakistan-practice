# Punctuation (Q5a, 5 marks)

The question gives a short passage (60–120 words) printed in small letters with no punctuation at all, and asks you to punctuate it. In the 2023 Special and 2024 papers it gave eight separate sentences instead and asked you to rewrite any five with correct punctuation, without adding or changing words.

Five marks, about seven minutes. With practice this is close to full marks.

Contents:

1. What past papers gave
2. Method: six passes
3. The rules
4. Twelve single-sentence drills

6. Answers

Two kinds of text come up: **dialogue or anecdote** (quotation marks are the main work) and **exposition** (finding where sentences end is the main work).

## 2. Method: six passes

Do not punctuate as you read. Make passes, each for one thing.

| Pass | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1. Read | Read the whole passage for sense. Hear where the voice stops |
| 2. Sentences | Mark every sentence end with a stroke. Decide: full stop, question mark or exclamation mark |
| 3. Speech | Find the speakers. Put quotation marks round the exact words spoken. Add the comma that separates "he said" from the speech |
| 4. Capitals | First word of every sentence and of every quotation; names of people, places, days, months, languages, nationalities, titles of books; the word "I" |
| 5. Commas inside sentences | Lists, opening phrases and clauses, non-essential clauses, names addressed, words like *however* and *therefore* |
| 6. Small marks | Apostrophes (don't, boy's, its/it's), hyphens, colons, semicolons |

Then write the passage out once, neatly. Do not squeeze marks into the printed text. Copy every word exactly. Do not add, drop or change a word.

## 3. The rules

### 3.1 Capital letters

| Use a capital for | Example |
|---|---|
| The first word of a sentence | The train left. |
| The first word of a quoted sentence | He said, "The train has left." |
| Names of people, places, rivers, mountains | Fatima, Multan, the Indus, K2 |
| Days, months, festivals (not seasons) | Friday, March, Eid; but: spring, winter |
| Nationalities, languages, religions | Pakistani, Urdu, Islam |
| Titles with a name; God and His names | Dr Khan, Professor Ali, the Prime Minister; Allah |
| Main words in titles of books, poems, newspapers | "Bang-e-Dra", "Great Expectations", Dawn |
| The pronoun I | He and I left. |
| Family words used as names | "Where is Grandfather?" but: my grandfather |

Do not capitalise the continuation of a broken quotation: "Wait," he said, "until spring."

### 3.2 Full stop, question mark, exclamation mark

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| . | End of a statement or command; after an indirect question | He asked where I lived. |
| ? | End of a direct question only | "Where do you live?" he asked. |
| ! | After an exclamation or interjection. Use sparingly | "Alas! We are ruined." What a fine day! |

After a question mark or exclamation mark inside quotation marks, do not add a comma: "Who is there?" he asked.

### 3.3 Comma

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Between items in a list | He bought rice, flour, sugar and tea. |
| After an opening clause or long phrase | When the rain stopped, we went out. After a long silence, he spoke. |
| Round a non-essential clause or phrase (two commas) | My brother, who lives in Doha, is an engineer. |
| Round an appositive | Karachi, the largest city, lies on the coast. |
| Round a name or title used in address | "Come here, Ali, and sit down." "Yes, sir." |
| After *yes, no, well, oh* | "No, I cannot." "Well, let us see." |
| Round *however, therefore, moreover, of course, in fact, for example* inside a sentence | He was, however, mistaken. |
| Before *and, but, or, so, yet, for* joining two full clauses | He ran fast, but he missed the bus. |
| To separate speech from "he said" | He said, "I am ready." "I am ready," he said. |
| Between a date and a year, a town and a country | on Monday, 3 January; in Lahore, Pakistan |
| Before a question tag | You are coming, aren't you? |
| Round an absolute or participial phrase | The work being done, we left. Smiling, she agreed. |

Do **not** put a comma:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| between subject and verb: The man who came yesterday, is my uncle. | The man who came yesterday is my uncle. |
| before *that* in reported speech: He said, that he was ill. | He said that he was ill. |
| round an essential clause: Students, who work hard, pass. | Students who work hard pass. |
| to join two sentences (comma splice): It was late, we went home. | It was late; we went home. / It was late, so we went home. |

The serial comma before "and" in a list (rice, flour, and tea) is optional. Be consistent. This pack omits it, as British usage does, except where it prevents confusion.

### 3.4 Semicolon

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Between two closely linked full sentences with no conjunction | Some wanted to stay; others wanted to leave. |
| Before *however, therefore, nevertheless, moreover, consequently, otherwise* when they join two sentences (comma after) | He worked hard; however, he failed. |
| Between list items that already contain commas | We visited Lahore, the cultural capital; Karachi, the port; and Peshawar. |

### 3.5 Colon

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| To introduce a list after a complete statement | He packed three things: a map, a torch and a rope. |
| To introduce an explanation or a restatement | He had one fault: he could not keep a secret. |
| To introduce a formal quotation or question | The question was this: who would pay? |

Do not put a colon straight after a verb or preposition: "The colours are red and green", not "The colours are: red and green".

### 3.6 Apostrophe

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Missing letters | don't, can't, it's (it is), who's (who is), I'll, haven't, o'clock |
| Singular possessive: 's | the boy's book, the council's decision, Iqbal's poetry |
| Plural possessive ending in s: s' | the boys' school, the readers' tables |
| Plural not ending in s: 's | the children's library, men's shoes |
| No apostrophe in possessive pronouns | its, hers, yours, ours, theirs, whose |

*its* (belonging to it) and *it's* (it is) is the single most tested point.

### 3.7 Quotation marks

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Enclose the exact words spoken | "I am tired," said Ali. |
| Speech tag first: comma, then the quotation, capital letter | Ali said, "I am tired." |
| Speech tag in the middle of one sentence: commas both sides, no new capital | "I am tired," said Ali, "and I want to sleep." |
| Speech tag between two sentences: full stop after the tag, new capital | "I am tired," said Ali. "Let us stop." |
| Full stop, comma, question mark and exclamation mark that belong to the speech go inside the closing mark | "Where are you going?" he asked. |
| A question mark that belongs to the whole sentence, not the quotation, goes outside | Did he really say, "I resign"? |
| A quotation inside a quotation takes the other kind of mark | "Who said 'Know thyself'?" asked the teacher. |
| Each new speaker starts a new paragraph in formal writing. In the exam passage, running on in one paragraph is accepted | |
| Titles of poems, essays, chapters, and (in handwriting) books: quotation marks or underlining | He was reading "Hamlet". |

Use double marks for speech and single marks for a quotation inside it, or the reverse. Be consistent.

### 3.8 Dash, brackets, hyphen

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dash | A sudden break or an added afterthought; a pair of dashes may replace brackets | There is one slavery no law can abolish – the slavery of caste. |
| Brackets | Extra information that could be removed | My uncle (my mother's brother) lives there. |
| Hyphen | Compound words and compound adjectives before a noun | a twenty-page report, the north-west, vice-chancellor, well-known writer |

## 4. Single-sentence drills

Rewrite each with correct punctuation. Do not add or change words. Answers follow.

1. he worked hard however he could not finish the task in time
2. the manager said please submit your reports by friday
3. my eldest brother who is a doctor lives in peshawar
4. she bought three things a notebook a pen and a ruler
5. have you read the essay of studies by francis bacon
6. its true that the company has changed its policy isnt it
7. did the minister really say i have nothing to hide
8. the conference will be held in lahore pakistan on 12 march 2027
9. when the bell rang the students who had finished their work left the hall
10. the teacher asked who wrote the line to be or not to be
11. some people like tea others prefer coffee
12. alas the old mans only son has left him

**Answers**

1. He worked hard; however, he could not finish the task in time.
2. The manager said, "Please submit your reports by Friday."
3. My eldest brother, who is a doctor, lives in Peshawar.
4. She bought three things: a notebook, a pen and a ruler.
5. Have you read the essay "Of Studies" by Francis Bacon?
6. It's true that the company has changed its policy, isn't it?
7. Did the minister really say, "I have nothing to hide"?
8. The conference will be held in Lahore, Pakistan, on 12 March 2027.
9. When the bell rang, the students who had finished their work left the hall.
10. The teacher asked, "Who wrote the line 'To be or not to be'?"
11. Some people like tea; others prefer coffee.
12. Alas! The old man's only son has left him.

Notes: in 3, a person has only one eldest brother, so the clause is non-essential and takes commas. In 9, "who had finished their work" picks out which students, so it takes no commas. In 7, the question mark belongs to the whole sentence and stands outside.

## 6. Answers

Other correct versions exist. A semicolon may often be replaced by a full stop, and a pair of brackets by a pair of commas. Marks are lost for missing capitals, missing sentence ends, wrong apostrophes and broken quotation marks.

### Passage 1

A hungry fox saw a crow sitting on a branch with a piece of cheese in her beak. "Good morning, dear lady," said the fox. "What a beautiful voice you must have! Will you not sing for me?" The crow, who was very vain, opened her beak to sing, and the cheese fell to the ground. The fox snatched it up and said, "Madam, your voice is fine, but your wits are wanting."

*Points to check:* Dialogue. Comma inside the closing mark before "said the fox". Exclamation after "What a...". Two commas round the non-essential clause "who was very vain". "Madam" is a form of address, so a comma follows it.

### Passage 2

The traveller packed four things for the journey: a map, a compass, a water bottle and a warm coat. He had been told that the mountains were dangerous in winter; nevertheless, he set out at dawn on Monday, 3 January. "If I am not back by Friday," he told his wife, "send someone to look for me." She did not reply.

*Points to check:* Colon before the list. Semicolon before "nevertheless" and comma after it. The speech is one sentence broken by the tag, so "send" has no capital.

### Passage 3

It's a pity that the children's library, which was opened only last year, has lost its roof in the storm. The librarian's assistant says that the books weren't damaged, but the readers' tables and the staff's records were ruined. "We can't reopen until the council's engineers have finished," she said. "Who's going to pay? That's anybody's guess."

*Points to check:* Apostrophes. "It's" = it is; "its roof" has none. "children's" (plural not ending in s), "readers'" (plural ending in s), "librarian's", "staff's", "council's", "anybody's". Contractions: weren't, can't, who's, that's.

### Passage 4

"Where are you going?" asked the old man. "To the city," replied the boy. "I want to find work there." "Do you know anyone in the city?" "No, sir, I don't." The old man shook his head. "Alas! You are too young to go alone," he said. "Wait until spring, and I will take you myself."

*Points to check:* No comma after a question mark inside quotation marks. "sir" is set off by commas. "Alas!" takes an exclamation mark. "spring" has no capital.

### Passage 5

Allama Iqbal, the national poet of Pakistan, was born in Sialkot in 1877. He studied in Lahore, Cambridge and Munich, where he wrote a thesis on Persian thought. His poetry, which is written in both Urdu and Persian, urges Muslims to rediscover their own strength. "Bang-e-Dra", his first Urdu collection, appeared in 1924.

*Points to check:* Appositives ("the national poet of Pakistan", "his first Urdu collection") and the non-essential clause take pairs of commas. Capitals for names, cities, languages and the title. The title is in quotation marks (or underlined).

### Passage 6

The committee met for three hours; however, it failed to reach a decision. Some members wanted to raise the fee; others wanted to abolish it. The chairman, who had said nothing all afternoon, finally spoke. "Gentlemen," he said, "we have three choices: to raise it, to abolish it, or to leave it alone. Since we cannot agree, I suggest that we leave it alone."

*Points to check:* Semicolon before "however"; semicolon between the two balanced clauses. Colon before the three choices. Comma after the opening clause "Since we cannot agree". A full stop after "decision" and a new sentence "However, it failed..." is also correct.

### Passage 7

One day Mulla Nasruddin went to the market to buy a donkey. "How much is this one?" he asked. "Fifty dinars," said the dealer. "Fifty dinars! That is far too much. I'll give you ten." "Ten? You must be joking," said the dealer. "Not at all," replied the Mulla. "I'm perfectly serious; it's the donkey that is a joke."

*Points to check:* Short exclamations and questions inside speech. Apostrophes in I'll, I'm, it's. "Mulla" is a title used as a name, so it has a capital. A comma after "One day" is optional.

### Passage 8

The teacher closed the book and said, "Who can tell me what Shakespeare meant by the line 'All the world's a stage'?" Nobody answered. "Come, come," she went on, "you have all read 'As You Like It', haven't you?" A girl at the back raised her hand. "Please, miss," she said, "I think he meant that everyone plays a part."

*Points to check:* Quotation inside a quotation takes single marks. The question mark belongs to the teacher's question, so it stands outside the single mark and inside the double. The play title takes single marks here because it is inside speech. Question tag "haven't you" is set off by a comma.

### Passage 9

On 14 August 1947, Pakistan came into being. Its first capital was Karachi, a busy port on the Arabian Sea; its first Governor-General was Quaid-i-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah. The new state faced many difficulties: millions of refugees, an empty treasury and a shortage of trained officials. Yet its people, who had sacrificed so much, were full of hope.

*Points to check:* No apostrophe in the possessive "its" (three times). Semicolon between the two parallel statements; a full stop is also correct. Colon before the list of difficulties. Capitals for the month, the sea, the title and the name.

### Passage 10

My uncle (my mother's elder brother) has lived in Quetta for forty years. He has three passions: gardening, chess and Persian poetry. Last winter, when the snow was two feet deep, he said to me, "Do you know why I never left this city?" "No," I said. "Because," he answered, "nobody ever asked me to."

*Points to check:* Brackets (or a pair of commas or dashes) round the explanatory phrase. Colon before the list. Commas round the clause "when the snow was two feet deep". "winter" has no capital; "Persian" has.

### Passage 11

Habit is a second nature. What we do every day, we soon do without thinking; what we do without thinking, we can hardly stop doing. A good habit, therefore, is the best of servants, and a bad one is the worst of masters. Is it not strange, then, that we take such care over a child's lessons and so little over his habits?

*Points to check:* Exposition: the work is finding sentence ends. Semicolon between the two balanced halves. "therefore" and "then" are set off by commas. The last sentence is a question.

### Passage 12

A miser once dreamed that he had found a bag of gold. He woke up, saw that his hands were empty, and began to weep. "Why are you crying?" asked his wife. "I have lost a fortune," he said. "Then go back to sleep," she replied, "and look for it where you found it."

*Points to check:* Commas between the three actions. The wife's reply is one sentence broken by the tag, so "and" has no capital.

### Passage 13

The delegation visited three cities: Lahore, the cultural capital; Karachi, the centre of trade; and Peshawar, the gateway to the north-west. In each city, the visitors asked the same question: "What do your young people need most?" The answers were different, but one word was heard everywhere: jobs.

*Points to check:* Semicolons separate list items that contain commas. Colon before the list, before the quoted question, and before the final word. Hyphen in "north-west".

### Passage 14

Dr Ayesha Khan, who teaches at the university, arrived at 9 a.m. with a twenty-page report. "Ladies and gentlemen," she began, "I have good news and bad news. Which would you like first?" "The bad news," said the vice-chancellor. "Very well. The laboratory's roof is leaking, the equipment is out of date, and we have no funds. The good news is that the students haven't noticed."

*Points to check:* Commas round the non-essential clause. "a.m." takes full stops (or write "am" consistently). Apostrophes in "laboratory's" and "haven't". A new speaker begins at "Very well"; in a fair copy, start a new line there.

### Passage 15

"Oh, Ahmed, have you seen my spectacles?" asked Grandfather. "Yes, Grandfather, they are on your head." "On my head? Well, well! Why didn't you tell me sooner?" "You didn't ask me sooner," said Ahmed, laughing. The old man smiled, patted the boy's shoulder and said, "You will go far, my boy, if nobody strangles you first."

*Points to check:* Names used in address are set off by commas. "Grandfather" has a capital because it is used as a name. Comma before the participle "laughing". Apostrophes in didn't and boy's.

## 7. The ten slips that cost marks

| No. | Slip | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No capital at the start of a sentence or a quotation | Pass 4 |
| 2 | Proper noun left in small letters | Pass 4 |
| 3 | Closing quotation mark missing | Count them: the total must be even |
| 4 | Comma or full stop placed outside the closing quotation mark | Inside, when it belongs to the speech |
| 5 | Comma after a question mark | Never |
| 6 | "its" and "it's" confused | Test: can you say "it is"? |
| 7 | Plural possessive written as singular (the boy's school for many boys) | Ask: one owner or several? |
| 8 | Comma splice | Two full sentences need a full stop, a semicolon, or a comma with a conjunction |
| 9 | One comma of a pair missing round a clause | Commas round an insertion come in twos |
| 10 | A word changed or dropped while copying | Read your copy against the print, word by word |
