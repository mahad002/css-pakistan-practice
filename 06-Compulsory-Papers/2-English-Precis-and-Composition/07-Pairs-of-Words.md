# Pairs of Words (Q6, 10 marks)

Eight pairs of similar-looking or similar-sounding words are given. You choose five and use both words of each pair in sentences that show the difference in meaning. Two marks a pair.

The syllabus wording: "Ten pairs shall be given of seemingly similar words with different meanings, generally confused in communication, for bringing out the difference in meaning of any five of them by first explaining them in parenthesis and then using them in sentences." In practice the paper gives eight.

This file has **265 pairs**: 71 from the 2016–2026 papers, 106 from older papers (1981–2013), and 88 other common pairs.

---

## 1. How to answer

The syllabus asks for two things: the meaning in brackets, then a sentence. Give both. It takes ten seconds more and protects the marks.

```
(i) Eminent / Imminent
    Eminent (distinguished): Dr Salam was an eminent physicist.
    Imminent (about to happen): Dark clouds showed that rain was imminent.
```

Rules:

1. Choose the five pairs where you are sure of **both** words. One wrong word loses the pair's second mark, and often both.
2. Spell the words exactly as printed. Attaching the right meaning to the wrong spelling is the commonest way to lose marks.
3. Write a sentence in which only that word fits. "This is a good principle" proves nothing. "The principal announced a holiday" does.
4. Keep the word in the form given where you can. If you must change it (plural, past tense), keep it recognisable.
5. Do not write one sentence containing both words unless both meanings are still clear. Two short sentences are safer.
6. If a word has two meanings, use the one that contrasts with its partner.

Time: 15 minutes for five pairs.

What to notice:

- **Repeats are real.** *Faint/feint* came in 2018, 2023 and 2024. *Veracity/voracity* came in 2017 and 2023 Special, and four times before 2011. *Dinghy/dingy* came two years running. *Allude/elude* and *moat/mote* each came twice. The 2026 paper took five of its eight pairs from pre-2011 papers.
- **Two styles alternate.** Standard confusables (2023, 2024, 2026) and rare or archaic words (2017, 2021, 2022, 2025). In a rare-word year you can still find five: each such paper had at least four or five ordinary pairs, except 2025.
- **Homophones dominate**: key/quay, leek/leak, peal/peel, taut/taught, sleigh/slay.
