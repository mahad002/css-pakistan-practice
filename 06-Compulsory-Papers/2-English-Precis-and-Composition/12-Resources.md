# Resources

This pack is meant to be enough on its own. The items below are for when you want more practice material, a second explanation of a rule, or the real papers. The list is short on purpose. More books do not raise marks on this paper. More handwritten practice does.

---

## 1. Must have

| Resource | What for | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Official FPSC past papers, 2016 to the latest** (fpsc.gov.pk, "Past Papers" section) | The real thing | The site did not open during the research for this pack (certificate error). Try it from another network or browser. Download the English (Précis and Composition) papers for 2019–2026 and keep them in `03-Past-Papers`. Sit 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2026 under exam conditions in weeks 42–51. Do not read them casually before then |
| **A good learner's dictionary** (Oxford Learner's Dictionaries or Cambridge Dictionary, both free online) | Meanings, example sentences, verb patterns, which preposition follows a word | Look up every word you get wrong. Read the example sentences, not only the definition. For pairs of words, look up both |
| **A quality English newspaper, editorial page** (Dawn is free online) | Précis material, vocabulary in context, argument structure | Ten minutes a day. Once a week from month 6, take one editorial of 500–700 words and cut it to one-third |
| **A quality Urdu newspaper or BBC Urdu, opinion columns** | Translation material in the register the exam uses | Once a fortnight after you finish the 25 paragraphs in `08`, take one paragraph of 80–120 words and translate it |
| **Flashcard software (Anki, free on desktop and Android) or paper cards** | Spaced repetition for `05`, `06`, `07` | One deck per file. Settings near the schedule in `05`, section 6 |

## 2. One book per skill, if you want one

| Skill | Book | Why this one | How to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar, self-study | *English Grammar in Use* (Raymond Murphy, Cambridge), intermediate level | Clear units, answers at the back, covers every rule in `03` | Do only the units for rules you miss in the practice sets. Do not work through the whole book |
| Grammar and usage, reference | *Practical English Usage* (Michael Swan, Oxford) | The best single answer to "which is correct, and why?" | Look things up. Use the index. Good for prepositions, articles, and confusable words |
| Traditional grammar, précis, narration, idioms in one volume | *High School English Grammar and Composition* (Wren and Martin) | This is the tradition the paper-setters come from. Its exercises on narration, voice, sentence correction and précis are close to the exam | Do the exercises on direct and indirect speech, active and passive, and correction of sentences. Read its chapter on précis writing once |
| Vocabulary | *Word Power Made Easy* (Norman Lewis) | Teaches words through roots, which is what you need for unknown MCQ words | Optional. Your GRE word list does the same job. Do not run three word lists at once |
| Précis practice passages | Any collection of classic English essays: Bacon, Hazlitt, Lamb, Ruskin, Emerson, J. S. Mill (all free on Project Gutenberg) | CSS précis passages are often in this older reflective style | Take a paragraph of 300–400 words and write a précis. There is no model answer, so check yourself with the rubric in `01`, section 6 |

Two other titles are commonly named for this paper in Pakistan: a locally published guide to English précis and composition for CSS (several exist), and *Exploring the World of English* (Saadat Ali Shah). They are useful mainly as banks of past-paper items. Their model answers vary in quality. Buy one only if you want every past paper since the 1970s in print.

The FPSC syllabus has its own list of suggested readings. From memory it includes *English Grammar in Use* (Murphy), *Practical English Usage* (Swan), *The Little, Brown Handbook* (Fowler and Aaron), *A University English Grammar* (Quirk and Greenbaum), *Write Better, Speak Better* (Reader's Digest) and *Modern English in Action* (Henry Christ). I could not open the official syllabus file to confirm the list, so check it when you can. You do not need all of them.

## 3. Free online sources for past-paper material

Use these to see past items. Do not trust their answer keys without checking.

| Source | Good for | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| cssprepforum.com (solved papers 2021–2026) | Corrections with explanations, pairs, prepositions, translation models | Explanations are sound in most places; some answers are debatable (for example a preposition key of "applied at the bank") |
| cssprep.com.pk (MCQ words by year; links to scans of papers) | Lists of MCQ words | Several keyed answers are plainly wrong (antonyms given for synonyms). Use the word lists, not the keys |
| t4tutorials.pk (text of Part II for many years) | Reading the actual questions | Year labels are wrong on most pages. Use the mapping table in `10-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`, section 1 |
| csspmsnotes.com (solved 2017–2019, 2023–2025, partial) | A second copy for cross-checking | Partial papers; model précis of mixed quality |
| cssforum.com.pk (discussion threads) | Other candidates' attempts at each year's précis | Quality varies widely. Read to see common mistakes, not to copy |

## 4. What not to spend time on

| Thing | Why not |
|---|---|
| Lists of 5,000 "CSS vocabulary words" | The return is too low. 579 words learned properly beat 5,000 skimmed |
| Memorising model précis | The passage is always new. Only the method transfers |
| Coaching-academy "guess papers" | The repeat patterns in `10` already tell you what repeats |
| Typing your practice | The exam is handwritten |
| Reading about précis instead of writing them | One précis written and marked is worth ten pages of advice |

## 5. Getting feedback when you study alone

Self-marking is the weak point of solo preparation, mainly for the précis and the translation.

1. Use the rubric and the point lists strictly. Count the points you missed, not the ones you got.
2. Leave a day between writing and marking. You will see more.
3. Once a month, give one précis and its passage to anyone whose English is good (a colleague, a relative, an online study partner from a CSS forum). Ask one question only: "Does my version say everything important in the original, and nothing else?"
4. An AI assistant can mark a précis against the rubric in `01` if you type in the passage and your answer. Ask it to list missing points, added points and lifted phrases, and to count the words. Treat the score as rough.
5. In the last three months, consider paying for one or two professionally checked mock papers from a reputable evaluation service, to calibrate your own marking. This is optional.
