## 2. The paper

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Marks | 100 |
| Time | 3 hours |
| Pass mark | 40 out of 100. Failing this one paper fails the whole attempt |
| Topics | Ten topics. You write on **one** |
| Length asked | Recent papers ask for an outline plus a comprehensive essay of **2,500–3,000 words** |
| Other instructions on recent papers | Use different forms of discourse (exposition, argumentation, description, narration). Credit is given for organisation, relevance and clarity |
| Language | English. Written by hand in the FPSC answer book |

**Official syllabus wording.** The FPSC syllabus (CE-2016 onwards) gives the Essay only a few lines. In summary: candidates write one or more essays in English from a wide choice of topics; they are expected to show comprehensive and research-based knowledge of the chosen topic; and their articulation, expression and technical treatment of the style of English essay writing will be examined. Source: [FPSC syllabus for CE-2016 and onwards (PDF)](https://fpsc.gov.pk/assets/media/2024-10-22-01-45-26-Syllabus_for_CE-2016_and_onwards.pdf). I could not open the FPSC PDF directly from my tools (see `COVERAGE.md`), so read the original once yourself.

**What the wording tells you.** Three things are tested, and they match the instruction printed on the paper:

1. **Knowledge**: real content, with evidence ("comprehensive and research-based").
2. **Language**: correct, clear English ("articulation, expression").
3. **Essay craft**: outline, thesis, paragraphs, coherence ("technical treatment of the style").

## 3. How it is marked

FPSC does not publish a marking scheme for the Essay. Anything more exact than the points below is a coaching-centre guess.

What we do know:

- **The paper's own instruction** says credit is given for *organisation, relevance and clarity*.
- **The CE-2017 examiners' report** (as reproduced by [CSS Exam Desk](https://cssexamdesk.substack.com/p/css-english-essay-feedback-from-subject)) says essays were judged on four things: **argumentation, content, language and intellectual level**.
- **The CE-2019 examiners' report** lists the qualities of a good essay: good and correct language, original thought, in-depth knowledge, critical thinking, organisation of ideas, coherence, cohesion, clarity and comprehensiveness. Source: a mirror of the FPSC analysis, [Examiner Reports CE-2019 (PDF)](https://cssplatformbytha.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Examiner-Reports-CE-2019.pdf).
- **Marks are low even for good scripts.** According to a Tribune article quoting the FPSC Annual Report 2020, no candidate scored 60 or above in the Essay that year ([Express Tribune, 10 Nov 2023](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2445648/entertainment)). A mark in the mid-40s is a good result. Above 50 is rare.
- **It is one long answer marked by one examiner's overall judgement.** There are no part marks to collect. A serious fault in relevance or language can sink the whole script.

Coaching sites often say that the examiner decides mostly from the outline, the introduction and the conclusion. That is a claim by teachers, not by FPSC. It is still sensible to treat those three parts as the most important pages you write.

The practical marking model used in this pack (see the rubric in `07-Practice-Plan.md`) is built from those official words: relevance and thesis, organisation, argument and content, language, and presentation.

## 5. Why candidates fail (from examiners' reports)

These come from FPSC examiners' reports as reproduced by the sources linked. I could not open FPSC's own annual-report PDFs, so I am relying on mirrors and press reports. Where a report is quoted, the quote is short and the rest is my paraphrase.

| # | Reason | Where it comes from | What you do about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crammed, ready-made essays.** Candidates reproduced memorised material and missed the "delicacy, twist and emphasis" of the exact topic | CE-2019 report ([mirror PDF](https://cssplatformbytha.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Examiner-Reports-CE-2019.pdf)) | Decode every word of the topic (see `01-Essay-Method.md`, section 2). Never pour a prepared essay into a new title |
| 2 | **Too short.** Many scripts did not reach even 1,200–1,300 words | CE-2019 report | Train handwriting stamina. Aim for a complete essay of 2,500+ words |
| 3 | **Same stock quotes and examples as everyone else**, showing easy sources and no original thinking or critical evaluation | CE-2019 report | Build your own evidence file. Explain each fact; do not just drop it |
| 4 | **Grammar errors**: subject–verb agreement, verb tense, verb forms | CE-2019 report | `06-Language-Toolkit.md`, section 5. Leave 15 minutes to proofread |
| 5 | **Badly built outline.** Outlines were not properly structured, and some outline points never appeared in the essay | CE-2016 report ([CSS Exam Desk](https://cssexamdesk.substack.com/p/css-english-essay-feedback-from-subject)) | Write the outline as claims, and follow it exactly, in order |
| 6 | **Random ideas with no logical reasoning or facts**; no coherence; could not argue from more than one angle | CE-2016 report | One thesis. Each paragraph proves one part of it. Include a counter-argument |
| 7 | **Did not find the real question in the topic.** Critical argument was very poor; content was thin and often irrelevant; some candidates did not understand the topic at all | CE-2017 report (same source) | Write the topic as a question, answer it in one sentence, and test every paragraph against that sentence |
| 8 | **Faulty sentences and wrong usage**; many grammar and spelling mistakes | CE-2017 report | Short sentences. Words you are sure of |
| 9 | **Do not know what a "comprehensive essay" is**; memorised stereotypes on factual topics such as global warming and CPEC | CE-2018 report (same source) | On "known" topics, add analysis: causes, consequences, trade-offs, your judgement |
| 10 | **Isolated sentences instead of paragraphs**; weak concepts; poor word choice | CE-2014 report (same source) | Paragraph formula in `01-Essay-Method.md`, section 6 |
| 11 | **Unclear ideas, arguments without logical structure, formula answers from academies and guidebooks** | FPSC briefing to a Senate committee, 2025 ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/06/05/heres-why-most-css-aspirants-fail/)) | Self-study from primary sources is an advantage, not a handicap |

**The pattern.** Year after year the complaints are the same four: (a) the essay does not answer the exact topic, (b) it has no argument, only information, (c) the structure is weak, (d) the English has basic errors. Length is a fifth. Nothing on this list needs talent. It needs method and practice.

**An honest note for you.** Your English is already stronger than most candidates'. Your risks are different: (1) no feel yet for what a CSS examiner counts as "relevant", (2) little Pakistan-specific evidence, (3) handwriting speed and stamina after years of typing, and (4) nobody to mark your work. This pack is organised around those four.

## 6. The 3-hour method

Total: 180 minutes. Practise with exactly these blocks until they are automatic.

| Minutes | Step | What you produce |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Choose the topic** | One topic, chosen and final |
| 10–25 | **Brainstorm and fix the thesis** | A page of rough ideas, one thesis sentence |
| 25–45 | **Write the outline** in the answer book | 1.5–2 neat pages |
| 45–60 | **Introduction** | 250–350 words ending in the thesis |
| 60–150 | **Body** | 12–16 paragraphs, about 1,900–2,200 words |
| 150–165 | **Conclusion** | 200–250 words |
| 165–180 | **Review** | Corrected script |

### Step 1: Choose the topic (10 minutes)

1. Read all ten topics slowly, twice. Underline the key words of each.
2. Cross out any topic where you do not understand a word or cannot say what is being asked.
3. Shortlist two or three. For each, give yourself 90 seconds and count how many separate arguments and concrete examples you can list. Choose the one where you reach **six arguments and six facts** fastest.
4. Prefer a topic whose question is clear to a topic you find beautiful. An abstract quote is safe only if you can turn it into a concrete claim and illustrate it from history, society and Pakistan.
5. Decide, and do not change. Switching after 30 minutes is the most expensive mistake available.

A senior police officer who wrote a guide for candidates gives the same advice: pick what you can handle, know whether you are stronger at argument or description, and avoid extreme positions ([CSS Exam Desk](https://cssexamdesk.substack.com/p/english-essay-paper-for-the-css-exams)).

### Step 2: Brainstorm and fix the thesis (15 minutes)

On the rough-work page:

1. Rewrite the topic as a question.
2. Mark the **scope words** (Pakistan? the world? "always"? "only"?) and the **task word** (discuss, how far, is, should).
3. Dump ideas for five minutes using a fixed grid: political, economic, social, legal, technological, environmental, international, historical, moral.
4. Sort the ideas into 3–5 groups. Drop anything that does not answer the question.
5. Write the thesis: your answer to the question plus the main reasons, in one or two sentences.

### Step 3: Outline (20 minutes)

Write it on the first pages of the answer book, neatly, with the heading "Outline". Thesis at the top, then numbered sections, each point a short claim. Aim for 20–30 points. Check that the order of points is the order you will write in. Details in `01-Essay-Method.md`, section 4.

### Step 4: Write (120 minutes)

- Introduction: 15 minutes.
- Body: 90 minutes. About 6 minutes per paragraph. Tick each outline point as you finish it.
- Conclusion: 15 minutes. Never skip it. If you are behind at minute 140, cut a body section, not the conclusion.

You must average about **21–23 words a minute** for two hours to produce 2,600 words. Measure your own speed in March 2027 (see `07-Practice-Plan.md`). If you are slower, plan a 2,300-word essay that is complete rather than a 3,000-word essay that stops in the middle.

### Step 5: Review (15 minutes)

Read the whole script once with a pen. Look for, in this order:

1. Missing words and unfinished sentences.
2. Subject–verb agreement and tense.
3. Articles (a, an, the) and prepositions.
4. Spelling of names and key terms.
5. Paragraphs that do not link back to the topic: add one linking sentence.

Correct by drawing one neat line through the error and writing above it. No scribbling.
