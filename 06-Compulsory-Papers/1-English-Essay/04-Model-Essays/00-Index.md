## How to study a model essay

1. **Read the outline, then cover it** and read the essay. In the margin, note which outline point each paragraph delivers.
2. **Underline the thesis** each time it returns: end of the introduction, turning points in the body, first lines of the conclusion.
3. **Take three paragraphs apart**: find the point, the explanation, the evidence and the link.
4. **Find the counter-argument** and note the three moves: state, concede, reply.
5. **Compare introduction and conclusion.** Each essay ends where it began (a scene, a date, a person). Copy the technique, not the words.
6. **Mark it with the rubric** in `07-Practice-Plan.md`. This trains your eye before you mark your own work.
7. **Rewrite, do not memorise.** Pick one essay, keep its outline, and write your own version from memory in three hours. Then compare.

## Cautions

- The facts were checked in November 2026 (see `05-Evidence-Bank.md` and `COVERAGE.md`). Figures on the economy, floods, rankings and the Gulf war will be out of date by February 2028. Update before you reuse them.
- These essays are a little under 2,600 words because that is what a fast writer can finish by hand in two hours. The paper asks for 2,500–3,000.
- FPSC examiners have criticised memorised essays in writing. If your topic in the hall resembles one of these, build a fresh outline for the exact wording.
