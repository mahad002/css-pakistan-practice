# Past Paper Analysis, 2016–2026

Twelve papers: eleven annual papers (2016–2026) and the Special CSS 2023 paper. Each had ten topics, so this analysis covers 120 topics.

**How to read this file.** The topics below are described in my own words. They are not the exact wording. Before you practise on a past topic, open the linked source and copy the exact wording, because the exact words decide what the essay must argue.

**Sources and reliability.** I could not open FPSC's own PDFs with my tools (see `COVERAGE.md`). All topics come from third-party sites that reproduce the papers. Where two independent sites agree, I say so. Download the official papers yourself from [fpsc.gov.pk](https://www.fpsc.gov.pk) and check them against this file once.

## 3. Classification by type

| Type | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Argumentative (claim or question to judge) | 39 | 33% |
| Quotation / proverb / abstract statement | 35 | 29% |
| Expository / issue-based | 33 | 27% |
| Descriptive / analytical | 13 | 11% |

What this shows:

1. **About three-fifths of all topics need a clear stand** (argumentative plus quotation topics). A candidate who can only describe has four or fewer safe choices on most papers.
2. **Every paper has at least one quotation or proverb topic, and most have two to five.** In February 2023 and in 2025 they were half the paper. You cannot plan to avoid them entirely; plan to be able to write one well.
3. **The style swings.** 2017 was heavy with dense philosophical statements. 2020, 2024 and 2026 were mostly concrete and Pakistan-centred. 2022 was broad and textbook-like. 2023 used school-debate statements. Prepare for all four moods.
4. **The 2026 paper was the most concrete of the twelve**: seven issue topics and three quotations, all three quotations about international relations. If that trend holds, a candidate with strong current-affairs knowledge is well placed. Do not rely on it holding.

### 4.2 Patterns

1. **The examiner likes to reverse or sharpen an old topic.** "Frailty, thy name is woman" became "frailty is no more the name of woman". "Gender equality is a myth" became "a popular slogan". A candidate who memorised the first essay fails the second. Prepare themes, then practise bending them to new wording.
2. **Current events enter one or two years late.** Brexit (2016) appeared in 2017. COVID-19 (2020) appeared in 2021. The fossil-fuel decision at COP28 (Dec 2023) appeared in Feb 2024. The Gaza war (Oct 2023) appeared in 2025. The multipolar debate and the 2025 India–Pakistan conflict coloured 2026 (multipolarity, preparedness, sea power, permanent interests).
3. **Every paper has at least two topics on Pakistan's state and economy.** Governance, democracy, economy.
4. **Every paper has at least one gender or education topic**, usually both. 2024 and 2026 are the exceptions for gender.
5. **Every paper since 2018 except 2025 has a technology topic.**
6. **Climate and environment appear about every second year**, each time from a new angle (water, global warming, recycling, fossil fuels, forests). Given the 2025 floods and the water dispute with India, another is likely.

**How to use the list.** Themes 1–16 cover the ground from which at least six of the ten topics have come in every recent paper. If you can write well on those sixteen, you will always have a real choice. The model outlines in `03-Model-Outlines/` and the essays in `04-Model-Essays/` are mapped to this list in `03-Model-Outlines/00-Index.md`.

**A caution.** Guess papers are sold every year and are usually wrong on wording. In 2023 candidates who had prepared current-affairs essays met a paper of proverbs and school-debate statements. Prepare themes and skills; expect the wording to surprise you.
