# Seerah Timeline, Treaties, Battles and the Rightly Guided Caliphate

Use this file for facts and dates. Use `03-Notes/2-Seerah-Role-Model.md` and `03-Notes/6-Public-Administration-and-Governance.md` for analysis.

**About the dates.** Early sources differ on many days and months, and sometimes on years. Hijri dates below follow the commonly accepted accounts (Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham, and modern summaries such as *Ar-Raheeq al-Makhtum*). Christian-era years are approximate conversions. Where sources differ in a way that matters, both are given. In the exam, write the Hijri year; it is the safest.

## Part 1 — Life of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH)

### A. Before Prophethood (c. 570–610 CE)

| Age | Year (CE, approx.) | Event |
|---|---|---|
| — | c. 570 or 571 | Birth in Makkah in the Year of the Elephant, on a Monday in Rabi al-Awwal (12th by the common account; 9th by some researchers). Clan: Banu Hashim of Quraysh. Father Abdullah died before the birth. |
| 0–5 | | Nursed by Halimah of Banu Sa'd in the desert. |
| 6 | c. 576 | Death of his mother Aminah at Abwa. |
| 8 | c. 578 | Death of his grandfather Abdul Muttalib. Guardianship passes to his uncle Abu Talib. |
| 12 | c. 582 | Journey to Syria with Abu Talib. |
| about 20 | c. 590 | The Fijar wars; then the *Hilf al-Fudul*, a pact to protect the wronged, in which he took part. |
| 25 | c. 595 | Trade journey to Syria with Khadijah's goods; marriage to Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (RA). |
| 35 | c. 605 | Rebuilding of the Ka'bah; he settles the dispute over placing the Black Stone. |
| up to 40 | | Known as *al-Sadiq* and *al-Amin*. Periods of retreat in the cave of Hira. |

### B. The Makkan period (610–622 CE; 13 years)

| Year of Prophethood | Year (CE, approx.) | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 610 | First revelation in the cave of Hira, in Ramadan: the opening verses of Surah Al-Alaq (96:1–5). First believers: Khadijah, Ali, Abu Bakr, Zayd ibn Harithah (may Allah be pleased with them). |
| 1–3 | 610–613 | Quiet, private preaching. Teaching at the house of al-Arqam. |
| 4 | c. 613 | Public preaching begins (Ash-Shu'ara 26:214; Al-Hijr 15:94): the call from Mount Safa. Persecution starts. Sumayyah (RA) becomes the first martyr. |
| 5 | c. 615 | First migration to Abyssinia (about a dozen men and four women), then a larger second group (over eighty men). Ja'far ibn Abi Talib (RA) speaks before the Negus. |
| 6 | c. 616 | Hamzah and then Umar (may Allah be pleased with them) accept Islam. |
| 7–10 | c. 616–619 | Social and economic boycott of Banu Hashim in the valley of Abu Talib; about three years. |
| 10 | c. 619 | The Year of Sorrow: deaths of Abu Talib and Khadijah (RA). Journey to Ta'if with Zayd ibn Harithah; rejection and stoning. |
| 10–12 | c. 620–621 | The Night Journey and Ascension (*Isra* and *Mi'raj*; Al-Isra 17:1). The five daily prayers are prescribed. The date is disputed; 27 Rajab is the popular one. |
| 11 | c. 620 | Six men of Yathrib accept Islam at the pilgrimage. |
| 12 | c. 621 | First Pledge of Aqabah: twelve men. Mus'ab ibn Umayr (RA) sent to Yathrib as teacher. |
| 13 | c. 622 | Second Pledge of Aqabah: seventy-three men and two women pledge to protect him. Companions begin to migrate. Quraysh plot to kill him (Al-Anfal 8:30). |

### C. The Hijrah (622 CE; 1 AH)

- Leaves Makkah with Abu Bakr (RA). Ali (RA) stays behind in his bed to return people's deposits.
- Three nights in the cave of Thawr (At-Tawbah 9:40). Guide: Abdullah ibn Urayqit. Pursued by Suraqah ibn Malik.
- Arrives at Quba in Rabi al-Awwal; builds the first mosque there. Enters Madinah a few days later; stays in the house of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (RA).
- The Hijri calendar, fixed later under Umar (RA), is counted from the year of the Hijrah.

### D. The Madinan period (1–11 AH; 622–632 CE)

| AH | CE (approx.) | Events |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 622–623 | Building of the Prophet's Mosque. Brotherhood (*mu'akhat*) between Emigrants and Helpers. Charter of Madinah. The call to prayer (adhan) instituted; Bilal (RA) the first muezzin. First treaties with neighbouring tribes. |
| **2** | 623–624 | Early expeditions (Abwa/Waddan is counted as the first led by the Prophet PBUH). Change of the qiblah from Jerusalem to the Ka'bah (Al-Baqarah 2:144). Fasting of Ramadan and zakat al-fitr prescribed; zakat on wealth is usually dated to this year. **Battle of Badr**, 17 Ramadan. Expulsion of Banu Qaynuqa. Marriage of Fatimah and Ali (may Allah be pleased with them). |
| **3** | 624–625 | **Battle of Uhud**, Shawwal. Pursuit to Hamra al-Asad. |
| **4** | 625–626 | Incidents of al-Raji and Bi'r Ma'unah (teachers killed by treachery). Expulsion of Banu Nadir (Surah Al-Hashr). |
| **5** | 626–627 | **Battle of the Trench** (Khandaq/Ahzab), Shawwal. Banu Qurayzah. Expedition of Banu Mustaliq (placed in 5 or 6 AH). |
| **6** | 627–628 | **Treaty of Hudaybiyyah**, Dhul Qa'dah. Pledge of Ridwan. |
| **7** | 628–629 | Letters to kings and rulers. **Khaybar**, Muharram. The fulfilled umrah (*Umrat al-Qada*), Dhul Qa'dah. |
| **8** | 629–630 | Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As accept Islam. **Battle of Mu'tah**, Jumada al-Ula. **Conquest of Makkah**, Ramadan. **Battle of Hunayn**, Shawwal. Siege of Ta'if. |
| **9** | 630–631 | **Expedition of Tabuk**, Rajab. Year of Delegations. Najran delegation. Hajj led by Abu Bakr (RA); proclamation of Surah At-Tawbah by Ali (RA). Hajj is usually said to have been made obligatory in this year. |
| **10** | 631–632 | Death of his son Ibrahim. **Farewell Pilgrimage**; sermon at Arafat on 9 Dhul Hijjah. Revelation of Al-Ma'idah 5:3. |
| **11** | 632 | Army of Usamah ibn Zayd prepared. Final illness. Abu Bakr (RA) leads the prayers. **Death on Monday, 12 Rabi al-Awwal 11 AH** (June 632), aged 63 lunar years, in the room of Aisha (RA), where he is buried. |

### E. Family (for MCQs)

- **Parents:** Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib; Aminah bint Wahb.
- **Children:** from Khadijah (RA): Qasim, Abdullah, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah. From Mariyah (RA): Ibrahim. All except Fatimah (RA) died in his lifetime.
- **Wives (Mothers of the Believers):** Khadijah, Sawdah, Aisha, Hafsah, Zaynab bint Khuzaymah, Umm Salamah, Zaynab bint Jahsh, Juwayriyah, Umm Habibah, Safiyyah, Maymunah (may Allah be pleased with them).
- **Uncles prominent in the Seerah:** Abu Talib (protector), Hamzah (RA), Abbas (RA), Abu Lahab (opponent).

## Part 2 — Treaties and documents

### 1. The Charter of Madinah (1 AH / 622 CE)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Parties | Emigrants, Helpers (Aws and Khazraj), and the Jewish tribes and their allies |
| Form | A written document (*sahifah*), preserved by Ibn Ishaq and Abu Ubayd. Dr Hamidullah counts 52 clauses; others 47. |
| Key terms | One political community; religious freedom ("the Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs"); joint defence of Madinah; no aid to Quraysh; clans keep their blood-money arrangements; no shelter for wrongdoers; Madinah a sanctuary; disputes referred to Allah and Muhammad (PBUH) |
| Importance | First written constitution of a plural state; citizenship by agreement, not blood; the Prophet (PBUH) recognised as head of state |

**Lessons:** unity through a written covenant; minority rights; rule of law; collective security.

### 2. The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (Dhul Qa'dah, 6 AH / 628 CE)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Occasion | About 1,400 Muslims set out for umrah; Quraysh stopped them at Hudaybiyyah |
| Envoy | Uthman (RA) sent to Makkah; rumour of his death led to the Pledge of Ridwan (Al-Fath 48:18) |
| Negotiators | The Prophet (PBUH); Suhayl ibn Amr for Quraysh. Written by Ali (RA). |
| Terms | Ten years' truce. Muslims to return this year and come next year for three days. A Makkan who comes to Madinah without his guardian's leave is returned; a Muslim who goes to Makkah is not. Tribes free to ally with either side. |
| Hard points | "Messenger of Allah" replaced by "son of Abdullah"; Abu Jandal returned |
| Outcome | Called "a clear victory" (Al-Fath 48:1). Recognition; freedom to preach; letters to kings; Khaybar; umrah next year; rapid growth. Broken by Quraysh's allies in 8 AH, leading to the Conquest of Makkah. |

**Lessons:** patience and foresight; substance over form; keeping one's word; peace opens hearts; the value of consultation.

### 3. Letters to rulers (end of 6 AH and 7 AH)

| Ruler | Envoy | Response as reported |
|---|---|---|
| Heraclius, Byzantine emperor | Dihyah al-Kalbi | Questioned Abu Sufyan; showed respect; did not accept (Sahih al-Bukhari 7) |
| Khusraw Parvez, Persian emperor | Abdullah ibn Hudhafah | Tore the letter |
| The Negus of Abyssinia | Amr ibn Umayyah al-Damri | Received it with honour |
| Al-Muqawqis of Egypt | Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah | Courteous reply with gifts |
| Rulers of Bahrain, Oman, Yamamah, Ghassan | Various | Mixed |

**Lessons:** universality of the message; courtesy in diplomacy; choice of capable envoys.

### 4. Other agreements

- **Pledges of Aqabah** (12th and 13th years of Prophethood).
- **Pacts with Banu Damrah, Juhaynah and others** around Madinah (1–2 AH).
- **Settlement of Khaybar** (7 AH): inhabitants keep their land for half the produce.
- **Treaty with the Christians of Najran** (9–10 AH): protection of life, property, churches and clergy in return for a fixed tribute.
- **Treaties with the border towns** during Tabuk (9 AH).

### 5. The Farewell Sermon (9 Dhul Hijjah, 10 AH / 632 CE)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Place | Arafat, during the only Hajj he performed after the Hijrah; further words at Mina |
| Audience | By the usual estimates more than a hundred thousand |
| Sources | Sahih al-Bukhari 67, 1739, 1741; Sahih Muslim 1218, 1679; Tirmidhi; Musnad Ahmad; Ibn Hisham |
| Points | Sanctity of life, property and honour. Blood-feuds of the age of ignorance cancelled. Usury cancelled. Rights of women; kind treatment. Equality of mankind; piety the only distinction. Individual responsibility. Brotherhood of believers. Hold to the Book of Allah. Convey the message to those absent. |
| Then | "Have I conveyed?" They said: "Yes." "O Allah, bear witness." |

**Lessons:** a charter of human rights; economic justice; equality; rule of law. See Notes 3.

## Part 3 — Battles and expeditions

*Ghazwah:* an expedition led by the Prophet (PBUH) in person; commonly counted as 27. *Sariyyah:* a detachment sent under a Companion. Numbers of troops and casualties below are the commonly cited figures; sources vary.

| Battle | Date (AH) | Muslim force | Opposing force | Key facts | Lessons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Badr** | 17 Ramadan, 2 | 313 | About 1,000 Quraysh | First major battle. 14 Muslims martyred; about 70 Quraysh killed and 70 captured. Abu Jahl killed. Captives ransomed or freed; some earned freedom by teaching. Called the "Day of the Criterion" (Al-Anfal 8:41). | Faith and discipline over numbers; consultation; intelligence; humane treatment of prisoners |
| **Uhud** | Shawwal, 3 | About 700 (after 300 withdrew) | About 3,000 | 50 archers posted on a hill under Abdullah ibn Jubayr; most left their post; Khalid's cavalry attacked from behind. About 70 martyrs, including Hamzah (RA). The Prophet (PBUH) wounded. | Obedience to command; danger of greed for spoils; steadfastness in setback (Al Imran 3:139–140, 3:152) |
| **Trench (Khandaq / Ahzab)** | Shawwal, 5 | About 3,000 | About 10,000 confederates | Trench dug on the advice of Salman al-Farisi (RA). Siege of several weeks. Amr ibn Abd Wudd killed by Ali (RA). Coalition broken by distrust and a storm (Al-Ahzab 33:9). | Innovation; patience; unity under threat; diplomacy to divide enemies |
| **Khaybar** | Muharram, 7 | About 1,400–1,600 | Fortified settlements | Forts taken one by one; Ali (RA) took Qamus. Land left with its people on a share basis. | Removing a persistent threat; just settlement after victory |
| **Mu'tah** | Jumada al-Ula, 8 | 3,000 | A much larger Byzantine and allied force | Commanders Zayd ibn Harithah, Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and Abdullah ibn Rawahah martyred in turn. Khalid ibn al-Walid (RA) withdrew the army in good order and was called "a sword among the swords of Allah". | Chain of command; courage; skilful withdrawal |
| **Conquest of Makkah** | Ramadan, 8 | 10,000 | — | Entered almost without fighting. General amnesty. Ka'bah cleared of 360 idols. Keys of the Ka'bah returned to Uthman ibn Talhah. | Secrecy and planning; mercy in victory; purpose of power |
| **Hunayn** | Shawwal, 8 | 12,000 | Hawazin and Thaqif | Ambush in a valley; initial panic; rally round the Prophet (PBUH); victory (At-Tawbah 9:25–26). About 6,000 captives later freed. | Numbers alone do not win; steadfast leadership; generosity to the defeated |
| **Tabuk** | Rajab, 9 | About 30,000 | Byzantines (did not appear) | Long march in great heat; called the "army of hardship". Treaties with border towns. Three sincere Companions who stayed behind were later forgiven (At-Tawbah 9:118). | Deterrence; mobilisation; sincerity tested |

**Other expeditions to know:** Banu Qaynuqa (2 AH), Banu Nadir (4 AH), Banu Mustaliq (5 or 6 AH), Banu Qurayzah (5 AH), siege of Ta'if (8 AH).

**Rules of war taught by the Prophet (PBUH):** no killing of women, children, the aged or monks; no treachery; no mutilation; no burning; no needless destruction of trees and buildings; keep treaties; treat prisoners well; accept peace when offered. Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 3014–3016; Sahih Muslim 1731; Muwatta Malik, Book of Jihad, no. 10; Al-Anfal 8:61.

## Part 4 — The Rightly Guided Caliphate (11–40 AH / 632–661 CE)

### Summary table

| Caliph | Titles | Period (AH) | Period (CE) | Length | Chosen by | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abu Bakr (RA) | al-Siddiq | 11–13 | 632–634 | About 2 years 3 months | Pledge at Saqifah, then general pledge | Died of illness in Madinah |
| Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) | al-Faruq; first called Amir al-Mu'minin | 13–23 | 634–644 | About 10 years 6 months | Nominated by Abu Bakr after consultation; public pledge | Stabbed by Abu Lu'lu while leading the dawn prayer; died end of Dhul Hijjah, 23 AH |
| Uthman ibn Affan (RA) | Dhun-Nurayn | 24–35 | 644–656 | About 12 years | Council of six appointed by Umar | Martyred in his house by rebels, Dhul Hijjah, 35 AH |
| Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) | al-Murtada; Asadullah | 35–40 | 656–661 | About 4 years 9 months | Pledge of the people of Madinah | Struck by a Kharijite at Kufa; died Ramadan, 40 AH |

Hasan ibn Ali (RA) was pledged to after his father for about six months, then made peace with Mu'awiyah (RA) in 41 AH, called the "Year of Unity". Many scholars count these months as completing the thirty years of the rightly guided Caliphate.

### Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (RA)

| Field | Events and measures |
|---|---|
| First acts | Steadied the community at the Prophet's (PBUH) death, reciting Al Imran 3:144. Sent out the army of Usamah as the Prophet (PBUH) had ordered. |
| Internal | Wars against the false claimants to prophethood (Musaylimah at Yamamah; Tulayhah; others) and the tribes that revolted or withheld zakat. "By Allah, I will fight whoever separates prayer from zakat." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1399–1400) |
| Quran | First collection in one volume by Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) after many memorisers fell at Yamamah (Sahih al-Bukhari 4986) |
| External | Campaigns opened in Iraq (Khalid ibn al-Walid) and Syria (four columns); victory at Ajnadayn |
| Government | Consultation; modest stipend; ten instructions on the conduct of war |
| Succession | Nominated Umar (RA) after consulting senior Companions |

### Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA)

| Field | Events and measures |
|---|---|
| Conquests | Syria: Yarmuk (15 AH / 636). Iraq and Persia: Qadisiyyah under Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (c. 15–16 AH / 636–637); Mada'in; Jalula; Nihawand, the "victory of victories" (21 AH / 642). Jerusalem surrendered to Umar in person (c. 16–17 AH / 637–638). Egypt under Amr ibn al-As (19–21 AH / 640–642). |
| Administration | Provinces under governors with separate officers for revenue, treasury, police and justice. New cities: Basra, Kufa, Fustat. |
| Finance | Central and provincial treasuries; register of stipends (*diwan*); land survey of Iraq; land tax; conquered farmland kept as public property |
| Justice | Independent, salaried judges; the letter to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari |
| Accountability | Asset lists for officials; complaints heard at Hajj; inspectors; dismissal of governors |
| Welfare | Stipends for children, the old, the disabled; relief in the famine of 18 AH; stipends for needy non-Muslims |
| Other | Hijri calendar (17 AH); census; prisons; night patrols; canals; congregational tarawih (Sahih al-Bukhari 2010) |
| Succession | Appointed a council of six: Uthman, Ali, Talhah, Zubayr, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abdur Rahman ibn Awf (may Allah be pleased with them) (Sahih al-Bukhari 3700) |

### Uthman ibn Affan (RA)

| Field | Events and measures |
|---|---|
| Quran | Standard copies prepared by a committee under Zayd ibn Thabit and sent to the provinces (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987) |
| Navy | First Muslim fleet; Cyprus (28 AH / 649); naval victory of the Masts (c. 34 AH / 655) |
| Expansion | North Africa (Ifriqiya), Armenia, Azerbaijan, Khurasan; end of the Sasanian dynasty with the death of Yazdegerd III (31 AH / 651) |
| Works | Enlargement of the Prophet's Mosque and the Sacred Mosque; wells, roads; second call to prayer on Fridays |
| Character | Known for generosity (the well of Rumah; equipping the army of Tabuk) and modesty |
| Later years | Complaints against some governors; unrest from Egypt, Kufa and Basra; siege of his house; martyrdom while reading the Quran |

### Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA)

| Field | Events and measures |
|---|---|
| Capital | Moved from Madinah to Kufa |
| Internal conflicts | Battle of the Camel (36 AH / 656); Siffin against the army of Syria (37 AH / 657); arbitration; Nahrawan against the Khawarij (38 AH / 658) |
| Government | Equal distribution from the treasury; strict supervision of governors; detailed letters of instruction (Nahj al-Balaghah) |
| Justice and learning | Renowned as a judge (Sahih al-Bukhari 4481) and for eloquence and knowledge |
| Approach to dissent | Debated the Khawarij first; fought them only after they killed innocents |

A note for answers: the conflicts of this period involved Companions on both sides. The mainstream Sunni position is to respect them all and to treat their differences as matters of sincere judgment. Shia Muslims hold their own considered view of these events. Keep to the governance record and avoid passing judgment.

### Salient features of the Rightly Guided Caliphate

1. Sovereignty of Allah; supremacy of Quran and Sunnah.
2. Leadership by consultation and public pledge; no dynasty.
3. The Caliph as trustee and servant, living simply.
4. Rule of law and equality before it.
5. Independent judiciary.
6. Accountability of rulers and officials; freedom to criticise.
7. A public treasury held as a trust.
8. Welfare for all citizens, Muslim and non-Muslim.
9. Merit in appointments.
10. Protection of minorities and observance of treaties.
