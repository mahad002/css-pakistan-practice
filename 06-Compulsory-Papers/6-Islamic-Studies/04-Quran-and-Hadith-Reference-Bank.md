# Quran and Hadith Reference Bank

Verses and ahadith arranged by theme, for quoting in answers.

## How to read this file

- **Verses:** surah name, then surah number : ayah number. The English is a short plain rendering of the meaning, checked against the Saheeh International translation on 23–24 November 2026. It is not a word-for-word translation. In the exam write "The Quran says (meaning): ..." or paraphrase without quotation marks.
- **Ahadith:** the collection is named with the number used on sunnah.com. In the exam the collection name is enough. Where the narrator was confirmed, the name is given.
- **★** marks the core set to memorise first: 75 verse entries and 57 ahadith. If time is short, learn the ★ entries of the themes you have chosen to prepare.
- **[verify]** marks a number I could not check. Quote the meaning and the collection only, or leave it out.
- A grading note is added where hadith scholars have discussed a narration's chain.
- Part 3 gives short Arabic phrases with exact text. Part 4 lists popular sayings you should not quote as hadith.

Counts: 298 verse entries (many cover more than one ayah) and 203 hadith entries.

# Part 4 — Sayings you should NOT quote as hadith

These are popular in guidebooks and speeches. Hadith scholars have judged them weak, baseless or not the words of the Prophet (PBUH). Using them with "the Prophet said" can cost marks with a knowledgeable examiner. Use the sound alternative.

|---|---|---|
| "Seek knowledge even if you have to go to China." | Judged very weak or fabricated by hadith scholars | Ibn Majah 224; Muslim 2699 |
| "Seek knowledge from the cradle to the grave." | Not a hadith; a wise saying | Ta-Ha 20:114 |
| "The ink of the scholar is holier than the blood of the martyr." | Weak or fabricated | Abu Dawud 3641 |
| "Love of one's homeland is part of faith." | Fabricated | Give your own argument without a hadith |
| "Difference of opinion in my community is a mercy." | No sound chain | Bukhari 946 (Banu Qurayzah) as evidence that differing was tolerated |

**Historical reports about the Caliphs** (the Copt and the governor's son; the question about Umar's cloak; the woman who corrected Umar on dowers; Ali's coat of armour before Judge Shurayh; the mule stumbling in Iraq) come from history and biography books, and some have weak chains.
