## 3. How references were checked

### Quran

- Every reference of the form "Surah-name number:ayah" in all files was checked by script: the surah name matches the surah number, and the ayah exists. Result: 488 distinct named references, no mismatch.
- The meaning of each cited ayah was read against the Saheeh International English text, fetched from an online Quran service (alquran.cloud), over the two days of building. About 650 individual ayahs were read this way.
- English renderings in the pack are short paraphrases in plain words. They are not exact translations and should not be quoted as if they were a published translation. Before memorising a verse, read it once on quran.com.
- The 37 Arabic verse phrases were copied by script from a standard Arabic text (the "simple" script with vowel marks), not typed from memory.
- quran.com itself was not opened directly; the same text was reached through the service above.

### Hadith

- 434 distinct collection-and-number citations are used across the pack (Sahih al-Bukhari 198, Sahih Muslim 134, Sunan Abi Dawud 41, Jami at-Tirmidhi 40, Sunan Ibn Majah 16, Sunan an-Nasa'i 5), plus four from the Muwatta.
- Each was looked up in an open hadith dataset that follows the numbering of sunnah.com, and the English text at that number was read to confirm that it is the hadith described. sunnah.com itself refused automated requests, so it was not opened directly.
- A script then confirmed that every number cited in the files is on the checked list. One number is not: **Jami at-Tirmidhi 1209** (the truthful merchant), whose text was missing from the dataset. It carries a [verify] tag.
- **Sahih Muslim 1467 and 1469:** the hadith texts were found in the dataset in the right place, but its numbering there is shifted by one. The numbers given follow sunnah.com from memory. Check before relying on the number.
- Narrators' names were taken from the dataset text. For Sahih Muslim 1827 the dataset's English reads "Abdullah b. Umar"; the pack gives Abdullah ibn Amr, which is the reading in the Arabic. Check if you intend to quote the narrator.
- The five Arabic hadith phrases were copied by script from the Arabic of the same dataset.

## 5. What is missing

1. **Original past papers.** The pack describes the questions. Download the actual papers from FPSC and keep them in `09-Past-Papers`.
2. **Model answers for some likely questions.** Full answers exist for 15 topics. For the Prophet (PBUH) as educator and as diplomat, responsibilities of civil servants, Islam and the West, the family, the political and judicial systems, Islamophobia, sectarianism, tolerance, moral values, Hajj, salah and fasting, there are full notes and outlines but no written-out answer. Writing these yourself is the best practice.
3. **Full Arabic text of verses and ahadith.** Only short phrases are given, by design.
4. **Urdu material.** Nothing in Urdu; the Urdu books on the official list are named only.
5. **The angels, the revealed books and qadr** are covered briefly. A full question on them alone has not appeared in this period.
6. **Detailed fiqh of worship** (conditions and invalidators of prayer, fasting, hajj) is not covered; the paper asks about effects, not rulings. A few such points appear in the MCQ bank.
7. **Islamic history after 661 CE** is limited to dates for MCQs.
8. **School-specific detail.** Positions are given in mainstream terms with a note where schools differ. The pack does not set out each school's view.
9. **MPT-specific pattern.** The MCQ bank serves both the written paper and the screening test, but no analysis of past MPT papers was done.
10. **Diagrams.** No flowcharts are included; the tables can be turned into simple diagrams in the exam.
11. **Examiner comments after 2016.**

## 6. Suggested first actions for the candidate

1. Read `00-How-to-Use-This-Pack.md`, then `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`.
2. Open the FPSC site in a browser; save the syllabus, the last ten papers and any examiners' reports; confirm the language rule and pass mark.
3. Look up the seven flagged narrations on sunnah.com or in a library copy and either fill in the numbers or strike them out.
4. Begin Notes 2 (Seerah) and Notes 3 (human rights and women): the two most frequently asked sections.
5. Start the ★ references in the bank at five a week.
