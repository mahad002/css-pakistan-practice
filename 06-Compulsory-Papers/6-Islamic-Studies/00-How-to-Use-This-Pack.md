## 2. The paper format (confirmed)

Source: the official FPSC question paper for CSS 2026 (see `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md` for the link) and the official FPSC syllabus.

| Part | Content | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part I | 20 MCQs, compulsory, on an OMR sheet | 20 | Maximum 30 minutes. The sheet is collected after 30 minutes. |
| Part II | 7 questions (Q.2 to Q.8). Attempt ONLY FOUR. Each carries 20 marks. | 80 | The remaining 2 hours 30 minutes |

Rules printed on the 2026 paper:

- No negative marking in MCQs. Attempt all 20.
- Overwriting or cutting on the OMR sheet earns no credit.
- All parts of one question must be answered in one place.
- Write the question number exactly as on the paper.
- Leave no blank page between answers. Cross out unused pages.
- An extra (fifth) answer is not marked.
- The paper is printed in both English and Urdu. Each question appears in both languages.

Language: the FPSC rules allow this paper to be answered in English or in Urdu. You will write in English. (Confirmed: CE Rules 2019, Rule 16(25), and Syllabus Note-4 — this is the only compulsory paper that may be answered in Urdu.)

Pass mark: 40 out of 100 (confirmed: CE Rules 2019, Rule 12(1); see `../../01-Plan/Exam-Rules.md`). A realistic target for a well-prepared candidate is 60–70.

There is no fixed section-wise choice. Any four of the seven questions may be chosen.

## 3. How answers are marked

FPSC does not publish a marking scheme. What is known comes from examiner reports and from the pattern of papers.

**From the official examiners' report on CSS 2016 (Islamic Studies):**

- Grade spread: 2% extraordinary, 3% excellent, 20% very good, 30% good, 40% ordinary, 5% failed.
- The best candidates "seemed to be well read" and cited references from various books.
- Common mistakes: question numbers not written on the script, and verses and ahadith written in Arabic with errors. The examiner asked candidates to improve their Arabic writing.

**From the same report, comments on other papers that apply here too:**

- Long answers do not guarantee marks. Irrelevant detail scored lowest.
- Candidates reproduced prepared notes instead of answering the question asked.
- Analysis and a clear structure were missing in weak scripts.

**From the 2019 examiners' report (seen only as a short excerpt):** on the question about the moral value system of Islam, most candidates wrote general features of Islamic civilisation and missed the specific demand.

**What this means in practice:**

1. Answer the exact question. If it has two parts, give each part its own heading.
2. Support every main point with a reference: a verse (surah and ayah number), a hadith (collection named), or a Seerah or Caliphate example.
3. Be accurate. A wrong verse number or a wrongly attributed hadith is worse than none.
4. Show reading: one or two scholars or books per answer.
5. Apply the point to today: Pakistan, the Muslim world, or a modern issue.
6. Write neatly, with headings. The examiner marks hundreds of scripts.

## 4. Time plan for the exam

| Minutes | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | MCQs. Finish in 15–20 minutes. Do not leave any blank. |
| 20–30 | Use any spare MCQ time to read Part II and pick four questions. (If the Part II paper is given only after the OMR is collected, do this at minute 30.) |
| 30–35 | Choose four. Write a 6–8 line outline for each on the last page of the answer book, then cross it out at the end. |
| 35–71 | Answer 1 (36 minutes) |
| 71–107 | Answer 2 |
| 107–143 | Answer 3 |
| 143–176 | Answer 4 (33 minutes) |
| 176–180 | Check question numbers. Cross blank pages. |

Start with your strongest question. Never spend 50 minutes on one answer. Four balanced answers beat two long and two short ones.

**Length:** about 6–8 sides of the answer book per answer, roughly 900–1,200 words. The model answers in this pack are 1,200–1,500 words so that they contain everything; in the hall you will write about three quarters of that.

## 5. The answer template that scores

Use the same skeleton for every 20-mark answer.

**1. Introduction (5–7 lines)**

- Define the key term (literal meaning, then technical meaning).
- Place it in Islam's framework with one strong verse or hadith.
- State your position or the line of the answer in one sentence.

**2. Body (10–14 headed points)**

- Each heading is a short claim, not a single word. Write "Zakat circulates wealth" rather than "Economic".
- Under each heading write 3–5 lines: the point, the reference, one example.
- Order: concept → Quran → Sunnah → Seerah and Rightly Guided Caliphs → scholars → impact or analysis.
- If the question has two parts, split the body under two main headings, A and B.

**3. Contemporary relevance (4–8 lines, or woven into points)**

- Pakistan: the Constitution, a law, an institution, or a current problem.
- Muslim world or global: Islamophobia, Palestine, debt, technology, family breakdown.
- Give 3–5 practical recommendations when the question says "suggest" or "how".

**4. Critical note (optional, 3–4 lines)**

- For "critically analyse" or "compare" questions. State where Muslims fall short of the ideal, or where two views differ, in a balanced tone.

**5. Conclusion (4–5 lines)**

- Answer the question directly in one sentence.
- End with a verse, or a line from Iqbal, only if you remember it exactly.

**Presentation**

- Use a blue pen for text and a black pen or marker for headings. Underline references.
- Leave one line between headings.
- A small diagram or flowchart is welcome where it helps (for example: sources of law; eight heads of zakat).
- Number your headings.

## 6. How to quote verses and ahadith

**Arabic or translation?**

- A translation with the correct reference is fully acceptable in an English-medium answer.
- Arabic adds weight only if it is written without a single mistake. The 2016 examiner specifically complained about errors in Arabic.
- Rule for this candidate: memorise 20–25 short Arabic phrases perfectly (exact text is in `04-Quran-and-Hadith-Reference-Bank.md`, Part 3). Write everything else in English.
- Never write Arabic from half-memory. Never transliterate long passages.

**Format to use for a verse**

> The Quran says: "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due, and when you judge between people, to judge with justice." (An-Nisa 4:58)

- Give the surah name and the numbers. If you remember only the surah, write the surah name alone. Do not guess the ayah number.
- If you are paraphrasing, do not use quotation marks. Write: The Quran commands justice even against one's own relatives (An-Nisa 4:135).

**Format to use for a hadith**

> The Prophet (PBUH) said: "Each of you is a shepherd and each of you is responsible for his flock." (Sahih al-Bukhari; Sahih Muslim)

- Name the collection. The hadith number is not needed in the exam. The narrator's name is a bonus.
- If you are not sure of the collection, write "It is reported in hadith that..." and give the meaning. Do not attach "Bukhari" to a saying you are unsure of.
- Do not use the doubtful sayings listed at the end of `04-Quran-and-Hadith-Reference-Bank.md`.

**How many?** About 5–8 verses and 3–5 ahadith per answer, each tied to a point. A wall of quotations with no explanation does not score.

**Honorifics:** write (PBUH) after the Prophet's name and (RA) after Companions. In the exam, the Arabic ﷺ is also fine if you can write it neatly.

## 7. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Writing everything you know on the topic | Underline the command word and the limits in the question. Answer only that. |
| Ignoring the second half of a two-part question | Give it its own heading and at least a third of the answer |
| Sermon tone, emotional language | Write like an analyst: claim, evidence, example |
| No references, or vague "Islam says" | One reference per point |
| Wrong ayah numbers, invented ahadith | Quote only what you have memorised from the reference bank |
| Arabic with errors | English meaning plus reference |
| No contemporary link | Add a "Contemporary relevance" heading every time |
| Sectarian or polemical statements | State mainstream positions. Where schools differ, say "jurists differ" and move on |
| Criticising other religions or the West crudely | Compare respectfully, with facts |
| Poor time split | 36 minutes per answer. Stop and move on. |
| Not writing the question number, or changing it | Copy the number from the paper |
| Narrating Seerah events as a story | State the event in two lines, then the lesson or principle |

## 8. Study plan (about 15 hours a week across all subjects)

Islamic Studies needs roughly 110–130 hours in total for a strong score. A workable split:

| Phase | When | Hours | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. First reading | Feb–May 2027 | 40 | Read `03-Notes/` file by file. One section per two weeks. Read the matching part of the reference bank. |
| 2. References | Jun–Aug 2027 | 25 | Memorise the core verses and ahadith marked ★ in the reference bank (75 verse entries, 57 ahadith). 15 minutes a day works better than long sessions. |
| 3. MPT period | Sep–Oct 2027 | 10 | The screening test (MPT) has Islamic Studies MCQs. Do the full `07-MCQ-Bank.md` twice. |
| 4. Writing | Nov–Dec 2027 | 30 | Write 12 timed answers (36 minutes each). Compare with `05-Model-Answers.md`. |
| 5. Revision | Jan–Feb 2028 | 20 | `09-Quick-Revision.md`, timeline, MCQs again, two full 3-hour mocks. |

After each study session, close the notes and write the outline of one answer from memory. This is the single most useful habit.

## 9. Which four questions to pick in the hall

1. Pick questions where you can recall at least 6 references and a clear structure.
2. Prefer questions with a defined scope (Charter of Madinah, zakat, ijtihad, accountability) over open ones ("role of Islam in the modern world") unless you have prepared the open one.
3. Read every word. "In Pakistan", "in the light of the Seerah", "critically", "compare" each change the answer.
4. Recent papers (2024–2026) have moved toward applied questions: Pakistan's accountability system, sectarianism, the family, the West. Prepare the contemporary sections of every notes file.
