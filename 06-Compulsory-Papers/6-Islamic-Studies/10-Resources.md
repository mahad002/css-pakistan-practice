# Resources

This pack is meant to be enough for the paper. Use outside resources for three purposes only: to check a reference, to read one or two original books so that your answers show real reading, and to see the actual past papers.

A candidate with about 110–130 hours for this subject should spend at most 20–25 of them outside this folder.

## 2. Checking references

| Site | Use |
|---|---|
| **quran.com** | Type the surah and ayah (for example 4:58). Read the Arabic and one or two translations. Use it to practise writing the short Arabic phrases. Its word-by-word view helps if your Arabic is weak. |
| **sunnah.com** | Search a hadith by number (for example "bukhari 7138") or by keyword. It shows the Arabic, the English, the narrator, the book and, for the four Sunan, a grading. The hadith numbers in this pack follow its numbering. |
| **tanzil.net** | A clean, verified Arabic text of the Quran for copying phrases exactly |

**Routine:** when you choose a reference to memorise, look it up once on these sites, read the whole verse or hadith, and write it by hand three times. This takes five minutes and fixes it in memory.

## 4. Books: the short list

Read in this order. Items 1–4 are enough for most candidates.

| # | Book | Author | Why | How to use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Introduction to Islam* | Dr Muhammad Hamidullah | First on the FPSC list. Short chapters on belief, worship, morals, the political, judicial and economic systems, women, non-Muslims, and Muslim contributions to science. | Read once fully (about 10 hours). It gives you authoritative sentences to cite for sections I, III, IV and VII. Freely available online. |
| 2 | *Ar-Raheeq al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar)* | Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri | A clear, dated biography of the Prophet (PBUH) | Read the Madinan chapters: Charter, battles, Hudaybiyyah, letters, Conquest, Farewell Pilgrimage. Use with `06-Seerah-Timeline.md`. |
| 3 | *Towards Understanding Islam* | Abul A'la Maududi | Simple treatment of faith, prophethood, worship and Shariah; the classic list of the effects of Tawhid | Read chapters on faith and worship (3–4 hours) |
| 4 | *The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*, Lecture 6, "The Principle of Movement in the Structure of Islam" | Allama Muhammad Iqbal | The standard text on ijtihad, ijma and the legislature | Read this one lecture twice. Note three or four ideas to cite. |
| 5 | *Islam: Its Meaning and Message* | Khurshid Ahmad (ed.) | Essays on Islam's basic principles, political and economic theory, women, and the modern world | Read the editor's essay on basic principles and characteristics |
| 6 | *Islamic Law and Constitution* | Abul A'la Maududi | Political system: sovereignty, vicegerency, rights of citizens, rights of non-Muslims | Read selectively for section VII |
| 7 | *Al-Farooq* | Shibli Nu'mani | Umar's (RA) life and administration | Read the second part, on administration, for section VI |
| 8 | Letter 53 of *Nahj al-Balaghah* (to Malik al-Ashtar) | Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) | A syllabus item | Read a full English translation once; it is about ten pages |
| 9 | *Islam and the Economic Challenge* or *An Introduction to Islamic Finance* | Umer Chapra; Muhammad Taqi Usmani | Economic system and riba | Read the introduction and the chapters on riba and on the principles of Islamic finance |
| 10 | *Islam at the Crossroads* | Muhammad Asad | Islam and Western civilization | Short; read for section V |
| 11 | *Human Rights in Islam* | Abul A'la Maududi | A short booklet | One sitting; for section III |
| 12 | *Administrative Development: An Islamic Perspective* | Muhammad Al-Buraey | On the FPSC list for public administration | Only if you find a copy easily; the notes cover the essentials |

**For hadith in book form:** *Riyad al-Salihin* (al-Nawawi) arranges sound ahadith by moral theme, and *Forty Hadith* (al-Nawawi) is the best short collection to memorise from. Both are on sunnah.com.

**Urdu readers:** *Sirat-un-Nabi* (Shibli and Sulaiman Nadvi), *Khutbat-e-Madras* (Sulaiman Nadvi), *Insan-e-Kamil* (Dr Khalid Alvi), *Islami Nizam-e-Zindagi aur us ke Bunyadi Tasawwurat* (Maududi) and *Islam ka Muashi Nizam* (Taqi Usmani) are all on the official list.

## 5. Local guidebooks

Bookshops sell single-volume "Islamiat for CSS" guides, and most candidates use one. They are convenient for coverage. Their weakness is in references: verse numbers are sometimes wrong, and weak or unsourced sayings are quoted as hadith. If you buy one, use it for extra outlines only, and check every reference against quran.com and sunnah.com before you memorise it. The list in Part 4 of the reference bank shows the commonest doubtful sayings.

## 7. A weekly routine that fits 15 hours across all subjects

| Day | Islamic Studies time | Task |
|---|---|---|
| One weekday evening | 45 minutes | Read one section of a notes file |
| Another weekday evening | 30 minutes | Memorise five references from the bank; revise last week's five |
| Weekend | 45–60 minutes | Outline one answer from memory, then check; or do 50 MCQs |

That is about two hours a week. From February 2027 to January 2028 it comes to roughly 100 hours, plus mock papers in the last two months.
