# Notes 2 — Seerah of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) as Role Model

Syllabus section II: study of the Sirah as a role model for the individual, the diplomat, the educator, the military strategist and the peacemaker.

Past questions: military strategist or commander (2016, 2019, 2023, 2024); peacemaker or prophet of peace (2017, 2018, 2022, 2024); tolerance and forgiveness (2020); Charter of Madinah (2020, 2023); Hudaybiyyah and Khaybar notes (2021); teacher (2023 Special); Farewell Sermon (2020, 2023); peace agreements and noble morals (2025, reported).

## 1. Why the Seerah is the model

| Point | Reference |
|---|---|
| The Quran names him the model | "There is for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent example, for whoever hopes in Allah and the Last Day and remembers Allah often." (Al-Ahzab 33:21) |
| His character is praised by Allah | "And indeed you are of a great moral character." (Al-Qalam 68:4) |
| His character was the Quran in practice | Aisha (RA): "His character was the Quran." (Sahih Muslim 746) |
| His mission was moral | "I was sent to perfect good character." (Muwatta Malik, Book of Good Character, no. 8; also Musnad Ahmad) |
| A mercy for all | Al-Anbiya 21:107 |
| Following him is the proof of loving Allah | Al Imran 3:31 |

**Why his life can serve as a model for everyone**

1. **Complete:** orphan, shepherd, trader, husband, father, neighbour, preacher, refugee, statesman, judge, commander, treaty-maker. Few lives cover so many roles.
2. **Recorded:** the Quran, the hadith collections and the early biographies (Ibn Ishaq as edited by Ibn Hisham; al-Waqidi; Ibn Sa'd) preserve it in detail.
3. **Practical:** he did what he taught.
4. **Human:** "I am only a man like you, to whom it is revealed." (Al-Kahf 18:110). A human example can be followed.
5. **Successful:** in 23 years he changed the beliefs, morals and politics of Arabia. Michael Hart placed him first in *The 100* because he succeeded at both the religious and the worldly level.

**Books to cite:** Shibli Nu'mani and Sulaiman Nadvi, *Sirat-un-Nabi*; Sulaiman Nadvi, *Khutbat-e-Madras* (eight lectures on why the Prophet's life is the perfect model: it is historical, complete, comprehensive and practical); Dr Hamidullah, *The Battlefields of the Prophet Muhammad*, *The First Written Constitution in the World* and *The Muslim Conduct of State*; Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri, *Ar-Raheeq al-Makhtum*; Martin Lings, *Muhammad*; Dr Khalid Alvi, *Insan-e-Kamil*.

## 2. Role model as an individual

Use for questions on character, noble morals, tolerance and forgiveness, or "the Prophet (PBUH) as an individual".

### 2.1 Before prophethood

- Known in Makkah as *al-Sadiq* (the truthful) and *al-Amin* (the trustworthy).
- Took part in *Hilf al-Fudul*, a pact to defend the wronged. He later said he would answer such a call even in Islam (reported in Musnad Ahmad and al-Bayhaqi [number not verified]).
- Settled the dispute over the Black Stone at age 35 by having the clans lift it together on a cloth (Ibn Hisham).
- Khadijah (RA) described him at the first revelation: he keeps ties of kinship, bears the burden of the weak, helps the destitute, honours the guest and assists those struck by calamity (Sahih al-Bukhari 3).
- Even his enemies left their valuables with him. On the night of the Hijrah he left Ali (RA) behind to return them.

### 2.2 Qualities, each with evidence

| Quality | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Truthfulness and trust** | Abu Sufyan, then an enemy, admitted before Heraclius that he had never known him to lie or break a promise (Sahih al-Bukhari 7). |
| **Mercy** | "I was not sent as one who curses; I was sent as a mercy." (Sahih Muslim 2599). He kissed his grandson and said: "He who shows no mercy will be shown none." (Sahih al-Bukhari 5997) |
| **Forgiveness** | At Ta'if he was stoned, yet refused the offer to crush the town, hoping their children would worship Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 3231; Sahih Muslim 1795). At the Conquest of Makkah he announced a general amnesty. |
| **Patience** | 13 years of abuse in Makkah; the three-year boycott; the loss of Khadijah (RA), Abu Talib and six of his seven children in his lifetime. |
| **Humility and simplicity** | He served his family at home (Sahih al-Bukhari 676), mended his own things, and died with his armour pledged to a Jewish trader for barley (Sahih al-Bukhari 2916). |
| **Generosity** | "More generous than the blowing wind", most of all in Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 6). He never said no to one who asked (Sahih al-Bukhari 6034). |
| **Justice** | "If Fatimah the daughter of Muhammad stole, I would cut off her hand." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475) |
| **Gentleness** | Anas (RA) served him ten years and was never rebuked (Sahih al-Bukhari 6038). He never struck a servant or a woman (Sahih Muslim 2328). "Allah is gentle and loves gentleness in all things." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6927) |
| **Moderation** | Given a choice, he took the easier way unless it was a sin (Sahih al-Bukhari 3560). |
| **Courage** | At Hunayn he held his ground when many fled (At-Tawbah 9:25–26). |
| **Devotion** | He prayed at night until his feet swelled: "Should I not be a grateful servant?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4836) |
| **Best to his family** | "The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895) |
| **Consultation** | "Consult them in the matter." (Al Imran 3:159). He consulted at Badr, Uhud and Khandaq. |
| **Keeping promises** | He returned Abu Jandal to Quraysh under the treaty just signed at Hudaybiyyah (Sahih al-Bukhari 2731). |

The Quran explains his success with people: "By mercy from Allah you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you." (Al Imran 3:159)

### 2.3 Tolerance and forgiveness (asked 2020)

1. **Ta'if:** forgave those who stoned him.
2. **Makkah, 8 AH:** general amnesty to those who had persecuted him for 21 years. The well-known words "No blame on you today; go, you are free" are reported in the Seerah of Ibn Ishaq and by al-Bayhaqi (the chain is discussed by hadith scholars; the amnesty itself is firmly established, see Sahih Muslim 1780).
3. **Individuals forgiven:** Wahshi, who killed Hamzah (RA) (Sahih al-Bukhari 4072); Hind; Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl; Safwan ibn Umayyah; Thumamah ibn Uthal, a captured chief freed without ransom, who then accepted Islam (Sahih al-Bukhari 4372).
4. **The Bedouin in the mosque:** he stopped the Companions from attacking the man and taught him gently: "You were sent to make things easy, not hard." (Sahih al-Bukhari 220)
5. **Respect for others:** he stood for a Jewish funeral: "Was it not a soul?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1312). He visited a sick Jewish boy (Sahih al-Bukhari 1356).
6. **Reply to insult:** when some visitors twisted the greeting into a curse, he told Aisha (RA) to stay gentle (Sahih al-Bukhari 6024).
7. **Prayer for his people:** "O Allah, forgive my people, for they do not know." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3477, said in the context of an earlier prophet; Sahih Muslim 1792)
8. **Quranic command:** "Hold to forgiveness, enjoin what is good and turn away from the ignorant." (Al-A'raf 7:199); "Repel evil with what is better." (Fussilat 41:34)

**Limits:** forgiveness covered personal wrongs. He did not waive the rights of others or the law (the Makhzumi woman case).

**Today:** intolerance, mob violence and abuse on social media contradict this Sunnah. Pakistan's *Paigham-e-Pakistan* declaration (2018) by scholars of all schools rejects violence in the name of religion.

## 3. Role model as a diplomat

Diplomacy is the management of relations with other groups and states by negotiation, treaty and envoy.

### 3.1 Principles of his diplomacy

| Principle | Basis |
|---|---|
| Peace is preferred when the other side inclines to it | Al-Anfal 8:61 |
| Treaties must be kept | Al-Ma'idah 5:1; An-Nahl 16:91; Al-Isra 17:34 |
| No treachery even toward an enemy | "Fulfil the trust of the one who trusted you and do not betray the one who betrayed you." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1264; Sunan Abi Dawud 3535) |
| Envoys are protected | He did not harm the envoys of Musaylimah, saying that envoys are not killed (Sunan Abi Dawud 2761) |
| Dialogue with wisdom | An-Nahl 16:125; Al Imran 3:64 |
| Justice even to those you dislike | Al-Ma'idah 5:8 |
| Asylum for those who seek it | At-Tawbah 9:6 |
| Realism: take the achievable now, think long-term | Hudaybiyyah |

### 3.2 Main acts of diplomacy

**1. Migration to Abyssinia (5th year of prophethood).** He chose a just Christian king as protector. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib (RA) presented Islam in the Negus's court with tact, reciting from Surah Maryam. Lesson: seek allies on shared values; use able spokesmen.

**2. Pledges of Aqabah (12th and 13th years).** Negotiated a new base in Yathrib before migrating. Appointed twelve representatives (*nuqaba*). Lesson: prepare the ground before a major move.

**3. Charter of Madinah (1 AH).** A written agreement among Muhajirun, Ansar and the Jewish tribes. See section 3.3.

**4. Treaties with neighbouring tribes (1–2 AH).** Pacts with Banu Damrah, Banu Mudlij and Juhaynah secured the routes around Madinah and isolated Quraysh.

**5. Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (6 AH).** See section 3.4.

**6. Letters to rulers (end of 6 AH and 7 AH).** To Heraclius of Byzantium (carried by Dihyah al-Kalbi), Khusraw Parvez of Persia (Abdullah ibn Hudhafah), the Negus of Abyssinia (Amr ibn Umayyah al-Damri), al-Muqawqis of Egypt (Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah), and rulers of Bahrain, Oman, Yamamah and Ghassan. The letter to Heraclius began in the name of Allah, invited him to Islam, and quoted Al Imran 3:64 (Sahih al-Bukhari 7). He had a seal made reading "Muhammad Rasul Allah". Lessons: universal message, respectful titles ("the great one of the Romans"), a clear invitation, envoys chosen for skill and knowledge of the land.

**7. Conquest of Makkah (8 AH).** Won almost without bloodshed through secrecy, a show of strength and an amnesty. Abu Sufyan's house was declared a place of safety (Sahih Muslim 1780).

**8. Year of Delegations (9 AH).** Dozens of tribal delegations were received in the mosque, hosted, taught and sent back with teachers. The Christian delegation of Najran was received in the mosque and, according to the Seerah of Ibn Ishaq, allowed to pray there. A treaty guaranteed their lives, property, churches and clergy in return for a fixed tribute.

**9. Intelligence and communication.** Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) learnt the Jewish script on his order to handle correspondence (Sunan Abi Dawud 3645; Jami at-Tirmidhi 2715).

### 3.3 The Charter of Madinah (Mithaq al-Madinah) — asked 2020, 2023

**Background.** Madinah in 622 had no central authority. Aws and Khazraj had fought for decades (Battle of Bu'ath). Three Jewish tribes (Qaynuqa, Nadir, Qurayzah) lived there, plus pagans and the new Muslim migrants. The Prophet (PBUH) drew up a written document (*kitab, sahifah*) preserved by Ibn Ishaq and by Abu Ubayd in *Kitab al-Amwal*. Dr Hamidullah divided it into 52 clauses (others count 47) and called it the first written constitution in the world.

**Main provisions**

1. **One political community.** The believers of Quraysh and Yathrib and those who join them are "one community (ummah) apart from other people".
2. **Jews included.** The Jews of Banu Awf (and, by extension, the other clans) are "a community along with the believers. The Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs."
3. **Religious freedom** for each group.
4. **Tribal structure kept for social duties:** each clan pays its own blood-money and ransoms its captives, with fairness.
5. **Collective security.** All defend Madinah against attack. Each group bears its own expenses and helps the other.
6. **No separate peace.** No group makes peace or war separately. No protection to Quraysh or their helpers.
7. **Rule of law.** A wrongdoer is not shielded, even by his own kin. Individual responsibility for crime.
8. **Protection of the weak.** The protection of Allah is one; the protection given by the least of them binds all. A neighbour is like oneself.
9. **Madinah a sanctuary** (*haram*) for the people of the document.
10. **Final authority.** Any dispute is referred "to Allah and to Muhammad".

**As a social contract**

- A social contract is an agreement by which people form a political society and accept a common authority in exchange for security and rights (Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau in the 17th–18th centuries).
- The Charter was a real, written, negotiated contract, about a thousand years earlier, and not a theory.
- It created a state from tribes: citizenship based on agreement and residence, not blood.
- It defined rights and duties of all parties, a central authority, a judicial reference, and a defence pact.
- It was plural: Muslims, Jews and pagans were citizens with equal protection.
- It balanced local autonomy (clans keep internal affairs) with central authority (defence, foreign policy, final judgment), which resembles a federal arrangement.

**Results:** end of the Aws–Khazraj feud; a secure base; the Prophet (PBUH) recognised as head of state; a model of pluralism.

**What happened later:** the three Jewish tribes were dealt with one by one after breaches of the pact in wartime, according to the Seerah sources. In an answer, state this in one neutral line and return to the principles.

**Today:** a model for constitutionalism, minority rights, and national unity in plural states such as Pakistan (compare Quaid-e-Azam's 11 August 1947 speech; Articles 20 and 36 of the Constitution).

Full answer: MA-8.

### 3.4 The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (6 AH) — asked 2021

**Background.** The Prophet (PBUH) set out with about 1,400 Companions for umrah, unarmed except travellers' swords. Quraysh blocked the way at Hudaybiyyah. Uthman (RA) was sent as envoy; a rumour of his death led to the Pledge of Ridwan under a tree (Al-Fath 48:18). Quraysh then sent Suhayl ibn Amr to negotiate. Ali (RA) wrote the treaty (Sahih al-Bukhari 2731; Sahih Muslim 1783–1784).

**Terms**

1. Truce for ten years.
2. Muslims return this year without umrah and come next year for three days, swords sheathed.
3. Any Makkan who goes to Madinah without his guardian's permission is returned; a Muslim who goes to Makkah is not returned.
4. Any tribe may ally with either side. (Khuza'ah joined the Muslims; Banu Bakr joined Quraysh.)

**Conduct in negotiation**

- Suhayl objected to "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" and to "Messenger of Allah". The Prophet (PBUH) accepted "In Your name, O Allah" and "Muhammad son of Abdullah" (Sahih Muslim 1784). He gave way on form and kept the substance.
- Abu Jandal, Suhayl's own son, arrived in chains. The Prophet (PBUH) returned him as the treaty required and told him to be patient.
- The Companions were distressed; Umar (RA) questioned the terms. On the advice of Umm Salamah (RA), the Prophet (PBUH) sacrificed and shaved first, and all followed (Sahih al-Bukhari 2731).

**Why it was a "clear victory" (Al-Fath 48:1)**

1. Quraysh recognised the Muslims as an equal party.
2. Peace allowed free contact; more people accepted Islam in the next two years than in the previous nineteen. Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As accepted Islam in 8 AH.
3. The southern front was quiet, so Khaybar was dealt with in 7 AH and letters were sent to kings.
4. The Muslims performed umrah the next year (Al-Fath 48:27).
5. The one-sided clause turned against Quraysh: Abu Basir and others formed a band on the coast road, and Quraysh themselves asked for the clause to be cancelled.
6. When Banu Bakr, helped by Quraysh, attacked Khuza'ah, the treaty was broken by Quraysh, which led to the Conquest of Makkah with 10,000 men (the pilgrims at Hudaybiyyah had been about 1,400).

**Lessons:** patience over emotion; long-term over short-term gain; honouring one's word even when it hurts; flexibility on wording; peace as an opening for the message; consult and take advice, including from women.

**Gains from Khaybar (7 AH), asked as a short note in 2021:** removed the threat from the north where hostile groups had gathered and had helped raise the confederate army of 5 AH; secured the route to Syria; strengthened state finances (the land stayed with its farmers for half the produce, an early share-cropping contract); raised Muslim standing among the tribes; Ali (RA) took the fort of Qamus (Sahih al-Bukhari 4210); led to peace with Fadak, Wadi al-Qura and Tayma.

### 3.5 Outline: "The Prophet (PBUH) as a diplomat"

Introduction (33:21; definition) → principles (6) → Abyssinia → Aqabah → Charter → tribal pacts → Hudaybiyyah → letters → Makkah → delegations and Najran → features (truth, patience, realism, respect, keeping promises) → relevance for Muslim foreign policy (OIC unity, conflict resolution, minorities) → conclusion.

## 4. Role model as an educator

Asked in 2023 Special with the saying "I have been sent as a teacher."

### 4.1 The Quran on his teaching role

"It is He who sent among the unlettered a Messenger from themselves, reciting to them His verses, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom." (Al-Jumu'ah 62:2; also Al-Baqarah 2:129, 2:151; Al Imran 3:164). Four functions: recitation, purification (*tazkiyah*), teaching the Book, teaching wisdom.

The first revelation was a command to read (Al-Alaq 96:1–5).

**The hadith.** "Allah did not send me to be harsh or to cause hardship; He sent me as a teacher who makes things easy." (Jabir, Sahih Muslim 1478). The wording "I have been sent only as a teacher" is in Sunan Ibn Majah 229; hadith scholars grade its chain as weak, so prefer the Sahih Muslim wording.

A Companion's witness: Mu'awiyah ibn al-Hakam (RA) spoke during prayer by mistake; afterwards he said: "I never saw a teacher, before or after him, who taught better. He did not scold, hit or insult me." (Sahih Muslim 537)

### 4.2 Value he gave to knowledge

| Saying | Source |
|---|---|
| "Seeking knowledge is a duty on every Muslim." | Sunan Ibn Majah 224 (graded sound by many scholars on account of its many chains) |
| "Whoever follows a path seeking knowledge, Allah makes easy for him a path to Paradise." | Sahih Muslim 2699 |
| "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it." | Sahih al-Bukhari 5027 |
| "The scholars are the heirs of the prophets." | Sunan Abi Dawud 3641; Jami at-Tirmidhi 2682 |
| "Convey from me, even if it is one verse." | Sahih al-Bukhari 3461 |
| "When Allah wishes good for someone, He gives him understanding of the religion." | Sahih al-Bukhari 71 |
| Knowledge that benefits continues after death | Sahih Muslim 1631 |
| He asked Allah for beneficial knowledge and refuge from knowledge that does not benefit | Sahih Muslim 2722 |

Quran: "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (Az-Zumar 39:9); "My Lord, increase me in knowledge." (Ta-Ha 20:114)

### 4.3 Institutions he set up

1. **Dar al-Arqam** in Makkah: the first teaching circle.
2. **Mus'ab ibn Umayr (RA)** sent to Yathrib as teacher before the Hijrah.
3. **The Prophet's Mosque** as school, with the **Suffah**, a residential platform for poor students. Abu Hurayrah (RA) was among them.
4. **Literacy drive after Badr:** literate captives who could not pay ransom were freed for teaching ten children of Madinah to write (reported in Musnad Ahmad and the Seerah works [number not verified]).
5. **Teachers and governors sent out:** Mu'adh ibn Jabal and Abu Musa (RA) to Yemen with the instruction "make things easy, do not make them hard; give good news, do not drive people away" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3038).
6. **Women's education:** a separate day for women at their request (Sahih al-Bukhari 101). Al-Shifa bint Abdullah was asked to teach Hafsah (RA) (Sunan Abi Dawud 3887). Aisha (RA) became a leading scholar.
7. **Writing:** scribes for revelation; letters and treaties; the Sahifah of Abdullah ibn Amr (RA).
8. **Languages:** Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) learnt the Jewish script in about two weeks on his order (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2715).

### 4.4 His teaching methods

| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| Teaching by example | "Pray as you have seen me pray." (Sahih al-Bukhari 631); "Take your rites from me." (Sahih Muslim 1297) |
| Gradualism | Wine was prohibited in stages; Mu'adh (RA) was told to call to faith first, then prayer, then zakat (Sahih al-Bukhari 1395) |
| Repetition | He repeated a key sentence three times so it was understood (Sahih al-Bukhari 95) |
| Question and answer | "Do you know who the bankrupt is?" (Sahih Muslim 2581) |
| Parables and comparisons | The five prayers as a river (Sahih al-Bukhari 528); believers as one body (Sahih Muslim 2586) |
| Drawing and gesture | Two fingers held together for the guardian of an orphan (Sahih al-Bukhari 5304) |
| Brevity and timing | He spaced out his talks so that people would not tire (Sahih al-Bukhari 68) |
| Kindness to the one who errs | The Bedouin in the mosque (Sahih al-Bukhari 220); the man who prayed badly was taught step by step (Sahih al-Bukhari 757) |
| Teaching the young with affection | "O boy, say the name of Allah, eat with your right hand, and eat from what is near you." (Sahih al-Bukhari 5376); advice to Ibn Abbas (RA) (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2516) |
| Addressing each by his need | Different people who asked "which deed is best?" received different answers |
| Encouraging questions | Jibril's questions "to teach you your religion" (Sahih Muslim 8) |
| Linking knowledge with action | He sought refuge from knowledge that does not benefit (Sahih Muslim 2722) |
| Moral warning against hiding knowledge | Sunan Abi Dawud 3658 |

### 4.5 Results

- An unlettered society became the carrier of a book-based civilization within one generation.
- Companions became jurists, governors, judges and teachers: Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Mu'adh, Ibn Abbas, Aisha, Zayd ibn Thabit (may Allah be pleased with them).
- The tradition led to the great centres of learning: Madinah, Kufa, Baghdad's Bayt al-Hikmah, Cordoba, al-Qarawiyyin, al-Azhar.

### 4.6 Lessons for today

Free and universal education including girls; teacher's character as the main tool; kindness, not humiliation; activity-based and gradual teaching; uniting religious and worldly knowledge (Pakistan's split between madrasah and school); education for character, not only for jobs; adult literacy drives modelled on Badr. Pakistan: Article 25-A (right to education, ages 5–16); a literacy rate of roughly 60% and millions of out-of-school children show the gap.

### 4.7 Outline: "The Prophet (PBUH) as educator"

Introduction (62:2; Muslim 1478) → importance he gave to knowledge → aims of his education (faith, character, skill) → institutions → methods (8) → women's education → results → relevance and recommendations → conclusion.

## 5. Role model as a military strategist

Asked 2016, 2019, 2023, 2024. A full answer is MA-7.

### 5.1 Framework: war in Islam

- Fighting was permitted only after 13 years of patience and after the migration: "Permission is given to those who are fought against, because they were wronged." (Al-Hajj 22:39)
- Limits: "Fight in the way of Allah those who fight you, but do not transgress." (Al-Baqarah 2:190)
- Peace when offered: Al-Anfal 8:61.
- Readiness as deterrence: "Prepare against them whatever force you can." (Al-Anfal 8:60)
- He said: "Do not wish to meet the enemy; but when you meet them, be steadfast." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3024)

**Scale.** Expeditions he led in person (*ghazawat*) are commonly counted as 27; fighting took place in about nine. Detachments he sent (*saraya*) are counted between about 38 and 56 in different sources. Dr Hamidullah estimated that in ten years an area of well over a million square miles came under Madinah with only a few hundred deaths on both sides. Give this as Hamidullah's estimate.

### 5.2 Strategic principles with examples

| Principle | Example |
|---|---|
| **Clear, limited aim** | To secure freedom of belief and end aggression, not to destroy the enemy. Makkah was taken with almost no bloodshed. |
| **Intelligence** | Scouts before Badr estimated enemy numbers from the camels slaughtered daily. Hudhayfah (RA) was sent into the enemy camp at Khandaq. Abbas (RA) and Khuza'ah supplied news from Makkah. |
| **Secrecy and surprise** | He would hint at a different direction when setting out (Sahih al-Bukhari 2947). The march on Makkah was kept secret until the army was at its gates. "War is deception." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3030) |
| **Consultation** | Badr: he moved camp to the wells on the advice of al-Hubab ibn al-Mundhir (Ibn Hisham). Uhud: he accepted the majority view to fight outside. Khandaq: he adopted Salman's (RA) trench. |
| **Choice of ground** | Badr: control of water, sun behind the Muslims. Uhud: mountain at the back, archers on the pass. Khandaq: trench on the only open side of Madinah. |
| **Innovation** | The trench was new to Arab warfare. Fighting in ranks (*saff*) at Badr replaced the tribal charge-and-retreat. Siege engines were tried at Ta'if. |
| **Economy of force; economic pressure** | Early patrols threatened Quraysh's Syrian trade route, forcing them to negotiate or fight on his terms. |
| **Alliances and isolating the enemy** | Pacts with the coastal tribes; Hudaybiyyah split Quraysh from Khaybar. At Khandaq, Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud (RA) sowed distrust between Quraysh and Banu Qurayzah. |
| **Speed and mobility** | Rapid marches to Banu Mustaliq, Khaybar and Tabuk reached the enemy before they were ready. |
| **Morale and faith** | Prayer before Badr; sharing hardship: he dug the trench with his men and tied a stone to his stomach from hunger (Sahih al-Bukhari 4101). |
| **Discipline and chain of command** | At Mu'tah he named three commanders in succession (Sahih al-Bukhari 4261). The archers' disobedience at Uhud showed the cost of indiscipline (Al Imran 3:152). |
| **Psychological operations** | Ten thousand campfires lit outside Makkah. Pursuit to Hamra al-Asad the day after Uhud showed the Muslims unbroken. |
| **Leading from the front, with courage** | At Hunayn he advanced when others fell back (At-Tawbah 9:25–26). |
| **Merit in appointments** | Khalid ibn al-Walid (RA), a recent convert, was given command; young Usamah ibn Zayd (RA) led an army that included senior Companions. |
| **Deterrence without battle** | Tabuk (9 AH): 30,000 marched north; the Byzantines did not appear; border tribes made treaties. |
| **Humane conduct** | No killing of women and children (Sahih al-Bukhari 3014–3015); no treachery, no mutilation (Sahih Muslim 1731); captives of Badr were fed before their captors and ransomed or freed (Al-Insan 76:8; Muhammad 47:4). |
| **Winning the peace** | Amnesty at Makkah; generous gifts to new converts after Hunayn (Sahih al-Bukhari 4330). Former enemies became commanders of Islam. |

### 5.3 Battle summaries for strategy answers

| Battle | Strategic point |
|---|---|
| **Badr (2 AH)** | 313 against about 1,000. Intelligence, choice of ground, control of water, ranks, morale. "Allah had already given you victory at Badr when you were weak." (Al Imran 3:123) |
| **Uhud (3 AH)** | Sound plan (50 archers on the pass). Initial success, then reversal when most archers left their post. Lesson in discipline; he regrouped on the hill and pursued the next day. |
| **Khandaq (5 AH)** | 3,000 behind a trench against a coalition of about 10,000. Defensive innovation, endurance, diplomacy to split the allies. The siege collapsed (Al-Ahzab 33:9). After it he said the initiative had passed to the Muslims. |
| **Hudaybiyyah (6 AH)** | Strategy by treaty: neutralise the main enemy. |
| **Khaybar (7 AH)** | Swift march, forts taken one by one, a political settlement that kept the land productive. |
| **Mu'tah (8 AH)** | First clash with Byzantium. Khalid (RA) saved the army by a skilful withdrawal. |
| **Makkah (8 AH)** | Secrecy, four columns, overwhelming force, amnesty. A near-bloodless conquest. |
| **Hunayn (8 AH)** | Ambush and initial panic; rally around the commander; lesson against pride in numbers (At-Tawbah 9:25). |
| **Tabuk (9 AH)** | Strategic deterrence and mobilisation in extreme heat. |

### 5.4 Were the ghazawat for peace? (asked 2024)

Argue yes, with evidence:

1. **Defensive origin:** Badr, Uhud and Khandaq were fought at or near Madinah against attacking forces.
2. **Aim stated by the Quran:** to end persecution (*fitnah*) (Al-Baqarah 2:193) and protect places of worship of all faiths (Al-Hajj 22:40).
3. **Peace accepted whenever offered:** Hudaybiyyah on hard terms.
4. **Minimal bloodshed:** very low casualties across ten years.
5. **No forced conversion:** Al-Baqarah 2:256; Makkans were not compelled.
6. **Result:** Arabia, torn by tribal feuds, became secure. He had foretold that a traveller would go from San'a to Hadramawt fearing none but Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 3612).
7. **Rules of war** protected non-combatants, crops and envoys (Abu Bakr's (RA) ten instructions, Muwatta Malik, Book of Jihad, no. 10).

### 5.5 Relevance today

Deterrence and preparedness (Pakistan's defence doctrine cites Al-Anfal 8:60); ethics of war and international humanitarian law (the Geneva Conventions of 1949 came thirteen centuries later); intelligence and unity of command; civil-military consultation; no targeting of civilians, which separates lawful defence from terrorism.

## 6. Role model as a peacemaker

The most repeated Seerah theme. A full answer is MA-6.

### 6.1 Peace in his message

- The name *Islam* shares its root with *salam*, peace. The greeting of Muslims is peace. One of Allah's names is *As-Salam* (Al-Hashr 59:23). Paradise is the "home of peace".
- "We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds." (Al-Anbiya 21:107)
- "If they incline to peace, incline to it also and trust in Allah." (Al-Anfal 8:61)
- "A Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand people are safe." (Sahih al-Bukhari 10)
- "Shall I tell you what is better in rank than fasting, prayer and charity? Making peace between people." (Sunan Abi Dawud 4919; Jami at-Tirmidhi 2509)

### 6.2 Peacemaking before prophethood

1. Hilf al-Fudul.
2. The Black Stone arbitration, which prevented bloodshed among the clans.

### 6.3 Peace with the pagans of Makkah and Arabia

1. **Thirteen years of non-violence in Makkah** under persecution. No retaliation was permitted.
2. **Ta'if:** forgiveness.
3. **Hijrah** instead of civil war.
4. **Treaty of Hudaybiyyah**, called a clear victory.
5. **Conquest of Makkah:** general amnesty; "this is a day of mercy". He rejected the cry "today is a day of slaughter" and took the flag from the one who said it (Sahih al-Bukhari 4280).
6. **After Hunayn:** released some six thousand captives of Hawazin on their request.
7. **Abolition of blood feuds** in the Farewell Sermon, beginning with the claim of his own clan (Sahih Muslim 1218).

### 6.4 Peace within Madinah

1. **Aws and Khazraj reconciled** after generations of war. "You were enemies and He joined your hearts, so by His favour you became brothers." (Al Imran 3:103)
2. **Brotherhood (*mu'akhat*)** between Emigrants and Helpers ended economic and social friction (Sahih al-Bukhari 3780; Al-Hashr 59:9).
3. **Personal mediation:** he went himself to reconcile Banu Amr ibn Awf (Sahih al-Bukhari 1218).
4. **Mosque of harm** (*masjid al-dirar*) removed because it was built to divide the community (At-Tawbah 9:107).

### 6.5 Peace with Jews

1. **Charter of Madinah:** equal citizens with religious freedom.
2. **Everyday dealings:** trade, loans, visits to the sick, respect at funerals.
3. **Khaybar settlement:** people stayed on their land.
4. Action against particular tribes followed breach of the pact in wartime, not their faith.

### 6.6 Peace with Christians

1. **Abyssinia:** trust in a Christian king; he prayed the funeral prayer in absence for the Negus (Sahih al-Bukhari 1245).
2. **Najran delegation:** hosted in the mosque; treaty of protection for life, property, churches and clergy.
3. **Letters** to Heraclius and al-Muqawqis in courteous terms.
4. **Call to common ground:** Al Imran 3:64.
5. The charter said to have been given to the monks of St Catherine's in Sinai is famous; its authenticity is disputed by historians. Do not rely on it.

### 6.7 Peace as policy

- **Peace through justice:** Al-Ma'idah 5:8.
- **Sanctity of life:** Al-Ma'idah 5:32; "Whoever kills a person under treaty will not smell the fragrance of Paradise." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3166)
- **Human equality:** Al-Hujurat 49:13; the Farewell Sermon.
- **Social peace:** bans on backbiting, spying and mockery (Al-Hujurat 49:11–12); rights of neighbours (Sahih al-Bukhari 6014).
- **War only to remove aggression,** with strict ethics.

### 6.8 For "prophet of peace for the contemporary transnational world" (2022)

Add: his model addresses today's problems. Refugees (Hijrah and brotherhood); plural societies (the Charter); conflict resolution by treaty and arbitration (Hudaybiyyah); protection of minorities (Najran); ethics of war (rules of combat); racism (Farewell Sermon); interfaith dialogue (3:64); Islamophobia answered by character, not anger. Western writers who acknowledged his peacemaking include W. Montgomery Watt (*Muhammad at Medina*) and Karen Armstrong (*Muhammad: A Prophet for Our Time*).

## 7. Other roles (short notes, useful for any Seerah question)

**As head of state:** founded a state on a written charter; appointed governors, judges and collectors; held officials to account (the case of Ibn al-Lutbiyyah, Sahih al-Bukhari 6979, 7174); set up market inspection (Sahih Muslim 102); consulted.

**As judge:** equality before law (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475); burden of proof on the claimant (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1341); no judgment in anger (Sahih al-Bukhari 7158); he warned that a clever litigant who wins by false argument gains only a piece of fire.

**As reformer:** ended idol-worship, female infanticide (At-Takwir 81:8–9), usury, blood feuds, drinking and gambling; raised the status of women, slaves and the poor.

**As husband and father:** helped at home; consulted his wives; honoured Fatimah (RA) by standing for her; raced with Aisha (RA); mourned his son Ibrahim with tears and patience.

**As trader:** honest partnership with Khadijah (RA). "The truthful, trustworthy merchant will be with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1209 [verify number])
