## 1. Meanings

### 1.1 Culture

- **English:** from Latin *cultura*, cultivation. E. B. Tylor (*Primitive Culture*, 1871) defined it as the complex whole of knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom and other capabilities acquired by people as members of society.
- **Arabic:** *thaqafah*, from a root meaning to sharpen, refine and become skilful.
- **In short:** the way of life of a people: beliefs, values, manners, language, arts, customs.

### 1.2 Civilization

- **English:** from Latin *civis* (citizen) and *civitas* (city). Life in organised cities with institutions.
- **Arabic:** *hadarah* (settled life, as opposed to nomadic life) and *tamaddun* (from *madinah*, city). Ibn Khaldun uses *umran* for organised human society.
- **Urdu:** *tahzib* (refinement) for culture; *tamaddun* for civilization. The two are often used loosely for each other.
- **In short:** the advanced, organised stage of a society: its political, economic, legal, scientific and artistic institutions.

### 1.3 Relation between the two

| Culture | Civilization |
|---|---|
| Inner: ideas, values, tastes | Outer: institutions, technology, cities |
| What a people *are* | What a people *have built* |
| Can exist without advanced civilization | Cannot exist without a culture |
| The soul | The body |

Maududi (*Islamic Civilization: Its Foundational Beliefs and Principles*) argues that the real basis of any civilization is its view of five things: the nature of worldly life, the purpose of life, basic beliefs, the training of the individual, and the social system. Arts and sciences are fruits, not roots.

### 1.4 Islamic culture and civilization defined

Islamic civilization is the way of life and the institutions built by Muslims on the foundation of Tawhid, prophethood and accountability in the Hereafter, as taught by the Quran and Sunnah. It is defined by belief and values, not by race, land or language. Arabs, Persians, Turks, Berbers, Indians, Malays and Africans all contributed to it.

## 2. Vital elements of a civilization

**General elements (any civilization)**

1. A worldview or belief system.
2. A moral code and law.
3. Political organisation.
4. An economic system and material resources.
5. Knowledge, science and education.
6. Language, literature and arts.
7. Social institutions: family, class structure.

Will Durant (*The Story of Civilization*) lists four: economic provision, political organisation, moral traditions, and the pursuit of knowledge and arts.

**Elements of Islamic civilization**

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Creed | Tawhid, Risalat, Akhirah |
| Sources | Quran, Sunnah, ijma, ijtihad |
| Worship | Prayer, zakat, fasting, hajj as training |
| Morality | A fixed code: truth, justice, modesty, mercy |
| Law | Shariah, with its aims (protection of religion, life, intellect, family, property) |
| Knowledge | Religious and worldly learning as one whole |
| Family | Marriage, kinship, rights of parents and children |
| Brotherhood | The Ummah above race and tribe |
| Institutions | Mosque, school, market under supervision, treasury, courts, endowments (*waqf*) |
| Arts | Calligraphy, architecture, geometry, poetry, within moral limits |

**Elements of Western civilization (for the 2023 Special comparison)**

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Roots | Greek philosophy, Roman law, Christianity, then Renaissance, Reformation, Enlightenment |
| Worldview | Secularism: religion kept private; human reason and experience as the final authority |
| Individualism | The individual's freedom and rights at the centre |
| Political form | Nation-state, democracy, constitutionalism |
| Economy | Capitalism, market, interest-based finance |
| Knowledge | Empirical science and technology |
| Values | Liberty, equality, human rights, materialism and consumerism |

**Comparison**

| Aspect | Islamic | Western (modern) |
|---|---|---|
| Source of values | Revelation with reason | Human reason and majority will |
| Sovereignty | Allah's; people as trustees | The people or the state |
| View of man | Servant and vicegerent of Allah | Autonomous individual |
| Aim of life | Allah's pleasure; success in both worlds | Worldly welfare and happiness |
| Morality | Fixed core | Changing with society |
| Religion and state | Joined in guidance | Separated |
| Family | Central, protected | Increasingly a private choice |
| Economy | Market with moral limits; zakat; no riba | Market with interest |
| Basis of unity | Faith | Nation, territory |
| Strengths to acknowledge | Moral and spiritual balance | Science, institutions, rule of law, accountability |

## 3. Role of civilization in developing human personality and community

A syllabus item not yet asked directly. Prepare the outline.

**How civilization shapes the person**

1. **Gives a worldview:** the individual learns who he is and what life is for.
2. **Forms character:** through family, school, worship and example. In Islam the five prayers, fasting and zakat are a training system.
3. **Trains the mind:** language, knowledge and habits of thought.
4. **Disciplines desire:** through moral norms and law.
5. **Gives identity and belonging:** a person sees himself as part of a community with a history.
6. **Refines taste:** manners, cleanliness, arts.
7. **Sets models to follow:** in Islam, the Prophet (PBUH) and the Companions.

**How civilization shapes the community**

1. **Common values create cohesion** (Ibn Khaldun's *asabiyyah*; in Islam, brotherhood of faith, Al-Hujurat 49:10).
2. **Institutions organise cooperation:** state, courts, markets, schools.
3. **Law gives security and justice.**
4. **Economy meets needs and shares wealth.**
5. **Knowledge is preserved and passed on.**
6. **Collective purpose:** in Islam, enjoining good and forbidding evil (Al Imran 3:110).

**The Islamic case in history.** Within one generation a tribal, feuding, mostly unlettered people produced upright individuals (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, may Allah be pleased with them) and a law-based community. The Quran notes the change: "You were enemies and He joined your hearts." (Al Imran 3:103)

**Law of rise and fall.** "Allah does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves." (Ar-Ra'd 13:11). When rulers and the wealthy become corrupt, decline follows (Al-Isra 17:16). Ibn Khaldun: civilizations pass from hardship and solidarity to luxury and weakness. Toynbee: civilizations grow by successful responses to challenges and die from inner failure, not outside attack.

## 4. Characteristics of Islamic civilization

The syllabus names eight. Learn each with a definition, three references, one historical example and one line on today. Any of the eight can be a full question. For "basic characteristics of Islamic civilization" (2018), write all eight with 4–5 lines each.

### 4.1 Tawhid

- The root of everything else: one God means one humanity, one moral law, one final accountability.
- References: Al-Ikhlas 112:1–4; Al-An'am 6:162; Al-Anbiya 21:25.
- Effects on civilization: freed the mind from superstition; made nature an object of study, not worship; ended sacred kingship and priesthood; unified peoples of many races.
- Detail: Notes 1, section 5.2.

### 4.2 Self-purification (Tazkiyah) — asked 2017, 2018, 2024

**Meaning.** *Tazkiyah* means to cleanse and to make grow. Technically: cleansing the heart from false beliefs and bad traits, and developing good ones, so that conduct follows the will of Allah.

**In the Quran**

| Point | Reference |
|---|---|
| A purpose of prophethood | "...reciting to them His verses, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom." (Al-Jumu'ah 62:2; Al-Baqarah 2:129, 2:151; Al Imran 3:164) |
| The key to success | "He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who corrupts it." (Ash-Shams 91:9–10) |
| | "He has succeeded who purifies himself, remembers the name of his Lord and prays." (Al-A'la 87:14–15) |
| The soul knows both paths | Ash-Shams 91:7–8 |
| Paradise for one who restrains the self | An-Nazi'at 79:40–41 |
| Only a sound heart avails | Ash-Shu'ara 26:88–89 |
| It benefits the person himself | "Whoever purifies himself does so for his own good." (Fatir 35:18) |
| Allah loves those who purify themselves | Al-Baqarah 2:222; At-Tawbah 9:108 |

**Three states of the self (nafs)**

1. *Nafs ammarah*: the self that urges evil (Yusuf 12:53).
2. *Nafs lawwamah*: the self that blames itself (Al-Qiyamah 75:2).
3. *Nafs mutma'innah*: the self at peace (Al-Fajr 89:27–30).

Purification is the journey from the first to the third.

**In the Sunnah**

- "There is a piece of flesh in the body; if it is sound, the whole body is sound; if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. It is the heart." (Sahih al-Bukhari 52; Sahih Muslim 1599)
- "Allah does not look at your bodies or your wealth; He looks at your hearts and your deeds." (Sahih Muslim 2564)
- "The fighter (mujahid) is one who strives against his own self." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1621)
- "The strong man is not the one who wrestles others down; the strong man is the one who controls himself when angry." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6114)
- "Fear Allah wherever you are, follow a bad deed with a good one to wipe it out, and treat people with good character." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1987)

**Ihsan (asked with tazkiyah in 2018).** "To worship Allah as though you see Him; and if you do not see Him, He sees you." (Sahih Muslim 8). Ihsan is the highest level of the religion: doing every act well, with awareness of Allah. "Allah has prescribed excellence (ihsan) in everything." (Sahih Muslim 1955). "Allah is with those who fear Him and those who do good." (An-Nahl 16:128). Tazkiyah is the process; ihsan is its fruit.

**Means of purification**

1. Sound belief and sincere intention.
2. The obligatory worship: prayer restrains from wrong (Al-Ankabut 29:45); zakat purifies (At-Tawbah 9:103); fasting builds taqwa (Al-Baqarah 2:183); hajj trains restraint (Al-Baqarah 2:197).
3. Remembrance of Allah (Ar-Ra'd 13:28) and recitation of the Quran with reflection.
4. Repentance and seeking forgiveness (Az-Zumar 39:53).
5. Self-accounting (*muhasabah*): "Let every soul look to what it has sent ahead for tomorrow." (Al-Hashr 59:18). A saying attributed to Umar (RA): take account of yourselves before you are taken to account (cited by al-Tirmidhi after hadith 2459).
6. Good company (Al-Kahf 18:28; At-Tawbah 9:119).
7. Lawful earning and food.
8. Remembering death and the Hereafter.
9. Voluntary worship: night prayer, extra fasts, charity. The hadith qudsi: "My servant keeps drawing near to Me with voluntary deeds until I love him." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6502)
10. Service to people.

**Traits to remove:** pride, envy, greed, anger, hypocrisy, showing off, love of status, miserliness. **Traits to gain:** sincerity, patience, gratitude, trust in Allah, humility, generosity, truthfulness, modesty.

Al-Ghazali's *Ihya Ulum al-Din* is built on this plan: a quarter on worship, a quarter on daily conduct, a quarter on the "destructive" traits and a quarter on the "saving" ones. Shah Waliullah treats ihsan as the third science of religion beside belief and law.

**Individual effects:** inner peace; self-control; sincerity; courage; steadiness in ease and hardship; a living conscience.

**Collective effects — why it is essential for society (2024)**

1. **Society is the sum of individuals.** Reform begins within (Ar-Ra'd 13:11).
2. **Honest dealings** without constant policing.
3. **Lower crime and corruption.** Law punishes after the act; a purified conscience prevents it.
4. **Trustworthy leadership and officials.** Umar (RA) and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz lived simply while holding great power.
5. **Social harmony.** Removing envy, pride and suspicion removes the causes of conflict (Al-Hujurat 49:11–12).
6. **Compassion and welfare.** Generosity replaces greed (Al-Hashr 59:9).
7. **Strong families.** Patience and kindness at home.
8. **Balanced material progress.** Wealth and technology serve people, not the reverse.
9. **Resistance to extremism.** Humility and self-criticism are the opposite of self-righteous violence.

**Today.** Pakistan's main problems (corruption, tax evasion, adulteration, intolerance, harassment) are moral before they are legal. Recommendations: character education from the Seerah in schools; mosques as centres of moral training; example-setting by leaders; media responsibility; ethics training in the civil service.

Full answer: MA-11.

**Sufism (Tasawwuf) — asked 2021**

- *What it is.* The discipline that developed around tazkiyah and ihsan: purifying the heart and deepening love of Allah. Early figures: Hasan al-Basri, Junayd al-Baghdadi. Al-Ghazali reconciled it with law and theology. Major orders: Qadiri (Abdul Qadir Jilani), Chishti, Suhrawardi, Naqshbandi.
- *Role in spreading Islam.* In South Asia, Islam spread widely through Sufis: Ali Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh, author of *Kashf al-Mahjub*) in Lahore; Mu'inuddin Chishti in Ajmer; Baha'uddin Zakariya in Multan; Fariduddin Ganj-i-Shakar in Pakpattan; Nizamuddin Auliya in Delhi; Lal Shahbaz Qalandar in Sindh; Shah Jalal in Bengal. Also in Central Asia, Anatolia, sub-Saharan Africa and Indonesia (the *Wali Songo*). Their means: simple living, service (free kitchens), equality across caste, local languages and poetry, love and tolerance.
- *Can it correct today's deviations?* Yes, in its sound form: it answers materialism with contentment, extremism with love and humility, sectarianism with a focus on the heart, and moral decay with self-accounting. Conditions: it must stay within the Quran and Sunnah, and be freed from later abuses such as superstition, hereditary shrine-keeping for profit, and withdrawal from social duty. Shaykh Ahmad Sirhindi and Shah Waliullah both worked to keep it tied to the Shariah. Iqbal criticised the passive, world-denying kind and praised the active kind.
- Scholars differ in their attitude to particular Sufi practices. Present both the contribution and the criticism.

### 4.4 Equality — and the 2025 question on a class-based lifestyle

**Principle**

- One origin: An-Nisa 4:1. One standard: "The most honoured of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you." (Al-Hujurat 49:13)
- Equality before law: the Makhzumi woman case (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475, 6788): earlier nations were ruined because they punished the weak and spared the noble.
- Equality in worship: one row in prayer; one dress in hajj.
- Tribal pride abolished: he declared the pride of the age of ignorance to be under his feet (Sunan Abi Dawud 4588); "Whoever calls to tribal partisanship is not one of us." (Sunan Abi Dawud 5121; some scholars grade its chain weak; the meaning is supported by Sahih al-Bukhari 4905, where he called such cries "foul".)

**What equality means and does not mean.** Equal human worth, equal legal rights, equal opportunity. It does not mean identical income or ability: "We have raised some of them above others in rank so that they may take one another into service." (Az-Zukhruf 43:32). Differences in wealth are a test (Al-An'am 6:165) and carry duties; they are not a ground for honour.

**Islam's judgment on a class-based, elitist lifestyle**

1. **Pride is condemned.** "No one who has an atom's weight of pride in his heart will enter Paradise ... Pride is rejecting the truth and looking down on people." (Sahih Muslim 91). "Do not turn your cheek away from people in contempt, nor walk on the earth arrogantly." (Luqman 31:18)
2. **Luxury and display are condemned.** "Rivalry in worldly increase distracts you." (At-Takathur 102:1–2). Qarun's display and his end (Al-Qasas 28:76–82). "The wasteful are brothers of the devils." (Al-Isra 17:26–27). "Eat and drink, but do not be excessive." (Al-A'raf 7:31)
3. **Hoarding is condemned.** At-Tawbah 9:34–35; Al-Humazah 104:1–3.
4. **Privilege in law is condemned.** The Makhzumi case.
5. **The poor are not to be pushed aside.** Abasa 80:1–10; Al-Kahf 18:28 (stay with those who call on their Lord; do not look past them for the glitter of the world).
6. **Rulers must live like the ruled.** Umar (RA) wore patched clothes and required governors not to ride fine horses, eat fine flour, wear fine cloth or shut their doors to the needy (reported by Abu Yusuf and al-Tabari). Ali (RA) wrote to his governor Uthman ibn Hunayf rebuking him for attending a feast to which the rich were invited and the poor were not (Nahj al-Balaghah, Letter 45).
7. **Enjoyment of lawful good things is allowed** (Al-A'raf 7:32) so long as there is no pride, waste or neglect of others' rights. Islam opposes arrogance and extravagance, not wealth.

**Effects of a class-based lifestyle on society**

1. Resentment, envy and class hatred.
2. Two systems of justice, schooling and health: one for the rich, one for the poor.
3. Corruption, since display needs money beyond lawful income.
4. Waste of resources amid poverty.
5. Moral decay among the elite; despair, crime and extremism among the excluded.
6. Loss of merit: birth and connections replace ability.
7. Weak national unity; elite capture of the state.
8. Decline of the civilization (Al-Isra 17:16; Ibn Khaldun on luxury).

**Remedies:** zakat and fair taxation; ban on riba and hoarding; inheritance law breaking up estates; simple living by leaders; one standard of law; equal access to education and health; ending VIP culture and protocol privileges.

### 4.5 Social justice — short note asked 2023

**Meaning.** *Adl* is to give each his due; *qist* is fairness in shares. Social justice means fair distribution of rights, opportunities and resources, and protection of the weak.

**References**

- "Allah commands justice, good conduct and giving to relatives." (An-Nahl 16:90)
- Messengers were sent "so that people may uphold justice" (Al-Hadid 57:25).
- "Stand firm for justice, as witnesses for Allah, even against yourselves, your parents or your relatives, whether rich or poor." (An-Nisa 4:135)
- "So that wealth does not circulate only among the rich among you." (Al-Hashr 59:7)
- "In their wealth is a right for the one who asks and the deprived." (Adh-Dhariyat 51:19)

**Dimensions**

| Dimension | Islamic provisions |
|---|---|
| Legal | One law for all; independent judges; evidence-based judgment |
| Economic | Zakat, ushr, inheritance, ban on riba, hoarding and fraud; fair wages (Ibn Majah 2443) |
| Social | Equality of birth; rights of women, orphans, neighbours, servants |
| Political | Consultation; accountability; office as trust |
| Welfare | State responsibility for basic needs. Umar's (RA) stipends for infants, the old and the disabled; his saying that if a lost animal died on the bank of the Euphrates he feared Allah would question him about it (reported in history works; wording varies). |

Sayyid Qutb (*Social Justice in Islam*) names three foundations: absolute freedom of conscience, complete human equality, and firm mutual responsibility in society (*takaful*).

**Today:** inequality, elite capture, regressive taxation, unequal schooling, slow courts. Recommendations: progressive taxation plus an efficient zakat system; universal basic services; land and inheritance reform in practice; speedy justice.

### 4.6 Moral values — asked 2019

**Place of morality.** "I was sent to perfect good character." (Muwatta Malik, Good Character, no. 8). "The most complete of the believers in faith are those best in character." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1162). "Nothing is heavier on the believer's scale on the Day of Resurrection than good character." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2002). "Righteousness is good character." (Sahih Muslim 2553)

**Features of the Islamic moral system**

1. **Divine source:** standards do not change with fashion.
2. **Motive:** the pleasure of Allah and accountability in the Hereafter, not profit or praise.
3. **Comprehensive:** covers private, family, business, political and international conduct.
4. **Balanced:** no monasticism, no licence.
5. **Practical:** shown in a living example (Al-Ahzab 33:21).
6. **Universal:** owed to all people, even enemies (Al-Ma'idah 5:8).
7. **Linked with worship:** worship without morals is rejected (the "bankrupt" hadith, Sahih Muslim 2581; Sahih al-Bukhari 1903 on fasting).
8. **Inner and outer:** intention counts (Sahih al-Bukhari 1).
9. **Social enforcement:** enjoining good and forbidding evil (Al Imran 3:104).

**Core values with references**

| Value | Reference |
|---|---|
| Truthfulness | At-Tawbah 9:119; Sahih al-Bukhari 6094 |
| Trustworthiness | An-Nisa 4:58; Al-Mu'minun 23:8; signs of the hypocrite (Sahih al-Bukhari 33) |
| Justice | An-Nahl 16:90; Al-Ma'idah 5:8 |
| Keeping promises | Al-Isra 17:34; Al-Ma'idah 5:1 |
| Patience | Al-Baqarah 2:153; Az-Zumar 39:10 |
| Gratitude | Ibrahim 14:7 |
| Humility | Al-Furqan 25:63; Luqman 31:18–19 |
| Modesty (haya) | "Modesty is part of faith." (Sahih al-Bukhari 24; Sahih Muslim 36) |
| Mercy | Sahih al-Bukhari 6013; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1924 |
| Forgiveness and control of anger | Al Imran 3:134; Ash-Shura 42:37 |
| Generosity and preferring others | Al-Hashr 59:9 |
| Kindness to parents, kin, neighbours | An-Nisa 4:36; Sahih al-Bukhari 6014 |
| Brotherhood | Al-Hujurat 49:10; Sahih al-Bukhari 13 |
| Chastity | Al-Isra 17:32; An-Nur 24:30–31 |
| Honest trade | Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–3; "Whoever cheats is not one of us." (Sahih Muslim 101–102) |

**Vices forbidden:** lying, backbiting, slander, mockery, suspicion, spying (Al-Hujurat 49:11–12); pride (Sahih Muslim 91); envy; miserliness; bribery (Sunan Abi Dawud 3580); oppression (Sahih Muslim 2577).

The moral charter passages worth knowing as wholes: Al-Isra 17:23–39; Al-An'am 6:151–153; Luqman 31:13–19; Al-Furqan 25:63–74; Al-Hujurat 49:6–13; Al-Mu'minun 23:1–11.

**Why it is a significant feature of the civilization (the 2019 demand):** it shaped trade (Islam reached South-East Asia and East Africa largely through honest merchants), law (courts where a Caliph could lose to a commoner), war (rules protecting non-combatants), and government (simple, accountable rulers). Contrast modern moral relativism.

**Today:** the gap between ritual and conduct; corruption; dishonesty in trade; abuse on social media. Remedies as under tazkiyah.

### 4.7 Tolerance — coexistence (2021) and tolerance in Muslim history (2025)

**Meaning.** Tolerance (*tasamuh*) is to respect the right of others to hold and practise beliefs different from one's own, and to deal justly and kindly with them. It does not mean giving up one's own belief or treating all beliefs as the same.

**Quranic basis**

| Principle | Reference |
|---|---|
| No compulsion in religion | Al-Baqarah 2:256 |
| Faith is a free choice | Yunus 10:99; Al-Kahf 18:29 |
| Difference is part of Allah's plan | Hud 11:118; Al-Ma'idah 5:48 |
| "For you your religion, for me mine." | Al-Kafirun 109:6 |
| Do not insult what others worship | Al-An'am 6:108 |
| Justice and kindness to peaceful non-Muslims | Al-Mumtahanah 60:8 |
| Protect all places of worship | Al-Hajj 22:40 |
| Debate in the best manner | An-Nahl 16:125; Al-Ankabut 29:46 |
| Call to common ground | Al Imran 3:64 |
| The preacher is not a controller | Al-Ghashiyah 88:21–22 |
| Social relations allowed: food and marriage with People of the Book | Al-Ma'idah 5:5 |
| Protection for one who asks | At-Tawbah 9:6 |

**The Prophet's (PBUH) practice:** the Charter of Madinah; the Najran treaty; respect for a Jewish funeral; dealing and trading with non-Muslims; amnesty at Makkah. See Notes 2.

**Tolerance within the Ummah:** differences of legal opinion were accepted. The Companions differed over the instruction to pray Asr at Banu Qurayzah and neither group was blamed (Sahih al-Bukhari 946). Imam Malik is reported to have refused the Caliph's wish to impose his *Muwatta* on all regions. Imam Shafi'i is reported to have said that his opinion was right with the possibility of error, and another's wrong with the possibility of being right.

**Tolerance in Muslim history after the Prophet (PBUH) (2025)**

| Period | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Abu Bakr (RA)** | Instructions to the army: do not kill women, children or the aged; do not cut fruit trees or destroy buildings; leave monks in their cells (Muwatta Malik, Jihad, no. 10). |
| **Umar (RA)** | The assurance to the people of Jerusalem (c. 637–638): safety for their persons, property, churches and crosses; no compulsion in religion (al-Tabari). He is reported to have declined to pray inside the Church of the Holy Sepulchre so that Muslims would not later claim it. He allowed Jews to return to Jerusalem. Stipend for an aged Jewish beggar. His dying instruction to his successor to honour the covenant with the protected people and not to overburden them (Sahih al-Bukhari 1392, 3052). |
| **Ali (RA)** | Letter to Malik al-Ashtar: people are of two kinds, "either your brother in religion or your equal in creation" (Nahj al-Balaghah, Letter 53). |
| **Conquest of Syria and Egypt** | Local Christians, persecuted under Byzantine rule for doctrinal reasons, often welcomed Muslim rule; churches continued. |
| **Umayyads of Spain (711–1492)** | *Convivencia*: Muslims, Christians and Jews lived and studied together in Cordoba and Toledo. The Jewish scholar Maimonides was born in Cordoba and later served as physician at Saladin's court in Egypt. |
| **Abbasids** | Christian and Jewish scholars worked as translators and physicians in Baghdad's House of Wisdom (for example Hunayn ibn Ishaq). |
| **Salahuddin Ayyubi (1187)** | Retook Jerusalem without a massacre, in contrast to the Crusaders' capture of 1099; allowed ransom and safe passage. |
| **Ottomans** | The *millet* system gave religious communities their own courts and schools. In 1492 Sultan Bayezid II welcomed Jews expelled from Spain. |
| **Mughal India** | Hindus held high office; Akbar's policy of *sulh-i-kull* (peace with all). |

**A fair note:** Muslim history also has episodes of intolerance, by particular rulers or in times of war. These were departures from the teaching, and scholars of the time often objected. Add this line to show balance.

**Coexistence in a multi-faith society today (2021):** equal citizenship (Charter of Madinah as precedent); freedom of worship; justice in courts; kindness in daily life; dialogue; no forced conversion; no hate speech; cooperation in good (Al-Ma'idah 5:2). Pakistan: Articles 20, 22, 25, 36; the 2014 Supreme Court judgment on minority rights (Justice Tassaduq Jillani); *Paigham-e-Pakistan* (2018). Global: the 2007 open letter *A Common Word*; the UN's International Day to Combat Islamophobia (15 March).

### 4.8 Rule of law — not yet asked directly

**Meaning.** Everyone, ruler and ruled, is subject to the same known law, applied by independent courts.

**Basis**

- Sovereignty and final law belong to Allah (Yusuf 12:40); rulers are trustees (An-Nisa 4:58).
- Judge by what Allah has revealed (Al-Ma'idah 5:44, 5:45, 5:47, 5:48).
- Obedience to rulers is conditional: disputes go back to Allah and the Messenger (An-Nisa 4:59). "There is no obedience in disobedience to Allah; obedience is only in what is right." (Sahih al-Bukhari 7257)
- No exception for the powerful: the Makhzumi case (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475).
- Even the Prophet (PBUH) offered himself for retaliation if he had wronged anyone, as reported in the Seerah accounts of his last days.

**Examples from the Caliphate**

1. Abu Bakr (RA): "Obey me as long as I obey Allah and His Messenger; if I disobey, you owe me no obedience." (inaugural address, Ibn Hisham; Ibn Kathir judged its chain sound)
2. Umar (RA) appeared before the judge Ubayy ibn Ka'b in a dispute and insisted on equal treatment; he had his own son punished; he held the governor of Egypt's son to account on the complaint of a Copt, with the words "Since when have you enslaved people whose mothers bore them free?" (reported by Ibn Abd al-Hakam; a widely quoted historical report).
3. Ali (RA), while Caliph, lost a case over a coat of armour before Judge Shurayh for lack of evidence acceptable to the court (a famous report; hadith scholars regard its chain as weak, so call it "a well-known report").
4. Judges were independent of governors from Umar's (RA) time.

**Features:** supremacy of Shariah; equality before law; independent judiciary; presumption of innocence; no retroactive guilt (Al-Isra 17:15); individual responsibility (Al-An'am 6:164); accountability of rulers.

**Today:** selective accountability, delays, elite impunity. Pakistan ranks low on international rule-of-law indices. Recommendations: judicial independence and capacity; equal application; police reform; legal aid.

## 5. Further characteristics (for a fuller "features" answer)

1. **Universality:** not tied to a race or land.
2. **Balance of spirit and matter:** Al-Qasas 28:77.
3. **Love of knowledge:** first revelation; schools, libraries, observatories, hospitals.
4. **Moderation:** "a middle community" (Al-Baqarah 2:143).
5. **Unity in diversity:** one creed, many cultures.
6. **Welfare orientation:** zakat, waqf (endowments funded hospitals, schools, inns and water works for centuries).
7. **Family-centred society.**
8. **Dynamism through ijtihad.**

Mustafa al-Siba'i (*Some Glittering Aspects of Islamic Civilization*, on the official reading list) highlights: unity of God, universal humanism, moral foundation, belief in knowledge, and religious tolerance.
