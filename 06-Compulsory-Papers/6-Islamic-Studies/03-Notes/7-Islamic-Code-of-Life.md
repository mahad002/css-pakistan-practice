## 2. The social system

### 2.1 Foundations

1. **One human family:** An-Nisa 4:1; Al-Hujurat 49:13.
2. **Brotherhood of faith:** "The believers are but brothers." (Al-Hujurat 49:10). "None of you believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself." (Sahih al-Bukhari 13)
3. **The family as the basic unit.**
4. **Rights and duties in widening circles:** parents, spouse, children, kin, neighbours, the needy, society (An-Nisa 4:36).
5. **Moral environment:** modesty, truthfulness, cooperation in good (Al-Ma'idah 5:2).
6. **Collective responsibility:** enjoining good and forbidding evil (At-Tawbah 9:71).
7. **Social justice and care for the weak.**

### 2.3 The family: role and present challenges (asked 2026)

**Role of the family in Islam**

1. **Tranquillity, love and mercy** between spouses (Ar-Rum 30:21).
2. **Lawful continuation of the human race** and clear lineage (An-Nahl 16:72).
3. **First school:** faith, language, manners and character are learned at home. "Guard yourselves and your families from the Fire." (At-Tahrim 66:6). "Enjoin prayer on your family and be steadfast in it." (Ta-Ha 20:132)
4. **Protection of chastity** (Sahih al-Bukhari 5066).
5. **Social security:** care of children, the old, the sick, the widowed within the kin group; the inheritance and maintenance rules back this.
6. **Economic unit:** shared earning, saving and support.
7. **Building block of society:** stable homes make a stable society.

**Structure:** marriage with consent as a "solemn covenant" (An-Nisa 4:21); mutual rights (Al-Baqarah 2:228); the husband responsible for maintenance and protection (An-Nisa 4:34); consultation in family decisions (Al-Baqarah 2:233); every member a "shepherd" in his or her sphere (Sahih al-Bukhari 5200); choosing a spouse for religion and character (Sahih al-Bukhari 5090; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1084).

**Present challenges**

| Challenge | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Individualism | "My life, my choice" ahead of duty to spouse, children and parents |
| Delayed and difficult marriage | Dowry demands, costly ceremonies, long education, unemployment |
| Rising divorce and separation | Low tolerance, unrealistic expectations, interference, misuse of the right of divorce |
| Weakening of the joint family | Urbanisation, migration; the old left alone; in the West, old-age homes |
| Both parents absent | Long working hours; children raised by screens |
| Digital media | Addiction, pornography, loss of privacy, less conversation at home, exposure to values hostile to the family |
| Redefinition of the family | Cohabitation without marriage, births outside marriage, and movements seeking to redefine marriage and gender, strongest in Western societies and spreading through global media |
| Domestic violence and denial of women's rights | Custom presented as religion |
| Neglect of fathers' and mothers' roles | Authority without affection, or affection without guidance |
| Economic pressure | Inflation, housing costs, debt |
| Falling birth rates in many countries | Children seen as a burden |
| Generation gap | Parents and children living in different mental worlds |
| Weak religious and moral training | Homes no longer teach prayer, Quran and manners |

**Guidance of Islam for each**

1. **Make marriage easy:** simple ceremonies; moderate dower. The saying that the most blessed marriage is the one with the least burden is reported in Musnad Ahmad [not verified; give the meaning without naming a collection]. Help the unmarried to marry (An-Nur 24:32).
2. **Choose for faith and character.**
3. **Mutual kindness:** "Live with them in kindness." (An-Nisa 4:19). "The best of you is the best to his family." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895). A believer should not hate a believing wife; if he dislikes one trait he will be pleased with another (Sahih Muslim 1469).
4. **Clear roles with consultation.**
5. **Conflict resolution:** advice, patience, then arbiters from both families (An-Nisa 4:35); settlement is best (An-Nisa 4:128); divorce as a last resort, with a waiting period that allows reunion (Al-Baqarah 2:228–229).
6. **Parents:** kindness, especially in old age (Al-Isra 17:23–24).
7. **Children:** time, affection, teaching by example, justice among them; prayer taught from seven (Sunan Abi Dawud 495).
8. **Guarding the home:** modesty, lowering the gaze, privacy rules (An-Nur 24:27–31, 24:58).
9. **Kinship ties** kept alive.
10. **Financial responsibility:** "It is enough sin for a man that he neglects those he is bound to provide for." (Sunan Abi Dawud 1692)
11. **The Prophet's (PBUH) example:** helping at home, affection to children, respect for his daughter, patience with his wives.

**Role of state and society:** family courts and mediation councils; premarital guidance; laws against dowry abuse and violence; media standards; family-friendly work rules; housing and marriage support for the poor; school curricula on family ethics. Pakistan: Article 35 (the state shall protect marriage, the family, the mother and the child); Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961; Family Courts Act 1964.

**Outline:** importance of the family in Islam → its role (6 points) → challenges (8–10) → Islamic guidance matched to each → role of state and society → conclusion (Ar-Rum 30:21).

### 2.4 Reconstruction of Pakistani society on Islamic teachings (asked 2022)

**Present ills:** moral decline (dishonesty, corruption); injustice and class divide; sectarian, ethnic and political polarisation; intolerance and violence; illiteracy; weak family and neglect of women's rights; materialism; disregard for law; decay of civic sense (cleanliness, queues, traffic).

**Islamic programme of reconstruction**

| Field | Measures | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Faith and character | Revive the link with the Quran and Seerah; tazkiyah | Ar-Ra'd 13:11; Al-Jumu'ah 62:2 |
| Education | One system; knowledge with character; universal literacy | Al-Alaq 96:1–5 |
| Justice | Rule of law; quick, cheap justice | An-Nisa 4:58, 4:135 |
| Economy | Zakat, end of riba, fair wages, jobs | Al-Hashr 59:7 |
| Unity | Brotherhood above sect and ethnicity | Al Imran 3:103 |
| Family | Strengthen marriage; women's rights | Ar-Rum 30:21 |
| Governance | Trustworthy, consultative, accountable leadership | Ash-Shura 42:38 |
| Tolerance | Ethics of disagreement; minority rights | Al-Baqarah 2:256 |
| Civic ethics | Cleanliness, honesty in trade, removing harm from the road | Sahih Muslim 223; Sahih al-Bukhari 2989 |
| Collective conscience | Enjoin good, forbid evil | Al Imran 3:104 |

Iqbal and the Quaid both saw Pakistan as a place to practise Islamic social justice. The Objectives Resolution (1949) and Article 31 state the aim. The model is Madinah: brotherhood, a charter, a mosque-school, a clean market.

## 3. The political system

### 3.1 Basic concepts

| Concept | Meaning | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Sovereignty (hakimiyyah) of Allah** | Supreme legal and political authority belongs to Allah alone | Yusuf 12:40; Al Imran 3:26; Al-A'raf 7:54 |
| **Vicegerency (khilafah) of man** | Authority on earth is a delegated trust. Promised to the believers collectively, so no one class owns it. Maududi calls this "popular vicegerency". | An-Nur 24:55; Al-Baqarah 2:30 |
| **Risalat as the source of law** | Obedience to the Messenger (PBUH); Quran and Sunnah as the constitution's foundation | An-Nisa 4:59, 4:65 |
| **Shura** | Government by consultation | Ash-Shura 42:38 |
| **Bay'ah** | The pledge by which the ruler takes office: a two-way contract of obedience in return for rule by the law | Al-Fath 48:10; Sahih al-Bukhari 7199 |
| **Obedience within limits** | | An-Nisa 4:59; Sahih al-Bukhari 7257 |
| **Purpose of the state** | Justice, prayer, zakat, enjoining good | Al-Hajj 22:41; Al-Hadid 57:25 |

### 3.2 Salient features

1. Sovereignty of Allah; supremacy of the Shariah.
2. Government as a trust; rulers as servants of the people.
3. Elective and consultative leadership; no hereditary right.
4. Rule of law; equality before law.
5. Fundamental rights guaranteed by revelation.
6. Accountability of the ruler; the right to criticise and advise.
7. Independent judiciary.
8. Conditional obedience.
9. Welfare state: basic needs for all.
10. Protection of non-Muslim citizens.
11. Merit in public office.
12. Unity of the Ummah; international relations based on treaties, justice and peace (Al-Anfal 8:61; Al-Ma'idah 5:1).
13. Flexible form: the Quran fixes principles, not one structure. Presidential or parliamentary, unitary or federal forms are matters of ijtihad.

### 3.3 Islam and democracy

| Agreement | Difference |
|---|---|
| Government by consent of the governed | In Islam the people's will operates within the limits of the Shariah; in secular democracy the majority is legally unlimited |
| Consultation / representation | Sovereignty: Allah's, with people as vicegerents; versus popular sovereignty |
| Accountability, rule of law, rights | Aim: Allah's pleasure and justice; versus the will and interest of the majority |
| Equality of citizens | Qualifications of piety and competence stressed for office |
| Peaceful transfer of power | Law-making cannot make the unlawful lawful |

Positions among Muslim thinkers: Iqbal: a republican form suits Islam, and an elected assembly is the proper vehicle of ijtihad today. Maududi: "theo-democracy", a limited popular sovereignty under God. Some reject the word democracy altogether while accepting elections and consultation. Pakistan's Constitution combines both ideas: sovereignty of Allah, exercised by chosen representatives (Article 2-A).

### 3.4 Islamic state and other systems

| | Theocracy (priestly rule) | Secular democracy | Dictatorship / monarchy | Islamic system |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sovereign | God, through a priestly class | People | One man or family | Allah; all believers as trustees |
| Law | Clergy's word | Majority's will | Ruler's will | Shariah plus ijtihad by consultation |
| Clergy | Rule by right | None | — | No priesthood; scholars advise |
| Ruler's accountability | None | To voters | None | To the law, the people and Allah |

**Problems:** dynastic and personality-based parties; money in elections; corruption; floor-crossing and vote-buying; polarisation and abusive language; weak local government; institutions overstepping their roles; politics of power, not service; weak rule of law.

**Reforms**

| Islamic principle | Reform |

## 4. The economic system

Asked 2017, 2020, 2022, 2023 Special, 2025. Full answer: MA-15. Zakat details are in Notes 1.

### 4.1 Basic philosophy

1. **Allah is the real owner; man is a trustee.** "Spend out of that in which He has made you successors." (Al-Hadid 57:7). "To Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth." (Al-Baqarah 2:284)
2. **Resources are created for all people.** "He created for you all that is on the earth." (Al-Baqarah 2:29)
3. **Economic activity is worship** when lawful: "When the prayer is ended, disperse in the land and seek the bounty of Allah." (Al-Jumu'ah 62:10)
4. **Material and moral welfare together** (*falah*): Al-Qasas 28:77.
5. **Justice in production, exchange and distribution.**

### 4.2 Principles and salient features

| # | Principle | Detail | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Private ownership, with limits** | Earned, inherited or gifted property is protected; it carries duties; some resources stay public | An-Nisa 4:29, 4:32; "People are partners in three: water, pasture and fire." (Sunan Abi Dawud 3477; Sunan Ibn Majah 2472) |
| 2 | **Lawful and unlawful (halal and haram)** | Ban on riba, gambling, fraud, bribery, theft, hoarding, trade in harmful goods | Al-Baqarah 2:168, 2:188; Al-Ma'idah 5:90 |
| 3 | **Prohibition of riba (interest/usury)** | See 4.3 | Al-Baqarah 2:275–279 |
| 4 | **Zakat and ushr** | Compulsory transfer to the poor | At-Tawbah 9:60, 9:103 |
| 5 | **Voluntary spending** | Charity, endowments (*waqf*), interest-free loans (*qard hasan*), spending the surplus | Al-Baqarah 2:219, 2:261; Al Imran 3:92 |
| 6 | **Circulation of wealth** | Wealth must not stay among the rich | Al-Hashr 59:7 |
| 7 | **Law of inheritance** | Breaks up large estates among many heirs every generation | An-Nisa 4:7, 4:11–12 |
| 8 | **Ban on hoarding and monopoly** | "No one hoards but a wrongdoer." | Sahih Muslim 1605; At-Tawbah 9:34 |
| 9 | **Free and fair market** | Trade by mutual consent; honest weights; no deception; no artificial price manipulation; state supervision (hisbah) | An-Nisa 4:29; Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–3; Sahih Muslim 102; Sahih al-Bukhari 2150 |
| 10 | **Dignity of work; fair wages** | Earning by one's own hand is best; pay the worker promptly | Sahih al-Bukhari 2072; Sunan Ibn Majah 2443; Sahih al-Bukhari 2227 |
| 11 | **Moderation in consumption** | Neither waste nor miserliness | Al-Furqan 25:67; Al-Isra 17:26–29; Al-A'raf 7:31 |
| 12 | **Risk-sharing in place of interest** | Partnership (*musharakah*), profit-sharing (*mudarabah*) | Practised by the Prophet (PBUH) with Khadijah (RA) |
| 13 | **Written contracts and honest dealing** | | Al-Baqarah 2:282; Al-Ma'idah 5:1 |
| 14 | **State responsibility for basic needs** | The treasury supports those who cannot earn | Umar's (RA) stipends |
| 15 | **No begging by the able** | Carrying firewood is better than asking | Sahih al-Bukhari 1471 |
| 16 | **Differences in income accepted, exploitation not** | | Az-Zukhruf 43:32; An-Nahl 16:71 |

### 4.3 Riba

- **Meaning:** literally increase. Technically, a fixed increase demanded on a loan (*riba al-nasi'ah*), or an unequal exchange of the same kind of certain goods (*riba al-fadl*: gold for gold, wheat for wheat must be equal and on the spot, Sahih Muslim 1587).
- **Texts:** "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden riba." (Al-Baqarah 2:275). "Allah destroys riba and gives increase for charities." (2:276). "Give up what remains of riba ... if you do not, then be informed of war from Allah and His Messenger." (2:278–279). Give time to the debtor in difficulty (2:280). Also Al Imran 3:130; Ar-Rum 30:39. The Prophet (PBUH) cursed the one who takes it, the one who pays it, the one who writes it and the two witnesses (Sahih Muslim 1598). He cancelled all riba in the Farewell Sermon (Sahih Muslim 1218). It is among the seven destructive sins (Sahih al-Bukhari 2766).
- **Why forbidden:** a guaranteed gain for the lender while the borrower carries all the risk; wealth flows from the many to the few; it burdens the poor and indebted nations; it rewards hoarded money, not work or enterprise; it feeds inflation and debt crises.
- **Difference from trade:** in trade both sides take risk and exchange real value.
- **Bank interest:** the great majority of contemporary scholars and the fiqh academies hold that bank interest is riba. A minority has argued for distinctions. State the majority view.

**Alternatives (Islamic finance)**

| Contract | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mudarabah | One party gives capital, the other labour; profit shared as agreed; financial loss borne by the capital owner |
| Musharakah | Partnership; all contribute capital; profit and loss shared |
| Murabahah | Sale at cost plus a disclosed profit, with deferred payment |
| Ijarah | Leasing |
| Salam | Full advance payment for goods to be delivered later (useful for farmers) |
| Istisna | Order to manufacture or build |
| Sukuk | Certificates of ownership in an asset or project (in place of interest-bearing bonds) |
| Takaful | Mutual, cooperative insurance |
| Qard hasan | Interest-free loan |

**Pakistan:** Article 38(f) of the Constitution requires the elimination of riba. The Federal Shariat Court declared bank interest to be riba in 1991 and again on 28 April 2022, directing an interest-free system within about five years. The 26th Constitutional Amendment (October 2024) wrote a deadline of 1 January 2028 into Article 38(f). Islamic banking already holds a growing share of the banking sector. **Check the latest position before the exam; this date falls just before CSS 2028.**

Justice Taqi Usmani's *An Introduction to Islamic Finance* and Umer Chapra's *Islam and the Economic Challenge* and *Towards a Just Monetary System* are the standard readings.

### 4.4 Concentration of wealth: harms and Islamic measures (asked 2025)

**Harms**

1. **Economic:** low spending power of the majority, so weak demand and unemployment; idle or speculative capital; monopoly and price-fixing; debt dependence.
2. **Social:** class division, envy, crime, begging; unequal access to education, health and justice.
3. **Political:** the rich capture the state and write the rules; corruption.
4. **Moral:** arrogance, luxury and waste among the few; despair and bitterness among the many. Qarun is the Quran's example (Al-Qasas 28:76–82).
5. **Civilizational:** when the affluent become corrupt, the society falls (Al-Isra 17:16). Shah Waliullah named the pressure of an idle rich class on peasants and craftsmen as a cause of the decay of empires.
6. **Spiritual:** "Rivalry in worldly increase distracts you." (At-Takathur 102:1–2). Woe to the one "who gathers wealth and counts it" (Al-Humazah 104:1–3).

**Measures**

| Type | Measure | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Compulsory** | Zakat and ushr | At-Tawbah 9:60 |
| | Inheritance divides estates | An-Nisa 4:11–12 |
| | Maintenance of dependants and poor relatives | Al-Baqarah 2:215 |
| | Expiations and sadaqat al-fitr feed the poor | |
| | A fifth of spoils and public revenue for social heads | Al-Anfal 8:41; Al-Hashr 59:7 |
| **Prohibitions** | Riba | Al-Baqarah 2:275 |
| | Hoarding of gold, silver and goods | At-Tawbah 9:34–35; Sahih Muslim 1605 |
| | Gambling and pure speculation | Al-Ma'idah 5:90 |
| | Fraud, bribery, unjust taking | Al-Baqarah 2:188 |
| | Private enclosure of common resources | "No reserved pasture except for Allah and His Messenger." (Sahih al-Bukhari 2370) |
| | Waste and luxury | Al-Isra 17:26–27 |
| **Voluntary** | Charity; spending the surplus; endowments; interest-free loans; freeing debtors | Al-Baqarah 2:219, 2:280; Al Imran 3:92 |
| **State action** | Collection and distribution of zakat; public ownership of key natural resources; market supervision; fair wages; grants of unused land to those who will cultivate it ("Whoever revives dead land, it is his", Sunan Abi Dawud 3073) | |
| **Precedent** | The Prophet (PBUH) gave the lands of Banu Nadir to the poor Emigrants and a few poor Helpers; Umar (RA) kept the lands of Iraq as public property, quoting Al-Hashr 59:7–10 | Abu Yusuf, *Kitab al-Kharaj* |

**Today:** global inequality is extreme; in Pakistan a narrow elite holds most land and capital, and taxes fall mainly on consumption. Recommendations: progressive direct taxation with an efficient zakat system; land and inheritance enforcement; interest-free micro-finance (the Akhuwat model); curbing cartels; financial inclusion.

### 4.5 Poverty elimination (asked 2017, 2023 Special)

**Islam's view of poverty:** not a virtue to be sought; the Prophet (PBUH) sought refuge from it; it is a trial that society must relieve. The needy have a *right* (Adh-Dhariyat 51:19).

**Framework**

1. **Work first:** duty to earn; dignity of labour; ban on begging by the able. The Prophet (PBUH) helped a man buy an axe to earn his living instead of begging (Sunan Abi Dawud 1641).
2. **Family:** maintenance of relatives.
3. **Zakat:** targeted at eight heads; best used to make the poor self-supporting.
4. **Voluntary charity and waqf:** historically funded hospitals, schools, water and inns.
5. **Interest-free credit and partnership finance:** access to capital without debt traps.
6. **Fair wages and market justice.**
7. **State guarantee of basic needs** from the treasury.
8. **Ban on the causes of poverty:** riba, hoarding, exploitation, waste.
9. **Moral and spiritual factors:** honesty, contentment, gratitude, and the promise that piety opens provision (Al-A'raf 7:96; At-Talaq 65:2–3).

**Evidence that it works:** the early Caliphate; the report about Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz's time. Modern illustrations: zakat-funded programmes, Islamic micro-finance, Malaysia's pilgrimage savings fund and zakat institutions.

### 4.6 Comparison with capitalism and socialism

| Aspect | Capitalism | Socialism | Islam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Private, nearly unlimited | State / collective | Private with duties; some public; Allah the real owner |
| Motive | Profit | State plan | Lawful profit plus Allah's pleasure |
| Interest | Central | Abolished with private capital | Forbidden; risk-sharing instead |
| Market | Free, self-regulating | Planned | Free within moral and legal limits; supervised |
| Distribution | By market power | By the state | Market plus zakat, inheritance and charity |
| Individual freedom | High | Low | Balanced |
| Result seen | Growth with inequality and crises | Equality claimed, with loss of freedom and incentive | Aims at growth with justice |

### 4.7 Scholars

Abu Yusuf (*Kitab al-Kharaj*); Abu Ubayd (*Kitab al-Amwal*); Ibn Khaldun (on taxation, labour and value); Shah Waliullah (economic justice as a basis of society); Maududi (*First Principles of Islamic Economics*; *Sood*); Baqir al-Sadr (*Iqtisaduna*); Umer Chapra; Khurshid Ahmad; Nejatullah Siddiqi; Taqi Usmani.

## 5. The judicial system

A syllabus item not yet asked directly, apart from Hazrat Ali's (RA) judicial wisdom (2020). Prepare a full outline.

### 5.1 Importance of justice

- "Allah commands justice." (An-Nahl 16:90)
- "When you judge between people, judge with justice." (An-Nisa 4:58)
- "Be firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, even against yourselves." (An-Nisa 4:135)
- "Do not let hatred of a people lead you to injustice." (Al-Ma'idah 5:8)
- "O Dawud, We have made you a successor on the earth, so judge between people in truth." (Sad 38:26)
- "Judges are of three kinds: one in Paradise and two in the Fire. The one in Paradise knew the truth and judged by it. One who knew the truth and judged unjustly is in the Fire, and one who judged in ignorance is in the Fire." (Sunan Abi Dawud 3573)
- The just ruler is first under Allah's shade (Sahih al-Bukhari 660).

### 5.2 Salient features

| # | Feature | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Divine law as the standard** | Al-Ma'idah 5:44–48 |
| 2 | **Equality before the law** | The Makhzumi case (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475) |
| 3 | **Independence of the judge** | Judges separate from governors under Umar (RA); the Caliph as a litigant before his own judge |
| 4 | **Qualified, upright judges** | Conditions listed by jurists: Muslim, adult, sane, just, knowledgeable in the law, sound senses. Schools differ on some conditions. |
| 5 | **Hear both sides** | Sunan Abi Dawud 3582 |
| 6 | **Burden of proof on the claimant; oath on the defendant** | Jami at-Tirmidhi 1341; Sahih al-Bukhari 4552 |
| 7 | **Presumption of innocence** | The original state is freedom from liability |
| 8 | **Doubt prevents the fixed punishments** | Jami at-Tirmidhi 1424 (weak chain; principle accepted by jurists) |
| 9 | **Judgment on outward evidence** | "I am only human ... I judge by what I hear. If I award someone his brother's right, it is a piece of fire." (Sahih al-Bukhari 7169) |
| 10 | **No judging in anger or distress** | Sahih al-Bukhari 7158 |
| 11 | **Truthful witnesses; false testimony a major sin** | An-Nisa 4:135; Al-Baqarah 2:283 |
| 12 | **No bribery, no intercession in fixed penalties** | Sunan Abi Dawud 3580; Sahih al-Bukhari 6788 |
| 13 | **Individual responsibility; no punishment without prior law** | Al-An'am 6:164; Al-Isra 17:15 |
| 14 | **Free, quick and simple justice** | No court fees in the early period; cases heard in the mosque |
| 15 | **Review of mistaken judgments** | Umar's (RA) letter: return to the truth |
| 16 | **Compromise and arbitration encouraged** | "Reconciliation between Muslims is permitted." (Sunan Abi Dawud 3594; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1352); An-Nisa 4:35 |
| 17 | **Ijtihad by the judge** | Two rewards if right, one if wrong (Sahih al-Bukhari 7352) |
| 18 | **Rights of the accused** | No torture; confession may be withdrawn; privacy respected (Al-Hujurat 49:12) |
| 19 | **Non-Muslims' rights** | Justice for all (Al-Ma'idah 5:8, 5:42); own courts in personal matters |

### 5.3 Structure

| Court | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| **Qada** (ordinary courts) | Civil, family, criminal cases on evidence |
| **Mazalim** (grievance court) | Complaints against officials and the powerful |
| **Hisbah** | Summary action on visible public wrongs in markets and streets |
| Chief judge (*qadi al-qudat*) | From the Abbasid period; Abu Yusuf was the first |

### 5.4 Kinds of punishment

| Kind | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hudud** | Penalties fixed by the texts, for crimes against public order and morals | Theft (Al-Ma'idah 5:38), highway robbery (5:33), unlawful intercourse (An-Nur 24:2), false accusation of unchastity (24:4). Strict proof is required. Jurists differ on the list. |
| **Qisas and diyat** | Retaliation or compensation for killing and injury; the victim's family may pardon | Al-Baqarah 2:178–179 |
| **Ta'zir** | Discretionary penalties set by the state or judge for all other offences | Fraud, bribery, traffic offences |

Aims: deterrence, reform, protection of society, satisfaction of the victim, mercy through pardon and repentance. Umar (RA) suspended the penalty for theft in the year of famine, showing that social conditions matter (reported in the early collections).

### 5.5 History and examples

- The Prophet (PBUH) as judge in Madinah; Ali and Mu'adh (RA) as judges in Yemen.
- Umar's (RA) letter to Abu Musa (Notes 6).
- Ali (RA) before Judge Shurayh (well-known report).
- Shurayh served as judge of Kufa for decades under several Caliphs.

### 5.6 Today

Pakistan: huge backlog (over two million pending cases by official counts), delay, cost, weak prosecution, low trust. Islamic remedies: more judges and merit in appointment; simpler procedure; alternative dispute resolution on the model of *sulh* and *tahkim*; strict rules against false testimony; equal application; legal aid; judicial accountability. Constitutional frame: Article 175 (independence of the judiciary), Article 10-A (fair trial), the Federal Shariat Court.

**Outline:** importance of justice → features (12) → structure → kinds of punishment → examples from the Prophet (PBUH) and the Caliphs → comparison with modern principles (rule of law, fair trial) → Pakistan → conclusion.

## 6. Ijma (consensus)

Asked 2017, 2023.

### 6.1 Definition

- **Literal:** to resolve firmly; to agree.
- **Technical:** the agreement of the qualified jurists (*mujtahidin*) of the Muslim community, in any period after the death of the Prophet (PBUH), on a ruling of the Shariah.

It is the third source of law in the four Sunni schools.

### 6.2 Proofs of its authority

| Proof | Text |
|---|---|
| Quran | "Whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows a way other than that of the believers, We shall leave him to what he has chosen." (An-Nisa 4:115). Imam Shafi'i is reported to have relied on this verse. |
| | "We have made you a middle community, to be witnesses over mankind." (Al-Baqarah 2:143) |
| | "You are the best community ... you enjoin right and forbid wrong." (Al Imran 3:110) |
| | "Obey Allah, obey the Messenger and those in authority among you." (An-Nisa 4:59) |
| Sunnah | "My community will not agree on misguidance." (Sunan Ibn Majah 3950; Jami at-Tirmidhi 2167). The separate chains have weakness; scholars accept the meaning because of the many supporting reports. |
| | "Allah's hand is with the united community." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2166) |
| | "Hold to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided Caliphs after me." (Sunan Abi Dawud 4607; Jami at-Tirmidhi 2676) |
| Reason | It is very unlikely that all qualified scholars would agree on a ruling without a sound basis in the texts. |

### 6.3 Conditions

1. Those agreeing must be qualified jurists.
2. All of them in that period must agree. A majority is not enough in the view of most jurists.
3. It must come after the Prophet's (PBUH) lifetime.
4. It must be on a legal (Shariah) question.
5. It must rest on some basis (*sanad*) in the Quran, Sunnah or analogy.

### 6.4 Kinds

| Basis of division | Kinds |
|---|---|
| **By how it is expressed** | *Explicit* (*sarih, qawli*): each jurist states his agreement by word or act. Binding by agreement of those who accept ijma. *Tacit* (*sukuti*): some state a view, the rest know of it and stay silent. Accepted as proof by Hanafis; many Shafi'is do not treat it as binding. |
| **By who agrees** | Consensus of the Companions (accepted by all, including those who doubt later ijma); consensus of later jurists; consensus of the people of Madinah (given weight by Imam Malik); in the Shia view, consensus is authoritative when it reveals the position of the infallible Imam. |
| **By strength** | Definitive (*qat'i*), transmitted by many; and probable (*zanni*), transmitted by few. |

### 6.5 Examples

1. Selection of Abu Bakr (RA) as Caliph.
2. Fighting those who refused zakat.
3. Collecting the Quran in one volume (Abu Bakr) and standardising its script (Uthman).
4. The grandmother's share of one-sixth in inheritance.
5. Tarawih in congregation behind one imam (from Umar's time; Sahih al-Bukhari 2010).
6. The second call to prayer on Friday (from Uthman's time).
7. Keeping the conquered lands of Iraq as public property.
8. Prohibition of pig fat along with its flesh.

### 6.6 Differences among scholars

- Zahiris (Ibn Hazm) accept only the consensus of the Companions.
- Imam Ahmad is reported to have cautioned that whoever claims consensus may be mistaken, since he cannot know that no one differed. This is read as caution about loose claims.
- Some modern writers think universal agreement is practically impossible to verify after the first generations.

### 6.7 Importance

1. Turns a probable ruling into a certain one.
2. Preserves the unity of the community on fundamentals.
3. Authenticates the transmission of the Quran and Sunnah themselves.
4. Gives the law a way to settle new questions collectively.
5. Protects against individual error.
6. Provides stability: what is settled need not be reopened without cause.

### 6.8 Can legislation by the parliament of an Islamic state be ijma? (asked 2023)

**View 1: Yes (Iqbal).** In *Reconstruction* (Lecture 6) Iqbal argues that, given the growth of opposing sects, the only form ijma can take today is the transfer of the power of ijtihad from individual representatives of schools to a Muslim legislative assembly. This lets laymen with insight into affairs contribute, and revives the dormant spirit of the legal system. He recognised the risk that members may not know Islamic law, and suggested that scholars form a vital part of the assembly or assist it, and that legal education be reformed.

**View 2: No, not in the technical sense.** Classical ijma is the agreement of *all qualified jurists* of the *whole community*. A national parliament is elected on popular vote, need not contain jurists, decides by majority, and represents one state. Its Act is law by the authority of the state (the ruler's choice among valid views ends dispute in practice), but it is not ijma.

**Synthesis for the answer**

1. Parliamentary legislation within the Shariah is valid **collective ijtihad** and binding law in that state.
2. It approaches ijma when (a) it is made with the participation or review of qualified scholars, and (b) scholars across the Muslim world come to agree with it.
3. Pakistan's design attempts this: Parliament legislates; the Council of Islamic Ideology advises (Articles 228–230); the Federal Shariat Court reviews (Article 203-D); Article 227 bars laws repugnant to the Quran and Sunnah.
4. At the Ummah level, bodies such as the OIC's International Islamic Fiqh Academy (Jeddah) give collective opinions that work toward consensus.
5. Limits: no legislation can change a clear text; ijma cannot abrogate the Quran or Sunnah.

## 7. Qiyas (analogy)

The syllabus names Ijma and Ijtihad; qiyas is the main tool of ijtihad and the fourth source. Know it for short notes and MCQs.

**Definition.** Literally, measuring one thing against another. Technically, extending the ruling of an original case found in the texts to a new case, because both share the same effective cause (*illah*).

**Four pillars**

| Pillar | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *Asl* | The original case in the text | Wine (*khamr*) |
| *Far'* | The new case | Any other intoxicant or narcotic |
| *Hukm al-asl* | The ruling of the original | Prohibition (Al-Ma'idah 5:90) |
| *Illah* | The shared effective cause | Intoxication |

**Proofs:** "Take lesson, O people of insight." (Al-Hashr 59:2); An-Nisa 4:59; the report of Mu'adh (RA); Umar's (RA) letter to Abu Musa; the Prophet's (PBUH) own analogies: he compared an unperformed pilgrimage to a debt that must be paid (Sahih al-Bukhari 1852) and answered a man's doubt about his child's colour by the example of camels (Sahih al-Bukhari 5305).

**Other examples:** all trade at the time of the Friday call is covered by the ban on "sale" in Al-Jumu'ah 62:9; a killer is barred from a bequest by analogy with his being barred from inheritance.

**Conditions:** the original ruling must be established by text or ijma; its cause must be identifiable and present in the new case; the new case must not have its own text; the analogy must not contradict a text.

**Differences:** the four Sunni schools accept it (Hanafis use it widely; Hanbalis sparingly); the Zahiris reject it; the Ja'fari school uses reason (*aql*) in its own framework and does not accept qiyas of this type.

## 8. Ijtihad

Asked 2018, 2022, 2023 Special, 2024. Full answer: MA-14.

### 8.1 Definition

- **Literal:** from *juhd*, to exert oneself to the utmost.
- **Technical:** the utmost effort of a qualified jurist to derive a ruling of the Shariah from its detailed evidences, in a matter where there is no definitive text.
- The one who does it is a *mujtahid*. Following a mujtahid's ruling without knowing his proof is *taqlid*.

### 8.2 Basis

| Source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Quran | "If they had referred it to the Messenger and to those in authority among them, those able to draw out its meaning would have known it." (An-Nisa 4:83). "Those who strive in Our cause, We shall guide them to Our ways." (Al-Ankabut 29:69). A group should gain deep understanding of the religion (At-Tawbah 9:122). Repeated calls to reflect (Sad 38:29; Muhammad 47:24). |
| Sunnah | "When a judge exercises ijtihad and is right, he has two rewards; when he exercises ijtihad and errs, he has one." (Sahih al-Bukhari 7352; Sahih Muslim 1716). The report of Mu'adh (RA) (Sunan Abi Dawud 3592). The Asr prayer at Banu Qurayzah: two groups understood the order differently and neither was blamed (Sahih al-Bukhari 946). Two travellers prayed with dry ablution, then found water; one repeated the prayer and one did not; both were approved (Sunan Abi Dawud 338). |
| Companions | Abu Bakr (RA): fighting those who withheld zakat; collecting the Quran. Umar (RA): stopping the share of "those whose hearts are to be reconciled" when Islam became strong; suspending the penalty for theft in famine; keeping the lands of Iraq undivided; the stipend register. Uthman (RA): one standard script. Ali (RA): the penalty for drinking by analogy. |

### 8.3 Scope

- **No ijtihad where a text is definitive** in both authenticity and meaning: the five prayers, the ban on riba, the fixed shares of inheritance, the basics of belief.
- **Ijtihad applies to:** texts open to more than one meaning; reports whose authenticity is probable; and matters on which the texts are silent.

### 8.4 Qualifications of a mujtahid

1. Sound faith, uprightness and piety.
2. Command of Arabic.
3. Knowledge of the Quran, especially the legal verses, their contexts, and the general and the specific.
4. Knowledge of the Sunnah and the science of hadith.
5. Knowledge of matters already settled by consensus.
6. Mastery of the principles of jurisprudence, including analogy.
7. Understanding of the objectives of the Shariah (*maqasid*). Al-Shatibi made this the first condition.
8. Knowledge of the people, customs and conditions of his time. Ibn al-Qayyim: a ruling may change with time, place, circumstance and custom.

### 8.5 Kinds and levels

| Division | Kinds |
|---|---|
| **By rank** | *Absolute, independent mujtahid* (founders of schools: Abu Hanifah, Malik, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad ibn Hanbal; also Ja'far al-Sadiq, al-Awza'i and others). *Mujtahid within a school* (Abu Yusuf, Muhammad al-Shaybani). *Mujtahid on particular questions.* Then those who extract, weigh and prefer among existing opinions. |
| **By method** | *Explanatory* (interpreting a text). *Analogical* (qiyas). *Interest-based* (*istislahi*): ruling by public interest and the objectives of the law where no text or analogy applies. |
| **By who does it** | *Individual.* *Collective* (*ijtima'i*): by councils of jurists with subject experts. Most suited to the present age. |
| **By extent** | *Complete* or *partial* (expertise in one field, such as finance or medicine). |

### 8.6 Principles and tools (for "explain its principles", 2022)

1. Order of sources: Quran, Sunnah, ijma, qiyas.
2. No ijtihad against a definitive text.
3. Analogy by a sound effective cause.
4. Juristic preference (*istihsan*): leaving a strict analogy for a stronger reason of fairness.
5. Public interest (*maslahah*), within the five objectives.
6. Custom (*urf*) where the text is silent.
7. Presumption of continuity (*istishab*): things stay lawful and people stay free of liability until proved otherwise.
8. Blocking the means to harm (*sadd al-dhara'i*).
9. Removal of hardship: "Allah intends ease for you." (Al-Baqarah 2:185); "No harm and no returning of harm." (Sunan Ibn Majah 2341)
10. Necessity permits the forbidden to the extent needed (Al-Baqarah 2:173).
11. Gradualism in application.
12. One ijtihad is not cancelled by another of equal rank; a mujtahid may revise his own view (Umar's letter).

### 8.7 History

- **The Prophet's (PBUH) time:** revelation settled matters; Companions used judgment when away from him.
- **The Companions and Successors:** wide ijtihad; regional schools (Madinah, Kufa).
- **2nd–4th centuries AH:** the great jurists; schools formed; principles written (al-Shafi'i's *Risalah*).
- **After the 4th century:** a growing tendency to follow the established schools. It is often said that "the gate of ijtihad was closed". Causes given: fear that unqualified people would damage the law; political decline and division; the fall of Baghdad (1258); respect for the founders. Iqbal adds: the reaction to rationalist controversies, and the spread of world-denying mysticism.
- **Voices for ijtihad in every age:** Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim; al-Suyuti; Shah Waliullah (*Iqd al-Jid*; *al-Insaf*), who showed the common roots of the schools; al-Shawkani; Muhammad Abduh; Iqbal, who called the closing of the gate "a pure fiction" and ijtihad "the principle of movement in the structure of Islam". The Prophet (PBUH) said that Allah raises for this community at the head of every century one who renews its religion (Sunan Abi Dawud 4291).

### 8.8 Importance in the modern world

**Why it is needed**

1. The texts are limited; events are unlimited.
2. Islam is for all times (Saba 34:28), so it must answer new questions.
3. Science, finance, medicine and global politics raise matters the early jurists never met.
4. Without ijtihad, Muslims either freeze or copy others.
5. It shows that Islam is a living system.

**Issues that need it today**

| Field | Questions |
|---|---|
| Finance | Interest-free banking, insurance, stock markets, paper and digital currency, crypto-assets, zakat on salaries and shares |
| Medicine | Organ transplant, brain death, IVF and surrogacy, genetic engineering, cloning, vaccines, end-of-life care |
| Technology | Artificial intelligence, data privacy, online contracts, moon-sighting by calculation, DNA and digital evidence |
| State and law | Democracy and parties, citizenship, international treaties, human-rights conventions, women's public roles |
| Minorities | Muslims living as minorities in non-Muslim states |
| Environment | Climate change, animal welfare, resource use |
| Emergencies | Mosque closures and distancing in the COVID-19 pandemic showed quick collective ijtihad at work |

**Institutions of collective ijtihad:** the International Islamic Fiqh Academy of the OIC (Jeddah, set up in 1981); the Islamic Fiqh Council of the Muslim World League (Makkah); al-Azhar's Islamic Research Academy; AAOIFI for Islamic finance standards (Bahrain, 1991); in Pakistan the Council of Islamic Ideology, the Federal Shariat Court and the Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court.

**Cautions:** ijtihad is not free opinion; it needs qualification, method and sincerity. Two extremes are to be avoided: rigid refusal of any new thinking, and "reform" that explains away clear texts to please the age.

**Recommendations:** reform legal education to produce scholars who know both the Shariah and modern disciplines; strengthen collective bodies; involve subject experts; build an Ummah-level academy whose resolutions carry weight; parliaments to legislate with scholarly review.

### 8.9 Ijtihad and Ijma compared

| | Ijtihad | Ijma |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Effort of a jurist or council | Agreement of all jurists of an age |
| Result | Probable ruling; others may differ | Binding, certain ruling |
| Relation | The process | The outcome when all ijtihad converges |
