## 1. Concept of Islam

### 1.1 Meaning

- **Literal:** from the Arabic root *s-l-m*. It carries two meanings: peace (*silm, salam*) and submission (*taslim*).
- **Technical:** willing submission to the will of Allah, as revealed through His final Messenger Muhammad (PBUH), in belief and in conduct.
- The two meanings connect: a person finds peace with Allah, with himself and with others by submitting to Allah.

### 1.2 Islam in the Quran

| Point | Reference | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Islam is the Deen accepted by Allah | Al Imran 3:19 | "Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam." |
| Completed and perfected | Al-Ma'idah 5:3 | "This day I have perfected for you your religion, completed My favour upon you and approved Islam as your religion." |
| It asks for the whole person | Al-Baqarah 2:208 | "O you who believe, enter into Islam completely." |
| It matches human nature | Ar-Rum 30:30 | Hold to "the fitrah of Allah upon which He created people". |
| It is the religion of all prophets | Ash-Shura 42:13 | The same Deen was enjoined on Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa and Isa (peace be upon them). |
| The whole universe submits | Al Imran 3:83 | All in the heavens and earth have submitted to Him, willingly or unwillingly. |
| Submission plus good conduct | Al-Baqarah 2:112 | Whoever submits himself to Allah and does good has his reward with his Lord. |

### 1.3 Islam, Iman and Ihsan (Hadith of Jibril)

Narrated by Umar (RA), Sahih Muslim 8 (also Abu Hurayrah, Sahih al-Bukhari 50). The angel Jibril asked the Prophet (PBUH) three questions.

| Level | Content |
|---|---|
| **Islam** (practice) | Testimony of faith, prayer, zakat, fasting in Ramadan, pilgrimage for those able |
| **Iman** (belief) | Belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the divine decree, its good and bad |
| **Ihsan** (excellence) | "To worship Allah as though you see Him; and if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." |

The Prophet (PBUH) also said: "Islam is built on five." (Ibn Umar, Sahih al-Bukhari 8; Sahih Muslim 16)

### 1.4 Sources of Islam

1. The Quran: the word of Allah, preserved (Al-Hijr 15:9).
2. The Sunnah: the sayings, actions and approvals of the Prophet (PBUH) (Al-Hashr 59:7; An-Nisa 4:80).
3. Ijma (consensus) and Qiyas (analogy) as derived sources (see Notes 7).

## 2. Deen: meaning and importance in human life

### 2.1 Meaning of Deen

The Arabic word *din* is used in the Quran in several senses.

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| Recompense, judgment | "Master of the Day of Recompense" (Al-Fatihah 1:4) |
| Obedience and submission | "Worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion" (Al-Bayyinah 98:5) |
| Law, system of a state | Yusuf could not have kept his brother "under the king's law" (*din al-malik*) (Yusuf 12:76) |
| A complete way of life | "The religion in the sight of Allah is Islam" (Al Imran 3:19) |

Maulana Maududi, in *Four Basic Quranic Terms*, concludes that *din* in the Quran stands for a whole system: sovereignty belongs to an authority, people obey it, live by its law and expect its reward or punishment. In Islam that authority is Allah.

**Working definition for the exam:** Deen is the complete code of life revealed by Allah, covering belief, worship, morals, and social, economic, political and legal conduct, with accountability in the Hereafter.

### 2.2 Why human beings need Deen

Write these as headed points. Each has a reference.

**A. For the individual**

1. **It answers the basic questions.** Who made me, why am I here, what happens after death? "I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me." (Adh-Dhariyat 51:56); "Did you think We created you without purpose?" (Al-Mu'minun 23:115)
2. **It meets a need built into human nature.** Every child is born on the fitrah (Sahih al-Bukhari 1385; Sahih Muslim 2658). See also Ar-Rum 30:30 and the primordial covenant (Al-A'raf 7:172).
3. **It gives peace of mind.** "By the remembrance of Allah hearts find rest." (Ar-Ra'd 13:28)
4. **It supplies a fixed standard of right and wrong.** Human reason alone changes with interest and fashion. Revelation gives stable values (Al-Isra 17:9).
5. **It builds character and self-control.** Belief in an all-seeing God and a Day of account restrains a person even when no one is watching (Qaf 50:16–18).
6. **It balances body and soul.** "Seek the home of the Hereafter through what Allah has given you, and do not forget your share of the world." (Al-Qasas 28:77)
7. **It gives hope and patience in hardship.** (Al-Baqarah 2:155–157; Az-Zumar 39:53)

**B. For society**

8. **Human unity and equality.** One Creator, one origin (An-Nisa 4:1; Al-Hujurat 49:13).
9. **Justice.** Messengers and scriptures were sent "so that people may uphold justice" (Al-Hadid 57:25).
10. **Protection of life, property and honour.** (Al-Ma'idah 5:32; the Farewell Sermon, Sahih al-Bukhari 1739)
11. **A sound family.** (Ar-Rum 30:21; Al-Isra 17:23–24)
12. **A fair economy.** Zakat, the ban on riba, and circulation of wealth (Al-Hashr 59:7).
13. **Accountable government.** Authority is a trust (An-Nisa 4:58).
14. **Social solidarity.** "The believers are but brothers." (Al-Hujurat 49:10)

**C. For civilization**

15. **Direction for knowledge and power.** Science tells how; Deen tells why and for what. Without moral direction, power produced two world wars, nuclear weapons and ecological damage.
16. **Rise and fall of nations follow moral laws.** "Allah does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves." (Ar-Ra'd 13:11). Ibn Khaldun in the *Muqaddimah* links the strength of a civilization to its moral cohesion (*asabiyyah*) and its decay to luxury and injustice.

**Scholars to cite:** Shah Waliullah (*Hujjatullah al-Balighah*) explains that divine law serves human welfare. Iqbal (*Reconstruction*) argues that religion gives the individual and society an inner anchor that science alone cannot. Muhammad Asad (*Islam at the Crossroads*) calls Islam a complete programme of life, not one compartment of it.

**Contemporary relevance:** rising anxiety and loneliness, family breakdown, corruption, and moral confusion over new technologies all show the cost of treating religion as a private hobby. In Pakistan, Article 31 of the Constitution asks the state to enable Muslims to order their lives by the Quran and Sunnah.

## 3. Difference between Deen and religion

### 3.1 The terms

- **Religion** (Latin *religio*): in common modern usage, a set of beliefs and rituals linking a person to God. In secular thought it is a private matter.
- **Madhhab** (Urdu *mazhab*): literally "a way taken". In Islamic scholarship it means a school of law (Hanafi, Shafi'i and others). The Quran does not use this word for Islam.
- **Deen:** the word the Quran itself uses for Islam (Al Imran 3:19, 3:85; Al-Ma'idah 5:3).

### 3.2 Comparison table

| Aspect | Religion (in the narrow, common sense) | Deen (Islam) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Belief and worship | All of life: belief, worship, morals, family, economy, state, law |
| Sphere | Private | Private and public (Al-Baqarah 2:208) |
| Source | Often shaped by human tradition | Revelation: Quran and Sunnah |
| Relation to the state | Separate (secularism) | Guides the state: justice, shura, accountability (An-Nisa 4:58–59) |
| Law | Rituals and some personal rules | A full legal system (Shariah) |
| World and Hereafter | Often stresses the next world, or spirituality alone | Balances both (Al-Baqarah 2:201; Al-Qasas 28:77) |
| Clergy | A priestly class often stands between man and God | No priesthood; direct relation with Allah (Al-Baqarah 2:186) |
| Aim | Individual salvation | Individual salvation and a just social order (Al-Hadid 57:25) |
| Completeness | Partial | Complete and final (Al-Ma'idah 5:3) |

### 3.3 Evidence that Islam is a Deen

1. "Enter into Islam completely." (Al-Baqarah 2:208)
2. "Say: my prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds." (Al-An'am 6:162)
3. Righteousness is defined as belief plus spending on the needy plus prayer plus keeping promises plus patience (Al-Baqarah 2:177). One verse joins creed, worship, economics and ethics.
4. The Prophet (PBUH) was at once preacher, head of family, judge, head of state and commander. His Sunnah covers all these roles (Al-Ahzab 33:21).
5. The Quran legislates on trade and debt (Al-Baqarah 2:275, 2:282), inheritance (An-Nisa 4:11–12), war and peace (Al-Anfal 8:61), and judging (An-Nisa 4:58).

### 3.4 A fair note

In ordinary English, "religion" is also used for Islam, and that is acceptable. The point of the distinction is scope: Islam cannot be reduced to private ritual. Write this sentence in your answer to show balance.

## 5. Islamic beliefs and their impact

### 5.1 The articles of faith

"O you who believe, believe in Allah, His Messenger, the Book He sent down to His Messenger, and the Scripture He sent before. Whoever disbelieves in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day has gone far astray." (An-Nisa 4:136). See also Al-Baqarah 2:177 and 2:285. Belief in the divine decree (qadr) is stated in the Hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim 8) and in Al-Qamar 54:49.

The three core beliefs for the exam are **Tawhid, Risalat and Akhirah**.

---

### 5.2 Tawhid (oneness of Allah)

**Meaning.** From *wahhada*, to make one. Tawhid is to believe that Allah is One in His being, His attributes and His right to be worshipped, with no partner.

**Core texts**

| Reference | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Al-Ikhlas 112:1–4 | "Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born. Nor is there any equal to Him." |
| Al-Baqarah 2:255 (Ayat al-Kursi) | Allah, no god but He, the Ever-Living, Sustainer of all; neither slumber nor sleep overtakes Him. |
| Al-Baqarah 2:163 | "Your God is one God. There is no god but He, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful." |
| Al-Anbiya 21:25 | Every messenger was told: "There is no god but Me, so worship Me." |
| An-Nahl 16:36 | To every nation a messenger was sent: "Worship Allah and avoid false gods." |
| Ash-Shura 42:11 | "There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing." |
| Al-Hashr 59:22–24 | The beautiful names of Allah. |

**Kinds of Tawhid** (a classification used by later scholars to teach the subject)

1. *Tawhid al-Rububiyyah*: Allah alone creates, sustains and governs (Al-A'raf 7:54; Yunus 10:31).
2. *Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah* (or *Ibadah*): worship is for Allah alone (Al-Fatihah 1:5; Al-An'am 6:162).
3. *Tawhid al-Asma wa'l-Sifat*: His names and attributes are unique (Al-A'raf 7:180; Ash-Shura 42:11).

Some scholars add *Tawhid al-Hakimiyyah*: the final authority in law belongs to Allah (Yusuf 12:40). Others treat it as part of the first two.

**Shirk (its opposite).** Associating partners with Allah. It is the gravest sin (An-Nisa 4:48; Luqman 31:13). Scholars distinguish major shirk (worshipping others) from minor shirk (showing off in worship, *riya*).

#### Rational arguments for the existence and oneness of Allah (asked 2026)

| Argument | Explanation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Causation (cosmological) | Nothing comes from nothing; the universe did not create itself; it needs a Creator who is not Himself created. | At-Tur 52:35–36: "Were they created by nothing, or were they the creators of themselves? Or did they create the heavens and the earth?" |
| 2. Design and order (teleological) | The fine balance of the universe points to knowledge and purpose. | Al-Mulk 67:3–4 (no flaw in creation); Al-Baqarah 2:164; Ya-Sin 36:38–40 (sun and moon in set orbits) |
| 3. Human nature (fitrah) | People in every age and place have turned to a higher power, above all in distress. | Ar-Rum 30:30; Al-A'raf 7:172; Al-Ankabut 29:61 |
| 4. The self | The human body and mind are themselves signs. | Adh-Dhariyat 51:20–21; Fussilat 41:53; Al-Mu'minun 23:12–14 |
| 5. Unity of the system | One coherent set of laws runs the universe; two wills would clash. | Al-Anbiya 21:22: "Had there been gods besides Allah in them, both would have been ruined."; Al-Mu'minun 23:91 |
| 6. Moral argument | A sense of right and wrong, and the demand for final justice, point to a moral Lawgiver and Judge. | Ash-Shams 91:7–10; Sad 38:28 |
| 7. Contingency | Everything in the world could have been otherwise and depends on something else; the chain must end in a Being that exists necessarily. Developed by Ibn Sina and by al-Ghazali (the *kalam* argument: whatever begins to exist has a cause). | Fatir 35:15: "You are in need of Allah, and Allah is the Self-Sufficient." |
| 8. Prophethood and revelation | Truthful men in every nation brought one message; the Quran challenges anyone to match it. | Ibrahim 14:10; Al-Baqarah 2:23; Al-Isra 17:88 |
| 9. Ibrahim's (AS) reasoning | Things that set and fade cannot be God. | Al-An'am 6:75–79; Al-Baqarah 2:258 |

Modern support: the universe had a beginning (the Quran speaks of the heavens and earth as once joined, Al-Anbiya 21:30) and its physical constants are finely set for life. Use this as an illustration, not as proof.

#### Impact of Tawhid on the individual

1. **Dignity and self-respect.** One who bows only to Allah bows to no one else. Iqbal: one prostration to God frees a person from a thousand prostrations to others.
2. **Freedom from fear and superstition.** Benefit and harm are in Allah's hand alone (Yunus 10:107; At-Tawbah 9:51).
3. **Humility.** All gifts are from Allah; pride has no ground.
4. **Courage and trust (tawakkul).** "Whoever relies on Allah, He is sufficient for him." (At-Talaq 65:3)
5. **Contentment and peace of mind.** (Ar-Ra'd 13:28)
6. **Sense of responsibility.** Allah sees and knows all (Qaf 50:16; Al-An'am 6:59).
7. **Broad outlook.** The Lord of all worlds is not the God of one tribe.
8. **Purpose and consistency.** One aim: the pleasure of Allah (Al-An'am 6:162).
9. **Hope.** No despair of Allah's mercy (Az-Zumar 39:53).

Maududi lists most of these in *Towards Understanding Islam*, chapter on faith.

#### Impact of Tawhid on society

1. **Human equality.** One Lord, one human family (An-Nisa 4:1; Al-Hujurat 49:13). Bilal (RA), a freed slave, called the adhan from the Ka'bah.
2. **End of man's lordship over man.** No king, priest or class is sacred. Rib'i ibn Amir told the Persian commander Rustam that Muslims came to bring people out of the servitude of men into the service of Allah (reported in al-Tabari's history).
3. **Rule of law.** The ruler is under the same law (the Makhzumi woman case, Sahih al-Bukhari 3475).
4. **Unity of the community.** One God, one Book, one qiblah (Al Imran 3:103).
5. **Justice and accountability.** Power is a trust from Allah (An-Nisa 4:58).
6. **Spirit of inquiry.** Nature is creation, not deity, so it can be studied and used (Al-Jathiyah 45:13). This helped the growth of science in Muslim civilization.
7. **Moral society.** Public and private conduct answer to the same God.

**Contemporary relevance:** materialism and consumer culture make wealth and status into idols; nationalism and racism make the group an idol; extremists treat their own opinions as divine. Tawhid corrects all three.

### 5.3 Risalat (Prophethood)

**Meaning.** *Risalah* means message. A *rasul* is a messenger sent with a divine message; a *nabi* is a prophet who receives revelation. Many scholars explain that every rasul is a nabi but not the reverse: a rasul brings a new law or scripture, a nabi may continue an earlier law.

**Why prophethood is needed**

1. Human reason can know that God exists but cannot find, by itself, how to worship Him, what lies after death, or the full moral law.
2. Allah's justice requires guidance before judgment: "We never punish until We have sent a messenger." (Al-Isra 17:15); "so that people would have no argument against Allah after the messengers" (An-Nisa 4:165).
3. People need a living model, not only a book (Al-Ahzab 33:21).

**Key texts**

| Point | Reference |
|---|---|
| A messenger to every nation | An-Nahl 16:36; Yunus 10:47; Fatir 35:24 |
| Messengers were human and spoke their people's language | Ibrahim 14:4; Fussilat 41:6 |
| Their purpose: justice among people | Al-Hadid 57:25 |
| No distinction in believing in them | Al-Baqarah 2:285 |
| Some are named, some are not | Ghafir 40:78; An-Nisa 4:164 |
| Obedience to the Messenger is obedience to Allah | An-Nisa 4:80, 4:64 |
| The four tasks of the Prophet (PBUH): recite, purify, teach the Book, teach wisdom | Al-Jumu'ah 62:2; Al Imran 3:164 |

**Qualities of prophets:** chosen by Allah (not earned); human; truthful and trustworthy; protected from sin in conveying the message (*ismah*); supported by miracles; they convey the whole message (Al-Ma'idah 5:67).

**Special features of the prophethood of Muhammad (PBUH)**

1. **Universal.** "We have sent you to all mankind." (Saba 34:28; Al-A'raf 7:158). Earlier prophets were sent to their own peoples (Sahih al-Bukhari 335).
2. **Final.** "Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets." (Al-Ahzab 33:40). The Prophet (PBUH) compared himself to the last brick that completes a building (Sahih al-Bukhari 3535; Sahih Muslim 2286) and said: "There is no prophet after me." (Sahih al-Bukhari 4416; Sahih Muslim 2404)
3. **Complete.** The Deen was perfected (Al-Ma'idah 5:3).
4. **Preserved.** Both the Book (Al-Hijr 15:9) and the Sunnah survive in detail.
5. **Mercy for all worlds.** (Al-Anbiya 21:107)
6. **A full, recorded life** as a model in every role (Al-Ahzab 33:21; Al-Qalam 68:4).

**Why prophethood ended:** the message was completed and preserved; humanity had reached an age of writing and communication; the Ummah was given the duty of conveying it (Al Imran 3:110); and ijtihad meets new needs. Iqbal reads finality as the birth of the "inductive intellect": mankind must now use reason and experience under the light of the final revelation (*Reconstruction*, Lecture 5).

Finality of Prophethood is an agreed belief of all Muslim schools. Pakistan's Constitution defines a Muslim by it (Article 260(3), inserted by the Second Amendment, 1974).

**Impact of belief in prophethood**

*On the individual:* certain knowledge of right and wrong; a perfect example to follow; love of the Prophet (PBUH) as a part of faith (Sahih al-Bukhari 15); discipline through the Sunnah; hope of intercession.

*On society:* one law and one model for all, which unites a diverse Ummah; obedience to the Messenger as the basis of legal order (An-Nisa 4:59, 4:65); respect for all earlier prophets, which is a ground for interfaith respect (Al-Baqarah 2:136); protection from false claimants and personality cults; the Ummah's duty to carry the message (Al Imran 3:110).

Full answer: MA-4.

### 5.4 Akhirah (the Hereafter)

**Meaning.** *Akhirah* means "what comes after". Belief in it includes: death; the interval in the grave (*barzakh*, Al-Mu'minun 23:99–100); the Last Hour; resurrection; the gathering; the record of deeds; the scale; the reckoning; Paradise and Hell.

**Core texts**

| Reference | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Al Imran 3:185 | "Every soul will taste death, and you will be paid in full only on the Day of Resurrection." |
| Az-Zalzalah 99:7–8 | "Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it." |
| Al-Anbiya 21:47 | "We shall set up the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be wronged at all." |
| Al-Kahf 18:49 | The record leaves out nothing small or great. |
| Al-Isra 17:13–14 | "Read your record. You are enough today as a reckoner against yourself." |
| Ya-Sin 36:65 | Hands and feet will bear witness. |
| Al-Mulk 67:2 | He created death and life "to test which of you is best in deed". |

#### Quranic arguments for life after death (asked 2023)

| Argument | Reference |
|---|---|
| 1. **First creation proves the second.** He who made man from nothing can remake him. | Ya-Sin 36:78–79; Maryam 19:66–67; Al-Isra 17:49–51; Ar-Rum 30:27 |
| 2. **The greater creation proves the lesser.** The Creator of the heavens and earth can re-create man. | Ya-Sin 36:81 |
| 3. **Revival of dead earth.** Rain brings barren land to life every year. | Fussilat 41:39; Al-Hajj 22:5–7 |
| 4. **Stages of human growth.** Dust, drop, clot, lump, child, old age: each stage is a new creation. | Al-Hajj 22:5 |
| 5. **Purpose.** A wise Creator does not create in play. | Al-Mu'minun 23:115; Ad-Dukhan 44:38–39; Al-Qiyamah 75:36 |
| 6. **Justice.** In this world many wrongdoers escape and many good people suffer. Justice requires another life. | Al-Jathiyah 45:21–22; Sad 38:28; Al-Qalam 68:35–36 |
| 7. **Precision of Allah's power.** He can restore even the fingertips. | Al-Qiyamah 75:3–4 |
| 8. **Human moral sense.** The self-reproaching soul testifies to a final judgment. | Al-Qiyamah 75:2 |
| 9. **Demonstrations given to prophets.** Ibrahim (AS) and the birds. | Al-Baqarah 2:260 |
| 10. **Sleep and waking** as a daily likeness of death and resurrection. | Az-Zumar 39:42 |

#### Impact on the individual

1. **Sense of accountability.** Every act is recorded (Qaf 50:18; Al-Infitar 82:10–12).
2. **Self-restraint in private.** "As for him who feared standing before his Lord and restrained himself from desire, Paradise will be his home." (An-Nazi'at 79:40–41)
3. **Purpose and seriousness.** Life is a test (Al-Mulk 67:2).
4. **Patience and courage.** Loss here is not final (Al-Baqarah 2:155–157). The magicians of Pharaoh lost their fear of death once they believed.
5. **Detachment from greed.** "The Hereafter is better and more lasting." (Al-A'la 87:17)
6. **Motivation for good done in secret.** Reward is with Allah (Al-Insan 76:8–9).
7. **Hope and consolation** for the oppressed and bereaved.

The Prophet (PBUH) said a person's feet will not move on the Day of Resurrection until he is asked about his life, his knowledge, his wealth (how he earned and spent it) and his body (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2417).

#### Impact on society

1. **Honest dealings.** Fraud is condemned with a reminder of the Day (Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–6).
2. **Accountable rulers.** Umar (RA) is reported to have feared being questioned even for an animal that stumbled on a road in Iraq (reported in history works; wording varies).
3. **Justice in courts.** Judge and witness fear a higher court (An-Nisa 4:135).
4. **Care for the weak.** Denial of the Recompense goes with pushing away the orphan (Al-Ma'un 107:1–3).
5. **Lower crime with less policing.** Inner restraint works where the law cannot reach.
6. **Sacrifice for the common good.** People give life and wealth for a reward they will not see here.
7. **Social peace.** The wronged need not take revenge in despair; final justice is certain.

**Contrast:** a purely material view ("there is nothing but our worldly life", Al-Jathiyah 45:24) leaves no reason to do right when wrong pays and is hidden.

**Contemporary relevance:** corruption in public office, tax evasion, adulteration and white-collar crime persist despite laws because inner accountability is weak. Anti-corruption law needs the support of belief and moral training.

Full answer: MA-3.

### 5.5 Other articles of faith (short notes)

**Angels.** Created beings who never disobey (At-Tahrim 66:6; Al-Anbiya 21:19–20). Jibril brings revelation (Al-Baqarah 2:97); recording angels write deeds (Al-Infitar 82:10–12). *Impact:* awareness of being observed; trust that revelation reached the Prophet (PBUH) safely.

**Books.** Tawrat to Musa, Zabur to Dawud, Injil to Isa (peace be upon them), the Quran to Muhammad (PBUH); also scrolls of Ibrahim and Musa (Al-A'la 87:18–19). The Quran confirms and guards earlier scripture (Al-Ma'idah 5:48). *Impact:* a written, checkable source of law; respect for earlier revelation.

**Divine decree (qadr).** Allah knows and has measured all things (Al-Qamar 54:49; Al-Hadid 57:22–23), yet people choose and are responsible (Al-Insan 76:3; Al-Kahf 18:29; Ar-Ra'd 13:11). *Impact:* no despair in loss, no arrogance in success, effort with trust. The Prophet (PBUH) told the man with the camel to tie it and then trust Allah (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2517).

## 6. Islamic worship (Ibadat)

### 6.1 Concept of worship in Islam

- *Ibadah* comes from *abd* (servant). It means obedience with humility and love.
- **Wide meaning:** Ibn Taymiyyah defined ibadah as a comprehensive term for everything Allah loves and approves, of words and deeds, outward and inward. Honest work, study, good treatment of family and service to people are worship when done for Allah within His limits.
- **Purpose of creation:** Adh-Dhariyat 51:56.
- **Conditions:** sincere intention (Sahih al-Bukhari 1) and conformity with the Sunnah (Sahih al-Bukhari 2697).
- **Balance:** the Prophet (PBUH) told Abdullah ibn Amr (RA) that his body, eyes and wife have rights over him (Sahih al-Bukhari 5199). There is no monasticism.
- The four formal acts of worship train a person for this wider obedience. Maududi (*Let Us Be Muslims / Worship in Islam*) calls them a training course.

**Answer pattern for any worship question:** definition → obligation (Quran and Hadith) → brief rules → spiritual impact → moral impact → social (and economic, political) impact → why the impact is missing today → conclusion.

---

### 6.2 Salah (prayer)

**Meaning.** Literally supplication. Technically, the prescribed worship beginning with takbir and ending with taslim.

**Obligation.** Made compulsory five times a day on the night of Mi'raj. "Prayer has been decreed for the believers at fixed times." (An-Nisa 4:103). It is the second pillar (Sahih al-Bukhari 8). It is the first deed to be reckoned on the Day of Resurrection (Jami at-Tirmidhi 413; Sunan an-Nasa'i 465).

**Kinds:** fard (five daily, Jumu'ah); wajib (witr and the two Eids in the Hanafi school; other schools class these differently); sunnah; nafl (tahajjud, ishraq and others); special prayers (janazah, eclipse, rain, istikharah, prayer of fear).

**Spiritual impact**

1. Direct link with Allah five times a day: "Establish prayer for My remembrance." (Ta-Ha 20:14)
2. Peace of heart (Ar-Ra'd 13:28). The Prophet (PBUH) is reported to have said to Bilal (RA): "Give us comfort by it." (Sunan Abi Dawud 4985)
3. Washing away of sins: the likeness of a river at one's door (Sahih al-Bukhari 528; Sahih Muslim 667); "good deeds remove evil deeds" (Hud 11:114).
4. Humility (khushu): "Successful are the believers who are humble in their prayer." (Al-Mu'minun 23:1–2)
5. Strength in hardship: "Seek help through patience and prayer." (Al-Baqarah 2:153)

**Moral impact**

1. Restraint from wrong: "Prayer restrains from indecency and wrongdoing." (Al-Ankabut 29:45)
2. Punctuality and discipline (fixed times).
3. Cleanliness (wudu, Al-Ma'idah 5:6; "purity is half of faith", Sahih Muslim 223).
4. Truthfulness and sincerity; no show (Al-Ma'un 107:4–6).
5. Steadiness of character: man is restless, "except those who pray" (Al-Ma'arij 70:19–23).

**Social impact**

1. Equality: ruler and labourer stand in one row.
2. Unity and brotherhood: congregation is 27 times better (Sahih al-Bukhari 645; Sahih Muslim 650).
3. Community organisation: the mosque as centre for teaching, consultation and welfare, as in Madinah.
4. Training in leadership and obedience: one imam, an orderly following, and the right to correct the imam's mistake.
5. Mutual awareness: neighbours meet daily; the Friday sermon informs the community (Al-Jumu'ah 62:9).
6. Mark of a believing government: "those who, if We give them authority in the land, establish prayer and give zakat" (Al-Hajj 22:41).

**Why the effects are missing today:** prayer without understanding or humility; ritual separated from conduct; mosques limited to ritual; sectarian division of mosques.

### 6.3 Zakat

**Meaning.** Literally purification and growth. Technically, a fixed share of specified wealth, due yearly from a Muslim who owns the minimum amount (*nisab*), to be given to specified recipients.

**Obligation.** "Establish prayer and give zakat." (Al-Baqarah 2:43, 2:110). "Take from their wealth a charity by which you cleanse and purify them." (At-Tawbah 9:103). Third pillar (Sahih al-Bukhari 8). Abu Bakr (RA) fought those who refused it, saying he would fight anyone who separated prayer and zakat (Sahih al-Bukhari 1399–1400; Sahih Muslim 20).

**Basic rules**

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rate on gold, silver, cash, trade goods | 2.5% after one lunar year |
| Nisab | 87.48 g gold (7.5 tola) or 612.36 g silver (52.5 tola) or equal value |
| Farm produce (ushr) | 10% if rain-fed, 5% if irrigated at cost |
| Livestock | Set scales (e.g. 1 goat for 5 camels; 1 goat for 40–120 goats) |
| Minerals and treasure (rikaz) | 20% |

**The eight heads of zakat (At-Tawbah 9:60)**

1. The poor (*fuqara*)
2. The needy (*masakin*)
3. Those employed to collect it (*amilin*)
4. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled (*mu'allafat al-qulub*)
5. Freeing captives and slaves (*fi'l-riqab*)
6. Those in debt (*gharimin*)
7. In the cause of Allah (*fi sabilillah*)
8. The stranded traveller (*ibn al-sabil*)

Jurists differ on details, for example how wide "in the cause of Allah" is, and whether the fourth head still applies. The classical commentators generally read *fi sabilillah* as those striving in jihad.

**Spiritual impact**

1. Purifies the soul from love of wealth and miserliness (At-Tawbah 9:103).
2. Expresses gratitude; brings increase (Ibrahim 14:7; Ar-Rum 30:39).
3. Brings Allah's mercy and reward (Al-Baqarah 2:261, 2:277).
4. Protects from punishment for hoarding (At-Tawbah 9:34–35; Sahih al-Bukhari 1403).

**Moral impact**

1. Generosity and compassion replace greed.
2. Honesty: self-assessed before Allah.
3. Humility: the poor have a *right* in the wealth (Adh-Dhariyat 51:19; Al-Ma'arij 70:24–25), so giving is no favour (Al-Baqarah 2:264).
4. Dignity of the receiver: it is his due, taken by the state, not begged.

**Social and economic impact**

1. **Circulation of wealth:** "so that it does not circulate only among the rich among you" (Al-Hashr 59:7).
2. **Poverty reduction and social security:** the Prophet (PBUH) told Mu'adh (RA) it is "taken from their rich and given to their poor" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1395; Sahih Muslim 19).
3. **Discourages hoarding; encourages investment:** idle wealth shrinks by 2.5% a year.
4. **Narrows class gaps; reduces envy and crime.**
5. **Relief of debtors, travellers and captives.**
6. **Demand and employment:** money moves to those who spend it.
7. **Historical proof:** under Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz (99–101 AH) officials in some provinces are reported to have found few people in need of zakat (reported in history works such as Ibn Abd al-Hakam's biography; treat as a historical report).

**Zakat and tax:** zakat is worship with fixed rates and fixed heads; tax is set by the state for general needs. One does not cancel the other.

**Pakistan:** Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980 (deduction at source from savings accounts on 1 Ramadan; zakat councils). Weaknesses: narrow base, leakages, distrust, exemptions. Reforms: transparent digital collection and disbursement, linking with the national socio-economic registry (BISP), spending on skills and micro-enterprise so receivers become givers, proper ushr collection.

Full answer: MA-5.

### 6.4 Sawm (fasting) — not asked 2016–2026; prepare an outline

**Meaning.** Literally to abstain. Technically, to refrain from food, drink and marital relations from dawn to sunset with the intention of worship.

**Obligation.** Prescribed in 2 AH. "Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may attain taqwa." (Al-Baqarah 2:183). Ramadan is the month of the Quran (Al-Baqarah 2:185). Concessions for the sick and travellers (Al-Baqarah 2:184).

**Spiritual impact**

1. Taqwa, its stated aim (Al-Baqarah 2:183).
2. Sincerity: only Allah knows if one is fasting. Hadith qudsi: "Fasting is for Me and I shall reward it." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1904; Sahih Muslim 1151)
3. Forgiveness: whoever fasts Ramadan with faith and hope of reward has his past sins forgiven (Sahih al-Bukhari 38; Sahih Muslim 760).
4. Closeness to the Quran; night prayer; Laylat al-Qadr, "better than a thousand months" (Al-Qadr 97:3).

**Moral impact**

1. Self-control over hunger, desire and anger. "Fasting is a shield." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1904)
2. Truthfulness: "Whoever does not give up false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need of his giving up food and drink." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1903)
3. Patience and willpower.
4. Gratitude for daily blessings.

**Social impact**

1. Empathy with the hungry; the rich feel what the poor feel.
2. Generosity: the Prophet (PBUH) was most generous in Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 6); sadaqat al-fitr ensures the poor share in Eid.
3. Unity: a whole society keeps one timetable; shared iftar and tarawih.
4. Equality: rich and poor fast alike.
5. Lower crime and quarrels where the fast is kept in spirit.
6. Health and moderation in consumption.

**Today:** price-gouging and waste in Ramadan defeat its purpose. Mention Ramadan relief packages and price control as state duties.

### 6.5 Hajj (pilgrimage)

**Meaning.** Literally to intend a visit. Technically, to visit the Ka'bah and the sacred sites in the appointed days and perform the prescribed rites.

**Obligation.** Once in a lifetime for those able. "Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by all who can find a way to it." (Al Imran 3:97). Proclaimed by Ibrahim (AS) (Al-Hajj 22:27). Fifth pillar (Sahih al-Bukhari 8). The Prophet (PBUH) performed one Hajj, in 10 AH, and said: "Take your rites from me." (Sahih Muslim 1297)

**Main rites:** ihram from the miqat; tawaf; sa'i between Safa and Marwah (Al-Baqarah 2:158); stay at Mina (8 Dhul Hijjah); standing at Arafat (9th), the central rite; night at Muzdalifah; stoning, sacrifice, shaving (10th); tawaf al-ifadah; days of Mina.

**Spiritual impact**

1. Renewal of Tawhid: the talbiyah declares that Allah has no partner.
2. Forgiveness: whoever performs Hajj without obscenity or sin returns as on the day his mother bore him (Sahih al-Bukhari 1521; Sahih Muslim 1350). An accepted Hajj has no reward but Paradise (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773; Sahih Muslim 1349).
3. Reminder of death and resurrection: two unsewn sheets like a shroud; Arafat like the plain of gathering.
4. Link with Ibrahim, Hajar and Isma'il (peace be upon them): trust, sacrifice, obedience.
5. Taqwa as the aim: "It is not their meat or blood that reaches Allah, but your piety." (Al-Hajj 22:37)

**Moral impact**

1. Self-restraint: "no obscenity, no wrongdoing, no quarrelling during Hajj" (Al-Baqarah 2:197).
2. Patience under hardship and crowding.
3. Humility and simplicity: one dress for all.
4. Sacrifice of wealth, time and comfort.

**Social, economic and political impact**

1. **Equality:** king and labourer wear the same two sheets. Malcolm X wrote from Makkah in 1964 that the Hajj showed him brotherhood across colour.
2. **Unity of the Ummah:** one place, one time, one call. The largest yearly human gathering.
3. **Universality of Islam:** every race and language together (compare the 2026 MCQ on the worship that shows Islam's universality).
4. **Exchange of knowledge and ideas:** historically scholars met and travelled on the Hajj routes.
5. **Economic benefits:** "that they may witness benefits for themselves" (Al-Hajj 22:28); trade is allowed (Al-Baqarah 2:198).
6. **A forum for the Ummah:** the Farewell Sermon was a charter of rights given at Hajj. The gathering could serve consultation among Muslim states.
7. **Peace:** the sanctuary is a place of safety (Al Imran 3:97; Al-Baqarah 2:125).

**Today:** the unity felt at Hajj is not carried into politics; commercial excess; repeated optional pilgrimages while neighbours are in need. Suggest using Hajj for Ummah-level dialogue and reviving its spirit of simplicity.

### 6.6 Fard ayn and fard kifayah (asked 2025)

| | Fard ayn | Fard kifayah |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Individual duty: every responsible Muslim must do it | Collective duty: if enough people do it, the rest are free of blame; if none do, all are sinful |
| Examples | Five prayers, fasting, zakat (if liable), basic religious knowledge | Funeral prayer, burial; enjoining good and forbidding evil; defence; judiciary and ifta; memorising and teaching Quran; essential professions |

**Basis:** "It is not for the believers to go out all together. Why should not a party from each group go forth to gain understanding in religion and warn their people?" (At-Tawbah 9:122). "Let there be a group among you who call to good." (Al Imran 3:104)

**Religious importance:** preserves the Deen (scholars, teachers, judges); keeps public religious life going.

**Social importance:** al-Ghazali (*Ihya*, Book of Knowledge) counts medicine and arithmetic among collective duties, since a community cannot live without them. Hence the idea covers every skill a society needs.

**What falls under it today:** medicine and public health; engineering, food and energy security; defence capability and technology (Al-Anfal 8:60); economics and Islamic finance; law and administration; media and accurate information; cyber-security and artificial intelligence; scientific research; disaster relief; answering misconceptions about Islam. If the Ummah depends wholly on others for vaccines, weapons or technology, the collective duty is unmet.

**Outline:** definition → difference from fard ayn → evidence → religious role → social role → modern scope (8 points) → who is responsible (state first, then able individuals) → conclusion.
