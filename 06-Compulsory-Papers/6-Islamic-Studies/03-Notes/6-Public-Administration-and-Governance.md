## 1. Concept of public administration in Islam

### 1.1 Meaning

- **Public administration:** the organisation and management of people and resources to carry out the policies of the state and serve the public.
- **In Islam:** the management of public affairs as a *trust (amanah)* from Allah, according to the Shariah, to establish justice and secure the welfare of people in this world and the next. Arabic terms: *idarah* (administration), *tadbir* (management), *siyasah shar'iyyah* (governance according to the Shariah), *wilayah* (public authority).

Muhammad Al-Buraey (*Administrative Development: An Islamic Perspective*, on the FPSC list) describes the Islamic model as value-based: its starting point is the moral character of the administrator, then structure and technique.

### 1.2 Foundations

| Foundation | Meaning | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Sovereignty of Allah** | Final authority is Allah's; rulers and officials are trustees | Yusuf 12:40; Al Imran 3:26 |
| **Vicegerency (khilafah)** | Man manages the earth on Allah's behalf and will answer for it | Al-Baqarah 2:30; Sad 38:26 |
| **Trust (amanah)** | Office is a trust, not a right or a prize | An-Nisa 4:58; Sahih Muslim 1825 |
| **Justice (adl)** | The main purpose of government | An-Nisa 4:58; An-Nahl 16:90; Al-Hadid 57:25 |
| **Consultation (shura)** | Decisions by consultation | Ash-Shura 42:38; Al Imran 3:159 |
| **Accountability** | Before Allah, the law and the people | Sahih al-Bukhari 7138; Al-Hijr 15:92–93 |
| **Merit** | Posts go to the competent and honest | Al-Qasas 28:26; Yusuf 12:55 |
| **Welfare (maslahah)** | Policy must serve public benefit | Juristic maxim: "The ruler's action toward the ruled depends on public interest." |
| **Excellence (ihsan)** | Work done as well as possible | Sahih Muslim 1955 |
| **Rule of law** | Everyone under the Shariah | An-Nisa 4:59; Sahih al-Bukhari 3475 |

### 1.3 How it differs from secular administration

| Aspect | Conventional theory | Islamic concept |
|---|---|---|
| Source of authority | The state, the constitution | Allah's law; people as trustees |
| Motive of the official | Salary, promotion, legal duty | These, plus worship and accountability to Allah |
| Control | External: rules, audits | External and internal: conscience and taqwa |
| Aim | Efficiency, economy, effectiveness | These, plus justice and moral welfare |
| Ethics | A code attached to the system | The base of the system |
| Relation to the public | Service provider and client | Shepherd and flock; servant of the people |

### 1.4 Functions of the Islamic state (what administration is for)

"Those who, if We establish them in the land, establish prayer, give zakat, enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong." (Al-Hajj 22:41)

Al-Mawardi (*al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyyah*) lists ten duties of the ruler: guard the religion; judge disputes; keep internal security; enforce the legal punishments; defend the borders; organise jihad when needed; collect lawful revenue; pay salaries and stipends on time without waste; appoint honest and capable officers; and personally supervise public affairs.

In modern terms: law and order, justice, defence, revenue, welfare, education, economic management and moral guidance.

### 1.5 The Prophet (PBUH) as administrator

1. Built the first institutions: the mosque as secretariat, court and school; a market with fair rules.
2. A written constitution (the Charter of Madinah).
3. Appointed governors (Attab ibn Asid in Makkah; Mu'adh and Abu Musa in Yemen), judges (Ali and Mu'adh in Yemen), collectors of zakat, scribes and commanders.
4. Chose on merit and refused those who asked for office (Sahih al-Bukhari 7149). To Abu Dharr (RA): "You are weak, and it is a trust." (Sahih Muslim 1825)
5. Briefed officials: "Make things easy and do not make them hard; give good news and do not drive people away." (Sahih al-Bukhari 3038)
6. Audited them: the case of Ibn al-Lutbiyyah, a collector who said "this is for you and this was given to me as a gift". The Prophet (PBUH) said from the pulpit: why did he not sit in his parents' house and see whether gifts came to him? (Sahih al-Bukhari 6979, 7174; Sahih Muslim 1832)
7. Paid officials adequately, so they would not need to take from the public: one who works for the state should be able to have a house, a spouse and a servant (Sunan Abi Dawud 2945); what an official takes beyond his fixed pay is embezzlement (Sunan Abi Dawud 2943).
8. Consulted on public matters; kept records and correspondence; used a seal.

## 2. Quranic guidance on good governance

"Good governance" in today's language means participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, equity, effectiveness and accountability. The Quran gives the principles behind each.

| Principle | Verse | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust and merit** | An-Nisa 4:58 | "Allah commands you to hand over trusts to those entitled to them." Commentators include public offices in "trusts". |
| **Justice** | An-Nisa 4:58, 4:135; Al-Ma'idah 5:8; An-Nahl 16:90 | Judge with justice, even against yourselves, even toward those you dislike |
| **Consultation** | Ash-Shura 42:38; Al Imran 3:159 | "Their affairs are by consultation among them." "Consult them in the matter." |
| **Obedience within limits** | An-Nisa 4:59 | Obey Allah, the Messenger and those in authority; refer disputes to Allah and the Messenger |
| **Competence and integrity** | Al-Qasas 28:26 | "The best one you can employ is the strong and the trustworthy." |
| **Knowledge and guardianship** | Yusuf 12:55 | Yusuf (AS): "Set me over the storehouses of the land; I am a knowing guardian." |
| **Knowledge and capacity above wealth and birth** | Al-Baqarah 2:247 | Talut was chosen for knowledge and strength, not riches |
| **No authority for the unjust** | Al-Baqarah 2:124 | "My covenant does not include the wrongdoers." |
| **Rule by revealed law, not desire** | Sad 38:26; Al-Ma'idah 5:48–49 | "Judge between people in truth and do not follow desire." |
| **Purposes of power** | Al-Hajj 22:41 | Prayer, zakat, enjoining good, forbidding evil |
| **Welfare and fair distribution** | Al-Hashr 59:7; At-Tawbah 9:60 | Wealth must not circulate only among the rich |
| **Anti-corruption** | Al-Baqarah 2:188 | Do not consume wealth wrongfully or offer it to rulers as bribes |
| **Keeping trusts and promises** | Al-Anfal 8:27; Al-Mu'minun 23:8; Al-Isra 17:34 | Do not betray trusts; the covenant will be asked about |
| **Verification of information** | Al-Hujurat 49:6; An-Nisa 4:83 | Check reports; refer security matters to those who can assess them |
| **Accountability** | Al-A'raf 7:6; Al-Hijr 15:92–93; At-Tawbah 9:105 | All will be questioned; "Work, for Allah will see your work, and His Messenger and the believers." |
| **Kindness in leadership** | Al Imran 3:159; Ash-Shu'ara 26:215 | Gentleness holds people together |
| **Against tyranny and corruption** | Al-Qasas 28:4, 28:83; Al-Baqarah 2:205; Hud 11:113 | Pharaoh as the model of misrule: arrogance, dividing people, oppression |
| **Do not obey the wasteful and corrupt** | Ash-Shu'ara 26:151–152 | |
| **Collective reform** | Al Imran 3:104, 3:110 | A community that enjoins good and forbids evil |
| **Planning and crisis management** | Yusuf 12:47–49 | Yusuf's (AS) seven-year food-storage plan |
| **Public works and refusal of personal gain** | Al-Kahf 18:94–95 | Dhul-Qarnayn built the barrier for a people and declined payment |
| **Consultative monarchy as example** | An-Naml 27:32 | The Queen of Saba: "I decide no matter until you are present with me." |

**Models of good and bad rule in the Quran:** good: Dawud and Sulayman (justice, wisdom, Sad 38:20, 38:26), Yusuf (competence, forgiveness), Dhul-Qarnayn (justice, service), Talut (merit). Bad: Pharaoh (tyranny), Qarun (corrupt wealth), Haman (the complicit official).

## 3. Concept of governance and its application: Quran, Sunnah and Fiqh

### 3.1 In the Sunnah

| Principle | Hadith |
|---|---|

| Betraying the public closes Paradise | "No servant is given charge of subjects by Allah and dies while cheating them but Allah forbids him Paradise." (Sahih al-Bukhari 7150–7151; Sahih Muslim 142) |

| Accessibility | A ruler who shuts himself off from the needs of the people, Allah will shut Himself off from his needs (Sunan Abi Dawud 2948; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1332) |
| Gentleness to the public | "O Allah, whoever is given charge of any affair of my Ummah and is hard on them, be hard on him; and whoever is gentle with them, be gentle with him." (Sahih Muslim 1828) |
| No bribes | The Prophet (PBUH) cursed the giver and the taker of bribes (Sunan Abi Dawud 3580; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1337) |
| Sincere advice to rulers | "The religion is sincere counsel ... to the leaders of the Muslims and their common people." (Sahih Muslim 55) |
| Speaking truth to power | "The best jihad is a word of truth before a tyrant ruler." (Sunan Abi Dawud 4344) |

### 3.2 In Fiqh and political thought

| Scholar | Work | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Abu Yusuf (d. 798) | *Kitab al-Kharaj*, written for Harun al-Rashid | Taxation, public finance, treatment of taxpayers and non-Muslims; advice to the ruler on justice |
| Al-Mawardi (d. 1058) | *Al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyyah* | First systematic constitutional and administrative law: caliphate, ministers, governors, judges, mazalim courts, hisbah, revenue |
| Al-Ghazali (d. 1111) | *Nasihat al-Muluk*; *Ihya* | Ethics of rulers; religion and state as twins; justice as the basis of prosperity |
| Nizam al-Mulk (d. 1092) | *Siyasatnama* | Practical statecraft: intelligence, appointments, justice |
| Ibn Taymiyyah (d. 1328) | *Al-Siyasah al-Shar'iyyah*; *Al-Hisbah* | Government as the discharge of trusts and justice (built on An-Nisa 4:58–59); appoint the most suitable person available |
| Ibn Khaldun (d. 1406) | *Muqaddimah* | Injustice ruins civilization; heavy taxes reduce revenue; rise and fall of states |
| Shah Waliullah (d. 1762) | *Hujjatullah al-Balighah*; *Izalat al-Khafa* | Stages of social organisation (*irtifaqat*); economic injustice as a cause of decline; the Rightly Guided Caliphate as model |
| Maududi (d. 1979) | *Islamic Law and Constitution*; *Khilafat-o-Mulukiyat* | Sovereignty of Allah; popular vicegerency; "theo-democracy"; the change from caliphate to monarchy |

**Juristic principles (maxims) used in governance**

1. The ruler's dealing with the ruled is tied to public interest.
2. Harm is to be removed. ("No harm and no returning of harm", Sunan Ibn Majah 2341.)
3. Preventing harm comes before gaining benefit.
4. A private harm is borne to prevent a public harm.
5. Necessity makes the forbidden permissible, within limits.
6. Custom is a basis for rulings where the text is silent.
7. Certainty is not removed by doubt.

**Maqasid al-Shariah as a policy test:** a policy is sound if it protects religion, life, intellect, family and property.

### 3.3 Application today

E-governance and open data (transparency); merit-based recruitment through public service commissions; ombudsman offices; citizen feedback portals; social protection programmes; independent audit. Each can be anchored in the principles above. Pakistan's constitutional basis: the Objectives Resolution (Article 2-A): sovereignty belongs to Allah and authority is a sacred trust to be exercised by the people's chosen representatives.

## 4. Governance structure in Islam

### 4.1 Shura (consultation)

**Meaning.** Seeking the considered opinion of those qualified, before deciding a matter of common concern.

**Basis.** Ash-Shura 42:38 lists consultation between prayer and spending, as a mark of believers. Al Imran 3:159 commands the Prophet (PBUH) himself to consult, revealed after Uhud, where consultation had led to a costly result. So the duty stands even when outcomes disappoint.

**Practice of the Prophet (PBUH):** Badr (camp site; prisoners, Sahih Muslim 1763); Uhud (where to fight); Khandaq (the trench); Hudaybiyyah (Umm Salamah's advice). "The one consulted is in a position of trust." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2822; Sunan Abi Dawud 5128)

**Practice of the Caliphs:** selection of each Caliph; Abu Bakr (RA) on the apostasy wars and the compilation of the Quran; Umar (RA) on the lands of Iraq, the calendar, the stipend register, the plague of Amwas, and going in person to Persia.

**Scope.** No consultation on matters fixed by clear text (Al-Ahzab 33:36). Consultation covers policy, administration, appointments, war and peace, and the application of law to new cases.

**Who is consulted.** *Ahl al-hall wa'l-aqd* (those who loose and bind): people of knowledge, integrity and public trust. In modern form, an elected parliament, with experts.

**Is the ruler bound by it?** Jurists differ. Many classical jurists held it advisory; many modern scholars (and Maududi) hold the ruler should be bound by the majority, citing the Prophet's (PBUH) acceptance of the majority view at Uhud.

**Benefits:** better decisions; shared responsibility; a check on tyranny; public trust; training of leaders.

### 4.2 Legislation

- **Law-giver:** Allah (Yusuf 12:40). The Prophet (PBUH) explains and legislates by revelation (Al-Hashr 59:7).
- **Human legislation has three zones:**
  1. *Clear texts:* no change; only rules for implementation.
  2. *Texts open to more than one meaning:* interpretation (ijtihad) and choice among views.
  3. *Matters on which the texts are silent:* free legislation for public interest, in the spirit of the Shariah. This is the widest zone: traffic, cyber law, civil service rules, environmental law.
- **Who legislates:** the consultative body, with scholars and experts. Iqbal proposed that ijtihad today be exercised by an elected Muslim assembly, with scholars of law helping it (*Reconstruction*, Lecture 6).
- **Pakistan:** Parliament legislates; Article 227 requires laws to conform to the Quran and Sunnah; the Council of Islamic Ideology advises (Articles 228–230); the Federal Shariat Court (1980) can strike down laws repugnant to Islam (Article 203-D).

### 4.3 Sources of Islamic law

| Source | Definition | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Quran** | The word of Allah; about 500 verses deal with legal rulings by the common count | An-Nisa 4:105; Al-Ma'idah 5:48 |
| **Sunnah** | Sayings, acts and approvals of the Prophet (PBUH). It explains, specifies and adds to the Quran. | Al-Hashr 59:7; An-Nisa 4:80; An-Nahl 16:44 |
| **Ijma** | Agreement of the qualified jurists of an age on a legal ruling | An-Nisa 4:115; see Notes 7 |
| **Qiyas** | Extending a known ruling to a new case that shares its effective cause | Al-Hashr 59:2; see Notes 7 |

**Secondary sources** (weight differs between schools): *istihsan* (juristic preference; chiefly Hanafi), *maslahah mursalah* (public interest; chiefly Maliki), *urf* (custom), *istishab* (presumption of continuity; chiefly Shafi'i and Hanbali), *sadd al-dhara'i* (blocking the means to harm), the opinion of a Companion, the practice of the people of Madinah (Maliki), and earlier revealed laws. The Ja'fari school lists Quran, Sunnah (including the teachings of the Imams), ijma and reason (*aql*).

The order is shown by the report of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA), who said he would judge by the Book of Allah, then the Sunnah, then his own reasoned opinion (Sunan Abi Dawud 3592; Jami at-Tirmidhi 1327). Hadith scholars point to weakness in its chain, but jurists have widely accepted and used it.

### 4.4 Organs of the state

| Organ | Classical form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Head of state | *Khalifah, Imam, Amir al-Mu'minin* | Chosen with consultation and confirmed by pledge (*bay'ah*). Qualifications listed by al-Mawardi include justice, knowledge, sound senses and limbs, judgment and courage. |
| Executive | Ministers (*wazir*), governors (*wali, amil*), departments (*diwan*) | Al-Mawardi distinguishes a minister with delegated powers from one who only executes orders |
| Consultative / legislative | *Majlis al-shura; ahl al-hall wa'l-aqd* | |
| Judiciary | *Qadi*; the grievance court (*mazalim*); the market inspector (*muhtasib*) | Independent of the governor from Umar's (RA) time |
| Treasury | *Bayt al-mal* | Public funds, separate from the ruler's own wealth |

## 5. Governance under the Rightly Guided Caliphate (Khilafat-e-Rashidah)

Period: 11–40 AH (632–661 CE), about thirty years. Dates and events are in `06-Seerah-Timeline.md`.

A note on balance: Sunni and Shia Muslims differ on the question of succession to the Prophet (PBUH). The syllabus asks about governance. Write about the governance record of all four Caliphs respectfully and leave the succession debate aside.

### 5.1 How each Caliph was chosen (asked 2017)

| Caliph | Method |
|---|---|
| **Abu Bakr (RA)** | Open discussion among the Ansar and Muhajirun at Saqifah Bani Sa'idah; Umar (RA) proposed him; those present pledged; the general pledge followed in the mosque the next day (Sahih al-Bukhari 6830). |
| **Umar (RA)** | Abu Bakr (RA), in his last illness, consulted leading Companions one by one, then nominated Umar in writing; the people accepted and pledged. |
| **Uthman (RA)** | Umar (RA) named six senior Companions to choose one of themselves within three days and excluded his own son from candidacy (Sahih al-Bukhari 3700). Abdur Rahman ibn Awf (RA) withdrew his own claim and consulted the people of Madinah widely; then Uthman was chosen and pledged to (Sahih al-Bukhari 7207). |
| **Ali (RA)** | After Uthman's (RA) martyrdom the people of Madinah pressed him to accept; he insisted that the pledge be public, in the mosque. |

**Common features:** no hereditary succession; consultation each time; no one put himself forward; the public pledge (*bay'ah*) completed the choice; the method was flexible. Umar (RA) said that whoever is given the pledge without consulting the Muslims is not to be followed (Sahih al-Bukhari 6830).

**How far democratic?**

| Democratic features | Differences from modern Western democracy |
|---|---|
| Rule by consent (pledge) | Sovereignty belongs to Allah, not to the people absolutely |
| Consultation | Legislation only within the Shariah |
| Accountability of the ruler; freedom to criticise | No parties, campaigns or fixed terms |
| Rule of law; ruler under the same courts | Candidates did not seek office |
| Equality of citizens; no privileged class | The consulted were chiefly the senior, knowledgeable people of the capital |
| Public treasury separate from private purse | Qualification by piety and competence, not popularity alone |
| Independent judiciary | |

Conclusion to write: it was democratic in spirit (consent, consultation, accountability, equality) and Islamic in foundation (divine sovereignty and law). Maududi calls this "theo-democracy". Iqbal held that a republican form of government is fully consistent with the spirit of Islam.

### 5.2 Salient features of governance under the Caliphs (asked 2016)

1. **Sovereignty of Allah; supremacy of the Shariah.**
2. **Elective, consultative leadership.**
3. **The Caliph as servant and trustee.** Abu Bakr's (RA) inaugural address: "I have been given authority over you and I am not the best of you. If I do right, help me; if I do wrong, set me right ... The weak among you is strong in my eyes until I secure his right, and the strong among you is weak in my eyes until I take the right from him ... Obey me as long as I obey Allah and His Messenger." (Ibn Hisham; Ibn Kathir graded the chain sound)
4. **Rule of law and equality before it.**
5. **Independent judiciary.**
6. **Accountability of Caliph and officials.**
7. **Public treasury as a trust.** Abu Bakr (RA) took a modest allowance and, at death, directed that it be repaid from his property (reported in the histories).
8. **Welfare state.** Stipends, relief in famine, care of non-Muslim poor.
9. **Freedom of expression.** Ordinary men and women questioned the Caliph in public.
10. **Simplicity of rulers.** Umar (RA) entered Jerusalem in plain clothes, taking turns riding with his servant, as the well-known account goes.
11. **Protection of minorities.**
12. **Merit in appointments;** dismissal of the unfit.
13. **Institutional development,** above all under Umar (RA).

### 5.3 Each Caliph's contribution

**Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (RA), 11–13 AH**

- Held the state together after the Prophet's (PBUH) death: sent out Usamah's army; defeated the false prophets and the rebels; refused to separate zakat from prayer (Sahih al-Bukhari 1399–1400).
- Compiled the Quran in one collection after Yamamah, through Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) (Sahih al-Bukhari 4986).
- Divided Arabia into provinces under governors; set the laws of war (ten instructions, Muwatta Malik, Jihad, no. 10).
- Model of consultation and humility.

**Umar al-Faruq (RA), 13–23 AH: the organiser of the state**

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Administration | Provinces and districts under a governor (*wali*), with separate officers for revenue, treasury, police and justice; written appointment orders read to the public |
| Consultation | A council of senior Companions; larger assemblies for major matters; general consultation at Hajj |
| Finance | The treasury (*bayt al-mal*) at the centre and in provinces; the register of stipends (*diwan*); land survey of Iraq; land tax (*kharaj*), tithes, customs duty on foreign traders; conquered lands of Iraq kept as public property for future generations and not divided among soldiers, after long consultation (Abu Yusuf, *Kitab al-Kharaj*) |
| Justice | Judges appointed and paid by the centre, independent of governors; the famous letter to Abu Musa (section 6) |
| Accountability | Assets of officials recorded on appointment and checked later; complaints heard at Hajj; inspectors (Muhammad ibn Maslamah); governors dismissed or fined; conditions of simple living |
| Welfare | Stipends for children from birth, the old and the disabled; relief during the famine of 18 AH; rest houses and wells on the routes; a stipend for an old Jewish beggar |
| Military | Garrison towns (Basra, Kufa, Fustat); salaried army; limits on time away from families |
| Public works | Canals in Iraq and a canal linking the Nile to the Red Sea; mosques, roads |
| Other | The Hijri calendar (17 AH); census; prisons; night patrols; market supervision; the title *Amir al-Mu'minin* |
| Minorities | The assurance to Jerusalem; his last advice to protect the people under covenant (Sahih al-Bukhari 3052, 3700) |

Shibli Nu'mani's *Al-Farooq* is the standard Urdu and English study.

**Uthman Dhun-Nurayn (RA), 24–35 AH**

- Standardised the written text of the Quran and sent copies to the provinces (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987).
- Built the first Muslim navy; Cyprus taken; victory at sea in the Battle of the Masts.
- Expansion in North Africa, Armenia and Khurasan.
- Enlarged the Prophet's Mosque and the Sacred Mosque; public works.
- Refused to let blood be shed in his own defence during the siege of his house, according to the histories.

**Ali al-Murtada (RA), 35–40 AH**

- Stressed equal distribution from the treasury and strict justice in a time of civil strife.
- Detailed written guidance to governors (section 6).
- Dealt with the Khawarij by argument first (sending Ibn Abbas (RA) to debate them), then by force only when they took up arms.
- Famous for judicial insight. Umar (RA) said: "The best judge among us is Ali." (Sahih al-Bukhari 4481)

### 5.4 Hazrat Ali (RA): courage and judicial wisdom (asked 2020)

**Courage**

1. Slept in the Prophet's (PBUH) bed on the night of the Hijrah, knowing assassins surrounded the house (Seerah of Ibn Hisham).
2. Badr: fought in the opening duels.
3. Uhud: stood by the Prophet (PBUH) when the lines broke.
4. Khandaq: defeated the champion Amr ibn Abd Wudd in single combat (Seerah works).
5. Khaybar: the Prophet (PBUH) said he would give the flag to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger and whom they love; he gave it to Ali, who took the fort (Sahih al-Bukhari 3701, 4210; Sahih Muslim 2406).
6. Hunayn: among the few who stood firm.
7. Titles: *Asadullah* (Lion of Allah), *Haydar*.
8. Moral courage: accepted the caliphate at the most dangerous moment; refused to compromise on principle for political gain.

**Judicial wisdom**

1. Sent as judge to Yemen while young. The Prophet (PBUH) prayed for him and instructed: "When two parties sit before you, do not decide until you have heard the second as you heard the first." Ali said he never doubted in a judgment after that (Sunan Abi Dawud 3582).
2. Umar's (RA) testimony (Sahih al-Bukhari 4481). Umar is also reported to have said: "Were it not for Ali, Umar would have perished," after Ali corrected a ruling (reported in biographical works).
3. Method: separate examination of witnesses to expose contradictions; attention to circumstantial evidence; reasoning by analogy.
4. Well-known rulings reported from him:
   - The punishment for drinking wine set at eighty lashes by analogy with slander, on his advice to Umar (RA): one who drinks becomes intoxicated, raves and slanders (reported in the Muwatta, Book of Drinks [number not verified]).
   - The case of several men who jointly killed one person: all are liable. Umar (RA) ruled so, saying that if all the people of San'a had joined in it he would have executed them all (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Blood Money, in a chapter heading report).
   - Cases settled by clever tests are told in the literature, such as the two women claiming one child. Label these "reported".
5. As Caliph he appeared before his own judge, Shurayh, as an ordinary litigant (the well-known report of the coat of armour; its chain is weak).
6. His written instructions on choosing judges (Letter 53, below): choose the best of your people, one whom cases do not confuse, who does not persist in error, who examines doubts carefully, is not swayed by praise, and pay him enough to remove need.
7. Many of the rulings of the Kufan school of law trace back through his students.

## 6. Letters of Hazrat Umar (RA) and Hazrat Ali (RA)

Short note asked in 2024. A full question is possible.

### 6.1 Letters of Hazrat Umar (RA)

**(a) To Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (RA), governor and judge of Basra, on the administration of justice**

Preserved by al-Daraqutni, al-Bayhaqi and others; discussed at length by Ibn al-Qayyim in *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*. It is often called the first charter of judicial procedure in Islam. Main points:

1. Judging is a firm religious duty and an established practice.
2. Understand the case fully when it is brought; a right that is not enforced is useless.
3. Treat people equally in your court, in how you look at them and seat them, so that the noble has no hope of your favour and the weak does not despair of your justice.
4. The burden of proof is on the claimant; the oath is on the one who denies.
5. Compromise between Muslims is allowed, except a compromise that makes the lawful unlawful or the unlawful lawful.
6. If you decided something yesterday and today see the right more clearly, return to the right. Truth is ancient, and returning to it is better than persisting in error.
7. Use your understanding in matters not found in the Quran or Sunnah; know the similar cases and reason by analogy.
8. Give a claimant time to produce his evidence.
9. Muslims are acceptable witnesses for one another, except those punished for false accusation, known for false testimony, or suspect because of relationship.
10. Avoid anger, irritation and impatience with litigants.

*Significance:* equality before law, burden of proof, judicial review of one's own decision, analogy as a method, judicial temperament. Compare modern codes of judicial conduct.

**(b) To governors generally**

- "The most important of your affairs in my view is the prayer. Whoever guards it guards his religion; whoever neglects it will neglect other things even more." (Muwatta Malik, Book of the Times of Prayer, no. 6)
- A reported address to governors: I have not sent you as tyrants but as leaders to guide. Do not beat people and humiliate them; do not deny them their rights; do not shut your doors against them so that the strong devour the weak (Abu Yusuf; al-Tabari).
- Conditions of appointment: no fine horse, no fine flour, no fine clothes, no closed door.

**(c) To Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (RA), commander in Iraq**

Fear Allah in all conditions; fear your own sins more than the enemy, for Muslims are helped because of their enemy's disobedience to Allah; Allah has no kinship with anyone except through obedience; be gentle with the troops (al-Tabari).

**(d) To Amr ibn al-As (RA), governor of Egypt**

Correspondence on revenue, on relief for Madinah during the famine, and on holding his son to account.

**(e) To Abu Ubaydah (RA) and the commanders in Syria**

On consultation, justice to the conquered, and care of the soldiers.

### 6.2 Letters of Hazrat Ali (RA)

Collected in *Nahj al-Balaghah*, compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi (d. 406 AH / 1015 CE). The collection has no chains of narration; many items are found in earlier histories. The letter to Malik al-Ashtar is widely accepted as a classic of governance by Sunni and Shia scholars alike. Letter numbers follow the common editions.

**(a) To Malik al-Ashtar, on appointing him governor of Egypt (Letter 53)**

The longest and most complete. Main points:

1. **Fear Allah and control yourself.** Rule your own desires first.
2. **Mercy to the people.** "Fill your heart with mercy, love and kindness for your subjects. Do not be like a ravenous beast over them. They are of two kinds: either your brother in religion or your equal in creation."
3. **Forgive as you hope Allah will forgive you.** Do not be proud of punishing or quick to anger.
4. **Justice even against yourself, your family and your favourites.**
5. **Seek the satisfaction of the common people** above that of the privileged few. The few are the heaviest burden in ease and the least help in hardship.
6. **Keep away those who search for people's faults.** Cover what should be covered.
7. **Advisers:** do not take the miser, the coward or the greedy as counsellor. Do not keep the ministers of former tyrants.
8. **Classes of society depend on one another:** soldiers, scribes and secretaries, judges, administrators, tax-payers, traders and craftsmen, and the poor. Give each its due.
9. **The army:** appoint commanders who are sincere, restrained and kind to the weak.
10. **Judges:** choose the best; pay them well; give them a rank near you so that none can influence them; review their decisions.
11. **Officials:** appoint after testing, not from favouritism; choose people of experience and modesty from good families of early service; pay them generously so they need not touch public money; keep watch over them through truthful observers; punish proven breach of trust.
12. **Revenue:** care more for the cultivation of the land than for collecting the tax. Revenue comes only from prosperity; whoever demands tax without developing the land ruins the country. Lighten the burden when crops fail.
13. **Secretaries:** choose the best for confidential work; judge them by their record, not by your impression.
14. **Traders and craftsmen:** protect them; prevent hoarding and overcharging; ensure fair weights.
15. **The poor, orphans, the aged, the disabled:** "Fear Allah regarding the lowest class." Reserve a share of the treasury for them; appoint someone to bring their cases to you.
16. **Open audience:** set a time to meet complainants in person, without guards, so they can speak without fear.
17. **Do not stay long in seclusion** from the people; it breeds ignorance of affairs and suspicion.
18. **Do not favour relatives and associates** with land or privileges.
19. **Keep treaties and promises,** even with an enemy; accept an honourable peace but stay alert after it.
20. **Never shed blood unlawfully;** nothing brings ruin faster.
21. **Avoid self-admiration, love of praise, haste, and reminding people of your favours.**

*Significance:* a complete manual of public administration: ethics of the ruler, human resource policy, judiciary, revenue policy focused on growth, social protection, access, anti-corruption and intelligence oversight. Commentators note its relevance to modern ideas of good governance.

**(b) To Uthman ibn Hunayf, governor of Basra (Letter 45)**

A rebuke for attending a rich man's banquet where the poor were not invited. A governor must live simply and share the condition of the poorest.

**(c) To Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, governor of Egypt (Letter 27)**

Be humble and gentle; treat people equally even in glances, so that the great do not expect your partiality and the weak do not despair of your justice.

**(d) To tax and zakat collectors (Letters 25 and 51)**

Approach people with courtesy; take only what is due; do not frighten them, enter their property without leave, sell their winter or summer clothing or work animals for tax, or flog anyone for a coin.

**(e) To his son (Letter 31)**

Moral counsel: make yourself the measure in dealing with others; want for them what you want for yourself.

### 6.3 Using the letters in other answers

Quote Letter 53 for civil servants, accountability, human rights, minorities ("equal in creation"), revenue policy and social justice. Quote Umar's letter to Abu Musa for the judicial system, rule of law and qiyas.

## 7. Responsibilities of civil servants (asked 2016, 2018, 2023 Special)

**Status.** A civil servant is a holder of a trust on behalf of the public and of Allah. The Arabic terms are *amil* (functionary), *wali* (one in charge) and *amin* (trustee).

**Qualities required:** competence and integrity ("strong and trustworthy", Al-Qasas 28:26); knowledge and care ("a knowing guardian", Yusuf 12:55); God-consciousness; justice; humility.

**Responsibilities**

| 1 | **Treat office as a trust** | An-Nisa 4:58; Al-Anfal 8:27; Sahih Muslim 1825 |

| 3 | **Honesty with public money** | "Whoever we appoint to a task and he hides from us a needle or more, it is embezzlement that he will bring on the Day of Resurrection." (Sahih Muslim 1833) |
| 4 | **No bribes or gifts** | Sunan Abi Dawud 3580; the Ibn al-Lutbiyyah case (Sahih al-Bukhari 7174) |
| 5 | **Merit in appointments and promotions under one's control** | An-Nisa 4:58; Sahih al-Bukhari 59 |
| 6 | **Efficiency and excellence** | Sahih Muslim 1955. A saying that Allah loves that when one of you does a job he does it well is reported by al-Bayhaqi and al-Tabarani [not in the six books; grading discussed]. |
| 7 | **Accessibility and responsiveness** | Sunan Abi Dawud 2948; Ali's (RA) instruction on open audience |
| 8 | **Kindness and ease for the public** | Sahih Muslim 1828; Sahih al-Bukhari 3038 |

**Rights of civil servants (mention briefly):** adequate pay (Sunan Abi Dawud 2945); security; respect; protection from unlawful orders; fair hearing.

**Pakistan:** the Quaid-e-Azam told civil officers (Peshawar, April 1948) to serve as servants of the people and the state without fear or favour and to stay out of politics. Present problems: politicisation, corruption, red tape, inaccessibility, colonial attitudes, weak accountability. Reforms: merit-based recruitment and postings; fixed tenures; ethics training built on the Seerah and the two letters; asset declarations; digitisation; citizen charters; performance measures; protection for honest officers.

**Outline:** definition and status → qualities → 12–15 responsibilities with references → examples from the Prophet's (PBUH) officers and Umar's (RA) governors → Pakistan's civil service and reforms → conclusion with Sahih al-Bukhari 7138.

## 8. System of accountability (hisbah) in Islam

Asked 2021, 2023 Special, 2024, 2026. Full answer: MA-13.

### 8.1 Meaning

- *Muhasabah / ihtisab:* calling to account.
- *Hisbah:* al-Mawardi's definition: enjoining what is right when it is clearly being neglected and forbidding what is wrong when it is clearly being done. As an institution, it is the office (the *muhtasib*) that supervises markets, public morals and public interest.
- Accountability in Islam is wider than hisbah: everyone who holds any charge is answerable.

### 8.2 Basis

- "We shall surely question those to whom messengers were sent, and We shall question the messengers." (Al-A'raf 7:6). So even prophets are questioned.
- "By your Lord, We shall question them all about what they used to do." (Al-Hijr 15:92–93)
- "Stop them; they are to be questioned." (As-Saffat 37:24)
- "Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it." (Az-Zalzalah 99:7–8)
- "Each of you is a shepherd ..." (Sahih al-Bukhari 7138)
- The duty to enjoin good and forbid evil (Al Imran 3:104, 3:110; At-Tawbah 9:71). "Whoever of you sees a wrong, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; if he cannot, then with his heart." (Sahih Muslim 49)
- Warning against silence: if people see a wrongdoer and do not stop him, Allah may soon include them all in punishment (Sunan Abi Dawud 4338); the parable of the ship (Sahih al-Bukhari 2493).

### 8.3 Levels of accountability

| Level | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **1. Before Allah** | The final court; nothing is hidden. This is the strongest deterrent. |
| **2. Self-accountability** | Conscience and taqwa (Al-Hashr 59:18) |
| **3. Before the people** | The right and duty to question rulers; freedom of expression |
| **4. Before the law and the courts** | The ruler has no immunity |
| **5. Institutional** | The muhtasib, the grievance court, audit of officials |
| **6. By the consultative body** | Those who appoint can question and remove |

### 8.4 Principles

1. **No one is above accountability,** including the head of state.
2. **Start at the top and with one's own.** The Prophet (PBUH) cancelled his own family's claims first; he refused Usamah's (RA) intercession for a noble thief (Sahih al-Bukhari 3475).
3. **Office is a trust; gifts to officials are suspect** (Ibn al-Lutbiyyah).
4. **Justice and due process:** proof lies on the accuser (Jami at-Tirmidhi 1341); hear both sides (Sunan Abi Dawud 3582); no spying on private life without cause (Al-Hujurat 49:12).
5. **No vengeance or selectivity** (Al-Ma'idah 5:8).
6. **Transparency:** rulers explain their conduct in public.
7. **Prevention before punishment:** good pay, careful selection, moral training.
8. **Recovery of public wealth** wrongly taken.
9. **Speed.**

### 8.5 The Prophet's (PBUH) practice

- Checked the market himself; put his hand in a heap of grain, found it wet underneath and said: "Whoever cheats is not one of us." (Sahih Muslim 102)
- Audited collectors (Ibn al-Lutbiyyah).
- Offered himself for accountability before his death, according to the Seerah accounts.
- Appointed supervisors over markets in Makkah and Madinah, as the historians report.

### 8.6 The Rightly Guided Caliphs as the highest standard (asked 2026)

**Abu Bakr (RA)**

1. Invited correction in his first address.
2. Kept his allowance minimal and had it repaid at death.
3. Held commanders to rules of war.

**Umar (RA)**

1. **Self:** his saying that he feared being asked about an animal that stumbled or died on a far road (history works; wording varies). He asked Salman (RA) whether he was a king or a caliph.
2. **Public questioning:** the report of the man who challenged him over the length of his cloth and was answered through his son; the report of a woman correcting him on dowers. These are well-known reports whose chains are discussed; cite them as "reported". He said there is no good in people who do not speak out and no good in a ruler who does not listen (reported).
3. **Officials:** inventory of wealth on appointment; half of unexplained increase taken into the treasury (cases reported of Abu Hurayrah, Amr ibn al-As and others, may Allah be pleased with them); complaints court at Hajj; Muhammad ibn Maslamah as investigating officer; Khalid ibn al-Walid (RA) removed from command so that people would not rely on a man instead of Allah; Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (RA) investigated on a complaint from Kufa and cleared, yet replaced.
4. **Equality:** the Copt and the governor's son.
5. **Family:** forbade his own household what he forbade the people, with double penalty for them, as reported.
6. **His dying concern:** he asked that his debts to the treasury be paid from his family's property (Sahih al-Bukhari 3700) and hoped only to come out even, with nothing for or against him (Sahih Muslim 1823).

**Uthman (RA)**

1. Heard delegations of complaint and wrote to the provinces inviting anyone wronged to come at Hajj, as the histories record.
2. Refused to use the army against protesters at the cost of his own life.

**Ali (RA)**

1. Appeared before his own judge.
2. Wrote detailed rules for monitoring officials (Letter 53) and rebuked a governor for a banquet (Letter 45).
3. Refused special shares from the treasury even to close relatives, as reported in the histories.

**Why this is the highest standard:** the rulers held absolute political power over a vast state, yet accepted questioning from ordinary people, lived more simply than their subjects, applied the law to their own families first, and feared Allah's court most. Accountability was internal and external together.

### 8.7 Institutions in Muslim history

| Institution | Function |
|---|---|
| **Hisbah (muhtasib)** | Weights and measures, prices and hoarding, quality of goods, cleanliness of streets and markets, safe buildings, public morals, treatment of animals and workers. Summary powers for visible, undisputed wrongs. |
| **Qada (judiciary)** | Disputes needing evidence and hearing |
| **Mazalim (grievance court)** | Complaints against powerful officials and nobles; chaired by the Caliph or a high judge with executive force. Forerunner of the administrative court and the ombudsman. |
| **Diwan al-zimam** (Abbasid) | Financial audit |

### 8.8 Pakistan's accountability system and how to reform it on the Prophet's (PBUH) teachings (asked 2024)

**Institutions:** National Accountability Bureau (1999 Ordinance); Federal Investigation Agency; provincial anti-corruption establishments; Auditor General; Public Accounts Committees; Wafaqi Mohtasib (federal ombudsman, 1983) and provincial ombudsmen; Federal Tax Ombudsman; the courts; Election Commission asset returns; Right of Access to Information Act 2017.

**Weaknesses:** selective and politically timed cases; long detentions without conviction; plea bargains seen as buying freedom; low conviction rates; overlapping bodies; exemptions for some classes; weak internal controls; public cynicism.

**Reforms drawn from the Seerah**

1. **Across the board:** no sacred cows (the Makhzumi case).
2. **Begin with the powerful and with oneself.**
3. **Independence** of accountability bodies from the government of the day (the judge's independence under Umar (RA)).
4. **Due process:** evidence, hearing, presumption of innocence.
5. **Asset declaration and audit** on entry, during and after service (Umar's (RA) practice).
6. **Gifts and conflict of interest rules** (Ibn al-Lutbiyyah).
7. **Recovery** of looted wealth to the treasury.
8. **Speedy trial.**
9. **Prevention:** merit, adequate pay, digitised procedures to remove discretion.
10. **Open government:** information, complaint portals, strong ombudsman (modern mazalim).
11. **Protection of whistle-blowers** (speaking truth as the best jihad).
12. **Moral training** to restore the inner check.
13. **Free press and public opinion** within the limits of truth (Al-Hujurat 49:6).
