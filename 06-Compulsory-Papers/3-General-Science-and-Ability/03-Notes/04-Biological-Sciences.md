# Part 1: The basis of life

## §1 The cell

**Definition.** The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living things.

- Robert Hooke first saw cells (in cork) in 1665.
- **Cell theory** (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow): (1) all living things are made of cells; (2) the cell is the basic unit of life; (3) all cells come from existing cells.

| Point | Prokaryotic cell | Eukaryotic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | None; DNA lies free (nucleoid) | True nucleus with a membrane |
| Organelles with membranes | None | Present (mitochondria, ER, Golgi) |
| Ribosomes | Small (70S) | Larger (80S) |
| DNA | Circular, plus plasmids | Linear chromosomes |
| Size | 1–10 micrometres | 10–100 micrometres |
| Division | Binary fission | Mitosis and meiosis |
| Examples | Bacteria, blue-green algae | Plants, animals, fungi, protists |

### Organelles and their functions

| Organelle | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cell membrane** | Double layer of phospholipids with proteins (fluid mosaic model) | Controls what enters and leaves; selectively permeable |
| **Cell wall** (plants, fungi, bacteria) | Rigid outer layer; cellulose in plants | Shape, support, protection |
| **Nucleus** | Double membrane (nuclear envelope) with pores; contains chromatin (DNA) and a nucleolus | Control centre; stores genetic information; directs protein making and cell division. The nucleolus makes ribosomes. |
| **Mitochondria** | Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae; fluid matrix; own DNA and ribosomes | "Powerhouse": aerobic respiration releases energy as ATP |
| **Ribosomes** | Tiny grains of RNA and protein; no membrane; two sub-units; free or on rough ER | Protein synthesis (join amino acids in the order set by mRNA) |
| Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) | Network of membranes. Rough ER carries ribosomes; smooth ER does not | Rough: makes and moves proteins. Smooth: makes lipids, detoxifies |
| Golgi apparatus | Stack of flattened sacs | Modifies, packs and ships proteins; makes lysosomes |
| Lysosomes | Small sacs of digestive enzymes | Break down worn-out parts and germs ("suicide bags") |
| Chloroplasts (plants) | Double membrane; stacks of thylakoids (grana); chlorophyll; own DNA | Photosynthesis |
| Vacuole | Large and central in plants; small in animals | Stores water, salts, wastes; keeps plant cells firm |
| Centrioles (animals) | Pair of short cylinders | Form the spindle in cell division |
| Cytoplasm | Jelly-like fluid | Site of many reactions; holds organelles |

Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs) in each body cell.

**Bacterial cell vs plant cell (asked 2023)**

|---|---|---|
| Type | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| Nucleus | None; nucleoid | True nucleus |
| Organelles | No mitochondria, chloroplasts, ER | All present |

| Extra DNA | Plasmids | None |

| Other | May have flagella, capsule, pili | Large vacuole |

**Cell wall vs cell membrane (asked 2021)**

| Point | Cell wall | Cell membrane |
|---|---|---|
| Found in | Plants, fungi, bacteria | All cells |
| Position | Outermost | Inside the wall; outermost in animal cells |
| Made of | Cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi), peptidoglycan (bacteria) | Phospholipids and proteins |
| Nature | Rigid, thick, non-living | Thin, flexible, living |
| Permeability | Fully permeable | Selectively permeable |
| Function | Shape, strength, stops bursting | Controls transport, cell signalling |

**Cells, tissues, organs (asked 2021)**

```
Cell -> Tissue -> Organ -> Organ system -> Organism
muscle cell -> cardiac muscle tissue -> heart -> circulatory system -> human
palisade cell -> mesophyll tissue -> leaf -> shoot system -> plant
```
- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells doing one job. Animal tissues: epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous. Plant tissues: meristematic, xylem, phloem, epidermis.
- An **organ** is several tissues working together (stomach: muscle, epithelium, nerve, blood).
- An **organ system** is a group of organs with one overall function.

**Diagrams to learn:** (1) animal cell: round outline, central nucleus with nucleolus, sausage-shaped mitochondria with inner folds, dotted rough ER, Golgi stack. (2) plant cell: box outline with double line (wall + membrane), large central vacuole, green oval chloroplasts, nucleus at the side. (3) mitochondrion: oval, outer membrane smooth, inner membrane folded (cristae), matrix.

## §2 Proteins

**Definition.** Large molecules made of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Elements: C, H, O, N, often S. There are 20 amino acids; 9 are essential (must come from food).

**Levels of structure**

| Level | Meaning | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Order of amino acids in the chain (MCQ 2026) | Peptide bonds |
| Secondary | Local folding: alpha helix, beta sheet | Hydrogen bonds |
| Tertiary | Overall 3-D shape of one chain | Disulphide bridges, ionic and hydrogen bonds |
| Quaternary | Several chains joined | Same forces. Example: haemoglobin (4 chains) |

**Functions**

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Structure | Collagen (skin, bone), keratin (hair, nails) |
| Enzymes | Amylase, pepsin |
| Hormones | Insulin |
| Transport | Haemoglobin (oxygen) |
| Defence | Antibodies |
| Movement | Actin and myosin in muscle |
| Storage | Albumin (egg), casein (milk) |
| Energy | 4 kcal per gram (used last) |

- Heat, acid or alkali change a protein's shape and stop it working: **denaturation** (a boiled egg).
- Sources: meat, fish, eggs, milk, pulses, nuts. Need: about 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day.
- Lack of protein in children: **kwashiorkor** (swollen belly, thin limbs).

## §3 Lipids

**Definition.** Fats, oils and related compounds. Made of C, H and a little O. Do not dissolve in water; dissolve in organic solvents.

**Structural diversity (asked 2026)**

| Class | Structure | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Fatty acids | Hydrocarbon chain with an acid group. **Saturated:** no double bonds, straight chain. **Unsaturated:** one or more double bonds, which bend the chain. | Building blocks; fuel |
| Triglycerides (fats and oils) | Glycerol + 3 fatty acids | Main energy store (9 kcal/g); insulation; padding. Main storage lipid in animals (MCQ 2026). |
| Phospholipids | Glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate head. Head loves water; tails avoid it. | Form the double layer of all cell membranes |
| Steroids | Four fused carbon rings | Cholesterol; sex hormones; cortisol; vitamin D; bile salts |
| Waxes | Long fatty acid + long alcohol | Waterproofing (leaf cuticle, beeswax, earwax) |
| Others | Glycolipids, sphingolipids | Cell recognition; myelin sheath of nerves |

Saturated fats (butter, ghee, animal fat) are solid at room temperature. Unsaturated fats (olive, sunflower, fish oils) are liquid. Trans fats (from partial hydrogenation, as in banaspati ghee) are the most harmful to the heart.

**Fatty acids and membrane fluidity**
- Straight saturated tails pack tightly. The membrane becomes stiff.
- Bent unsaturated tails cannot pack tightly. The membrane becomes more fluid.
- Shorter chains also raise fluidity.
- Cholesterol acts as a buffer: it stiffens the membrane when warm and stops it freezing when cold.
- Organisms adjust: fish and bacteria in cold water put more unsaturated fatty acids in their membranes.
- A membrane must be fluid enough for proteins to move, for transport and for cell division.

**Fatty acids and cell signalling**
- Membrane phospholipids are split by enzymes into messenger molecules (for example IP₃ and DAG) that carry a signal from a receptor into the cell.
- Arachidonic acid (an omega-6 fatty acid) is released from the membrane and turned into prostaglandins, which drive inflammation, pain and fever. Omega-3 fatty acids give less inflammatory products.
- Patches rich in cholesterol and saturated lipids ("lipid rafts") gather receptors together.
- Steroid hormones are lipids. They pass through the membrane and act on receptors inside the cell.

**Cholesterol (asked 2016, 2019)**
- A waxy steroid. The liver makes most of it; the rest comes from animal foods.
- Needed for: cell membranes, steroid hormones, vitamin D, bile salts.
- Carried in blood by lipoproteins: **LDL** ("bad", deposits in artery walls) and **HDL** ("good", carries it back to the liver).

| Blood level (mg/dL) | Desirable |
|---|---|
| Total cholesterol | Under 200 (high: 240 or more) |
| LDL | Under 100 |
| HDL | Over 40 in men, over 50 in women |
| Triglycerides | Under 150 |

- Danger: excess LDL builds fatty plaques in arteries (**atherosclerosis**). Arteries narrow and harden. Result: high blood pressure, angina, heart attack, stroke.

## §4 Carbohydrates (asked 2018, 2020, 2024)

**Definition.** Compounds of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, with hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio 2:1 as in water. General formula (CH₂O)n. The body's main and quickest source of energy (4 kcal/g).

**Classification**

| Class | Units | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharides (simple sugars) | 1 | Glucose (blood sugar), fructose (fruit), galactose; ribose and deoxyribose (in RNA and DNA) |
| Disaccharides | 2 | Sucrose = glucose + fructose (table sugar). Lactose = glucose + galactose (milk). Maltose = glucose + glucose (malt). |
| Oligosaccharides | 3–10 | Raffinose (beans) |
| Polysaccharides | Hundreds to thousands | **Starch** (plant store). **Glycogen** (animal store in liver and muscle). **Cellulose** (plant cell wall; dietary fibre). **Chitin** (insect shells, fungal walls). |

**Roles in the body:** immediate energy (glucose is the brain's main fuel); stored energy (glycogen); spares protein from being burned; fibre keeps the bowel working; part of DNA, RNA and cell-surface markers.

Tests: Benedict's solution turns brick-red with reducing sugars; iodine turns blue-black with starch.
Sources: cereals, rice, bread, potatoes, sugar, fruit. Excess is stored as fat.

## §5 Enzymes

**Definition.** Biological catalysts, almost all proteins, that speed up reactions in living cells without being used up. They work by lowering the activation energy.

**Properties**
1. **Specific.** Each enzyme acts on one substrate. The substrate fits the enzyme's **active site**.
   - Lock-and-key model: fixed shapes fit exactly.
   - Induced-fit model: the active site adjusts around the substrate.
2. Needed in very small amounts and can be reused.
3. **Temperature.** Rate rises with warmth up to an optimum (about 37 °C in humans). Higher heat denatures the enzyme.
4. **pH.** Each has an optimum: pepsin about 2, salivary amylase about 7, trypsin about 8.
5. Rate also depends on substrate and enzyme concentration.
6. **Inhibitors** slow them. Competitive inhibitors block the active site; non-competitive ones change its shape.
7. Many need helpers: **cofactors** (metal ions such as zinc, iron) or **coenzymes** (made from vitamins).

```
Enzyme + Substrate -> Enzyme–substrate complex -> Enzyme + Product
```

| Enzyme | Action |
|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch → maltose |
| Pepsin, trypsin | Proteins → peptides |
| Lipase | Fats → fatty acids + glycerol |
| Lactase | Lactose → glucose + galactose |
| Catalase | Hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen |
| DNA polymerase | Builds DNA |

Uses: biological washing powders, cheese making, baking and brewing, fruit-juice clearing, medicines, DNA testing (PCR), making biofuel from cellulose.

**Diagram:** an enzyme drawn as a blob with a notch (active site); a matching substrate shape fitting in; then two product pieces leaving and the enzyme unchanged.

## §6 Nucleic acids: DNA and RNA (asked 2022)

Nucleic acids are chains of **nucleotides**. Each nucleotide = a sugar + a phosphate + a nitrogen base.

| Point | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Deoxyribonucleic acid | Ribonucleic acid |
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Bases | Adenine, guanine, cytosine, **thymine** | Adenine, guanine, cytosine, **uracil** |
| Strands | Two, twisted in a double helix | One |
| Place | Nucleus (a little in mitochondria and chloroplasts) | Nucleus and cytoplasm |
| Job | Stores and passes on genetic information | Carries the message and builds proteins |
| Types | — | mRNA (message), tRNA (brings amino acids), rRNA (in ribosomes) |
| Stability | Stable | Short-lived |

- Base pairing in DNA: A with T, G with C, held by hydrogen bonds.
- Double-helix model: Watson and Crick, 1953, using X-ray images by Rosalind Franklin.
- **Central dogma:** DNA → (transcription) → RNA → (translation) → protein.
- A **gene** is a length of DNA that codes for one protein. The human genome has about 3 billion base pairs and about 20,000 genes.

# Part 2: Plant and animal kingdom

## §7 Classification of living things

- Carl Linnaeus began modern classification and two-part naming (**binomial nomenclature**): *Homo sapiens*.
- Ranks: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species.
- About 1.5–2 million species are named. The total may be near 9 million.

**Five kingdoms (Whittaker, 1969)**

| Kingdom | Cell type | Nutrition | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monera | Prokaryotic, one-celled | Varied | Bacteria |
| Protista | Eukaryotic, mostly one-celled | Varied | Amoeba, Paramecium, algae |
| Fungi | Eukaryotic; chitin walls | Absorb food from dead or living matter | Yeast, mushrooms, moulds |
| Plantae | Eukaryotic, many-celled; cellulose walls | Make own food (autotrophic) | Mosses, ferns, pines, flowering plants |
| Animalia | Eukaryotic, many-celled; no walls | Eat other organisms (heterotrophic) | Insects, fish, birds, mammals |

Viruses are not cells. They multiply only inside living cells and sit on the border of living and non-living.

## §8 Plant kingdom

**Features:** many-celled, eukaryotic, cellulose cell walls, chlorophyll, make food by photosynthesis, fixed in place, grow throughout life.

| Group | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Bryophytes | No true roots or vessels; small; need water to reproduce; spores | Mosses, liverworts |
| Pteridophytes | Vessels (xylem, phloem); true roots, stems, leaves; spores, no seeds | Ferns |
| Gymnosperms | Seeds "naked" in cones; needle leaves; no flowers | Pine, deodar, juniper |
| Angiosperms | Flowers; seeds enclosed in fruit; the largest group | Wheat, mango, rose |

Angiosperms: **monocots** (one seed leaf, parallel veins, fibrous roots; wheat, rice, maize) and **dicots** (two seed leaves, net veins, tap root; pea, cotton, mango).

### Photosynthesis

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (using light energy and chlorophyll)

- Takes place in chloroplasts.
- Light stage (in the thylakoids): light splits water, oxygen is released, ATP is made.
- Dark stage, the Calvin cycle (in the stroma): CO₂ is built into glucose.

**Factors affecting the rate (asked 2025)**

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Light intensity** | Rate rises in step with light at first. Then it levels off (light saturation) because something else, such as CO₂ or temperature, becomes limiting. Very strong light can damage chlorophyll. |
| **Temperature** | The dark stage is run by enzymes. Rate rises with temperature up to an optimum (about 25–35 °C for most plants), then falls quickly as enzymes denature and stomata close. |
| CO₂ concentration | More CO₂ raises the rate up to a limit. |
| Water, chlorophyll, minerals (Mg, N) | Shortage lowers the rate. |

The slowest factor sets the pace: the **law of limiting factors**.

**Graphs to sketch:** (1) rate against light intensity: a straight rise that bends into a flat plateau. (2) rate against temperature: a hill that rises to a peak near 30 °C and drops steeply after.

### Transpiration (asked 2021)

**Definition.** Loss of water vapour from the above-ground parts of a plant, mainly through the stomata of leaves.

- **Stomata** are tiny pores, mostly on the lower leaf surface. Each is bordered by two bean-shaped **guard cells**.
- In light, guard cells take up water and swell. Their thick inner walls make them curve apart, and the pore opens.
- In darkness or drought they lose water, go limp, and the pore closes.
- Leaf structure helps: thin, broad leaves give a large surface; air spaces in the spongy layer let vapour move to the pores; the waxy cuticle limits other loss.
- Importance: pulls water and minerals up the xylem (transpiration pull); cools the leaf; lets CO₂ in.
- Rate rises with light, heat, wind and dry air. It falls in humid air or when soil water is short.

**Diagram:** two bean-shaped guard cells facing each other with a gap "stoma (open)"; beside it the same cells straight and touching "closed".

## §9 Animal kingdom

**Features:** many-celled, eukaryotic, no cell wall, feed on other organisms, most can move, have nerves and muscles, growth stops at adult size.

**Invertebrates (no backbone; over 95% of animal species)**

| Phylum | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Porifera | Pores; simplest animals | Sponges |
| Cnidaria | Stinging cells; radial body | Jellyfish, corals |
| Platyhelminthes | Flatworms | Tapeworm, liver fluke |
| Nematoda | Roundworms | Ascaris, hookworm |
| Annelida | Segmented worms | Earthworm, leech |
| Mollusca | Soft body, often a shell | Snail, octopus |
| Arthropoda | Jointed legs, outer skeleton; the largest phylum | Insects, spiders, crabs |
| Echinodermata | Spiny skin; sea-living | Starfish |

**Vertebrates (backbone)**

| Class | Body covering | Breathing | Blood temperature | Heart chambers | Young | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fish | Scales | Gills | Cold-blooded | 2 | Eggs in water | Trout, shark |
| Amphibians | Moist skin | Gills, then lungs and skin | Cold-blooded | 3 | Eggs in water | Frog, toad |
| Reptiles | Dry scales | Lungs | Cold-blooded | 3 (crocodiles 4) | Shelled eggs on land | Snake, lizard |
| Birds | Feathers | Lungs | Warm-blooded | 4 | Hard-shelled eggs | Sparrow, eagle |
| Mammals | Hair or fur | Lungs | Warm-blooded | 4 | Born live, fed on milk | Human, cow, whale |

**Why bats and whales are mammals (asked 2023)**
1. They give birth to live young.
2. Mothers feed the young on milk from mammary glands.
3. They have hair (bats have fur; whales have a few bristles).
4. They are warm-blooded.
5. They breathe air with lungs. A whale must surface and breathes through a blowhole.
6. They have a four-chambered heart and a diaphragm.
- A bat's wing is skin stretched over finger bones, not feathers. A whale's flipper has the same bones as a human arm.

**Egg-laying mammals (asked 2019).** Yes. They are the **monotremes**: the duck-billed platypus and the echidnas (spiny anteaters). They live in Australia (including Tasmania) and New Guinea. They lay leathery eggs, but the young drink milk and the animals have hair, so they are mammals. The other two mammal groups are marsupials (pouch; kangaroo) and placental mammals.

**Cold-blooded vs warm-blooded (asked 2023)**

| Point | Cold-blooded (ectotherm) | Warm-blooded (endotherm) |
|---|---|---|
| Body temperature | Changes with surroundings | Kept constant |
| Heat source | Outside (sun, warm ground) | Inside (metabolism) |
| Food need | Low | High |
| Examples | Fish, frogs, reptiles, insects | Birds, mammals |

Why cold slows cold-blooded animals: their body cools with the air. Enzymes work more slowly at low temperature, so respiration releases less energy. Muscles and nerves slow down, and the animal becomes sluggish or hibernates.

### Similarities and diversities between plants and animals

**Similarities:** both are living and made of eukaryotic cells with a nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes; both store information in DNA with the same genetic code; both respire, grow, reproduce, excrete and respond to stimuli; both need water and minerals; both depend on each other (oxygen–carbon dioxide cycle, food chains, pollination).

| Point | Plants | Animals |
|---|---|---|
| Nutrition | Make their own food | Eat ready-made food |
| Cell wall, chloroplasts | Present | Absent |
| Movement | Fixed; only parts move | Move from place to place |
| Growth | Throughout life, at tips | Stops at maturity; all over the body |
| Food store | Starch | Glycogen and fat |
| Response | Slow; by hormones | Fast; by nerves and hormones |
| Gas exchange | Stomata | Lungs, gills, skin |
| Excretion | No special organs | Kidneys and others |
| Role in ecosystem | Producers | Consumers |

# Part 3: Human physiology

## §10 Digestive system

**Path of food:** mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine (colon, rectum) → anus.
**Helper organs:** salivary glands, liver, gall bladder, pancreas.
**Stages:** ingestion → digestion → absorption → assimilation → egestion.

**Chemical digestion (asked 2018, 2023)**

| Place | Juice and enzyme | Carbohydrates | Proteins | Fats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mouth | Saliva: salivary amylase | Starch → maltose | — | — |
| Stomach | Gastric juice: hydrochloric acid (pH about 2), pepsin | — (amylase stops) | Proteins → peptides | Very little |
| Duodenum | **Bile** (liver): no enzyme | — | — | Emulsified into droplets |
| Duodenum | Pancreatic juice: amylase, trypsin, lipase | Starch → maltose | Proteins → peptides | Fats → fatty acids + glycerol |
| Small intestine | Intestinal juice: maltase, sucrase, lactase, peptidases | Maltose, sucrose, lactose → glucose, fructose, galactose | Peptides → amino acids | Finished by lipase |
| **End products** | | **Simple sugars** | **Amino acids** | **Fatty acids + glycerol** |

**Absorption.** The ileum wall has millions of finger-like **villi** that give a huge surface. Sugars and amino acids pass into blood capillaries and go to the liver through the hepatic portal vein. Fatty acids and glycerol pass into lymph vessels (lacteals). The large intestine absorbs water. Its bacteria make vitamin K.

**Liver: "chief chemist of the body" (asked 2016, 2024).** The largest gland (about 1.5 kg), in the upper right abdomen.

1. Makes **bile**.
2. Controls blood sugar: stores extra glucose as glycogen and releases it when needed.
3. Processes fats; makes cholesterol and lipoproteins.
4. Breaks down surplus amino acids and turns the toxic ammonia into **urea**.
5. Makes blood proteins: albumin and clotting factors (fibrinogen, prothrombin).
6. **Detoxifies** alcohol, drugs and poisons.
7. Stores iron and vitamins A, D, E, K and B12.
8. Breaks down old red blood cells; excretes bilirubin.
9. Produces heat; helps defence (cells that engulf germs).
It can regrow after damage.

**Bile (asked 2023).** A greenish-yellow, alkaline fluid made by the liver, stored and concentrated in the gall bladder, and released into the duodenum. It contains water, bile salts, bile pigments (bilirubin), cholesterol and bicarbonate. It has **no enzymes**.
Functions: breaks fat into tiny droplets (emulsification) so lipase can act; neutralises stomach acid; helps absorb fats and vitamins A, D, E, K; carries away bilirubin and excess cholesterol; colours the stool. A blocked bile duct causes jaundice.

**Pancreas (asked 2024).** A double gland behind the stomach.
- Exocrine part: pancreatic juice (amylase, trypsin, lipase and bicarbonate) flows through a duct into the duodenum.
- Endocrine part (islets of Langerhans): **insulin** lowers blood glucose; **glucagon** raises it. Lack of insulin action causes diabetes mellitus.

**Diagram:** outline of the gut with labels: mouth, oesophagus, stomach (J-shaped), liver (large, right side) with gall bladder, pancreas (behind stomach), coiled small intestine, large intestine framing it, rectum, anus.

## §11 Circulatory system

Parts: heart, blood vessels, blood.

**Heart.** A hollow, muscular pump the size of a fist, in the chest between the lungs.
- Four chambers: two **atria** (upper, receive blood) and two **ventricles** (lower, pump blood). A wall (septum) separates right from left.
- Valves stop backflow: tricuspid (right), bicuspid or mitral (left), and semilunar valves at the pulmonary artery and aorta.
- The left ventricle has the thickest wall because it pumps to the whole body.
- The beat starts at the SA node, the natural pacemaker. Normal rate: about 72 beats a minute. Normal blood pressure: 120/80 mmHg.
- The heart muscle is fed by the coronary arteries.

**Path of blood**
```
Body -> venae cavae -> RIGHT ATRIUM -> RIGHT VENTRICLE -> pulmonary artery -> LUNGS
LUNGS -> pulmonary veins -> LEFT ATRIUM -> LEFT VENTRICLE -> aorta -> Body
```

**Double circulation (asked 2021).** Blood passes through the heart twice in one full circuit.
- Pulmonary circuit: heart → lungs → heart (picks up oxygen).
- Systemic circuit: heart → body → heart (delivers oxygen).
How the heart is adapted: four chambers and a septum keep oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood apart; the thick left ventricle gives high pressure for the body; the thinner right ventricle gives low pressure that protects the lungs; valves keep flow one-way; cardiac muscle does not tire.

**Blood vessels**

| Point | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | Away from heart | Towards heart | Links arteries to veins |
| Wall | Thick, elastic, muscular | Thin | One cell thick |
| Pressure | High | Low | Low, slow flow |
| Valves | None | Present | None |
| Blood | Oxygen-rich (except pulmonary artery) | Oxygen-poor (except pulmonary veins) | Changes along its length |

**Capillaries (asked 2025).** Structure: the smallest vessels, about 5–10 micrometres wide, so red cells pass in single file. The wall is a single layer of flat cells on a thin membrane, with no muscle. They form dense networks (capillary beds) with a very large total surface. Functions: exchange oxygen, glucose and hormones out to the tissues and carbon dioxide and wastes back into the blood, by diffusion; form tissue fluid by filtration; let white cells squeeze out to reach infections; help control body heat through the skin.

**Blood** (about 5 litres)

| Part | Share / count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma | 55%; mostly water | Carries food, wastes, hormones, heat |
| Red cells | About 5 million per mm³; no nucleus; contain haemoglobin; live 120 days | Carry oxygen |
| White cells | 4,000–11,000 per mm³ | Fight infection |
| Platelets | 150,000–400,000 per mm³ | Clotting |

Blood groups (ABO): A, B, AB (universal recipient), O (universal donor). Rh factor: positive or negative.

**Heart attack (asked 2017, 2019).** A coronary artery is blocked, usually by a clot on a fatty plaque. The part of the heart muscle it feeds dies.
Causes and risk factors: high LDL cholesterol and fatty diet, high blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, obesity, lack of exercise, stress, age, family history.
Signs: crushing chest pain spreading to the left arm or jaw, sweating, breathlessness, nausea.
Prevention: balanced diet, exercise, no smoking, control of blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol.

**Diagram:** heart in front view, four boxes: RA and RV on the left of the page, LA and LV on the right; thick wall on LV; arrows for flow; label aorta, pulmonary artery, venae cavae, pulmonary veins, valves, septum.

## §12 Respiratory system

**Path of air:** nose → pharynx → larynx (voice box) → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → **alveoli**.

- The lungs hold hundreds of millions of alveoli. Their walls are one cell thick, moist and wrapped in capillaries. Total surface: about 70 m².
- **Gas exchange** is by diffusion: oxygen into the blood, carbon dioxide out.
- **Breathing in:** the diaphragm contracts and flattens, ribs move up and out, chest volume rises, pressure falls, air enters. Breathing out is the reverse.

| Gas | Air breathed in | Air breathed out |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | 21% | 16% |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04% | 4% |
| Nitrogen | 78% | 78% |

- **Cellular respiration:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP). It happens in mitochondria.
- Without oxygen (anaerobic): muscles make lactic acid; yeast makes alcohol and CO₂.
- Normal rate: 12–18 breaths a minute.
- Diseases: asthma, tuberculosis, pneumonia, lung cancer, COVID-19.

## §13 Excretory system (asked 2023)

Parts: two kidneys, two ureters, urinary bladder, urethra.

**Kidney.** Bean-shaped, at the back of the abdomen. Outer cortex, inner medulla, central pelvis. Each has about a million filtering units called **nephrons**.

**Nephron parts:** glomerulus (a knot of capillaries) inside Bowman's capsule → proximal tubule → loop of Henle → distal tubule → collecting duct.

**How urine forms**
1. **Ultrafiltration.** Blood enters the glomerulus under high pressure. Water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea are forced into Bowman's capsule. Blood cells and proteins are too big and stay. About 180 litres of filtrate form each day.
2. **Selective reabsorption.** As filtrate moves along the tubule, all the glucose and amino acids, and most of the water and salts, go back into the blood. The loop of Henle and the collecting duct recover more water under the control of the hormone ADH.
3. **Tubular secretion.** Extra hydrogen and potassium ions and some drugs are added to the tubule.
Result: about 1.5 litres of **urine** a day (about 95% water, with urea, salts, uric acid and creatinine). It flows through the ureters to the bladder.

**Roles of the kidney:** remove nitrogen wastes (urea); control water and salt balance (osmoregulation); control blood pH; help control blood pressure (renin); make erythropoietin, which triggers red-cell production; activate vitamin D.
Kidney failure is treated by dialysis or transplant.

Other excretory organs: lungs (CO₂), skin (sweat), liver (bile pigments).

**Diagram:** a nephron: cup (Bowman's capsule) holding a ball of capillaries (glomerulus), then a coiled tube, a hairpin loop, another coil, and a collecting duct; arrows marked "filtration", "reabsorption", "secretion".

## §14 Nervous system

| Division | Parts | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Central nervous system | Brain, spinal cord | Control and processing |
| Peripheral nervous system | 12 pairs of cranial and 31 pairs of spinal nerves | Links CNS to the body |
| Autonomic (part of peripheral) | Sympathetic ("fight or flight"); parasympathetic ("rest and digest") | Involuntary control of organs |

- **Neuron:** nerve cell. Parts: dendrites (receive), cell body, axon (sends), myelin sheath (insulates, speeds the impulse). Neurons meet at **synapses**, where chemicals (neurotransmitters) carry the signal across.
- Types: sensory, relay, motor.

| Brain part | Function |
|---|---|
| Cerebrum | Thought, memory, speech, senses, voluntary movement |
| Cerebellum | Balance and coordination |
| Medulla oblongata | Heartbeat, breathing, swallowing |
| Hypothalamus | Body temperature, hunger, thirst; controls the pituitary |

**Reflex arc:** receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in spinal cord → motor neuron → effector (muscle). Fast, automatic, protective.

## §15 Endocrine system

Ductless glands release **hormones** (chemical messengers) straight into the blood.

| Gland | Hormone | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary ("master gland") | Growth hormone, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH; ADH, oxytocin | Growth; controls other glands; water balance |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine (needs iodine) | Metabolic rate. Lack of iodine causes goitre. |
| Parathyroid | Parathormone | Blood calcium |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline; cortisol; aldosterone | Emergency response; stress; salt balance |
| Pancreas (islets) | Insulin, glucagon | Blood sugar |
| Testes / ovaries | Testosterone / oestrogen, progesterone | Sexual development, reproduction |
| Pineal | Melatonin | Sleep cycle |

Nervous control is fast, electrical and brief. Hormonal control is slower, chemical and longer-lasting.

## §16 Skeletal and muscular systems

- An adult has **206 bones**. Longest: femur. Smallest: stapes (in the ear).
- Functions: support, protection (skull, ribs), movement, blood-cell production in red marrow, storage of calcium and phosphorus.
- **Joints:** fixed (skull), hinge (elbow, knee), ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip), pivot (neck).
- Ligaments join bone to bone. Tendons join muscle to bone. Cartilage cushions.
- **Muscles:** skeletal (voluntary, striped), smooth (involuntary; gut, vessels), cardiac (heart). Skeletal muscles work in opposing pairs (biceps and triceps).

## §17 Reproductive system (brief)

- Male: testes make sperm and testosterone.
- Female: ovaries release an egg about once every 28 days and make oestrogen and progesterone. Fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube; the embryo grows in the uterus, fed through the placenta. Pregnancy lasts about 40 weeks.
- Each parent gives 23 chromosomes. The sperm (X or Y) decides the sex of the child.

## §18 Sense organs

**Ear (asked 2017).** It serves hearing and balance.

| Part | Structures | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Outer ear | Pinna, ear canal, eardrum | Collects sound; the eardrum vibrates |
| Middle ear | Three tiny bones: hammer (malleus), anvil (incus), stirrup (stapes); Eustachian tube | Bones amplify vibrations and pass them to the oval window. The tube equalises pressure with the throat. |
| Inner ear | Cochlea; three semicircular canals; auditory nerve | Cochlea hair cells turn vibrations into nerve impulses. The canals sense movement and keep balance. |

Humans hear from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

**Diagram:** pinna and canal on the left, eardrum as a slanted line, three small linked bones, a snail-shaped cochlea, three loops above it, a tube running down to the throat.

**Eye.** Light passes cornea → pupil (in the iris) → lens → retina. The lens forms a real, inverted image on the retina. Rods work in dim light; cones see colour. The optic nerve carries impulses to the brain.
Defects: short sight (corrected by a concave lens), long sight (convex lens), astigmatism (cylindrical lens), cataract (cloudy lens).

Frame for any disease: **cause → spread → symptoms → prevention → treatment → Pakistan.**

| Disease | Cause | Spread by |
|---|---|---|
| Polio | Poliovirus | Faecal–oral route |
| Diarrhoea | Rotavirus, bacteria, parasites | Dirty water and food |
| Malaria | *Plasmodium* (protozoan) | Female *Anopheles* mosquito |
| Hepatitis | Hepatitis viruses A–E | A, E: water. B, C, D: blood |
| Dengue | Dengue virus | Female *Aedes* mosquito |

## §19 Polio (asked 2018, 2022)

- **Cause:** poliovirus, an RNA virus that lives in the gut. Of three wild types, only type 1 still circulates.
- **Spread:** faecal–oral route: water or food contaminated with stool of an infected person; dirty hands. Mostly affects children under five.
- **What it does:** multiplies in the intestine. In about 1 in 200 infections it attacks motor nerve cells in the spinal cord and causes permanent paralysis, usually of the legs. Some of those paralysed die when breathing muscles fail.
- **Symptoms:** most infections show none. Others: fever, tiredness, headache, vomiting, stiff neck, limb pain, then sudden floppy paralysis.
- **Treatment:** no cure. Only supportive care and physiotherapy.
- **Prevention:**
  - **Oral polio vaccine (OPV, Sabin):** live weakened virus; drops; cheap; gives gut immunity.
  - **Inactivated polio vaccine (IPV, Salk):** killed virus; injection.
  - Clean water, toilets, hand washing.
- **World:** the Global Polio Eradication Initiative (1988) has cut cases by over 99%.
- **Pakistan:** one of only two countries where wild polio is still endemic (with Afghanistan). Cases rose to 74 in 2024 and were around 30 in 2025. Obstacles: vaccine refusals and rumours, attacks on vaccination teams, missed and mobile children, cross-border movement, poor sanitation.

## §20 Diarrhoea

- **Definition:** three or more loose or watery stools in a day.
- **Types:** acute watery (includes cholera), acute bloody (dysentery), persistent (14 days or more).
- **Causes:** viruses (rotavirus is the leading cause in young children), bacteria (*E. coli*, *Vibrio cholerae*, *Shigella*, *Salmonella*), parasites (*Giardia*, amoeba). Spread through contaminated water and food, dirty hands and flies. Malnutrition makes it worse.
- **Danger:** loss of water and salts (dehydration). Signs: thirst, dry mouth, sunken eyes, little urine, weakness. It is one of the leading killers of children under five worldwide.
- **Treatment:**
  1. **Oral rehydration salts (ORS):** glucose and salts in clean water. Glucose helps the gut absorb sodium and water.
  2. **Zinc** for 10–14 days.
  3. Keep feeding and breastfeeding.
  4. Drip (IV fluids) for severe cases. Antibiotics only for dysentery and cholera.
- **Prevention:** safe drinking water (boil or chlorinate), toilets and sewage disposal, hand washing with soap, breastfeeding only for the first six months, rotavirus vaccine, clean food handling, fly control.

## §21 Malaria

- **Cause:** *Plasmodium*, a one-celled parasite. *P. falciparum* is the most deadly; *P. vivax* is the most common in Pakistan.
- **Vector:** female *Anopheles* mosquito. It bites from dusk to dawn and breeds in still water. Ronald Ross proved mosquito transmission in 1897.
- **Life cycle:**
```
Mosquito bite -> parasites enter blood -> LIVER (multiply) -> RED BLOOD CELLS (multiply, burst)
      ^                                                              |
      |-------- mosquito takes up parasites when it bites <----------|
```
  The bursting of red cells every 48–72 hours causes the bouts of fever.
- **Symptoms:** shivering, then high fever, then sweating, in cycles; headache, vomiting, anaemia, enlarged spleen. Severe cases: coma (cerebral malaria), kidney failure, death.
- **Diagnosis:** blood film under a microscope; rapid test.
- **Treatment:** chloroquine (with primaquine to clear liver stages) for vivax; artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) for falciparum.
- **Prevention:** insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor spraying, draining or covering stagnant water, larva-eating fish, repellents, window screens. Two vaccines for children are now recommended by WHO: RTS,S (2021) and R21 (2023).
- **Burden:** over 260 million cases and about 600,000 deaths a year, mostly African children. In Pakistan cases jumped after the 2022 floods.

## §22 Hepatitis (asked 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025)

**Definition.** Inflammation of the liver. Most often caused by viruses; also by alcohol, some drugs and toxins.

| Type | Spread | Course | Vaccine | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Contaminated food and water (faecal–oral) | Short-term; recovery | Yes | Common in children |
| B | Blood, unsafe injections, sex, mother to baby | Can become chronic; cirrhosis, liver cancer | Yes | Drugs control it but do not cure |
| C | Blood: reused syringes, unscreened transfusion, unsterile dental or barber tools | Mostly chronic; silent for years | **No** | **Curable** in 8–12 weeks with direct-acting antiviral tablets |
| D | Blood; only infects people who have B | Makes B worse | Hepatitis B vaccine protects | |
| E | Contaminated water | Short-term | Limited | Dangerous in pregnancy |

**Symptoms:** tiredness, fever, loss of appetite, nausea, pain in the upper right abdomen, dark urine, pale stools, **jaundice** (yellow skin and eyes). Chronic B and C may show nothing until the liver is badly damaged.

**Prevention**
- A and E: clean water, sanitation, hand washing, safe food.
- B, C, D: new syringe every time; screened blood; sterilised surgical, dental and barber instruments; no sharing of razors or toothbrushes; safe sex; hepatitis B vaccine at birth; test pregnant women.

**Pakistan:** among the highest hepatitis C burdens in the world (roughly 9–10 million people), driven by unsafe injections and reuse of equipment. World Hepatitis Day: 28 July.

## §23 Dengue (asked 2016, 2026)

- **Causative agent:** dengue virus, an RNA virus of the *Flavivirus* group. Four types (DENV-1 to DENV-4). Recovery gives lifelong immunity to that type only. A second infection with a different type is more likely to be severe.
- **Vector:** female ***Aedes aegypti*** (also *Aedes albopictus*). Black with white stripes. Bites by day, mostly early morning and late afternoon. Breeds in clean standing water in containers: tyres, flower pots, room coolers, water tanks.
- **Transmission cycle:**
  1. A mosquito bites a person who has virus in the blood (from about 2 days before fever to 5 days after it starts).
  2. The virus multiplies in the mosquito's gut and reaches its salivary glands in 8–12 days.
  3. The mosquito stays infective for life and passes the virus on with each bite.
  4. In the new person the virus enters skin immune cells, copies its RNA, spreads in blood, and illness starts after 4–10 days.
  - It does not pass directly from person to person.
- **Role of *Aedes*:** it is the only natural link between people. It lives close to homes, bites several people for one blood meal, lays eggs that survive dry for months, and thrives in crowded cities with stored water. Monsoon rain and warmth raise its numbers.
- **Symptoms:** sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain ("breakbone fever"), rash, nausea. **Severe dengue:** bleeding from gums or nose, leaking blood vessels, low platelets, shock.
- **Treatment:** no specific drug. Rest, fluids, paracetamol. Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen (they increase bleeding). Hospital care for severe cases.
- **Prevention:** remove standing water weekly, cover tanks, larvicides, spraying during outbreaks, repellents, long sleeves, nets and screens, community clean-ups. Vaccines exist (Dengvaxia for people infected before; Qdenga) but are not in wide use in Pakistan.
- **Pakistan:** major outbreaks in Lahore (2011), nationwide (2019) and after the 2022 floods.

| Point | *Aedes* (dengue) | *Anopheles* (malaria) |
|---|---|---|
| Disease agent | Virus | Protozoan parasite |
| Biting time | Day | Night |
| Breeding water | Clean water in containers | Still pools, ponds, rice fields |
| Look | Black with white bands | Brown; rests with body tilted |

# Part 5: Biofuel

## §24 New model concept of producing biofuel (asked 2018, 2022, 2025)

**Definition.** Biofuel is fuel made from recently living matter (biomass): crops, wood, algae, animal dung, food and farm waste.

**Main biofuels and how they are made**

| Biofuel | Raw material | Process |
|---|---|---|
| Bioethanol | Sugarcane, molasses, maize, wheat | **Fermentation** by yeast: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂, then distillation. Blended with petrol (E10 = 10% ethanol). |
| Biodiesel | Vegetable oils (soy, palm, jatropha), animal fat, used cooking oil | **Transesterification:** oil + methanol with an alkali catalyst → biodiesel + glycerol |
| Biogas | Dung, sewage, food and crop waste | **Anaerobic digestion** by bacteria in a sealed tank → gas of about 60% methane and 40% CO₂. The leftover slurry is a fertilizer. |
| Syngas, bio-oil | Wood, crop residue | Gasification and pyrolysis (heating with little or no oxygen) |

**Generations of biofuel (the "new model")**

| Generation | Feedstock | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| First | Food crops: sugar, starch, vegetable oil | Simple and proven, but competes with food and land |
| Second | Non-food plant matter: straw, husks, bagasse, wood, grasses, jatropha | Cellulose is broken to sugars by enzymes and then fermented ("cellulosic ethanol"); no food conflict; costlier |
| Third | **Algae** | Very high oil yield per hectare; grows in ponds or tanks on waste water and waste CO₂; needs no farmland |
| Fourth | Genetically engineered algae and microbes; fuels made with captured CO₂ and sunlight | Aims to be carbon-negative; still at research stage |

**Biomass vs biogas (asked 2025)**

| Point | Biomass | Biogas |
|---|---|---|
| What | Organic matter used as a fuel source | Gas made from biomass by bacteria without oxygen |
| State | Solid (wood, dung, crop waste) | Gas (methane + CO₂) |
| Use | Burned directly, or converted | Cooking, lighting, engines, electricity |
| Smoke | Much smoke and ash | Clean, smokeless flame |
| Efficiency | Low when burned in open stoves | Higher |
| By-product | Ash | Slurry, a good manure |

**Ways to get energy from biomass**
1. **Direct combustion:** burn wood, dung cakes, bagasse for heat and steam.
2. **Thermochemical:** pyrolysis (charcoal, bio-oil) and gasification (syngas of CO and H₂).
3. **Biochemical:** anaerobic digestion (biogas) and fermentation (ethanol).
4. **Chemical:** transesterification (biodiesel).
5. Landfill gas capture; pressing waste into briquettes and pellets.

**Biogas plant, in steps:** dung and water are mixed → fed into an airtight digester → bacteria act in four stages (hydrolysis, acid formation, acetate formation, methane formation) → gas collects under a dome → piped to the kitchen → spent slurry flows out for use as manure.

**How biofuel promotes clean energy (asked 2018)**
- Plants absorb CO₂ while growing, so burning the fuel returns roughly the same CO₂: close to carbon-neutral.
- Less sulphur dioxide and soot than coal or diesel.
- Renewable and biodegradable.
- Turns waste into energy and cuts methane from rotting waste.
- Reduces oil imports; gives rural jobs and clean cooking fuel.

**Limits:** food-versus-fuel conflict, land and water use, deforestation for palm oil, lower energy content than petrol, energy spent in growing and processing.

**Pakistan:** sugar mills make ethanol from molasses and power from bagasse; household biogas plants run on cattle dung; large potential from livestock and crop residues.

**Diagram (biogas plant):** underground tank "digester" with a dome on top "gas holder"; inlet pipe on one side "dung + water"; outlet on the other "slurry"; pipe from the dome "gas to stove".
