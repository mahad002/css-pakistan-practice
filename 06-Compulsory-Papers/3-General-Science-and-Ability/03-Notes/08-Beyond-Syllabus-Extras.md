## §1 Mirage (asked 2017)

**Definition.** An optical illusion in which a distant object appears displaced or doubled, often looking like a pool of water. It is caused by the bending (refraction) and total internal reflection of light in layers of air at different temperatures.

**How a desert or hot-road mirage forms (inferior mirage)**
1. The ground is very hot. Air next to it is hotter and less dense than the air above. Less dense air has a lower refractive index.
2. Light coming down from the sky or a distant tree passes from cooler, denser air into warmer, thinner layers. It bends away from the normal at each layer.
3. Near the ground the ray becomes so shallow that its angle exceeds the critical angle. **Total internal reflection** occurs and the ray curves upward.
4. The eye assumes light travels in a straight line, so it sees the image below the real object, upside down, like a reflection in water.

**Superior mirage (looming).** Over cold seas or ice, cold dense air lies below warm air. Light bends downward and distant ships appear raised or floating.

```
 cool, dense air      tree  *
                             \
                              \   ray bends gradually
 hot, thin air near ground     \_______  total internal reflection
 ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\~~~~~~~~
                                         \---> eye sees an inverted image "in water"
```

## §2 AC and DC; why power stations generate AC (asked 2017)

| Point | Alternating current (AC) | Direct current (DC) |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Reverses many times a second | One direction only |
| Frequency | 50 Hz in Pakistan | Zero |
| Sources | Power-station generators (alternators) | Batteries, solar cells, adapters |
| Voltage change | Easy, with transformers | Difficult |
| Use | Mains supply, motors, heaters | Electronics, phones, LEDs, vehicles |

**Why power stations generate AC**
1. **Transformers work only on AC.** Voltage can be raised or lowered easily and cheaply.
2. **Efficient transmission.** Power is stepped up to very high voltage (132–500 kV). For the same power, high voltage means low current, and heat loss in wires (I²R) falls sharply.
3. AC generators are simpler, cheaper and more reliable than DC generators.
4. AC motors are simple and robust.
5. Where DC is needed, AC is easily converted by a rectifier (the adapter of a phone or laptop).

Most electronic appliances convert the mains AC to low-voltage DC inside. Very long lines sometimes use high-voltage DC (the Matiari–Lahore line).

## §3 Tuberculosis (TB) (asked 2024)

- **Cause:** the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (found by Robert Koch, 1882).
- **Spread:** through the air, in droplets from coughs and sneezes of a person with lung TB. Crowding, poor ventilation, malnutrition, HIV, diabetes and smoking raise the risk.
- **Affects:** mainly the lungs; can spread to bones, kidneys, brain.
- **Symptoms:** cough for more than two to three weeks, blood in sputum, evening fever, night sweats, weight loss, chest pain, tiredness.
- **Diagnosis:** sputum test, GeneXpert test, chest X-ray.
- **Treatment:** curable. Four antibiotics for two months, then two for four more months, taken under supervision (DOTS). Stopping early causes drug-resistant TB.
- **Prevention:** BCG vaccine at birth, early detection and full treatment, covering coughs, ventilation, good nutrition.
- **Burden:** TB is again the world's leading infectious killer (over a million deaths a year). Pakistan is among the five countries with the most cases. World TB Day: 24 March.

## §4 COVID-19

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cause | SARS-CoV-2, a coronavirus (RNA virus) |
| First reported | Wuhan, China, December 2019 |
| Pandemic declared | 11 March 2020 (WHO). The global emergency was ended on 5 May 2023. |
| Spread | Respiratory droplets and fine aerosols; close contact; crowded indoor spaces |
| Symptoms | Fever, cough, tiredness, sore throat, loss of taste or smell, breathlessness; severe cases: pneumonia, organ failure |
| High risk | The elderly; people with diabetes, heart or lung disease |
| Tests | RT-PCR; rapid antigen test |
| Prevention | Vaccination, masks, hand washing, distance, ventilation, isolation of cases |
| Vaccines | mRNA (Pfizer–BioNTech, Moderna), viral vector (Oxford–AstraZeneca), inactivated (Sinopharm, Sinovac) |

| Pakistan | First case 26 February 2020; about 1.5 million reported cases and about 30,000 deaths; response run by the National Command and Operation Centre with "smart lockdowns" |

Lessons: early warning, strong public health systems, vaccine equity, trust in science.

## §5 Biotechnology, GM crops and cloning (asked 2019)

**Definition.** Biotechnology is the use of living organisms, cells or their parts to make useful products or solve problems.

- **Traditional:** fermentation for bread, yoghurt, cheese, vinegar.
- **Modern:** **genetic engineering** (recombinant DNA technology): a useful gene is cut from one organism with restriction enzymes, joined into a carrier (plasmid) with ligase, and put into a host cell, which then makes the product.

| Field | Uses |
|---|---|
| Medicine | Human insulin from bacteria (1982), hepatitis B vaccine, growth hormone, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapy, DNA tests (PCR), mRNA vaccines |
| Agriculture | Genetically modified (GM) crops; tissue culture; biofertilizers and biopesticides |
| Industry | Enzymes for detergents and food; biofuels; bioplastics |
| Environment | Bacteria that clean oil spills and sewage (bioremediation) |
| Forensics | DNA fingerprinting |

**GM crops.** Plants whose DNA has been changed to add a useful trait. Examples: **Bt cotton** (carries a bacterial gene that kills bollworms; widely grown in Pakistan), herbicide-tolerant soybean, Golden Rice (extra vitamin A).
For: higher yields, less pesticide, better nutrition, tolerance of drought and salt.
Against: pests may become resistant; possible effects on other species; dependence on seed companies; public concern about safety and labelling.

**Gene editing.** CRISPR-Cas9 cuts DNA at a chosen point so a gene can be removed or corrected (Nobel Prize 2020).

**Cloning.** Making genetically identical copies.
- Gene cloning: copying a gene in bacteria.
- Reproductive cloning: a whole animal. Dolly the sheep (1996) was made by putting the nucleus of a body cell into an egg cell with its nucleus removed.
- Therapeutic cloning: making stem cells to repair tissue.
Issues: low success rate, animal welfare, and ethical objections to human cloning.

## §6 Forests and deforestation (asked 2019, 2025)

**Importance of forests to the economy**
1. Timber, firewood, paper pulp, resin, gum, medicinal plants, fruit and nuts (pine nuts), honey.
2. Jobs in forestry, furniture, sports goods, matches and tourism.
3. Protect watersheds: trees hold soil, so less silt enters dams such as Tarbela and Mangla, and floods are reduced.
4. Regulate climate and rainfall; store carbon.
5. Shelter wildlife; support grazing.
Pakistan's forest cover is only about 5% of its land. About 25% is considered desirable.

**Deforestation.** The permanent clearing of forest for other land uses.
**Causes:** fuel-wood cutting, illegal logging, farmland and housing expansion, overgrazing, fires, roads and dams, population growth.

**Environmental harm**
1. Soil erosion and landslides.
2. More floods; silting of rivers and dams.
3. Loss of wildlife habitat and species.
4. More CO₂ in the air; global warming.
5. Less rainfall; disturbed water cycle; drying springs.
6. Desertification.
7. Loss of livelihoods for forest communities.

**Remedies:** large planting drives (Billion Tree Tsunami in KP; Ten Billion Tree Tsunami nationally), alternative fuels (gas, biogas, solar), strict action against the timber mafia, community forestry, protected areas.

## §7 Waterlogging and salinity in Pakistan (asked 2017)

- **Waterlogging:** the water table rises into the root zone. Soil pores fill with water, roots get no air, and crops fail.
- **Salinity:** water rising to the surface evaporates and leaves salts behind as a white crust.
They usually occur together: the "twin menace".

**Main reasons**
1. Seepage from unlined canals and watercourses (Pakistan has one of the world's largest canal systems).
2. Over-irrigation by flooding fields.
3. Flat land with poor natural drainage.
4. Roads, railways and embankments that block drainage.
5. Year-round irrigation raising the water table.
6. High-water crops (rice, sugarcane); heavy monsoon rain and floods.

**Effects:** falling yields, abandoned land, damaged buildings and roads, poverty.

**Remedies:** lining canals; tube wells to lower the water table (the SCARP projects from 1959); surface and sub-surface drains (Left Bank Outfall Drain in Sindh); drip and sprinkler irrigation and laser land-levelling; gypsum on salt-affected soil; salt-tolerant crops and trees.

## §8 Drug abuse and drug addiction (asked 2017)

| Point | Drug abuse | Drug addiction (dependence) |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Using a drug in a harmful way, or for non-medical reasons | A long-term brain disorder with compulsive use despite harm |
| Control | The person can still choose to stop | Loss of control |
| Tolerance and withdrawal | Not necessarily | Present: needs larger doses; falls ill without the drug |
| Pattern | May be occasional | Continuous |
| Relation | May lead to addiction | The end stage of abuse |

**Drugs:** opioids (heroin, morphine), stimulants (cocaine, "ice"), depressants (alcohol, sleeping pills), cannabis (charas), hallucinogens, inhalants (glue), nicotine.
**Effects:** damage to brain, liver and heart; HIV and hepatitis from shared needles; overdose deaths; mental illness; broken families; crime; lost income.
**Control:** education in schools, treatment and rehabilitation centres, counselling, action against trafficking (Anti-Narcotics Force), control of prescription medicines.

## §9 Fossils and palaeontology (asked 2020)

**Fossils.** The preserved remains, impressions or traces of ancient organisms, found mostly in sedimentary rock.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Body fossils | Bones, teeth, shells |
| Trace fossils | Footprints, burrows, droppings |
| Moulds and casts | Shell impressions filled with mineral |
| Petrified remains | Wood turned to stone |
| Whole preserved bodies | Insects in amber, mammoths in ice |

Formation: an organism is buried quickly in mud or sand; soft parts decay; minerals replace the hard parts over a very long time.

**Palaeontology** is the scientific study of ancient life through fossils.

**Importance**
1. Evidence for evolution (linking forms such as early whales with legs).
2. Dating of rock layers and building the geological time scale.
3. Reconstruction of past climates, seas and environments.
4. Support for continental drift (the same fossils on separate continents).
5. Finding coal, oil and gas (tiny fossils guide drilling).
6. Understanding mass extinctions.
Dating methods: position in rock layers; radioactive dating (carbon-14 for remains up to about 50,000 years; uranium and potassium methods for older rocks).
Pakistan: *Pakicetus*, an early land-living ancestor of whales, and *Baluchitherium*, one of the largest land mammals, were found here.

## §10 Pollution caused by human activity (asked 2017, 2025)

| Type | Human sources | Main effects |
|---|---|---|
| Air | Vehicles, factories, power plants, burning waste | Lung and heart disease, smog, acid rain, warming |
| Water | Sewage, industrial effluent, farm run-off | Water-borne disease, death of aquatic life |
| Land / soil | Solid waste, plastics, pesticides, mining | Loss of fertile soil, polluted groundwater, disease |
| Noise | Traffic, horns, machinery, loudspeakers, aircraft | Hearing loss, stress, high blood pressure, poor sleep. Sound above about 85 decibels harms hearing over time. |
| Thermal | Hot water from power plants | Low oxygen in water; fish deaths |
| Radioactive | Nuclear tests and accidents, medical and mining waste | Cancer, genetic damage |
| Light | Excess artificial lighting | Disturbs wildlife and sleep |
| Plastic and e-waste | Throw-away packaging; discarded electronics | Microplastics; toxic metals |

**Root causes:** population growth, rapid and unplanned urbanisation, industrialisation without treatment, more vehicles, fossil fuels, deforestation, intensive farming, a throw-away lifestyle, weak enforcement of law, low awareness.

## §11 Environmental impact assessment (EIA)

**Definition.** A study made before a project is approved to predict its effects on the environment and people, and to plan how to avoid or reduce harm.

**Steps:** screening (is an EIA needed?) → scoping (which issues?) → baseline survey → prediction of impacts → mitigation plan → public hearing → review and decision → monitoring.

Pakistan: required under Section 12 of the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act 1997 and the provincial environment laws. Small projects need an Initial Environmental Examination (IEE); large ones need a full EIA.
