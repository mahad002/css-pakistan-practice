# General Science & Ability (GSA): How to Use This Pack

Paper: General Knowledge-I (General Science & Ability), CSS Competitive Examination, FPSC.
Target: **70+ out of 100**. Pass rule (FPSC CE Rules 2019, Appendix-I para 5): the three General Knowledge papers (this one, Current Affairs and Pakistan Affairs) are passed together at 120 out of 300, plus 50% aggregate across the written exam. See `../../01-Plan/Exam-Rules.md`.
Pack written: 23 Nov 2026. Latest paper analysed: CSS 2026 (held Feb 2026).

| Item | Detail |

- Science 60 = 20 MCQs + 2 written questions (40).
- Ability 40 = 2 written questions (40).

Format history: in 2016 (first year of the new syllabus) Part II had 12 questions of 10 marks and you attempted 8 (5 science + 3 ability). From 2017 the paper has used the current format.

Section B is the lever. A candidate who writes neat, fully worked ability answers can score 36+ there. Science answers are capped by examiner habit, so do not spend extra time polishing them at the cost of an ability part.

## 4. Study plan for this paper (fits 15 h/week across all subjects)

Total budget for GSA: about **110 hours** over 12 months. It is a small paper. Do not overspend on it.

| Phase | Dates | Hours | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ability warm-up | Feb–Mar 2027 | 12 | Read `04-General-Ability/01` once. Do Quantitative problems 1–75. Mark any you got wrong. |
| 2. Science first pass | Apr–Jul 2027 | 40 | One notes file per fortnight. After each file: draw every diagram once from memory, do that section's MCQs in `06-MCQ-Bank.md`. Order: Environmental → Biological → Physical C → Physical B → Physical A → Food → IT. |
| 3. MPT period | Aug–Oct 2027 | 10 | The MCQ bank doubles as MPT General Science practice. Do all 300 twice. |
| 4. Second pass + answers | Nov–Dec 2027 | 30 | Reread notes with `07-Quick-Revision.md` open. Hand-write 20 of the 40 model answers under time (8 minutes each). Finish Quantitative 76–150, all Reasoning and Mental Ability items. |
| 5. Timed papers | Jan 2028 | 15 | Three full past papers (2024, 2025, 2026) in 3 hours each, by hand, no calculator. Mark with this pack. |
| 6. Last week | Feb 2028 | 3 | `07-Quick-Revision.md` only. Redo the problems you marked wrong. |

Why Environmental Science first: it is the largest, most repeated science section and overlaps with Essay, Current Affairs and Pakistan Affairs.

For each notes file use this loop:
1. Read one topic.
2. Close the file. Write the 5-mark answer in 8 minutes by hand, with the diagram.
3. Compare with the notes. Fix gaps in a different colour.
4. Two days later, redraw only the diagram and list the headings.

## 5. Exam-day time plan

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | MCQs. Fill the OMR as you go. Leave no blank. Keep 3 minutes to check that bubbles match question numbers. |
| 0:25–0:30 | While waiting for the OMR to be collected, read Part II and tick your four questions. |
| 0:30–1:45 | **Section B first** (2 questions, about 37 minutes each, about 9 minutes per part). You are fresh and these marks are certain. |
| 1:45–2:55 | Section A (2 questions, 35 minutes each, 8–9 minutes per part). |
| 2:55–3:00 | Check question numbers, cross blank pages, underline headings. |

Rules for choosing questions:

- Read all four parts of a question before choosing. One part you cannot answer costs 5 marks. Pick the question where you can write at least 3.5 parts well.
- In Section A, prefer questions where you can draw a diagram for at least two parts.
- In Section B, prefer calculation parts over theory parts ("define sampling", "explain mental ability scales"). Calculation gets 5/5; theory gets 3/5.
- If a problem statement is flawed (it happens: see 2026 Q7(a) and Q7(b) in `02-Past-Paper-Analysis.md`), state your assumption in one line and solve. Do not leave it.

## 6. How to write a 5-mark science answer

Length: 120–180 words plus one diagram. About three-quarters of a page. Time: 8 minutes.

Structure:

1. **Definition** in one or two sentences. Underline the term.
2. **Diagram**, labelled, in pencil, drawn early so the examiner sees it first.
3. **Body** as 4–6 short points under 2–3 small headings (for example: Causes, Effects, Control).
4. **Example or figure**: one named example, date, or number. Use a Pakistan example where one exists (2005 Kashmir earthquake, 2010 and 2022 floods, Lahore smog, Tarbela, Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park).
5. **One closing line** only if the question says "evaluate" or "discuss".

How marks are usually split (inferred from solved papers and examiner comments; FPSC does not publish a scheme):

| Element | Share of 5 marks |
|---|---|
| Correct definition or statement | 1 |
| Core explanation (mechanism, types, steps) | 2 |
| Diagram, table or equation | 1 |
| Example, application, neat layout | 1 |

Match the command word:

| Word | What to give |
|---|---|
| Define | One precise sentence. Add an example. |
| Differentiate / distinguish | A table. 4–5 rows. One example row. |
| Explain / describe | Mechanism in steps, with a diagram. |
| Discuss / evaluate | Both sides (benefits and problems), then a one-line judgement. |
| Write a short note | Definition, 3–4 points, one use or example. |
| Draw | Large labelled diagram plus 3–4 lines on function. |

If a part has two sub-parts, split the space and the marks evenly (2.5 + 2.5). Label them (i) and (ii).

Diagrams:
- Use pencil and a ruler. Label with straight horizontal lines. Give the figure a title.
- Size: about one-third of the page width, not thumbnail.
- Twelve diagrams cover most papers. They are listed in `07-Quick-Revision.md`. Learn them until you can draw each in under 90 seconds.

Chemical equations and formulas count as "diagrams" for chemistry answers. Write them on their own line.

## 7. How to write an ability answer

- Write **Given**, **Required**, **Solution**, **Answer** for each part. Box the final answer with units.
- Show every step. A CSS coaching note based on checked scripts says a 5-mark part needs all steps shown and reasoning answers should be explained in words.
- No calculator: simplify fractions before multiplying, use π = 22/7 unless told otherwise, and leave awkward results as fractions (for example 28 4/7 days).
- For puzzles (seating, ordering, blood relations): draw the diagram or table, then write the deductions as numbered steps.
- For "explain" parts (fallacy, assumptions, sampling, mental ability scales): definition, 3 points, one example. Keep to half a page.
- Check by substitution. It takes 20 seconds and protects 5 marks.

## 8. What examiners have said

From the FPSC Examiners' Report for CE-2018 on this paper (read from a third-party copy of the FPSC report; wording paraphrased):

- Most candidates were weak in space science.
- Most lacked the analytical skill needed for Section II. Many confused compass directions (north with south, east with west). Some lacked elementary maths.
- Answers should be precise, neat, clean and readable. No unnecessary explanation.
- Choose questions with care.
- Use a graph, diagram or table where needed.
- Keep all parts of a question together and in order.

A summary of the CE-2019 report (seen only as a search summary, not the full text) says the general standard in this paper was average, over half the scripts scored under 39%, and the ability section was done better than the science section.

What this means for you: space science (universe, black holes, eclipses, Kepler) and compass-direction problems are easy marks that most candidates lose. Both are covered here in full.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. Spending 50 minutes on the first science question and rushing ability.
2. Writing essays for 5-mark parts. Two pages get the same 3 marks as three-quarters of a page.
3. No diagram where one is obvious (fibre, cell, atmosphere layers, water cycle, eclipse, heart).
4. Attempting three questions from one section. The extra one is not marked.
5. Parts of one question scattered across the answer book.
6. In ability, writing only the final number. No steps, no marks if the number is wrong.
7. Using a memorised formula in the wrong setting (average speed is total distance over total time, not the mean of speeds).
8. Getting compass turns wrong. Always draw the path with N at the top.
9. Leaving MCQs blank. There is no negative marking.
10. Old facts: Pluto as a planet, 9 planets, ozone hole "still growing" (it is slowly recovering), Kyoto as the current climate treaty (Paris Agreement, 2015, now leads).
11. Ignoring units. Write m², cm³, km/h, Rs.
12. Choosing a theory-heavy ability question when a calculation one was available.
