# Mental Abilities: Theory, Methods and 50 Practice Items

Syllabus (Section VIII): Mental Abilities Scales that measure specific constructs such as verbal, mechanical, numerical and social ability.

Two kinds of question come from this line:
1. **Theory** (asked 2016, 2020, 2022, 2026): what mental ability scales are, how they differ from IQ tests, aptitude vs intelligence, factors affecting intelligence, social skills, what a mechanical reasoning scale measures.
2. **Test items** (every year): jumbled words, number puzzles, counting figures, and in 2026 a gear problem.

A theory part earns about 3–3.5 out of 5. A solved item earns 5. Prefer items when you have a choice, but know the theory: it is short.

# Part 1: Theory

## §1 Mental ability and its measurement

**Mental ability** is a person's capacity to learn, understand, reason, solve problems and adapt to new situations.

**Mental ability scale.** A standardised psychological test that measures one or more specific abilities (verbal, numerical, mechanical, spatial, social, memory, speed of perception) and reports a separate score for each. The result is a profile of strengths and weaknesses.

**Qualities of a good test:** reliability (gives consistent results), validity (measures what it claims), standardisation (same instructions, timing and scoring for all), norms (scores are compared with a reference group).

### Mental ability scales vs IQ tests (asked 2016, 2020)

| Point | Mental ability scales | IQ tests |
|---|---|---|
| Measure | Separate, specific abilities | General intelligence as a whole |
| Result | A profile of several scores | One summary score (IQ) |
| View of intelligence | Many factors | One general factor "g" |
| Use | Career guidance, selection, job placement, finding specific strengths and weaknesses | Classifying overall ability; identifying gifted children or learning difficulty |
| Examples | Differential Aptitude Tests, Thurstone's Primary Mental Abilities test, mechanical and clerical tests | Stanford–Binet, Wechsler scales (WAIS, WISC), Raven's Progressive Matrices |

**IQ (intelligence quotient).** Originally IQ = mental age ÷ chronological age × 100. Modern tests compare a person with others of the same age: the average is 100 and about two-thirds of people score between 85 and 115. Alfred Binet and Theodore Simon made the first intelligence test in 1905.

### Primary and secondary mental abilities (asked 2016)

**Primary mental abilities (L. L. Thurstone, 1938).** Seven separate basic abilities:

| Ability | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verbal comprehension | Understanding words and passages |
| Word fluency | Producing words quickly |
| Number facility | Fast, accurate arithmetic |
| Spatial visualisation | Picturing shapes and their rotation |
| Associative memory | Rote recall |
| Perceptual speed | Spotting details quickly |
| Reasoning | Finding rules and patterns |

**Secondary mental abilities.** Broader abilities found when primary abilities are grouped, because they tend to go together. The best known (Cattell and Horn):
- **Fluid intelligence:** reasoning on new problems, independent of learning. Peaks in early adulthood.
- **Crystallised intelligence:** knowledge and skills gained from education and experience. Keeps growing with age.

| Point | Primary abilities | Secondary abilities |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Specific, narrow | Broad clusters |
| Number | Seven (Thurstone) | Few (fluid, crystallised and others) |
| Derived from | Test scores directly | Relations among primary abilities |

**Other theories (one line each)**
- Spearman: a general factor "g" plus specific factors "s".
- Gardner: multiple intelligences (linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalist).
- Sternberg: analytical, creative and practical intelligence.
- Goleman: emotional intelligence (knowing and managing emotions).

### Aptitude vs intelligence (asked 2022)

| Point | Aptitude | Intelligence |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Potential to learn a **particular** skill or do well in a particular field with training | **General** capacity to learn, reason and adapt |
| Scope | Specific (mechanical, musical, clerical, numerical) | Broad |
| Tells us | What a person can become good at | How able a person is overall |
| Measured by | Aptitude tests (Differential Aptitude Tests) | IQ tests |
| Use | Career and course selection | Educational classification |

A third term: **achievement** is what a person has already learned (an exam result).

### Factors affecting intelligence (asked 2022)

| Group | Factors |
|---|---|
| Heredity | Genes set a range of potential. Identical twins have very similar scores. |
| Biological | Nutrition before birth and in early childhood (protein, iodine, iron); health of mother and child; birth injuries; diseases; toxins such as lead; sleep |
| Home | Stimulation, books, talk and play; parents' education; emotional security; family income |
| Education | Quality and years of schooling; good teachers |
| Social and cultural | Peer group, language, opportunities, cultural value placed on learning |
| Personal | Motivation, emotional state, stress, age |

Heredity and environment act together: genes set the limits; environment decides how far the potential is reached.

## §2 Verbal ability

The ability to understand and use words. Test items: jumbled words, analogies, odd one out, word order, comprehension, sentence completion, verbal reasoning about arguments.

**Jumbled words: method** (asked every year 2021–2026; five words, one mark each)
1. Count the letters. Circle the vowels.
2. Look for common endings: -TION, -ING, -ER, -ED, -LY, -ICAL, -ENCE, -SIS.
3. Look for common starts and pairs: TH, CH, SH, ST, QU, PR, IN-, AB-, HY-.
4. Use the theme if one is given. In 2026 the words were "logical reasoning and research terms".
5. Write the answer in capitals and tick off each letter.

| Year | Jumbled | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | THRSI, GNDREA, SCHAMOT, ONLNDO, HIODALY | SHIRT, GARDEN (DANGER also fits), STOMACH, LONDON, HOLIDAY |
| 2023 | LNUGEF, CKANS, CIREFE, EERAANMOGTP, MNIKPPU | ENGULF, SNACK, FIERCE, POMEGRANATE, PUMPKIN |
| 2026 | NALYLTCAIA, EFNRECNIE, OCNDIUTNI, NRBTASACIOT, SHTESYPHOI | ANALYTICAL, INFERENCE, INDUCTION, ABSTRACTION, HYPOTHESIS |

If two words fit (GARDEN and DANGER), write both.

Vocabulary for future themed sets: DEDUCTION, PREMISE, SYLLOGISM, VARIABLE, SAMPLING, FALLACY, ANALOGY, VALIDITY, CORRELATION, ASSUMPTION, CONCLUSION, EVIDENCE.

## §3 Numerical ability

The ability to work with numbers quickly and accurately: operations, series, number puzzles, counting, simple word problems. Methods are in `01-Quantitative-Ability-Methods.md` and `03-Logical-and-Analytical-Reasoning.md` §1.

**Counting figures (asked 2021)**
- Triangles formed by lines from one vertex to n points on the opposite side: the base has (n + 1) segments, giving (n + 1)(n + 2)/2 triangles.
- Squares in an n × n grid: 1² + 2² + … + n².
- Rectangles in an m × n grid: C(m + 1, 2) × C(n + 1, 2).
- Otherwise count by size: smallest first, then those made of 2 pieces, 3 pieces, and so on. Label the pieces.

## §4 Mechanical ability

The ability to understand and apply physical and mechanical principles: forces, levers, gears, pulleys, pressure, simple circuits.

| Device | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Gears (meshed)** | Neighbouring gears turn in **opposite** directions. Teeth × turns is the same for both: T₁N₁ = T₂N₂. The smaller gear turns faster. In a row, gears 1, 3, 5 turn one way and 2, 4, 6 the other. |
| Belt and pulleys | Open belt: same direction. Crossed belt: opposite. Diameter × speed is the same for both. |
| **Lever** | Load × load arm = effort × effort arm. Class 1: pivot in the middle (seesaw, scissors). Class 2: load in the middle (wheelbarrow). Class 3: effort in the middle (tweezers, fishing rod). |
| Pulleys | A fixed pulley only changes direction. With n supporting ropes the effort is load ÷ n, and the rope must be pulled n times as far. |
| Inclined plane | A longer ramp needs less force for the same height. Work done is the same. |
| Pressure | Pressure = force ÷ area. Liquid pressure rises with depth. Hydraulic press: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂. |
| Stability | A low centre of gravity and a wide base make an object stable. |
| Circuits | Series: one path; if one bulb fails all go out. Parallel: separate paths; each bulb gets the full voltage. |

**Worked (2026).** Gear A has 12 teeth and gear B has 36. They mesh.
(i) A turns clockwise, so **B turns anticlockwise**.
(ii) For B to make 2 turns: 12 × N = 36 × 2 → **N = 6 turns of A**.
(iii) How a mechanical reasoning scale tests generalisation: it shows pictures of unfamiliar devices and asks what will happen, so the person must apply a principle rather than recall a fact. The same principle (for example the gear ratio) appears in different settings such as bicycles, clocks and belts. Items get harder step by step. Pictures keep reading skill out of the score. The score is compared with norms. A person who answers novel items correctly shows that the principle has been understood and can be transferred. Example of such a test: the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test.

## §5 Social ability

**Social ability (social intelligence)** is the capacity to understand other people and to act wisely in dealing with them. E. L. Thorndike introduced the idea in 1920.

**Social skills (asked 2020)** are the learned behaviours we use to communicate and get on with others.

| Skill | Shown by |
|---|---|
| Communication | Clear speech; eye contact; suitable tone and body language |
| Active listening | Attention; not interrupting; checking understanding |
| Empathy | Seeing the other person's feelings and point of view |
| Cooperation | Sharing, teamwork, taking turns |
| Conflict resolution | Staying calm; negotiating; finding common ground |
| Assertiveness | Stating one's view firmly and politely |
| Respect and manners | Courtesy to all, whatever their rank |

**Causes of weak social skills (asked 2020)**
1. **Shyness and social anxiety:** fear of being judged leads to avoidance, so skills are never practised.
2. **Poor early socialisation:** over-protective, harsh or neglectful parenting; few chances to play with other children.
3. **Too much screen time:** phones and games replace face-to-face talk.
4. **Developmental and health conditions:** autism spectrum conditions, ADHD, speech or hearing problems, depression.
Others: low self-esteem, bullying or trauma, a new language or culture.

**How it is measured:** situational judgement tests (choose the best response to a described situation), rating scales, observation, interviews.

**Answering situational items:** choose the response that is calm, respectful, honest, solves the problem at the lowest level first, and follows rules. Avoid aggressive, passive or dishonest options.

## Numerical (21–32)

21. A number is doubled and 9 is added. The result is 37. Find the number.
22. Find the missing number: (2, 3, 7), (4, 5, 21), (6, 7, ?)
23. If 5 workers make 5 toys in 5 minutes, how long do 100 workers take to make 100 toys?
24. A snail climbs 3 m up a 10 m wall each day and slips back 2 m each night. On which day does it reach the top?
25. How many squares of all sizes are there in a 3 × 3 grid of small squares?
26. A clock strikes once at 1 o'clock, twice at 2 o'clock, and so on. How many times does it strike in 12 hours?
27. Find the missing number: 3 (12) 4; 5 (30) 6; 7 (?) 8
28. If the day before yesterday was Thursday, what day is the day after tomorrow?
29. A father is three times as old as his son. Their ages add up to 48. Find both ages.
30. Six people each shake hands once with every other person. How many handshakes?
31. How many times is the digit 3 written when you write the numbers from 1 to 50?
32. A soap packet carries one token. Four tokens can be exchanged for a free packet (which also carries a token). How many free packets can a person get by buying 64 packets?

**Solutions 21–32**

21. 2x + 9 = 37 → x = 14. **14**
22. Third number = first × second + 1: 6 × 7 + 1. **43**
23. One worker makes one toy in 5 minutes. A hundred workers make a hundred toys in the same time. **5 minutes**
24. Net gain is 1 m a day. After 7 days it is at 7 m. On day 8 it climbs 3 m and reaches 10 m before slipping. **Day 8**
25. 1 × 1 squares: 9; 2 × 2: 4; 3 × 3: 1. **14**
26. 1 + 2 + … + 12 = 12 × 13/2. **78**
27. The middle number is the product: 7 × 8. **56**
28. Yesterday was Friday; today is Saturday; tomorrow Sunday. **Monday**
29. s + 3s = 48 → s = 12. **Son 12, father 36**
30. C(6, 2) = 6 × 5/2. **15**
31. Units place: 3, 13, 23, 33, 43 (5 times). Tens place: 30 to 39 (10 times). **15**
32. 64 tokens → 16 free; 16 tokens → 4 free; 4 tokens → 1 free. **21**

## Mechanical (33–40)

33. Gear A with 15 teeth meshes with gear B with 45 teeth. A turns clockwise at 120 revolutions a minute. Give B's direction and speed.
34. Four gears A, B, C, D mesh in a row. A turns clockwise. Which way does D turn?
35. A 40 kg child sits 1.5 m from the pivot of a seesaw. How far from the pivot must a 30 kg child sit to balance it?
36. A 600 N load hangs from a pulley system with 4 supporting ropes. Ignoring friction, what effort lifts it? How much rope must be pulled to raise the load 1 m?
37. A crate must be raised 1 m onto a truck. Is it easier to push it up a ramp 2 m long or 5 m long? Is less work done?
38. Pulley A (diameter 10 cm) drives pulley B (diameter 30 cm) by an open belt. A turns clockwise at 300 revolutions a minute. Give B's direction and speed.
39. In a hydraulic press a force of 50 N acts on a small piston of area 10 cm². What force acts on the large piston of area 200 cm²?
40. Two identical bulbs are joined to a battery (a) in series, (b) in parallel. In which case are they brighter? What happens in each case if one bulb fails?

**Solutions 33–40**

33. Meshed gears turn opposite ways. 15 × 120 = 45 × N → N = 40. **Anticlockwise, 40 rpm**
34. A clockwise, B anticlockwise, C clockwise, D anticlockwise. **Anticlockwise**
35. 40 × 1.5 = 30 × d → d = 2. **2 m**
36. Effort = 600 ÷ 4. Rope = 4 × 1 m. **150 N; 4 m**
37. The 5 m ramp needs less force: about one-fifth of the weight instead of one-half, ignoring friction. The work done is the same, because the height is the same. **The 5 m ramp; no, the work is the same**
38. An open belt keeps the same direction. 10 × 300 = 30 × N → N = 100. **Clockwise, 100 rpm**
39. Pressure is equal: 50/10 = F/200 → F = 1,000. **1,000 N**
40. **Parallel is brighter**: each bulb gets the full battery voltage; in series they share it. If one fails: in series the other goes out; in parallel the other stays on.

## Spatial and non-verbal (41–46)

41. In triangle ABC, three straight lines are drawn from A to three points on BC. How many triangles are in the figure?
42. A clock seen in a mirror appears to show 3:15. What is the real time?
43. A cube painted red on all faces is cut into 27 equal small cubes. How many small cubes have paint on exactly (i) three faces, (ii) two faces, (iii) one face, (iv) no face?
44. On a standard die opposite faces add up to 7. A die shows 1 on top and 2 at the front. What numbers are on the bottom and the back?
45. How many lines of symmetry does the capital letter H have? Which of the letters A, B, H, N, S have no line of symmetry?
46. How many rectangles (including squares) are there in a grid 2 squares high and 3 squares wide?

**Solutions 41–46**

41. BC is cut into 4 segments. Triangles = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 (or C(5, 2)). **10**
42. Subtract from 11:60. 11:60 − 3:15. **8:45**
43. Corners 8; edge middles 12; face centres 6; centre 1. **(i) 8 (ii) 12 (iii) 6 (iv) 1** (total 27)
44. Bottom = 7 − 1; back = 7 − 2. **Bottom 6; back 5**
45. H: one vertical and one horizontal. **2. N and S have none** (they have only rotational symmetry).
46. Choose 2 of the 3 horizontal lines and 2 of the 4 vertical lines: 3 × 6. **18**

## Social (47–50)

Choose the best response and give one line of reason.

47. In a meeting a colleague presents your work as his own.
(a) Challenge him angrily in front of everyone. (b) Say nothing, now or later. (c) Speak to him privately and calmly, state the facts, and ask that credit be shared; go to the supervisor only if it happens again. (d) Tell other colleagues about it.

48. Two members of the team you lead keep arguing and the work is suffering.
(a) Ignore it. (b) Report both to the head office at once. (c) Listen to each one separately, then bring them together to agree on how they will work. (d) Take one side.

49. An angry citizen comes to your office about a file delayed for two months.
(a) Tell him to calm down or leave. (b) Listen without interrupting, acknowledge the delay, check the file, tell him the next step and a date, and follow up. (c) Blame another department. (d) Promise it will be done today without checking.

50. A new colleague has sat alone and silent for a week.
(a) Leave him alone; he will settle in. (b) Introduce yourself, show him around, and invite him to join the team for lunch. (c) Joke about how quiet he is. (d) Ask the supervisor to move him.

**Solutions 47–50**

47. **(c)** Direct, calm and private. It protects the working relationship and uses the lowest level first.
48. **(c)** A leader first understands both sides, then mediates and sets clear rules.
49. **(b)** Listening and a concrete next step reduce anger; honesty builds trust. (d) is a promise you may not keep.
50. **(b)** Shows empathy and initiative; inclusion builds the team.
